Daily Devotional Verses



Proverbs 11:7

NKJV (New King James Version)

The Folly of Wickedness

When a wicked man dies, his expectation will perish, And the hope of the unjust perishes.

GUIDE REFLECTION:

Some Hopes Last Only for This Life

There are many things we expect to give us a better future—money, a home, a job, possessions, and security for our family. There is nothing wrong with working hard for these things. But we need to ask:

What happens when these things become the foundation of our hope?

Solomon said:

“When the wicked die, their hope dies with them; the hope they placed in wealth comes to nothing.”

The day will come when we must leave behind everything we have accumulated in this world. A person may possess great wealth and achieve tremendous success, but when death comes, none of these things can save him or keep him from standing before God.

Proverbs is not saying that having money is wrong. The warning is about trust. We must not place in wealth the hope that only God can provide.

👉 The things that pass away are not a sufficient foundation for a hope that must endure for eternity.

Wealth Is a Tool, Not a Savior

Money has value and usefulness. We use it for food, housing, education, the needs of our families, helping others, and supporting the work of God.

But there are things money can never buy. It cannot erase sin, cleanse the conscience, restore our relationship with God, or purchase eternal life.

This is where wealth becomes dangerous. Something we are meant to use can become something we begin to trust. When we have plenty of money, we may think we are secure. When we have little, we may believe that everything will finally be all right once our income increases.

But no matter how much we possess, wealth has its limits.

👉 Money can be a blessing from God, but it can never do the work that only God can do.

Where Our Hope Is Placed

We do not have to be wealthy to make money the center of our lives. We may have very little and still constantly believe that having more money will finally give us security.

We can see this in our fears. When our savings decrease, our peace disappears with them. When our livelihood is not improving, it can feel as though there is no longer any hope for the future.

We can also see it in our ambitions. We may gradually sacrifice family, rest, service, and our relationship with God because we think we first need to become successful.

There is nothing wrong with working hard. But we need to ask:

“Once I have this, will I truly be secure?”

👉 We need to examine not only how much money we have, but where our security and hope are truly rooted.

In Christ We Have a Hope That Does Not End in Death

If our hope is tied only to the things we possess in this world, that hope will disappear when we leave this world behind.

Jesus taught that it is worthless for a person to gain the whole world and yet lose his own soul. Our deepest problem is not a lack of money but the sin that separates us from God. And no amount of wealth can redeem us from it.

Our redemption was accomplished by Jesus Christ through His life given for us on the cross. In His death, He paid the penalty we could never pay. In His resurrection, He showed that even death cannot bring an end to the hope found in Him.

Jobs, businesses, savings, and possessions may be lost, but the life Christ gives reaches beyond death.

👉 We leave our wealth behind at death, but the hope rooted in Christ reaches into eternal life.

Use Wealth Without Trusting in It

This verse does not mean that we should neglect our livelihood. We can work, save, provide for our families, and use wisely what God has entrusted to us.

But we need to guard our hearts. Savings can be part of responsible preparation, but they should never become the foundation of our peace. When additional income comes, ask:

“How can I use this faithfully for God?”

When we face financial need, we should bring our fears to God rather than allowing fear to control us.

In this way, money becomes a tool rather than a master. We use it responsibly while keeping our hearts anchored in Christ.

👉 Live responsibly with the things that pass away, but build your hope on Christ, who never passes away.

Living It Out

Examine your heart:

  • Where do I truly place my security and hope?
  • When I lose money or my source of income, where do I turn first?
  • Am I sacrificing anything because of an excessive pursuit of money or success?
  • How can I faithfully use what God has entrusted to me?

A heart that has learned to hope in Christ can still work hard, save, and prepare for the future. But it no longer asks wealth to provide the security that only God can give.

CONCLUSION

Proverbs 11:7 reminds us that not every hope can carry us all the way to the end. The day will come when we must leave behind everything we have accumulated and achieved.

So the most important question is not how much we possess, but where our hope is rooted. If it rests on wealth or success, the moment will come when those things can no longer help us.

But in Jesus Christ, there is a hope that does not end in death. His grace cannot be bought, His salvation does not depend on our wealth, and the life He gives is eternal.

👉 True riches are found in a hope rooted in Jesus Christ and in the eternal life found in Him.

The question:

If you had to leave behind everything you have accumulated and achieved in this world, would there still remain a steadfast hope because your heart is truly resting in Jesus Christ?
