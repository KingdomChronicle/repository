Daily Devotional Verses



Proverbs 11:9

NKJV (New King James Version)

The Folly of Wickedness

The hypocrite with his mouth destroys his neighbor, But through knowledge the righteous will be delivered.

GUIDE REFLECTION:

Our Words Can Destroy or Deliver

There are wounds that cannot be seen but take a long time to heal. Sometimes, they are not caused by a blow or any form of physical harm. It may take only a few words to destroy a person's reputation, friendship, trust, or spirit.

This is the power of speech that Solomon places before us.

Solomon said:

“The lips of the godless destroy others, but the wisdom of the righteous delivers their neighbor.”

The verse presents two contrasting kinds of speech. One is used to destroy, while the other is guided by wisdom that becomes a means of delivering others from harm.

Words can destroy through lies, slander, false accusations, deception, or the deliberate spreading of information that can harm others. When the fear of God does not govern the mouth, words can easily be used as weapons to get what we want.

But the person guided by God's wisdom is different. He does not simply accept everything he hears. Nor does he immediately pass along a story merely because it is interesting. He seeks the truth and considers how what he says will affect others.

👉 The mouth can become a weapon of destruction or an instrument of deliverance; God's wisdom teaches us how to use it.

Wisdom Knows How to Handle the Truth

Lying is not the only thing that can harm others. Careless speech, listening to only one side of a story, and passing along information without first determining whether it is true can also cause harm.

When we hear a negative story about someone, it is easy to form an immediate conclusion. This becomes even more difficult when the story is about someone we do not like or someone who has hurt us before.

But wisdom knows how to pause. It asks questions before passing judgment. It listens before speaking. It understands that there may be parts of the story we do not yet know.

There are also times when we need to speak in order to defend someone who has been falsely accused, correct false information, or stop a story that could harm others. In this way, knowledge and wisdom can become a means of deliverance.

This does not mean that we should cover up genuine sin or avoid speaking difficult truths. Wisdom is not the concealment of truth. It is the use of truth in a way that is just, careful, and consistent with the will of God.

👉 A wise person does not only ask whether what he is about to say is true, but whether it is right, just, and necessary to say it in the way he has chosen.

Destructive Speech Begins in the Heart

We often regret the words that come out of our mouths. But if we truly want to change, it is not enough merely to guard our tongues. We also need to examine what drives us to speak the way we do.

When we are envious, it may become easy for us to talk about the weaknesses of someone who has succeeded. When we are angry, we may say something not to correct the problem but to hurt the person who hurt us.

When we want to be accepted by a group, we may join in gossip so that we can feel that we belong. When we want to prove that we are right, we may choose only the details of a story that make our side look good and the other side look bad.

Sometimes, the root is pride. We may feel better about ourselves when someone else's failures are exposed because, by comparison, we seem better.

Here we see that the problem of destructive speech is not merely a problem of the mouth. It is also a problem of a heart that desires revenge, recognition, control, or a sense of superiority over others.

So before asking, “Should I say this?” it can also help to ask, “Why do I want to say this?” The information may be true, but the motive driving us to reveal it may be wrong.

👉 If we want God to transform our speech, we must allow Him to examine not only our words but also the motives from which they come.

In Christ We See Words Full of Truth and Love

When we examine our speech, we see that all of us have failed. There are words we can no longer take back, stories we should never have passed along, and times when we used the truth not to help but to hurt.

That is why it is not enough simply to say that we need to be more careful with our words. We need Jesus Christ.

In Jesus, we see the perfect union of truth and love. He did not cover up sin or change the truth merely to make Himself acceptable to people. But neither did He use the truth to humiliate those who came to Him in repentance.

Jesus taught that love for God cannot be separated from love for our neighbor. Therefore, the way we speak to other people is also part of our obedience to Him.

But before we are called to speak like Christ, we first need to receive the grace that comes from Him. On the cross, He bore the punishment even for the sins we have committed with our mouths. There is forgiveness for our lies, slander, gossip, and hurtful words.

And we are not only forgiven. Through the Holy Spirit, God changes our hearts so that we learn to love the truth, love our neighbor, and use our mouths in ways that give grace to others.

👉 In Christ, there is forgiveness for destructive speech and a new heart that is learning to speak the truth in love.

Use Your Words to Protect Others

Words can spread very easily in our time. A single message in a group chat, a post, a comment, or one click of the share button can send information to many people within seconds.

That is why we need wisdom that knows how to pause before speaking or sharing. Something is not necessarily true just because many people have shared it. And just because a story is shocking does not mean that we need to pass it along.

When you hear an accusation against someone, do not immediately pass judgment. If you do not know the whole story, be careful about drawing conclusions. And if the information does not need to be known by others, the wisest thing may be not to pass it along at all.

At home, use your words to correct without humiliating. At work, do not destroy a colleague's reputation merely to advance yourself. In the church, do not make a fellow believer's weakness a topic of conversation when that person should instead be helped and prayed for.

There are also times when loving our neighbor requires us to speak. If someone is being mistreated, falsely accused, or harmed by lies, God may use your voice to defend the truth and prevent further harm.

Before speaking, ask: Is this true? Do I know the whole story? Does this need to be said? Am I speaking to the right person? And does the way I am speaking reflect the love of Christ?

👉 A mouth governed by wisdom does not speak merely because it knows something; it speaks in the right way to serve the truth and the good of others.

Living It Out: How Do I Use My Words?

Take time to examine yourself.

  • When I hear a negative story about someone, what do I usually do before believing it or passing it along to others?

  • What motive most often drives me to speak against others—anger, envy, revenge, the desire for acceptance, or the desire to prove that I am right?

  • Is there someone I have hurt through lies, gossip, careless accusations, or words that I need to repent of and, when appropriate, make right?

  • In my home, workplace, church, group chats, and social media, how can I use my words more intentionally to protect the truth and the good of others?

  • What part of my heart do I need to surrender to Christ so that my words may be shaped more by His truth, wisdom, and love?

As we allow Christ to transform our hearts, He gradually transforms our speech as well, so that our mouths become not a means of destruction but instruments of truth, love, and grace.

CONCLUSION

Proverbs 11:9 reminds us of the tremendous power of our words. They can destroy a person's reputation, trust, relationships, and spirit. But they can also be used to correct lies, defend the oppressed, give wisdom, and keep others from harm.

So the question is not merely how often we speak, but what kind of heart is speaking through us. When our mouths are governed by anger, envy, pride, or revenge, our words can easily become destructive.

But in Jesus Christ, there is forgiveness for the words we have used wrongly. And through His Spirit, He can transform the heart from which our speech flows so that we learn to speak the truth in love and use our voices for the good of others.

👉 A mouth truly surrendered to Christ does not look for opportunities to destroy, but learns to use truth and wisdom to love, protect, and serve others.

The question:

When you examine the way you speak about other people, does it reveal a heart being shaped by the truth and love of Christ, or is there still anger, envy, pride, or revenge that you need to surrender to Him?
