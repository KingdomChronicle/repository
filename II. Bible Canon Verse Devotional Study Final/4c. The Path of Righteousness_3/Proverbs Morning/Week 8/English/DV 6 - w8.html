Daily Devotional Verses



Proverbs 11:16

AMP (Amplified Bible)

The Folly of Wickedness

A gracious and good woman attains honor, and ruthless men attain riches [but not respect].

GUIDE REFLECTION:

Some Things Matter More Than Riches

It is easy to measure success by what we can see. How much do we earn? What have we accumulated? What position have we reached? But Solomon reminds us that not everything of true value can be measured by material success.

He said:

“A gracious and good woman attains honor, and ruthless men attain riches [but not respect].”

This verse places two very different kinds of gain before us. A gracious and good woman attains honor, while ruthless men may attain riches—but not the respect that comes from good character.

Solomon is not teaching that riches are evil. A person can prosper honestly and use what he has for good. The warning is this: material success is not the same as honorable success. A person may become wealthy or successful while losing the trust of others and damaging his character and relationships.

👉 True success is measured not only by what we attain, but also by the kind of person we become while pursuing it.

Honor Grows from a Gracious Character

The woman in this verse is described as “gracious and good.” Being gracious does not mean being weak or passive. A gracious person can be strong, determined, courageous, and wise without using that strength to trample on others.

Abigail is a beautiful example of this. Her husband Nabal was wealthy, but he was harsh and badly behaved. When Nabal's foolish response to David placed their household in danger, Abigail responded with wisdom, courage, humility, and grace, helping to prevent bloodshed.

Abigail's graciousness was not weakness. She faced danger without resorting to pride, force, or manipulation. She could not control her husband's character, but she remained responsible for how she herself responded.

👉 Honor is not something we can simply demand from others. Often, it grows out of a character that consistently demonstrates wisdom, goodness, humility, and graciousness.

Riches Do Not Automatically Bring Respect

It is significant that Solomon does not say ruthless people always fail materially. He acknowledges that “ruthless men attain riches.” A person may be manipulative, exploitative, or willing to trample on others and still become financially successful.

But the Amplified Bible adds an important clarification: “[but not respect].”

There is a difference between having your power acknowledged and having your character genuinely respected. People may comply because they are afraid of losing their jobs, flatter someone who controls money or opportunities, or remain silent because they fear retaliation. Fear, dependence, and forced submission are not genuine respect.

A ruthless person may possess riches, influence, or authority, but none of these can replace the trust and respect that come from good character.

👉 Riches may give a person influence, but they cannot buy the kind of honor that comes from integrity and gracious character.

Do Not Trade Character for Gain

There are times when a shortcut to success seems tempting. We may earn more by hiding the truth, advance more quickly by tearing others down, win a negotiation by exploiting someone else's weakness, or gain control through intimidation.

In moments like these, the important question is not only, “What can I attain?” We should also ask, “What kind of person will I become if I attain it this way?”

Money can sometimes be recovered, a business can be rebuilt, and new opportunities can come. But damaged integrity, lost trust, and a ruined reputation are not easily restored. There is nothing wrong with prospering; the danger begins when we are willing to sacrifice integrity, goodness, and honor in order to attain it.

👉 Do not trade a character worthy of respect for a gain that may eventually be lost.

Strength Can Be Used in Different Ways

The word “ruthless” also challenges us to examine how we use whatever power we have. That power may come from money, authority, knowledge, influence, position, experience, connections, or ability.

The question is: how do we use that advantage?

  • A business owner or leader can use authority to exploit others or to treat them with justice and dignity.
  • A person with money can use riches to control others or to become a channel of help and blessing.
  • A person with influence, knowledge, or ability can use these things to promote himself or to strengthen the people around him.

Strength itself is not the problem. What matters is the kind of character that governs how we use it.

Graciousness is not the absence of strength. It is strength that refuses to become ruthless, exploitative, or selfish.

👉 The question is not only whether we have strength, influence, or resources, but whether they are governed by graciousness.

Christ Shows Us Strength Governed by Grace

When we honestly examine ourselves, we see that we do not always choose gracious character over personal gain. We can become selfish, controlling, or proud, and we may use our advantages to get what we want. That is why we need more than the advice, “Be kinder.” We need Jesus Christ.

In Christ, we see perfect strength without ruthlessness. He possesses the highest authority, yet He did not use His power to exploit the weak. Jesus said:

“For even the Son of Man did not come to be served, but to serve, and to give His life a ransom for many.” — Mark 10:45

Ruthlessness asks, “What can I get from others for myself?” Christlike graciousness asks, “How can I use what has been given to me for the good of others and according to God's will?” His greatness was revealed through service and sacrificial love.

At the cross, He bore the penalty for our sin—including greed, pride, selfish ambition, ruthlessness, and the times when we valued personal gain more than faithfulness to God. Through the Holy Spirit, He continues to shape our character so that our strength, resources, position, and abilities can be used for good rather than to control or exploit others.

👉 In Christ, we learn that true greatness is not measured by how much we can get from others, but by how faithfully we use what God has entrusted to us for His glory and the good of others.

Prosper Without Losing Your Character

As we seek to excel in our work, improve our family's circumstances, develop our abilities, or increase our capacity to help others, we must also pay attention to the kind of person we are becoming.

At work, pursue excellence without damaging another person's reputation. In business, pursue profit without exploiting the weaknesses of others. If you have authority, do not use fear simply to get your way. If you have greater knowledge, experience, wealth, or influence, do not use those advantages to look down on others.

A bank account can grow while character shrinks; an organization can grow while trust disappears; influence can expand while kindness, humility, and integrity fade. So it is not enough to ask whether we are prospering—we must also ask how we are growing as people while we prosper.

👉 Seek to grow and prosper, but do not let your riches, influence, or success grow faster than your integrity, goodness, and gracious character.

Putting It into Practice: What Matters Most to Me?

Take time to examine yourself.

  • When I think about success, am I more focused on what I can attain, even if attaining it requires me to sacrifice honesty, kindness, or integrity?

  • How do I use the authority, money, position, influence, knowledge, or abilities God has entrusted to me, especially when dealing with people who have less power or nothing to offer me in return?

  • When I face pressure or conflict, do I respond with wisdom and self-control like Abigail, or do I become controlling, ruthless, and selfish?

  • What part of my character do I need to surrender to Christ so that He can develop greater graciousness, goodness, humility, and integrity in me?

CONCLUSION

Proverbs 11:16 reminds us that riches and honor are not the same. A ruthless person may attain riches, but riches cannot buy the trust and respect that come from good character.

In Jesus Christ, we see strength perfectly governed by grace. Through His Spirit, He can shape us to use our power, resources, influence, and abilities not to exploit others, but to serve, to be a blessing, and to honor God.

👉 You may attain riches through ruthlessness, but riches cannot buy the respect that gracious character attains. So do not measure your life only by what you have accumulated or achieved. Ask whether Christ is shaping you into a person whose character is worthy of honor.

The question:

As you pursue a better life, greater success, or more resources, are you using your strength, influence, abilities, and opportunities with graciousness—or are there areas of your life where the desire for gain is becoming more important than the kind of person Christ is shaping you to become?
