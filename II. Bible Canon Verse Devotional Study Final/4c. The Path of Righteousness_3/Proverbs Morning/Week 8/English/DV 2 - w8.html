Daily Devotional Verses



Proverbs 11:12

NKJV (New King James Version)

The Folly of Wickedness

He who is devoid of wisdom despises his neighbor, But a man of understanding holds his peace.

GUIDE REFLECTION:

The Wisdom of Knowing When to Be Silent

There are times when we are hurt because of what someone has done or said. When our emotions become intense, it is easy to think that we need to respond immediately. We want to defend ourselves, prove that we are right, or say something hurtful in return to the person who hurt us.

But Solomon shows us that there are moments when the wisest response is not to answer right away.

He says:

“He who is devoid of wisdom despises his neighbor, but a man of understanding holds his peace.”

A person who lacks wisdom is quick to show contempt. He may belittle another person's character, abilities, mistakes, or circumstances. Instead of seeking to resolve a problem, he uses his words to tear others down.

But a person of understanding responds differently. He knows that not every thought needs to be spoken and not every situation requires an immediate response. Some words, once spoken, cannot be taken back.

The silence described here is not a sign of weakness. It is restraint that comes from wisdom—the ability to control our words when we know that speaking will only cause more hurt or increase conflict.

👉 Wisdom is not only shown by our ability to speak what is right, but also by our ability to recognize when it is better to remain silent.

Not Every Word Needs an Answer

We often think that if we do not respond, others may see us as weak or unable to defend ourselves. So when someone insults us, we want to fight back. When someone criticizes us, we want to immediately explain our side.

But not every word requires a response. Some arguments only become bigger when we engage in them. Some comments lose their power when we refuse to respond with the same kind of speech.

Silence also gives us time to think. When we do not respond immediately, we have an opportunity to examine what we actually heard, what the real issue is, and how we can answer without being controlled by anger.

This does not mean that we should never speak against what is wrong. There are times when we need to explain, correct, set boundaries, and stand for truth. But there is a difference between speaking to correct what is wrong and speaking to shame another person.

👉 We do not need to answer every word; sometimes, controlling our speech is the first step toward preventing a conflict from becoming worse.

Contempt Reveals the Heart

The deeper problem is not only the hurtful words that come from our mouths. Before we speak harshly against someone, something has already happened within our hearts.

We may be angry because we were hurt. We may be jealous and searching for another person's weaknesses. We may also have an overly high view of ourselves, causing us to look down on those who are not as skilled, intelligent, or successful as we are.

Sometimes, we use mocking words because we want others to laugh with us. Another person's weakness, appearance, mistakes, or situation becomes a joke. But the fact that many people find it funny does not mean it is harmless.

This happens even more easily on social media because we do not see the face of the person we are criticizing. A sarcastic comment that takes only a few seconds to write can remain in someone's mind and heart for a long time.

That is why we must examine our hearts before we only focus on controlling our words.

  • Why do we want to put this person down?
  • Why do we feel satisfaction when we see someone embarrassed?
  • Why is it so important for us to prove that we are better than them?

👉 When we despise others, we do not only reveal their weaknesses; we also reveal what may be within our own hearts—pride, anger, or a lack of love.

In Christ We Learn to Respond with Grace

When we honestly examine our speech, we realize that all of us have words we wish we could take back. There have been times when we mocked others, retaliated, spoke out of anger, or used someone's weakness to make ourselves look better.

That is why it is not enough to simply say that we need to learn how to be silent. We need a heart that is transformed by God.

In Jesus Christ, we see the perfect example of wisdom. He was insulted, falsely accused, and rejected. Yet He was not controlled by revenge. He knew when to speak truth and when there was no need to answer those who only wanted to attack Him.

He also did not come to crush sinners who approached Him with repentance. His truth carried complete authority, but His dealings with people were also filled with grace.

And for those of us who have failed with our words, there is forgiveness in Him. On the cross, Christ carried the punishment for our pride, anger, contempt, and every sin that has come from our mouths.

Through the Holy Spirit, He also changes our hearts so that we no longer feel the need to retaliate every time we are hurt. We can entrust our reputation to God and learn to speak or remain silent according to what pleases Him.

👉 In Christ, there is forgiveness for our hurtful words and grace that teaches us when to speak and when to remain silent.

Stop Words Before They Create Wounds

This wisdom is deeply practical in our everyday relationships. At home, there may be moments when a spouse, child, parent, or sibling says something painful. We do not need to respond immediately while our emotions are still intense.

At work, someone may criticize us or fail to recognize our efforts. Before making a sarcastic remark or speaking against a coworker, we can pause and examine our hearts.

On social media and in group chats, it is even more important to learn patience before responding. We do not need to answer every post that irritates us. Sometimes, waiting a few minutes is enough to realize that what we were about to write was only a reaction of anger.

When we truly need to speak, let us do so clearly and respectfully. Restraining contempt does not mean that we ignore wrongdoing. It simply means that we do not have to destroy someone's dignity in order to express the truth.

👉 Before speaking while angry or hurt, give your heart time to submit to Christ so that your response does not become a wound you will later regret.

Living It Out: Do I Know When to Be Silent?

Take time to examine yourself.

  • When someone hurts or irritates me, what is my usual first response—retaliating immediately, embarrassing the person, or pausing before I speak?

  • What anger, jealousy, pride, or insecurity may be behind the moments when I belittle or mock others?

  • Is there someone I have hurt through sarcasm or harsh words who I need to ask forgiveness from?

  • In my home, workplace, church, group chats, or social media, where do I need to learn not to respond to every criticism or provocation?

  • How can I entrust my reputation and emotions to Christ so that I do not feel the need to respond by putting others down?

A heart that learns to trust Christ does not need to win every argument. It can speak when necessary and remain silent when silence brings greater good.

CONCLUSION

Proverbs 11:12 reminds us that true wisdom is not measured by how quickly we respond. Sometimes, greater wisdom is required to restrain a word than to speak it.

Contempt easily comes out when we are controlled by anger, pride, or the desire for revenge. But a person of understanding learns to pause and consider not only what he can say, but what is right to say before God.

In Jesus Christ, there is forgiveness for the words we cannot take back. And through His Spirit, He can shape our hearts so that our mouths become not instruments of contempt, but channels of truth, self-control, love, and grace.

👉 A person being shaped by Christ does not need to use his words to tear others down; he learns to speak with wisdom and remain silent when silence better demonstrates love.

The question:

When you are hurt, criticized, or angry, are your words controlled by the desire to defend and exalt yourself, or can you surrender them to Christ and allow Him to teach you when to speak and when it is better to remain silent?
