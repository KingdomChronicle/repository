Daily Devotional Verses



Proverbs 11:15

NKJV (New King James Version)

The Folly of Wickedness

He who is surety for a stranger will suffer, But one who hates being surety is secure.

GUIDE REFLECTION:

Goodness Guided by Wisdom

A willingness to help others is a good quality. As believers, we are called to be generous, giving, and willing to use what has been entrusted to us for the good of others. But Scripture also teaches us that good intentions must be guided by wisdom.

This is Solomon's warning:

“He who is surety for a stranger will suffer, but one who hates being surety is secure.”

Solomon is referring to becoming surety for another person's obligation—putting our own name, property, or ability to pay on the line as security for a debt or obligation that someone else is expected to fulfill. If that person fails to pay, what was originally their responsibility may become ours and may even affect others who depend on us.

But Solomon is not teaching that generosity, helping others, or making sacrifices for their good is wrong. His warning concerns the danger of becoming surety for someone else's obligation and taking on a responsibility that we may not fully understand or be able to bear.

👉 A willingness to help is good, but it must be guided by wisdom that understands what is being promised, what it may cost, and what responsibility is actually being assumed.

Do Not Make a Promise Without Understanding What You Are Taking On

Do not take on a major financial obligation simply because you are embarrassed to say no, afraid of damaging a relationship, pressured by others, or eager to prove that you are a good friend or relative.

There is a difference between a sacrifice whose cost we clearly understand and willingly choose to bear and an obligation we enter into without carefully considering its possible consequences. It may be wise to refuse a risky obligation while still being willing to give or make a sacrifice when we understand the cost and believe it is a faithful use of what has been entrusted to us.

👉 The size of a sacrifice alone does not determine whether a decision is wise or reckless. What matters is whether we understand what we are taking on, why we are doing it, and whether we are prepared to bear its true cost.

Stewardship Is About More Than Protecting What We Have

As believers, we should not treat our income, possessions, abilities, and opportunities as though we can use them however we please without any accountability to God. He has entrusted these things to us, and we are called to manage them wisely.

But good stewardship does not mean that our highest goal is to keep everything safely for ourselves. A steward is not the owner. So the question is not only, “How can I protect what I have?” but also, “How can I faithfully use what God has entrusted to me?”

Good stewardship also means considering our spouse, children, parents, employees, ministry, or others who depend on us. It is not wise to make a commitment out of compassion for one person while neglecting other responsibilities that God has already entrusted to our care.

👉 A good steward does not merely know how to protect what has been entrusted to him. He also knows when to give, when to sacrifice, and when a commitment is no longer wise but has become a responsibility taken on recklessly.

There Is a Difference Between Reckless Obligation and Willing Sacrifice

Not every costly sacrifice is unwise. There are times when we clearly understand what we may lose and still willingly choose to make the sacrifice as an expression of faithfulness to God. We see this in the life of Jonathan.

As King Saul's son, Jonathan would naturally have been expected to succeed his father. But when he recognized what God was doing through David, he did not treat David as a rival who had to be removed in order to protect his own future. Instead, he loved and protected David even though he knew that doing so would come at a real personal cost.

Jonathan was not blind to the possible consequences of his actions. He understood who David was and where God's purpose for him was leading. He told David:

“Do not fear... You shall be king over Israel, and I shall be next to you.” — 1 Samuel 23:17

Jonathan knew that David becoming king meant that he would not succeed his father. Even so, he helped preserve David's life, and although Saul became angry with him because of it, Jonathan chose faithfulness over personal advantage.

In time, David did become king of Israel. We should not say that Jonathan alone was responsible for the fulfillment of God's purpose for David—God Himself preserved David and brought him into the purpose He had appointed for him. But Jonathan's faithfulness and sacrifice were genuinely used as part of God's preservation of David during an important period of his life.

👉 Jonathan shows us that not every decision involving great personal cost is unwise. There are times when we clearly understand what we may lose and still willingly choose to give, help, or sacrifice because we value faithfulness to God and His purpose more than personal gain.

We see the same principle in the believers in Macedonia, who were generous despite their poverty:

“Even though they experienced many trials and hardships, their joy overflowed, and even though they were very poor, they were still generous.” — 2 Corinthians 8:2

They were not commended for being careless. Their generosity flowed from hearts that had willingly given themselves to God.

👉 Wisdom is not the enemy of sacrifice. It helps us recognize the difference between a responsibility we take on recklessly and a sacrifice whose cost we clearly understand, willingly choose, and are prepared to bear as part of our faithfulness to God.

Compassion Should Not Be Separated from Wisdom

There are times when love calls for genuine sacrifice, but there are also times when repeatedly taking responsibility for someone who continues to make irresponsible decisions is no longer truly helpful. There is a difference between helping someone in need and removing a responsibility that person needs to learn to face.

True love seeks what will be beneficial in the long run. Sometimes money or other resources are what someone genuinely needs; at other times, it may be more helpful to guide that person in creating a sound budget, facing existing obligations, finding work or another practical solution, and learning to live within their means.

So the question is not only, “Am I willing to help?” but also, “What kind of help will truly be beneficial?” We can be generous without allowing pressure, guilt, manipulation, or impulsive emotion to make the decision for us.

👉 Godly love does not blindly agree to every request. It is willing to sacrifice when doing so will truly help, but it is also willing to set boundaries when that is the wiser and more loving thing to do.

In Christ We Learn to Be Both Generous and Wise

When it comes to money, helping others, and taking responsibility, it is easy to fall into one of two extremes. We may act recklessly and make commitments we have not carefully considered, or we may use the word “wisdom” simply to protect ourselves and avoid helping whenever it would genuinely cost us something. We need hearts that are both generous and wise, and for this we need Jesus Christ.

In Christ, we see love that is truly willing to sacrifice according to the Father's will and for the genuine good of others. He said:

“Greater love has no one than this, than to lay down one's life for his friends.” — John 15:13

We cannot make avoiding every sacrifice a principle of the Christian life, but neither do we have to accept every demand placed upon us. Christ is our Lord, so our money, time, strength, and abilities should be submitted to His will, not merely to the pressure of other people.

And for those of us who have failed to be good stewards, there is grace in Him. There is forgiveness for our recklessness, selfishness, fear, and the decisions we have made without seeking the wisdom of God.

Through the Holy Spirit, He can transform the way we use what has been entrusted to us so that we manage it with love, faith, wisdom, and faithfulness to God.

👉 In Christ, we learn to be generous without being reckless, careful without becoming selfish, and willing to sacrifice without simply giving in to human pressure.

Help While Understanding the Responsibility You Are Taking On

When you are asked to become a guarantor or co-signer, first understand the responsibility you may be assuming and how it could affect those who depend on you. Do not make the decision merely out of embarrassment, pity, pressure, or guilt. If becoming surety would be unwise but you still want to help, look for another way to help that you can responsibly afford.

When making major financial decisions, pray, seek wise counsel, and examine your motives. A genuine sacrifice whose cost you clearly understand and willingly accept in obedience to God is not automatically wrong simply because it costs you something.

👉 Do not let pressure push you into a responsibility you do not understand, but neither let the fear of losing something keep you from making a willing sacrifice that is in accordance with God's will.

Putting It into Practice: Is My Help Guided by Wisdom?

Take time to examine yourself.

  • When I help others or make commitments on their behalf, do I understand what I may become responsible for, or am I giving in to embarrassment, guilt, or pressure?

  • Do I consider the responsibilities and people entrusted to my care before making a major commitment?

  • Do I use “being careful” as a reason to avoid helping, or am I willing to sacrifice when doing so is wise and honors God?

  • How can I use my money, abilities, and other resources both generously and wisely according to God's will?

CONCLUSION

Proverbs 11:15 reminds us that some promises are easy to make but difficult to carry. When we put our name down as surety for another person's obligation, we may take on a responsibility we never expected to bear. But God's wisdom does not teach us to avoid every sacrifice. A good steward knows when to say “no” to the wrong responsibility and when to say “yes” to help or sacrifice whose cost is clearly understood and that is in accordance with God's will.

👉 Wisdom from God does not teach us to avoid every sacrifice. It teaches us not to be reckless with our commitments, so that when we give, help, or sacrifice, we do so with understanding, willingly, responsibly, and with hearts submitted to God's will.

The question:

When you have an opportunity to help or take responsibility for someone else, are you willing to ask God for wisdom to discern whether what is being asked of you is a reckless commitment you should refuse or a sacrifice you can willingly accept as a faithful use of what He has entrusted to you?
