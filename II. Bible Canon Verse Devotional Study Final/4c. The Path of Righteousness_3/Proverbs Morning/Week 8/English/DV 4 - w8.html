Daily Devotional Verses



Proverbs 11:14

NKJV (New King James Version)

The Folly of Wickedness

Where there is no counsel, the people fall; But in the multitude of counselors there is safety.

GUIDE REFLECTION:

The Wisdom to Seek Counsel

Some decisions are easy to make on our own. But the more important a decision becomes, the greater the risk of relying solely on our own perspective.

This is the principle Solomon places before us:

“Where there is no counsel, the people fall; But in the multitude of counselors there is safety.”

Solomon gives us the picture of a nation in need of wise leadership and sound guidance. Without good counsel, the poor decisions of its leaders can affect countless people. But when wise counsel is available, dangers become easier to recognize, and things that one person might overlook can be brought to light.

This principle is just as important in our personal lives. God did not create us to know everything. There are times when we need the wisdom of others to help us see more clearly the path we are taking.

👉 A wise person does not assume that he can see everything; he is willing to listen to counsel that can help correct and guide his path.

Not All Advice Is Wisdom

This verse does not mean that a decision is automatically right simply because many people agree with it. Having many advisers does not guarantee wisdom. It also matters whom we listen to, where their counsel comes from, and whether that counsel is truly consistent with the wisdom and will of God.

We see a clear example of this in the life of Rehoboam, Solomon's son. When he became king after his father, the Israelites came to him and asked him to lighten the heavy burden that had been placed upon them during Solomon's reign. Rehoboam first sought the advice of the older counselors who had served his father.

They advised him to serve the people and respond to them with kindness. They said:

“If you will be a servant to these people today, and serve them, and answer them, and speak good words to them, then they will be your servants forever.” — 1 Kings 12:7

But Rehoboam did not like their advice. Instead, he turned to the young men who had grown up with him and now served him. Their advice was the exact opposite: rather than lighten the people's burden, he should show them that his rule would be even harsher and more demanding than his father's.

Rehoboam chose the advice of the young men he had grown up with and rejected the counsel of the experienced elders. The result was far more serious than a minor leadership mistake. A large part of Israel rebelled against the house of David, and the kingdom was divided.

Bad counsel, combined with a heart willing to follow it, became part of a decision with enormous consequences.

It is important to notice that Rehoboam did not lack people to consult. He sought advice from more than one group. The problem was which advice he chose to follow. Wisdom had already been placed before him, but he rejected it in favor of counsel that better suited the kind of leadership he wanted to exercise.

We can fall into the same trap. We may say that we have sought advice because we talked to several people, but if we keep asking until someone finally tells us what we want to hear, we are not really seeking wisdom. We are seeking approval.

That is why it matters whom we ask for advice. A good counselor is not simply someone who is close to us or someone who always agrees with us.

A good counselor is someone who fears God, has godly character, understands the situation well enough to offer sound counsel, and is willing to tell us the truth even when it is not what we want to hear.

Truly seeking counsel also requires a heart that is willing to change its mind. When others see something we have missed, we need the humility to listen and reconsider our decision. Simply disliking the advice we receive is not a good reason to reject it. Sometimes the advice that is hardest to accept is the very advice we need to consider most carefully.

What happened to Rehoboam reminds us that having many advisers is not enough. What matters is whom we listen to and whether we have a heart willing to receive wisdom even when it goes against what we want to do.

👉 Good counsel does not simply tell us what we want to hear; it helps us see truths we may not want to face.

Pride Does Not Want to Be Corrected

Sometimes the problem is not that we lack people who can give us advice. There may already be people willing to tell us what is right, but we simply do not want to listen.

Once we have made up our minds, it is easy to treat anyone who questions our decision as an obstacle. When someone points out a weakness in our plan, we may assume that person does not support us. When someone warns us, we may feel that our ability or judgment is being questioned.

Pride can be behind this response. We want to be right. We do not want to admit that there may be something we have failed to see. At other times, our desire for something may be so strong that what we call “seeking advice” is really nothing more than looking for someone to give us permission to do what we have already decided to do.

A wise person learns to receive even the advice that is difficult to hear. A faithful friend may question our motives, point out weaknesses in our plans, or warn us about the direction we are taking.

Not every criticism is right, but neither is every criticism an attack. Sometimes God uses other people to show us something we cannot see because we are too close to the situation.

👉 A heart that truly seeks wisdom is not only willing to receive advice; it is also willing to be corrected.

In Christ We Learn Humility

When we honestly examine ourselves, we begin to see how difficult it can be to listen when advice goes against what we want. Our problem is not simply a lack of information. There is also pride in our hearts that wants to have the final word.

This is where we need Jesus Christ. He is the perfect example of a life submitted to the wisdom and will of the Father. He did not build His life around personal ambition, but lived in complete obedience to the Father, even to the cross.

We, on the other hand, have chosen our own way many times. But on the cross, Christ bore the punishment for our pride and disobedience. In Him, there is forgiveness when we confess that we have chosen to trust ourselves rather than God.

And through the Holy Spirit, He gives us hearts that are willing to be taught. We learn to come to God's Word as our primary standard and to value the people He uses to bring wisdom into our lives.

Human counsel is not a substitute for God's guidance. Rather, when it is consistent with His Word, it can be one of the ways He chooses to guide and protect us.

👉 In Christ, we learn to lay down the pride that always wants to be right and develop hearts that are willing to be taught, corrected, and guided by God.

Do Not Make Major Decisions Inside Your Own World

This principle is especially important when we face major decisions. They may involve marriage, work, business, finances, ministry, moving to a new place, or responsibilities that will also affect other people.

Before making a decision, pray and examine God's Word. Then seek out people who have wisdom and a sufficient understanding of the situation. Do not choose only those you already know will agree with you.

When someone offers a different perspective, do not immediately become defensive. Listen first. Ask whether that person sees a danger you have overlooked or whether there may be a motive in your heart that you need to examine.

At the same time, do not hand over responsibility for your decision to someone else. Seeking counsel does not mean allowing others to make the decision for you. Its purpose is to give you a clearer understanding so that you can make a wise and responsible decision before God.

👉 Before making an important decision, have the humility to pray, listen to God's Word, and receive honest counsel from people who have wisdom.

Living It Out: Am I Willing to Listen and Be Corrected?

Take time to examine yourself.

  • When I am making an important decision, who are the people I trust to tell me the truth even when it is not what I want to hear?

  • When someone gives me advice that goes against my plans, do I listen first, or do I immediately defend myself?

  • Is there a decision I am making right now in which I am only looking for approval rather than truly seeking wisdom?

  • Do the people I seek advice from help me follow God's Word, or do I choose only those who reinforce what I already want?

  • What part of my pride or self-reliance do I need to surrender to Christ so that I can become more willing to be taught and corrected?

A person who knows how to listen does not lose the ability to make decisions. Instead, he allows God to use His Word and the wisdom of others to help him see the path ahead more clearly.

CONCLUSION

Proverbs 11:14 reminds us that it is dangerous to go through life listening only to our own voice. Our knowledge is limited, and there are things we cannot see clearly—not only about our circumstances, but even about our own hearts.

Seeking counsel, then, is not a sign of weakness. It can be an expression of humility and wisdom, especially when we are willing to listen to people who will not simply agree with us but will faithfully help us see what is right.

Above all, we need Christ. In Him, there is forgiveness for our pride and grace that shapes us into people who are willing to be taught. As His Spirit leads us and His Word shapes us, we learn to receive faithful counsel as one of the ways God may choose to guard and guide our path.

👉 True wisdom is not having all the answers, but having the humility to submit to God and listen to the faithful counsel He uses to guide us.

The question:

When the advice you receive goes against what you want to do, are you willing to surrender to Christ your desire to always be right and listen with a heart that is genuinely seeking God's wisdom?
