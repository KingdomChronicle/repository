Daily Devotional Verses



Proverbs 11:11

NKJV (New King James Version)

The Folly of Wickedness

By the blessing of the upright the city is exalted, But it is overthrown by the mouth of the wicked.

GUIDE REFLECTION:

Words That Build a Community

The words we speak do not affect only our own lives. In our homes, workplaces, churches, and communities, our speech can strengthen trust and unity, or it can produce doubt, confusion, and division.

Solomon presents this contrast in a very clear way:

“By the blessing of the upright the city is exalted, but it is overthrown by the mouth of the wicked.”

A righteous person does more than simply avoid speaking lies. His words are shaped by truth, wisdom, and integrity. Because of this, his speech can contribute to trust, fairness, peace, and stability wherever God has placed him.

In contrast, lies can cause damage far beyond the person who first hears them. False reports, gossip, misleading promises, and intentional twisting of the truth can destroy relationships and cause people to lose confidence in one another.

👉 Godly speech is not merely a personal quality; it can become a means of strengthening the people and communities around us.

Truth Creates Trust

A healthy family, church, organization, or community needs trust to remain strong. But trust becomes difficult when people cannot depend on one another's words.

When we know that someone consistently speaks honestly, we do not have to doubt everything he tells us. When he gives his word, we can have confidence that he means it. And when he makes a mistake, he can acknowledge it instead of creating excuses or false explanations to protect himself.

This is one way a righteous person becomes a blessing to those around him. His honesty gives others a greater sense of confidence and security.

This also means seeking the welfare of the place where God has positioned us. Rather than constantly contributing complaints, criticism, or division, we can pray, speak wisely, work faithfully, and do what we can to promote the good of those around us.

👉 Whenever we choose honesty and pursue what is good for others, we help create an environment where trust and stability can grow.

A Lie Can Grow Into Something Much Bigger

There are times when we assume that a false statement will not cause much harm. Perhaps we simply want to protect our image, escape blame, or make someone feel sorry for us.

Yet a lie can quickly spread beyond what we intended. A false statement about someone can become gossip. That gossip can develop into an accusation, and the accusation can eventually damage someone's reputation, relationships, and even the unity of an entire group.

This danger is even greater today. One post, comment, screenshot, or message sent to a group can quickly reach many people before anyone has taken the time to determine whether the information is actually true.

But every lie also points to something within the heart that needs examination. Why do we change the truth? Maybe we fear embarrassment. Perhaps we want to control how others view us. Or maybe our words are being driven by anger, jealousy, or the desire to make another person look bad.

For this reason, guarding our mouths is not enough. We must also allow God to search and transform the heart from which our words come.

👉 A lie may begin in one person's mouth, but the damage it causes can reach many lives.

Christ Shows Us the Truth That Brings Life

When we honestly evaluate the way we speak, we know that we have not always handled the truth faithfully. There have been moments when we have exaggerated something, withheld important facts, spoken without knowing the whole situation, or used our words to defend ourselves while hurting someone else.

We cannot overcome this simply by deciding to watch our words more carefully. What we truly need is a heart that God Himself is renewing and changing.

Jesus Christ gives us the perfect example of truthfulness. He never changed the truth simply to gain approval or protect His reputation. He spoke honestly, but His truth was always expressed with love, compassion, and grace.

For those of us who have failed in this area, Christ is not merely someone we should imitate. At the cross, He carried the punishment for our sins. This means there is forgiveness even for the lies, harmful words, and deception we have spoken.

Through the Holy Spirit, He also works within us and teaches us to value and love the truth. The transformation of our speech is not something we do to earn God's acceptance; it is evidence of the new life God has given us through Christ.

👉 In Christ, we find forgiveness for the ways we have misused our words and receive a new heart that is learning to speak truth that brings life to others.

Use Your Words to Strengthen Others

We do not need an important title or powerful position to make a positive difference in our community. Wherever God has placed us, our words can influence others.

At home, we can speak honestly instead of manipulating our spouse or children. At work, we can refuse to join conversations that spread gossip or damage someone's reputation. In the church, we can choose words that encourage others and help preserve unity.

As members of our communities, we can pray for those who have authority and seek the welfare of the place where God has allowed us to live. Seeking what is good does not mean remaining silent about wrongdoing. We can stand for truth and what is right without using lies, slander, or hatred.

Before passing information along, we should consider whether it is true and whether we have enough knowledge to speak about it responsibly. Before speaking against another person, we should examine our motives and ask whether we are trying to help correct something wrong or simply trying to cause that person pain.

👉 Let us use our voices not to increase the conflict and noise around us, but to speak truth, share wisdom, and promote what is good wherever God has placed us.

Living It Out: Are My Words Building Others Up?

Take a moment to honestly examine your life.

  • When I talk about other people, do my words strengthen trust, or do they create doubt and division?

  • What fear, anger, jealousy, or desire for approval may be influencing me to hide, change, or exaggerate the truth?

  • Is there a lie or harmful statement I need to confess before God and, when necessary, correct with the people who were affected?

  • At home, at work, in church, on social media, and in my community, what can I do to use my voice to promote truth and what is good?

  • How can I surrender my words more fully to Christ so that my speech increasingly reflects His truth, wisdom, and love?

Whenever we choose to speak truthfully and lovingly, we have an opportunity to help strengthen the people and places God has entrusted to us.

CONCLUSION

Proverbs 11:11 reminds us that our words can influence many people beyond ourselves. Truth can strengthen trust and unity, while falsehood can produce suspicion, conflict, and destruction.

Therefore, we should ask more than whether the words we speak are technically true. We should also examine the condition of our hearts and consider what kind of fruit our words are producing in the lives of others.

In Christ, we receive forgiveness for the times we have used our words wrongly. Through His Spirit, He can transform us into people whose speech is increasingly shaped by truth and love, allowing us to become a blessing in our families, churches, workplaces, and communities.

👉 A heart surrendered to Christ learns to use words not to destroy or divide, but to strengthen others, speak truth, and pursue their good.

The question:

When you look at the words you regularly speak and the way you use your voice, are you helping strengthen the people God has placed around you, or is there still something in your heart and speech that needs to be surrendered to Christ's rule?
