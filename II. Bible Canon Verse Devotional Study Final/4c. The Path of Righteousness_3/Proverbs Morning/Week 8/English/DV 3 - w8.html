Daily Devotional Verses



Proverbs 11:13

NKJV (New King James Version)

The Folly of Wickedness

A talebearer reveals secrets, But he who is of a faithful spirit conceals a matter.

GUIDE REFLECTION:

A Person Who Can Be Trusted

Some things are shared with us because someone trusts us. A friend may open up about what they are going through, a family member may confide in us about a personal struggle, or a fellow believer may share a burden they have been carrying for a long time.

In moments like these, we are receiving more than information. Someone is placing their trust in our hands.

Solomon said:

“A talebearer reveals secrets, but a faithful person knows how to keep a confidence.”

The contrast is clear. A gossip cannot keep something that was never meant to be passed along, while a trustworthy person knows how to guard what has been entrusted to them.

This does not mean that everything we know must remain secret under every circumstance. There are times when we need to seek help, especially when someone is in danger or when abuse or serious harm is involved. But those situations do not give us permission to turn someone else's private life into casual conversation.

👉 Trustworthiness is revealed by how we handle what others have entrusted to us.

Not Everything We Know Needs to Be Shared

Knowing something that others do not can bring a particular kind of temptation. We may feel important because we heard it first, or we may enjoy becoming the center of attention because we have something new to tell.

This is where wisdom is needed. The question is not simply whether the information is true. We must also ask whether it is ours to share.

It may be true that a married couple is going through difficulties, but that does not mean we are free to spread their story. It may be true that a friend made a mistake, but that information was not entrusted to us so that we could make it a topic of conversation.

Even the truth can be handled wrongly. When we share someone's personal matters with people who have no reason to know them, we can destroy trust that may have taken years to build.

A wise person knows to ask:

  • Does this need to be said?
  • Am I the right person to say it?
  • Does the person I am speaking to need to know this?
  • Do I have a good reason for sharing it?

👉 Not everything we know to be true is ours to share; wisdom teaches us to honor the boundaries of the trust others have placed in us.

When Someone Else's Story Becomes Our Entertainment

Gossip is not merely a problem of the mouth. Like many sins of speech, it begins in the heart.

Sometimes we talk about others because we want attention. Other times, we share someone's failure because we are angry with them. We may even feel a strange sense of satisfaction when we discover that someone admired by others has weaknesses too.

We may even disguise our motives with reasons that sound good. We say that we are only concerned or that the matter needs prayer. But if we are really passing along personal information to people who cannot actually help, then we need to be honest about what is happening in our hearts.

It is better to ask:

  • If I were in this person's situation, would I want my weakness to be handled this way?
  • If I had trusted someone with this, how would I feel if I discovered that what I shared had become a topic of conversation?

Love for our neighbor does not delight in opportunities to expose their weaknesses. Instead, it seeks to treat others with dignity and wisdom.

👉 When we enjoy talking about the weaknesses of others, we need to examine not only what comes out of our mouths, but also the heart that takes pleasure in information that was never ours to use.

In Christ We Learn to Be Faithful with What Is Entrusted to Us

When we honestly examine ourselves, we may remember times when we failed to guard the trust others placed in us. Perhaps we passed along stories, shared personal matters without permission, or spoke words that damaged someone's reputation or relationships.

It is not enough simply to promise ourselves that we will be more careful next time. We need to come to Jesus Christ, because the problem with our speech is ultimately connected to the condition of our hearts.

In Jesus, we see perfect love for others. He knows the whole truth about those who come to Him—their sins, weaknesses, and failures—yet He never uses their weakness to entertain others or to exalt Himself.

More than that, Christ knows the whole truth about us. No sin, secret, or shameful part of our lives is hidden from Him. Yet when we come to Him in repentance and faith, He does not respond with contempt, but with forgiveness and grace.

On the cross, He bore even the sin of our destructive speech. And through the Holy Spirit, He transforms our hearts so that we learn to use what we know not for gossip, but for love, prayer, and wisdom.

👉 In Christ, there is forgiveness for the times we have broken another person's trust and grace that shapes us into people others can trust.

Guard the Trust That Has Been Placed in You

This principle is especially important in an age of group chats and social media. It is incredibly easy to forward a message, share a screenshot, or repeat a private conversation to someone else.

Before you share something, pause. If something was told to you personally, do not assume that you are free to tell others. Being close to someone does not give us the right to share everything we know about them.

In your family, be careful about sharing the personal struggles of your spouse or children. At work, do not turn sensitive information about a coworker into entertainment. In the church, do not allow a prayer concern shared in confidence to become gossip.

At the same time, being trustworthy does not mean hiding something that puts a person or others in danger. When there is a serious threat or abuse, love may require us to go to the right person who can help. The purpose should be to protect and help, not to spread the story.

👉 A trustworthy person knows when to remain silent and when to seek the right kind of help because someone's genuine well-being requires it.

Living It Out: Am I Trustworthy?

Take some time to examine yourself.

  • When someone shares personal information with me, do I protect it, or do I immediately look for someone else to tell?

  • What motivates me when I talk about other people—is it genuine concern, or a desire for attention, revenge, or simply something interesting to share?

  • Have I broken someone's trust by passing along something that was not mine to tell, and is there anything I need to confess or make right?

  • In my family, workplace, church, group chats, and social media, what boundaries do I need to guard so that I can become more trustworthy?

  • How can I surrender my desire to speak to Christ so that I learn to use what I know for love, prayer, and the genuine good of others?

Trustworthiness is built through many small moments when we choose to honor another person's dignity, even when we could easily share what we know.

CONCLUSION

Proverbs 11:13 reminds us that trust is precious. It can take a long time to build, yet only a few careless words to destroy. When someone opens their life to us, we have a responsibility to guard what they have entrusted to us.

So it is not enough to ask whether a story is true. We must also ask whether it is ours to tell and whether sharing it genuinely serves the good of the other person.

And when we remember the times we have failed to be trustworthy, we can come to Christ. In Him there is forgiveness, and through His Spirit He can transform our hearts and our speech so that we become people others feel safe approaching—people who know how to love and how to guard another person's trust.

👉 A heart being shaped by Christ does not turn another person's weakness into entertainment; it guards the trust it has been given and uses what it knows for the genuine good of others.

The question:

When someone trusts you with their weakness, struggle, or personal burden, does the way you handle it reflect the love and wisdom of Christ, or is there still an area of your heart and speech that needs to be surrendered to Him so that you can become truly trustworthy?
