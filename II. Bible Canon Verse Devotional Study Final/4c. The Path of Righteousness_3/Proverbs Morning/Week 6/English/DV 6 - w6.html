Daily Devotional Verses



Proverbs 11:4

NKJV (New King James Version)

The Folly of Wickedness

Riches do not profit in the day of wrath, But righteousness delivers from death.

GUIDE REFLECTION:

True Riches Are Not Measured by Money

Many people spend their entire lives striving for a bigger house, greater savings, a higher position, and a more comfortable way of life. There is nothing wrong with working hard or wanting to provide for the needs of one's family. But it becomes dangerous when wealth becomes the center of our hope and security.

Solomon said:

"Riches do not profit in the day of wrath, But righteousness delivers from death."

This verse reminds us of a truth we often forget—there will come a day when we must leave behind everything we have accumulated. At the time of death, no amount of money, property, business success, or achievement can save a person. Everything we have worked for in this world will remain here, but our standing before God will determine our eternity.

👉 The most important preparation in life is not accumulating wealth, but being ready to stand before God.

Wealth Cannot Save Us from God's Judgment

Many people assume that their success, goodness, or influence is enough to make them right with God. But Scripture clearly teaches that no amount of wealth can purchase forgiveness or cancel God's judgment.

Jesus told of a rich man who lived in luxury but paid no attention to God. When he died, his wealth could not save him, and he faced destruction. In the same way, a person who places his trust in money may feel secure while he is alive, but when the day of judgment comes, everything he depended on will lose its value.

Proverbs is not saying that being wealthy is sinful. The warning is against placing our trust in wealth instead of in God. Money may help meet our needs in this life, but it cannot save our souls.

👉 The money people trust in has its limits, but God alone can save for eternity.

Where Does Your Security Rest?

This verse asks each of us a personal question:

What gives me a sense of security?

Is it my savings?

My job?

My business?

Or my relationship with God?

When our security rests only on the things of this world, we easily become afraid when those things are lost. But the person who trusts in God can have peace even in times of loss, because he knows that his true treasure cannot be stolen, destroyed, or taken away.

The righteousness Solomon speaks of is not merely about doing good deeds. It is a life centered on God and lived according to His ways. Such a life carries a hope that reaches beyond death and into the eternal presence of the Lord.

👉 A heart that trusts in God possesses a security that no amount of wealth can provide.

In Christ We Find Eternal Treasure

All of us are spiritually poor if we depend on our own righteousness. No matter how many good works we do, they cannot pay for our sin. We have nothing we can offer God that is sufficient to obtain salvation.

But Jesus Christ came to do what we could never do. He lived a sinless life and gave His life on the cross to pay for our sins. Through His death and resurrection, He gives His own righteousness to all who place their faith in Him.

This is the great hope of the Christian. When the day of our death comes, we will not stand before God carrying our own goodness or wealth. We will stand clothed in the righteousness of Christ. Because of this, death is no longer the end, but a doorway into eternal fellowship with the Lord.

👉 The greatest treasure is not what we hold in our hands, but the righteousness we have received through Jesus Christ.

Invest in Things of Eternal Value

There is nothing wrong with working, saving, or planning for the future. But as followers of Christ, we are called to use these blessings as stewards rather than treating them as our god. Our time, talents, and resources should be used to glorify God and serve others.

When our hearts are set on God's eternal kingdom, we learn to value the things that cannot perish—faith, love, holiness, and faithfulness. These are treasures that will remain even after this life is over.

👉 A person with an eternal perspective uses his wealth for God rather than living merely for wealth.

Living It Out: What Is My True Treasure?

  • Where does my security rest—in my wealth or in God?
  • Do I prioritize accumulating wealth more than growing in my relationship with Christ?
  • How do I use my money and possessions to glorify God?
  • If the Lord were to call me today, would I be ready to stand before Him?
  • What can I change so that I invest more in things of eternal value?

CONCLUSION

Proverbs teaches that a day will come when all the wealth of this world will lose its value. No amount of money can prevent death or save a person from God's judgment. But the one who trusts in the righteousness that comes from God has a hope that death cannot take away.

In Jesus Christ, we find the only treasure that will last forever. He is our righteousness, our salvation, and our eternal hope. May we use the blessings entrusted to us not merely to accumulate more for this world, but to live for the kingdom of God that will never end.

👉 The person who is truly rich is not the one who possesses much, but the one whose life rests securely in Christ.

The question:

If you were to stand before God today, what would you be relying on most—the treasures you have accumulated on earth, or the righteousness you have received through Jesus Christ, which alone can save you on the day of judgment?
