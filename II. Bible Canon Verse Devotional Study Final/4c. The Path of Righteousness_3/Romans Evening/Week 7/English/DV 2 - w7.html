Daily Devotional Verses



Romans 6:7-11

NKJV (New King James Version)

Dead to Sin, Alive to God

7 For he who has died has been freed from sin. 8 Now if we died with Christ, we believe that we shall also live with Him, 9 knowing that Christ, having been raised from the dead, dies no more. Death no longer has dominion over Him. 10 For the death that He died, He died to sin once for all; but the life that He lives, He lives to God. 11 Likewise you also, reckon yourselves to be dead indeed to sin, but alive to God in Christ Jesus our Lord.

GUIDE REFLECTION:

Died with Christ, We Shall Also Live with Him

In the previous verse, Paul showed that the person who has died with Christ has been freed from the power of sin. Sin's former dominion has been broken, so the believer no longer needs to live as its slave. But union with Christ is not only about leaving the old life behind. It also carries a positive truth: those who have died with Christ have the assurance that they will also live with Him.

"Now if we died with Christ, we believe that we shall also live with Him."

Paul begins with the truth he has already explained: "Now if we died with Christ." He is not referring to the believer's physical death. He is speaking of spiritual union with Christ in His death. Because the believer has been united with Him, Christ's death has become effective in relation to his former condition under sin.

But Paul does not stop with death. He says, "we believe that we shall also live with Him." Union with Christ does not bring the believer into an empty condition where he has merely left his old life behind. Death with Christ is followed by life with Him. If the former condition was brought to an end through union with His death, then the new condition is connected to the life of the risen Christ.

The word "believe" is important. The assurance Paul expresses is not based on the strength we see in ourselves. He is not saying that we believe we will live with Christ because we have changed enough, because our faith is already strong, or because we have successfully overcome every sin. Our confidence is rooted in Christ and in the truth that union with Him cannot be separated from either His death or His life.

This is an important truth because we often measure our spiritual condition by our present experience. When our walk with God is going well, assurance comes easily. But when we see our weakness, failures, and ongoing struggle with sin, we may feel as though no real change has taken place within us. When we make ourselves the basis of our assurance, our confidence will rise and fall as well.

But Paul brings us back to a firmer foundation. The believer's hope does not rest on his own sufficiency but on the sufficiency of Christ. The same Christ with whom he died is the Christ with whom he will also live. Therefore, the assurance of new life does not depend on our ability to hold on to Christ perfectly, but on the reality that we have been united with Him.

Nor should we think that living with Christ refers only to some distant future. Paul has already shown that the believer is called to walk in newness of life now. Union with Christ already has an effect on the way we live in the present. But this life also has a complete fulfillment that extends beyond the present. The person who has been united with Christ has a hope that does not end in this world, because his life is joined to the Lord who has triumphed over death.

Here we see the breadth of God's saving work. He does not merely remove the believer from his former slavery to sin; He also brings him into a life that is joined to Christ. The gospel is not only a message about what we have left behind. It is also a message about the new life we now possess because we belong to Him.

This is especially important in our daily struggles. There are times when we see more clearly the things that still need to change than what God has already done. We see our weaknesses, our failures, and the sins we still need to fight. These are real, and we should not ignore them. But neither should we allow them alone to define the way we see ourselves. The deepest truth about the believer is not merely that there are things he must leave behind, but that his life is now joined to Christ.

Therefore, the Christian life is not simply an effort to stay away from former sins. It is a life lived with and for the Lord to whom we now belong. We are not merely called to turn away from our former master; we have been brought into a new relationship with Christ. The freedom we saw in the previous verse is not freedom without direction. It is freedom that leads us into a life centered on Him.

This assurance does not depend on the believer's changing condition but on Christ, to whom he belongs. If we died with Him, we can have firm confidence that we shall also live with Him. CONCLUSION:

Paul shows that union with Christ does not end in death. Those who have died with Him have the firm assurance that they will also live with Him. Salvation is not only freedom from sin's former dominion; it is entrance into a new life that is joined to Jesus Christ.

This assurance does not depend on our own strength or on the perfection of our spiritual life. It is rooted in Christ. Because of this, even when we see our weaknesses and ongoing struggles, we can look to Him as the solid foundation of our hope.

Therefore, let us not focus only on the life we have left behind. Let us also live in the light of the life that has been given to us in Christ. The believer is not merely someone who has been freed from former slavery; he is someone whose life and hope are now joined to his Lord.

In the end, if we died with Christ, God has not left us in the death of our former condition. He has joined us to the life of His Son, so our hope does not rest on our own ability but on Jesus Christ, who is the source and assurance of the life possessed by all who belong to Him.
