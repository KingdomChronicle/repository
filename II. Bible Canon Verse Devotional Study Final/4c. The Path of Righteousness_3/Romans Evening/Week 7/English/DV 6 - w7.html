Daily Devotional Verses



Romans 6:7-11

NKJV (New King James Version)

Dead to Sin, Alive to God

7 For he who has died has been freed from sin. 8 Now if we died with Christ, we believe that we shall also live with Him, 9 knowing that Christ, having been raised from the dead, dies no more. Death no longer has dominion over Him. 10 For the death that He died, He died to sin once for all; but the life that He lives, He lives to God. 11 Likewise you also, reckon yourselves to be dead indeed to sin, but alive to God in Christ Jesus our Lord.

GUIDE REFLECTION:

Dead to Sin but Alive to God

In the preceding verses, Paul carefully explained what is true of Christ and of those who are united with Him. The believer's old self was crucified with Christ. He has been freed from sin's former rule. And because he is united with Christ in His death, his life is now also joined to the risen Lord. Having established these truths, Paul now comes to an important question:

How should believers see themselves in light of what God has done?

"Likewise you also, reckon yourselves to be dead indeed to sin, but alive to God in Christ Jesus our Lord."

The word "Likewise" is important. Paul does not begin with a command that has no foundation. Before telling believers how they should regard themselves, he first explains what God has done in Christ and what that means for those who are united with Him. The Christian life does not begin with trying to create a new identity for ourselves. It begins with recognizing the new reality God has already established.

That is why Paul says, "reckon yourselves." He is not telling us to imagine something that is untrue until we somehow convince ourselves that it is real. This is not positive thinking meant to change reality. Rather, Paul calls believers to recognize, accept, and regard as true what God has already declared about their standing in Christ.

This matters because we often allow our feelings and experiences to define who we are. When temptation feels strong, we may feel as though we are still slaves to sin. When we fail repeatedly, we may wonder whether anything has truly changed. When memories of our past return, we may allow former sins to keep defining our identity.

But Paul does not tell us to begin with how we feel about ourselves. He directs us to what God has done. The truth about the believer does not rise and fall with the strength of his emotions. His new standing is rooted in his union with Christ.

The first truth he must recognize is that he is "dead indeed to sin." This does not mean that sin is no longer present or that he can no longer be tempted. Paul has already shown that the issue is a change in the believer's relationship to sin's dominion. Sin may still tempt him, but it is no longer the master with the right to rule over him. The person who is united with Christ should no longer see himself as someone who has no choice but to obey sin.

But Paul's statement does not end there. We are not called merely to think about what is no longer true of us. He also tells us to reckon ourselves "alive to God." This is the positive side of the believer's new condition. Salvation is not merely freedom from sin's former rule. It is entrance into a new life with a new relationship and a new direction toward God.

In the previous verse, Paul said that the risen Christ lives to God. Now he applies the same pattern to those who are united with Christ. Because their lives are joined to Him, their new life is no longer meant to revolve around themselves. They are alive to God. Their time, abilities, decisions, relationships, and desires now have a new center.

Here we see the difference between simply avoiding sin and the kind of life described by the gospel. We may think that being a Christian is mainly about staying away from sinful behavior. But God's purpose goes much further. He does not merely free us from our former master; He brings us into a life centered on Himself.

If all our attention is focused only on the sins we are trying to avoid, self can still remain at the center of our lives. We may want to become holy because we want to feel better about ourselves, protect our reputation, or avoid the consequences of bad decisions. But new life in Christ asks a deeper question:

Are we living for God?

Paul gives the reason this new way of seeing ourselves is possible in the final words of the verse:

"in Christ Jesus our Lord."

This is the foundation of everything. We do not declare ourselves dead to sin and alive to God because our determination is strong enough. We do not create this reality through greater effort. It is true because our lives are united with Christ.

This also gives us the right balance in our struggle against sin. We should not minimize temptation or assume that every sinful desire will simply disappear. But neither should we fight as though God has done nothing. When temptation comes, we can remember that sin is no longer the master we are bound to obey. And when God calls us to obedience, we can remember that the new life we have in Christ is a life that belongs to Him.

There is a great difference between saying,

"I need to change so that I can belong to Christ,"

and saying,

"Because I belong to Christ, I am called to live according to the new life He has given me."

The first makes our own transformation the foundation. The second makes what God has done the foundation, and obedience grows from that foundation.

That is why Paul's command to "reckon yourselves" has practical meaning for everyday life. When our past tells us that our former sins are our true identity, we must remember who we are in Christ. When temptation tells us that we have to obey it, we must remember that its former dominion has been broken. And when our own desires try to become the center of our decisions, we must remember that the life God has given us is a life for Him.

This is not something we remind ourselves of only once. As believers continue to live in this world, they must keep bringing their minds back to the truth of the gospel. Not to make something true that is not yet true, but so that their thinking and their way of life may increasingly come into line with the reality God has already established in them through Christ.

CONCLUSION:

In Romans 6:11, Paul takes the truths he has explained about Christ and applies them to the way believers are to see themselves. Because we are united with Christ, we are called to recognize as true the new condition God has given us:

dead to sin but alive to Him.

Being dead to sin does not mean that temptation and struggle have disappeared. It means that sin is no longer the master with the right to define our lives. And being alive to God means that the new life we have has a new center, a new direction, and a new purpose.

So let us not allow our past, our failures, or our present feelings to have the final word about who we are. Instead, let us learn to see ourselves in light of what God has done in Christ. When temptation comes, let us remember that we are no longer its slaves. And with every opportunity God gives us to live, let us choose to live this new life for Him.

In the end, the Christian life is not an attempt to create a new identity through our own strength. It is learning to live according to the new reality God has already given us: united with Christ Jesus, we are dead to sin's former dominion and alive to God. Our calling, then, is to recognize this truth and allow it to shape the way we live each day.
