Daily Devotional Verses



Romans 6:7-11

NKJV (New King James Version)

Dead to Sin, Alive to God

7 For he who has died has been freed from sin. 8 Now if we died with Christ, we believe that we shall also live with Him, 9 knowing that Christ, having been raised from the dead, dies no more. Death no longer has dominion over Him. 10 For the death that He died, He died to sin once for all; but the life that He lives, He lives to God. 11 Likewise you also, reckon yourselves to be dead indeed to sin, but alive to God in Christ Jesus our Lord.

GUIDE REFLECTION:

The Life He Now Lives Is to God

In the first part of the verse, Paul showed that when Christ died, He died to sin once for all. His death was a complete work that never needs to be repeated. But the truth Paul wants to show does not end with death. The Christ who died was raised from the dead, and now Paul describes His life with a brief but deeply important statement.

"But the life that He lives, He lives to God."

There is a clear contrast between the two parts of the verse. Christ's death in relation to sin happened once for all. But the life He now has continues. He will never return to the death He once experienced. He is alive, and that life is "to God."

This does not mean that before His death Christ did not live for God. The entire life of the Lord Jesus was one of perfect obedience to the Father. He always did the Father's will and never lived in rebellion against Him. Paul's point is not a change from disobedience to obedience. Rather, he is describing the condition of the risen Christ: His once-for-all dealing with sin through death is finished, and the life He now has will never again come under the dominion of death.

Here we see the great contrast between death and life that Paul emphasizes. Christ's death was in relation to sin and happened once for all. His life, on the other hand, is to God and continues. The cross was not the end of His story. The Lord who was crucified and died is the same Lord who rose again and now lives in complete victory.

This is important to the whole argument of Romans 6 because Paul's subject is the believer's union with Christ. We are not merely united with a Christ who died. We are united with the Christ who is alive. So when Paul shows us what is true of Christ, he is also preparing us to understand what this means for those who belong to Him.

There is an important truth here about the purpose of life. A person naturally looks for something that will become the center of his life. He may live for personal success, comfort, the approval of others, wealth, or the things he wants to achieve. Even good things can become the center of life when they become the highest reason behind our decisions and desires.

In our sinful condition, we naturally place ourselves at the center. We often ask what will make us happy, what will give us security, or what will help us get what we want. Even in our relationship with God, we may view Him primarily in terms of what He can give us. In this way, God becomes merely a part of our plans instead of being the center of our lives.

But when we look at Christ, we see a different picture. His life is "to God." God is the center and purpose. And because believers are united with Christ, the new life given to them cannot remain without direction. Freedom from the power of sin is not freedom to live merely for ourselves. It is freedom that opens the way to a life centered on God.

This is the important difference between simply changing our behavior and having new life in Christ. A person may avoid certain sinful practices because he wants a better life, a good reputation, or a clear conscience. But the Christian life is more than avoiding what is wrong. The question is not only, "What should I no longer do?" We must also ask, "For whom am I living?"

When faith becomes merely a list of prohibitions, it is easy to think that holiness is simply staying away from certain sins. But Paul's picture goes deeper. The new life has a positive direction. The purpose is not merely to be free from our former master but to live in a right relationship with God. A life that has truly been changed gradually develops a new center, a new purpose, and a new direction.

That is why it is important to examine not only the sins we are trying to leave behind but also what we are truly living for. Certain sinful practices may decrease while self still remains at the center of our lives. We may become more disciplined, more religious, and more outwardly respectable, while our deepest desire is still our own glory, comfort, or will.

Paul places the life of Christ before us. Not to say that we can imitate Him in our own strength, but to show us the direction of a life that is united with Him. The Christ who died once for all in relation to sin now lives to God. And those who are united with Him are not merely delivered from a former condition; they are brought into a new life with God at the center.

In this truth, we clearly see the direction of the risen Christ's life: His life is to God. It is not a life centered on self or on anything apart from God. God is its center and purpose. So Paul's brief statement reminds us that true life is measured not only by what we turn away from, but also by the One toward whom our lives are directed. In the life of Christ, we see the perfect picture of a life lived to God.

CONCLUSION:

After showing that Christ's death in relation to sin happened once for all, Paul directs our attention to the life of the risen Lord. Christ is no longer under the power of death. He is alive, and the life He now lives is to God.

This reminds us that the purpose of salvation is not merely to remove us from our former slavery. New life has a new direction. We are not set free from sin so that self can become the center of our lives instead. In our union with Christ, we are called to live with God as our highest purpose.

So let us not only ask what sins we need to turn away from. Let us also examine whom we are truly living for. In our decisions, desires, time, abilities, and the things we value, let us learn to live in light of the truth that new life in Christ is a life directed toward God.

In the end, the Christ who died once for all in relation to sin now lives to God. And because our new life is united with Him, freedom from sin is not the end of God's purpose for us. It is the beginning of a life that no longer revolves around self but gradually learns to live for God.
