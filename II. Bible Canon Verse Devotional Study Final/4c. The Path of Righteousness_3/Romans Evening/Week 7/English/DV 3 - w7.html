Daily Devotional Verses



Romans 6:7-11

NKJV (New King James Version)

Dead to Sin, Alive to God

7 For he who has died has been freed from sin. 8 Now if we died with Christ, we believe that we shall also live with Him, 9 knowing that Christ, having been raised from the dead, dies no more. Death no longer has dominion over Him. 10 For the death that He died, He died to sin once for all; but the life that He lives, He lives to God. 11 Likewise you also, reckon yourselves to be dead indeed to sin, but alive to God in Christ Jesus our Lord.

GUIDE REFLECTION:

Death No Longer Has Dominion over Christ

In the previous verse, Paul declared that if we died with Christ, we believe that we will also live with Him. But what is this assurance based on? It does not rest on the strength of our faith or on our ability to remain steadfast. Our assurance is rooted in Christ Himself—He has been raised from the dead and will never die again.

“Knowing that Christ, having been raised from the dead, dies no more. Death no longer has dominion over Him.”

Once again, Paul uses the expression “Knowing that.” This shows that the Christian's hope is not speculation or merely wishful thinking. It is grounded in a certain reality: Jesus Christ died and was raised from the dead.

But Christ's resurrection was not simply a return to His former life only to face death again, as happened with other people in the Bible who were raised from the dead. They came back to life, but they did not remain alive forever; eventually, they died again. Christ's resurrection was different. When He rose, He entered a life that could never again come under the power of death. That is why Paul said, “Death no longer has dominion over Him.”

Death is a reality that no human being can escape. No amount of wealth, wisdom, position, or strength can prevent it. We may take care of our bodies and do everything we can to prolong our lives, but we cannot eliminate death.

But in Christ, we see a victory that humanity could never achieve for itself. He entered death and came out of it as the risen Lord. The grave could not hold Him, and death will never rule over Him again.

This matters because our hope is directly connected to Christ. If our assurance depended on our own strength, we would always have reason to be afraid. We know how weak we are, how easily we can be tempted, and how many things lie beyond our control.

But Paul does not point us to ourselves as the foundation of our hope. He points us to the risen Christ.

We cannot free ourselves from the power of sin, nor can we overcome death by our own ability. This is why we need a Savior. The gospel does not say that we must first conquer death before we can come to Christ. It declares that Christ Himself entered death and triumphed over it.

Because of this, we have a firm hope even in the midst of life's weakness and uncertainty. Our bodies may grow weak, our circumstances may change, we may lose things that are precious to us, and events may come that we cannot prevent. But for the person who is united with Christ, hope does not rest on things that fade and have limits. His life is joined to the Lord over whom death can never again rule.

This does not mean that we will never experience fear, pain, or grief when we face death. Death remains an enemy and still brings real sorrow. But for those who belong to Christ, it is no longer the enemy that has the final word.

Christ's resurrection gives us reason to look beyond the grave and trust God even in the face of life's greatest limitation. Christ is not the only One who has been raised; the day will also come when those who belong to Him will be raised. Paul said:

“In a moment, in the twinkling of an eye, at the last trumpet. For the trumpet will sound, and the dead will be raised incorruptible, and we shall be changed.”
— 1 Corinthians 15:52

This is also the foundation of Paul's statement that we will live with Christ. Our assurance is firm because the Christ to whom we belong is alive, and the life He possesses can no longer be conquered by death. His resurrection is the guarantee that death is not the final destination of those who are united with Him.

So before we think about how we should live, Paul first wants us to fix our attention on Christ. Our new life does not begin with our own ability, but with what the Lord has done. He died, He rose again, and He lives forever.

CONCLUSION:

The assurance of the believer's life is rooted in one unchanging truth: Jesus Christ has been raised from the dead and will never die again. The death He once entered no longer has any power to rule over Him.

Because of this, our faith does not rest on our own ability to overcome sin, weakness, or death. We cannot save ourselves, but the Lord in whom we trust has already triumphed over the things humanity could never defeat.

So when we face our weakness, uncertainty, or even the reality of death, let us not make our own strength the foundation of our hope. Let us fix our faith on Jesus Christ.

Our hope is secure not because we are strong, but because the Christ in whom we trust is alive, and death no longer has dominion over Him.

In the end, the hope of those who are united with Christ rests on the victory of the risen Lord. He died but was raised from the dead, and He will never die again. Therefore, those who belong to Him have a firm hope that death does not have the final word, because their lives are joined to Christ, who has triumphed over it.
