Daily Devotional Verses



Romans 6:7-11

NKJV (New King James Version)

Dead to Sin, Alive to God

7 For he who has died has been freed from sin. 8 Now if we died with Christ, we believe that we shall also live with Him, 9 knowing that Christ, having been raised from the dead, dies no more. Death no longer has dominion over Him. 10 For the death that He died, He died to sin once for all; but the life that He lives, He lives to God. 11 Likewise you also, reckon yourselves to be dead indeed to sin, but alive to God in Christ Jesus our Lord.

GUIDE REFLECTION:

Freed from the Power of Sin

In the previous part of his explanation, Paul showed that the believer's old self was crucified with Christ so that he would no longer remain a slave to sin. Now Paul brings that truth to an important consequence: dying with Christ means that sin's former dominion over the believer's life has been broken. He is no longer under the same enslaving power that once ruled over him.

"For he who has died has been freed from sin."

The word "freed" emphasizes the great change that has taken place in the condition of the person who has been united with Christ. Earlier, Paul described sin not merely as individual wrong actions that a person occasionally commits, but as a power that rules and enslaves. A person under sin does not simply commit sins; he lives under its dominion. But according to Paul, a real change has taken place: sin's former power no longer has the right to rule over the person who has died with Christ.

This does not mean that the believer will never again be tempted or fall into sin. The verse makes a more specific point: he is no longer a slave whose only condition is to submit to sin's dominion. He may still feel temptation, and old desires and sinful habits may return, but they are no longer the power that determines who he is or to whom he belongs. The former master has lost its dominion over his life.

This is why union with Christ is so important in Paul's argument. Freedom from the power of sin is not merely the result of stronger self-control, greater determination, or better moral living. It is rooted in what happened to the believer because of his union with Christ. The old self that lived under sin's dominion is regarded as having died with Him; therefore, the former relationship of slavery to sin has been broken.

This shows how deep humanity's problem with sin really is. We often think that changing a few wrong behaviors is enough. If we have a bad habit, we try to eliminate it. If we repeatedly give in to temptation, we promise ourselves that we will do better next time. But Paul shows that the problem of sin is not limited to our actions. Sin has the power to enslave. That is why human effort alone is not enough to put one's life in order; a person must be set free from a dominion he cannot break by his own strength.

There are also times when a believer may live as though he were still a slave to his former master. When he repeatedly falls into the same sin, he may begin to think, "This is just who I am. I will never change." Little by little, he may begin to accept sin as something he can no longer resist. But Paul's words challenge that way of thinking. He is not saying that temptation has become weak or that every sin is now easy to overcome. What he is saying is that the spiritual condition of the person who has been united with Christ has changed:

his life no longer belongs to sin.

Therefore, when the believer faces temptation, he does not have to fight as though he were still trying to escape from a master who has complete power over him. He can fight from the reality that sin's former dominion has already been broken because of his union with Christ. His struggle against sin is not an attempt to create freedom through his own strength; it is living in accordance with the freedom that has already been given to him.

This does not minimize sin. Instead, it gives us an even stronger reason not to surrender to it. When you are tempted to return to your former way of life, remember that sin may call, entice, and deceive, but it is no longer the master you are bound to serve. Your past may have left deep marks, and some old habits may be difficult to abandon, but they are no longer the highest truth about your standing in Christ.

This is a truth every believer must hold firmly. Freedom from the power of sin is not a condition that must be created through personal effort, but a reality rooted in union with Christ. Temptation and the struggle against sin may continue, but sin no longer has the right to rule over the believer's life. Through union with Christ, the former relationship of slavery to sin has been broken.

CONCLUSION:

Romans 6:7 declares an important change in the believer's condition. Sin, which once ruled as a master, no longer has the same power over the person who has died with Christ. This freedom is not the result of human strength but the result of union with Christ.

Through what Jesus Christ has done, God does not merely deal with us as people who need to improve our behavior. In our union with Christ, He addresses our deeper problem—our slavery to sin. Because the old self was crucified with Him, the power that once ruled over us should no longer be regarded as our master.

Therefore, do not accept the lie that you have no choice but to obey sin. When temptation comes and old desires return, do not allow the strength of the temptation to define your condition. Hold firmly to the truth God has declared: through union with Christ, sin's former dominion has already been broken.

In the end, freedom from the power of sin does not begin with our ability to change ourselves, but with what Christ has done and with our union with Him. Sin may continue to confront us, but we no longer have to live as its slaves, because in Christ its former dominion over our lives has been broken.
