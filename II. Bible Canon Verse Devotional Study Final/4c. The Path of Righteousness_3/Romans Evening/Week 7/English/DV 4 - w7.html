Daily Devotional Verses



Romans 6:7-11

NKJV (New King James Version)

Dead to Sin, Alive to God

7 For he who has died has been freed from sin. 8 Now if we died with Christ, we believe that we shall also live with Him, 9 knowing that Christ, having been raised from the dead, dies no more. Death no longer has dominion over Him. 10 For the death that He died, He died to sin once for all; but the life that He lives, He lives to God. 11 Likewise you also, reckon yourselves to be dead indeed to sin, but alive to God in Christ Jesus our Lord.

GUIDE REFLECTION:

Christ Died to Sin Once for All

In the previous verse, Paul showed that Christ, having been raised from the dead, will never die again and that death no longer has dominion over Him. His resurrection is a complete victory. But why does Christ never need to face death again? In this verse, Paul gives an important reason: His death to sin happened once for all and never needs to be repeated.

“For the death that He died, He died to sin once for all;”

The words “once for all” are important. Christ's death is not an act that must be repeated in order to remain effective. When He died, His death in relation to sin was a complete and unique event. He does not need to return to the cross to accomplish again what He has already completed.

These words also show that the effectiveness of Christ's death is not limited only to those who lived after His coming. What about those who believed in God and died before Jesus was born as a man?

God's plan of salvation did not begin only when Christ was born in Bethlehem. From the very beginning, God had promised the coming of a Savior. After sin entered the world, God said to the serpent:

“And I will put enmity between you and the woman, and between your seed and her Seed; He shall bruise your head, and you shall bruise His heel.” — Genesis 3:15

In this promise, we see the first glimpse of the good news. Not every detail of how salvation would be accomplished had yet been revealed, but the promise was already there: the Seed of the woman would come and crush the enemy.

We can also see pictures of this promise in several events in the Old Testament. For example, David's victory over Goliath can be seen as a picture of Christ's greater victory over the enemy. After David struck Goliath down, he cut off his head, and Scripture notably records that “David took the head of the Philistine and brought it to Jerusalem” (1 Samuel 17:54). In this picture, we see a victory in which the enemy's head is crushed—a picture that helps us understand God's earlier promise in Genesis 3:15 that the Seed of the woman would crush the serpent's head.

David was not the complete fulfillment of that promise, nor was Goliath himself the serpent of Genesis. But David's victory can be seen as a shadow of the far greater victory that would come through Christ. The head of Goliath that David brought to Jerusalem gives us a powerful picture: the enemy was defeated and his head was crushed. But what that picture foreshadowed was fully accomplished in Jesus Christ. At Golgotha, when He was crucified and gave His life because of sin, He accomplished the victory that had been promised since Genesis—the power of the enemy was defeated, and through His death and resurrection He triumphed over sin and death.

In the same way, Abraham's offering of Isaac gives us a powerful picture of the sacrifice God would provide. Isaac was the son God had promised, and when God asked Abraham to offer him, we see the depth of Abraham's faith and obedience. But instead of Isaac dying, God provided a ram to be offered in his place. This event was not itself the fulfillment of salvation, but it clearly reflects the greater truth that would be fulfilled in Christ: God Himself would provide the sacrifice, and His Son would offer His life because of the sin of the world.

These events show that God's plan of salvation was progressively revealed throughout Scripture until it was fully accomplished in Jesus Christ. The believers who came before Christ had not yet seen the complete fulfillment of that promise as we see it today. They did not know every detail about Jesus, but they believed God and trusted His promise.

Their faith looked forward to what God had promised to do; our faith looks back to the work already accomplished in Jesus Christ.

God did not provide two different ways of salvation—one for those who lived before Christ and another for those who lived after Him. Salvation has always rested on the grace of God and on the work fully accomplished by Christ. The difference is simply that earlier believers trusted in the promise, while we now trust in its fulfillment.

👉 They believed in the God who promised salvation; we believe in the God who fulfilled that promise in Jesus Christ. But the basis of salvation is the same—the once-for-all and sufficient work of Christ.

This deepens the meaning of Paul's statement that Christ died “once for all.” His death was not a temporary solution for only one generation. It was God's once-for-all work in Christ, the sufficient basis for the salvation of His people. The promise that believers once held on to is the work that has now been accomplished on the cross.

The words “to sin” do not mean that Christ died because He had sin of His own. The Lord Jesus was without sin. Rather, He willingly faced death because of the sin He came to deal with. He who was without sin did what sinful humanity could never do for itself.

Here we see how serious sin is before God. We can easily call it merely a mistake, a weakness, or simply part of being human. But the cross of Christ shows us that sin is not a small problem that can be corrected merely through better behavior.

If our own efforts were enough to restore our relationship with God, Christ's death would not have been necessary. If good works could erase sin, people could save themselves. But the gospel shows us that God had to act through His Son.

👉 The cross reminds us that sin is serious and that we cannot save ourselves.

But the word “once” does not only reveal the seriousness of sin. It also reveals the sufficiency of Christ's work. The Lord did not leave His work incomplete so that we would need to add something to it. His death is complete and never needs to be repeated.

This is especially important for believers who easily fall back into trusting in their own works. When we see our failures, we may think that we need to prove ourselves worthy of God's acceptance all over again. But our repentance, obedience, and holy living do not add to the effectiveness of Christ's work. We obey because what He has done is the sufficient foundation of our new life.

👉 We do not need to add anything to Christ's sufficient work; we are called to trust in Him and live according to His grace.

The sufficiency of Christ's death is not a reason to take sin lightly. Instead, it shows us even more clearly how serious sin is. Grace does not teach us to remain in sin; it calls us to turn away from sin and live in the new life God has given us.

So when the memory of our sins troubles us or we begin to wonder whether what Christ has done is truly sufficient for us, let us return to this truth: Christ's death was once for all and is sufficient. It does not need to be repeated every time we sin or feel that we have fallen short.

CONCLUSION:

Paul showed that Christ's death to sin was a once-for-all event. The sinless Lord willingly faced death to deal with the problem that sinful humanity could never solve. What He accomplished was complete, sufficient, and never needs to be repeated.

This truth reveals both the seriousness of sin and the sufficiency of Christ. We must not take sin lightly, because the cross itself shows us how serious it is. But neither should we think that we need to add anything to Christ's work in order to make it sufficient.

Christ's once-for-all death also gives us assurance that God's salvation is not limited to a single generation. Believers who lived before Christ came trusted in God and in His promised salvation; we now look to the fulfillment of that promise in Jesus Christ. The pictures of victory over the enemy and of a sacrifice provided by God help us see how God gradually prepared the way for the complete work of Christ.

So when we see our failures, let us not return to our own efforts as the foundation of our acceptance before God. Let us repent of our sin and walk in obedience, but let us do so with our eyes fixed on Christ. Our hope does not rest on our ability to be sufficient, but on His once-for-all and sufficient death.

👉 Christ's work is not lacking and does not need to be repeated. The promise that believers held on to in the past has now been fulfilled in Christ, and we are called to trust in that once-for-all and sufficient work.

In the end, Jesus Christ's death to sin happened once for all and never needs to be repeated. The promises and pictures of salvation that God revealed from the beginning point to Christ, the Son who offered His life because of the sin of the world. Therefore, believers can place their trust not in their own ability or works, but in Christ's once-for-all and sufficient work in dealing with sin.
