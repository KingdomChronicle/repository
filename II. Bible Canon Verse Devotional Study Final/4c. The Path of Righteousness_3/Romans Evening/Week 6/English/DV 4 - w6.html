Daily Devotional Verses



Romans 6:1-6

NKJV (New King James Version)

Dead to Sin, Alive to God

1 What shall we say then? Shall we continue in sin that grace may abound? 2 Certainly not! How shall we who died to sin live any longer in it? 3 Or do you not know that as many of us as were baptized into Christ Jesus were baptized into His death? 4 Therefore we were buried with Him through baptism into death, that just as Christ was raised from the dead by the glory of the Father, even so we also should walk in newness of life.

5 For if we have been united together in the likeness of His death, certainly we also shall be in the likeness of His resurrection, 6 knowing this, that our old man was crucified with Him, that the body of sin might be done away with, that we should no longer be slaves of sin.

GUIDE REFLECTION:

United with Christ in Death, Burial, and New Life

In the previous verse, Paul explained that those who believe in Jesus Christ were baptized into Christ and into His death. He showed that believers have a genuine spiritual union with the Lord, and therefore His death has meaning and saving significance for them. But this union does not end with death alone. In this verse, Paul expands his explanation to reveal the purpose of that union.

“Therefore we were buried with Him through baptism into death, that just as Christ was raised from the dead by the glory of the Father, even so we also should walk in newness of life.”

The word “Therefore” shows that this verse follows directly from the truth previously stated. Because the believer has been united with Christ in His death, it can also be said that he died and was buried with Him. This did not literally happen to the physical body of every believer. Rather, it is a spiritual reality accomplished by God in all who truly believe in Christ.

Paul’s mention of burial is also significant. Historically, the burial of the Lord Jesus confirms that He truly experienced death. Spiritually, it shows that the believer’s former condition was not merely altered in part but was regarded as having come to an end through union with Christ. The former life under the dominion of sin must no longer define a person who belongs to Christ. Although the believer continues to struggle against sin and temptation, that former life is no longer the life to which he should return or allow to rule over him.

However, death and burial are not the final point of Paul’s message. He immediately directs our attention to the resurrection of Christ. Just as the Father raised His Son from the dead through His glorious power, God’s purpose for His children is not that they remain in death but that they walk in newness of life. The central message of the verse is not merely the passing away of the old life but the beginning of the new life that God gives.

This “newness of life” is not simply another opportunity to begin again. It is not like a person deciding to change a few harmful habits or begin living a better life. Paul is speaking of a new kind of life that flows from union with Christ. It is a life with a new relationship with God, a new identity, and a new purpose.

It is also important to notice that this new life is rooted in the power of God, not in human ability. Christ was raised from the dead “by the glory of the Father.” In the same way, the transformation of a believer’s life is not merely the result of personal determination. God Himself works to give new life to those He has saved. Therefore, living as a true believer is not an attempt to transform ourselves through our own strength but a life lived in the light of what God has already accomplished.

This is the great difference between the gospel and every system based solely on morality. Many people try to change their behavior through personal discipline, yet the same heart and identity remain unchanged. In the gospel, transformation begins with what God has accomplished through Christ. Outward conduct is simply the fruit of the new life He has given.

Because of this, believers must not think that their former lives will continue to determine their future. It is true that we still carry weaknesses and continue to struggle against temptation. But we must no longer live as though nothing has changed. God has not merely forgiven us; He has given us a new life that should become visible in our daily walk with Him.

In this verse, Paul clearly shows that God’s purpose is not merely to save people from the judgment of sin. His purpose is to give those who believe a new life so that they may live according to the new standing He has graciously given them in Christ. Therefore, the believer’s life is not only about the past that has been left behind but also about the new life that God gives and enables him to live through His power.

CONCLUSION:

Paul shows that union with Christ does not end with His death. Those who believe are also regarded as having died and been buried with Him so that they may share in the new life God gives.

This new life is not the result of our own efforts but of the glorious power of God that raised Christ from the dead. Therefore, the believer’s life is not merely about avoiding sin but about living according to the new standing God has graciously given.

Let us therefore stop looking continually at who we once were. Instead, let us trust in what God has accomplished through Christ and learn to walk each day in the new life He has given us. As we grow in our understanding of our union with Christ, we will also see more clearly the depth of the transformation brought about by His salvation.

In the end, God has not merely saved us from the punishment of sin. Through our union with Jesus Christ, He has given us new life so that we may live for His glory.
