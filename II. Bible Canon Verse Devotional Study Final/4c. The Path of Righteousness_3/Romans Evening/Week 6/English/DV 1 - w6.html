Daily Devotional Verses



Romans 6:1-6

NKJV (New King James Version)

Dead to Sin, Alive to God

1 What shall we say then? Shall we continue in sin that grace may abound? 2 Certainly not! How shall we who died to sin live any longer in it? 3 Or do you not know that as many of us as were baptized into Christ Jesus were baptized into His death? 4 Therefore we were buried with Him through baptism into death, that just as Christ was raised from the dead by the glory of the Father, even so we also should walk in newness of life.

5 For if we have been united together in the likeness of His death, certainly we also shall be in the likeness of His resurrection, 6 knowing this, that our old man was crucified with Him, that the body of sin might be done away with, that we should no longer be slaves of sin.

GUIDE REFLECTION:

Shall We Continue in Sin That Grace May Abound?

At the close of Romans 5, Paul revealed a profound truth: where sin increased, God's grace abounded all the more. He declared that God's grace is far more powerful than sin and that through Jesus Christ there is justification and eternal life for those who believe. However, this glorious truth can give rise to a misunderstanding. If God's grace abounds even more where sin increases, does that mean a person may continue sinning so that God's grace may be displayed even more? This is the question Paul raises at the beginning of Romans 6.

“What shall we say then? Shall we continue in sin that grace may abound?”

This is not the question of someone looking for an excuse to sin. Rather, it is a question that could naturally arise after hearing Paul's teaching about grace. If salvation depends not on our works but on God's grace, some might conclude that it no longer matters how a believer lives. They may think that the more sin increases, the more opportunities there are for God's grace to be demonstrated.

Through this question, Paul exposes a false understanding of the gospel. God's grace was never given to make sin acceptable. It is not permission to disregard God's holiness or to live according to one's sinful desires. The purpose of grace is to save people from sin, not to give them a reason to remain in it.

This question also reveals the true condition of the human heart. Because of our sinful nature, we often look for ways to serve ourselves even in the face of God's clear truth. Instead of asking how we may glorify God more fully, we are tempted to ask how far we can continue in sin without losing His grace. Such thinking reveals that the true purpose of the gospel has not yet been fully understood.

Many people today still think this way. Some say that because they have already been saved by faith, the way they live no longer matters. Others assume that since God will always forgive, there is no need to take seriously the battle against sin. Although their words may differ, the heart of their question is the same—can we continue in sin because grace is abundant?

Such a perspective does not exalt the grace of God; instead, it diminishes its true meaning. Grace is not cheap forgiveness that can be abused. It is the result of the immeasurably costly sacrifice of the Lord Jesus Christ on the cross. The believer's forgiveness was not made possible because God ignored sin, but because His Son paid its full penalty. Therefore, anyone who truly understands the value of grace will not seek an excuse to continue in sin.

This does not mean that believers will never sin again. As long as we live in this world, we continue to experience our weakness and our struggle against temptation. Nevertheless, there is a great difference between falling into sin because of weakness and deliberately using grace as an excuse to continue sinning. The former leads to repentance and a return to God, while the latter reveals a false understanding of God's grace.

At this point, Paul has not yet given the full explanation of why believers cannot continue in sin. His purpose is first to expose the wrong question before presenting the proper foundation for the answer. In the verses that follow, he will explain that the believer's relationship with Christ has been completely transformed, and it is that transformation which makes a life of continual sin incompatible with the believer's new identity.

Therefore, even before we hear Paul's emphatic response in the next verse, it is important for us to examine our own hearts. What is our view of God's grace? Do we treat it as permission to become comfortable with sin, or do we recognize it as God's glorious gift that calls us to live for Him? The way we view grace is reflected in the way we deal with sin.

CONCLUSION:

At the beginning of Romans 6, Paul presents an important question that could arise from a misunderstanding of grace: can we continue in sin so that God's grace may abound even more? Through this question, he prepares his readers to understand the true purpose of grace and the new life that belongs to those who are in Christ.

God's grace was never given to make sin acceptable. It was given to save sinners through the finished work of Jesus Christ and to bring them into a new relationship with God.

Therefore, let us never use grace as an excuse to become complacent toward sin. Instead, let us strive to understand more deeply what Christ has accomplished and the transformation brought about by His salvation, for it is here that we discover why a life of continual sin can never be the pattern of one who truly belongs to Christ.

In the end, God's grace is not an invitation to continue in sin but an invitation to know the depth of His saving work. As Paul's explanation unfolds, we will see that those who have truly received grace have not only been forgiven by God but have also been given a new standing in Jesus Christ.
