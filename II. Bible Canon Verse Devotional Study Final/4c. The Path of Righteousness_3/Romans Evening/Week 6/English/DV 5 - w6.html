Daily Devotional Verses



Romans 6:1-6

NKJV (New King James Version)

Dead to Sin, Alive to God

1 What shall we say then? Shall we continue in sin that grace may abound? 2 Certainly not! How shall we who died to sin live any longer in it? 3 Or do you not know that as many of us as were baptized into Christ Jesus were baptized into His death? 4 Therefore we were buried with Him through baptism into death, that just as Christ was raised from the dead by the glory of the Father, even so we also should walk in newness of life.

5 For if we have been united together in the likeness of His death, certainly we also shall be in the likeness of His resurrection, 6 knowing this, that our old man was crucified with Him, that the body of sin might be done away with, that we should no longer be slaves of sin.

GUIDE REFLECTION:

United with Christ in His Death and Resurrection

In the previous verse, Paul explained that those who believe in Jesus Christ have died and been buried with Him so that they might walk in newness of life. God has not only forgiven them but has also given them a new standing and a new way of living. Yet Paul does not end his explanation there. In this verse, he teaches that the believer's union with Christ is not only about the new life we experience now but also about the certainty of the resurrection.

“For if we have been united together in the likeness of His death, certainly we also shall be in the likeness of His resurrection.”

The word “For” shows that Paul is continuing his logical explanation. The new life he mentioned in the previous verse is not an isolated truth. It is rooted in the deeper reality that the believer is truly united with Christ. If that union is true in His death, then it is equally true in His resurrection.

When Paul says that we have been “united together in the likeness of His death,” he does not mean that every believer repeats Christ's death. Christ's death was unique and fully sufficient as the sacrifice for sin. Rather, he is referring to the believer's spiritual union with Christ, through which the benefits of His death are applied to all who believe in Him.

Because of this, every believer has a firm assurance: “certainly we also shall be in the likeness of His resurrection.” This is not a hope that rests on human ability or on the number of one's good works. This assurance rests on Christ Himself. Just as His death has become effective for us, so His resurrection is the foundation of our hope.

The resurrection Paul speaks of has two important aspects. Even now, believers already possess new spiritual life in Christ. Yet there is also a future fulfillment, when all who belong to Christ will share fully in the glory of His resurrection. Therefore, the believer's hope is not limited to this present life but extends into eternal life.

This shows that salvation is not merely God's solution to the problem of sin. It is also His preparation for the complete restoration of His children. Death is no longer the final destiny of those who believe, because the risen Christ has become the guarantee that death will never have the final victory.

Amid life's hardships and weaknesses, this truth gives believers an unwavering hope. They may experience aging, sickness, suffering, and even physical death, but they know these are not the end of their story. Because Christ has risen from the dead, His followers have the assurance that they too will share in His victory.

At the same time, this hope transforms the way believers live today. Those who are certain of the resurrection no longer live as though this world were their only home. Their decisions, goals, and values are gradually shaped by the reality that they already belong to Christ and have an eternal future with Him.

At this point, Paul presents one of the great certainties of the Christian faith: the believer's union with Christ does not end with His death but extends to His resurrection as well. Therefore, the hope of every true believer rests not on personal ability but on the finished work of Jesus Christ. This assurance gives us the strength to live with faith, confidence, and a hope that will never fail.

CONCLUSION:

Paul reminds us that our union with Christ comes with a glorious promise. If we have shared in the benefits of His death, we will also share in His resurrection. Our hope rests not in our own strength but in the finished work of the Lord Jesus Christ.

This assurance gives us the strength to live with faith and confidence in the midst of life's trials. We know that death no longer has the final word because Christ has already triumphed over it.

Therefore, let us fix our hearts on the Lord Jesus Christ. The more deeply we understand our union with Him, the more clearly we will see that our future is secure in His hands and that our lives today should be lived in the light of that hope.

In the end, the resurrection of Jesus Christ is not merely an event of the past. It is the unshakable assurance that all who are truly united with Him will also share in His victory and in everlasting life.
