Daily Devotional Verses



Romans 6:1-6

NKJV (New King James Version)

Dead to Sin, Alive to God

1 What shall we say then? Shall we continue in sin that grace may abound? 2 Certainly not! How shall we who died to sin live any longer in it? 3 Or do you not know that as many of us as were baptized into Christ Jesus were baptized into His death? 4 Therefore we were buried with Him through baptism into death, that just as Christ was raised from the dead by the glory of the Father, even so we also should walk in newness of life.

5 For if we have been united together in the likeness of His death, certainly we also shall be in the likeness of His resurrection, 6 knowing this, that our old man was crucified with Him, that the body of sin might be done away with, that we should no longer be slaves of sin.

GUIDE REFLECTION:

Our Old Self Was Crucified with Christ

In the previous verse, Paul showed that those who believe in Jesus Christ have been united with Him in His death and have the assurance that they will also share in His resurrection. Their hope is not only for this present life but also for eternal life. But there is another important truth that must be understood. If the believer has truly been united with Christ, what happened to the old self that was once under the power of sin? This is what Paul explains in this verse.

“We know that our old self was crucified with Him, so that our sinful nature might be put to death and we would no longer be enslaved to sin.”

Once again, Paul begins with the words “We know.” This is not a new theory or some secret knowledge reserved for a select few. It is a foundational truth that every genuine believer should understand. Living the Christian life rightly begins with rightly understanding what God has already done in Christ.

The “old self” refers to our former condition before we were united with Christ. It is the person who lived under the power of sin, separated from God, and dependent on his own strength. Paul is not saying that God destroyed our personality or humanity. He is referring to our former identity as people living under the dominion of sin.

When Paul says that the old self was “crucified with Him,” he once again points to the believer's union with Christ. We were not literally nailed to the cross, but in a spiritual sense, what Christ accomplished on the cross is effective for us as well. Because of our union with Him, our former condition is no longer the basis of our identity before God.

Paul also explains the purpose of this: “so that our sinful nature might be put to death.” This does not mean that all temptation has disappeared or that a believer is no longer capable of sinning. Rather, it means that God has broken sin's dominion as the power that once held complete control over the believer's life. Sin still remains as an enemy, but it is no longer the master.

The final part of the verse shows the result of this change: “and we would no longer be enslaved to sin.” Before Christ, a person is enslaved to sin. No matter how hard he tries, he cannot set himself free. Some outward behaviors may change, but the heart remains in bondage. Only through what Christ has done can there be true freedom from that power.

It is important to notice that Paul is not saying the believer no longer struggles. In the chapters that follow in Romans, he will clearly describe the ongoing battle against sin. But there is a great difference between the struggle of a slave and the struggle of someone who has been set free.

A slave has no choice but to obey his master.

The one who has been set free may still be tempted, but he is no longer under the same dominion.

This truth gives tremendous hope to every believer. Many become discouraged because of their ongoing struggle with sin. But Paul does not first call us to look at our own strength. He directs us to the finished work of Christ. Our victory over sin does not begin with our ability but with the truth that our old self was crucified with Him.

At the same time, this truth removes every excuse for becoming complacent about sin. If God has already broken its dominion over our lives, why would we willingly return to our former slavery? God's grace does not only forgive; it also sets His children free to live in a way that pleases Him.

As this section draws to a close, we can see Paul gradually building his argument. He began by rejecting the misuse of grace, then showed the believer's union with Christ in His death, burial, and resurrection. Now he shows that the old self was also crucified with Christ so that the believer would no longer be enslaved to sin. In the verses that follow, he will explain this freedom even further and show how it should shape the believer's daily life.

CONCLUSION:

Paul shows that God's salvation is more than the forgiveness of sin. Through union with Jesus Christ, the believer's old self has been crucified so that sin's dominion over his life might be broken.

This does not mean there is no longer any struggle against sin, but it does mean that we are no longer its slaves. The power of sin no longer has the final word in defining who we are, because we now belong to Christ.

Therefore, let us not live as though we were still captives of our former life. Instead, let us trust in the finished work of the Lord Jesus Christ and live in the freedom He has given us. The grace that saved us is the same grace that gives us power to walk in obedience to God.

In the end, the old self was crucified with Christ so that sin's dominion would lose its power over the believer's life. Because of this, those who belong to Christ are no longer called to live as slaves of sin but as people who have been set free by the grace of God.
