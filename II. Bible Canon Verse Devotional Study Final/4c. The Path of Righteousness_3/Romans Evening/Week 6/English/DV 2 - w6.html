Daily Devotional Verses



Romans 6:1-6

NKJV (New King James Version)

Dead to Sin, Alive to God

1 What shall we say then? Shall we continue in sin that grace may abound? 2 Certainly not! How shall we who died to sin live any longer in it? 3 Or do you not know that as many of us as were baptized into Christ Jesus were baptized into His death? 4 Therefore we were buried with Him through baptism into death, that just as Christ was raised from the dead by the glory of the Father, even so we also should walk in newness of life.

5 For if we have been united together in the likeness of His death, certainly we also shall be in the likeness of His resurrection, 6 knowing this, that our old man was crucified with Him, that the body of sin might be done away with, that we should no longer be slaves of sin.

GUIDE REFLECTION:

Dead to Sin—How Can We Continue Living in It?

In the previous verse, Paul raised an important question that could naturally arise after understanding the abundance of God's grace: "Shall we continue in sin that grace may abound?" This question is the result of a misunderstanding of the gospel. If grace is greater than sin, can grace then become an excuse for continuing in sin? In this verse, Paul does not hesitate to give his answer.

"Certainly not!"

"How shall we who died to sin live any longer in it?"

Paul's response is one of the strongest expressions of rejection found in his letters. His "Certainly not!" is far more than a simple denial. It is a forceful declaration that such thinking is completely contrary to the truth of the gospel. Grace can never be used as permission to continue in sin because that is not the purpose of the life that has been saved.

After his emphatic rejection, Paul immediately gives the reason:

"We died to sin."

In this brief statement, Paul reveals that something has already changed in the standing of those who truly believe in Christ. Their relationship with sin is no longer what it once was. Because of this, continuing to live in sin can no longer be considered consistent with their new standing.

Being "dead to sin" does not mean that a believer is no longer capable of committing sin. Paul himself knew that believers still struggle with temptation and weakness. What he is referring to is the believer's changed standing before God.

Sin is no longer the ruling power that defines the believer's identity and standing.

For this reason, the second half of the verse becomes a logical question: "How shall we... live any longer in it?" If our standing has changed, how can our former way of life still be considered normal? Paul is not saying that a Christian will never sin again. His point is that sin can no longer be the natural, continual, and deliberate pattern of life for someone who truly belongs to Christ.

This is the great difference between much of religion today and the gospel. Religion often focuses on outward behavioral change. The gospel, however, begins with the transformation that God Himself accomplishes in a person. Paul does not command us to die to sin so that God will accept us. Rather, he proclaims that because of what God has done through Christ, there is now a new reality for those who believe, and from that new reality flows a new way of living.

This is also why merely trying harder to be a good person is never enough. If the root of the problem is our former condition under sin, it cannot be solved by accumulating more good works or by exercising greater self-discipline.

True transformation begins with what God has done, and holy living is simply the fruit of that transformation.

In our own day, there are two opposite dangers.

The first is assuming that we may continue living in sin because God's grace is abundant.

The second is believing that God will accept us only if our obedience is sufficient.

Both of these views depart from the gospel. The first minimizes the holiness of God, while the second diminishes the sufficiency of what Christ has accomplished.

The proper response is to recognize that the grace of God does more than forgive; it also places the believer into a new standing.

Because of this, our struggle against sin is no longer an attempt to earn salvation. It is the response of someone who already belongs to Christ and lives under His grace.

At this point, it is clear that Paul's first response to a misunderstanding of grace is not a call to strive harder in order to become acceptable to God. Instead, he declares a truth that every believer must embrace: continuing to live in sin is no longer consistent with the believer's new standing. This is the foundation we should meditate on as we seek to understand the depth of his words.

CONCLUSION:

Paul's first response to a misunderstanding of grace is clear and unequivocal: "Certainly not!" The grace of God is never permission to continue in sin, because those who belong to Christ have been given a new standing before God.

Although Paul has not yet fully explained how this transformation took place, he has already shown that the person who truly belongs to Christ can no longer be regarded as the same as before. His relationship with sin has changed, and that change will become the foundation of the explanations that follow.

Therefore, let us not measure our lives merely by outward effort or by the mistaken assumption that grace alone is sufficient reason to remain in sin. Instead, let us prepare our hearts to understand more deeply what God has accomplished through Christ, for it is there that we discover why the believer possesses a new life and a new relationship with sin.

In the end, the grace of God does more than forgive the sinner. It also transforms the believer's standing so that the one who once lived under the dominion of sin may now live according to the new reality that God has graciously given through Jesus Christ.
