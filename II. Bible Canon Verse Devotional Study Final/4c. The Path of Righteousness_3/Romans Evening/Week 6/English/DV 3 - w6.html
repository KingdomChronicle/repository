Daily Devotional Verses



Romans 6:1-6

NKJV (New King James Version)

Dead to Sin, Alive to God

1 What shall we say then? Shall we continue in sin that grace may abound? 2 Certainly not! How shall we who died to sin live any longer in it? 3 Or do you not know that as many of us as were baptized into Christ Jesus were baptized into His death? 4 Therefore we were buried with Him through baptism into death, that just as Christ was raised from the dead by the glory of the Father, even so we also should walk in newness of life.

5 For if we have been united together in the likeness of His death, certainly we also shall be in the likeness of His resurrection, 6 knowing this, that our old man was crucified with Him, that the body of sin might be done away with, that we should no longer be slaves of sin.

GUIDE REFLECTION:

Baptized into Christ and into His Death

In the previous verse, Paul emphatically rejected the mistaken idea that a believer may continue living in sin simply because God's grace is abundant. He explained that those who belong to Christ have "died to sin," and therefore it is no longer fitting for them to live under its dominion. But a reader may naturally ask: How did a believer become dead to sin? What is the basis of such a profound change? In this verse, Paul begins to answer these questions by pointing to the believer's union with Jesus Christ.

"Or do you not know that as many of us as were baptized into Christ Jesus were baptized into His death?"

Paul begins with the words "Or do you not know..." This indicates that the truth he is about to explain is not a new teaching being introduced for the first time. Rather, it is a foundational truth that every believer should already know and understand. The problem is not a lack of information but a failure to grasp the significance of their relationship with Christ.

When Paul speaks of being "baptized into Christ Jesus," his primary emphasis is not on the outward ceremony of water baptism. His focus is on the believer's spiritual union with Christ. The moment a person places his faith in Jesus Christ, God brings that person into a new relationship with His Son. He is no longer regarded as separate from Christ but as belonging to Him.

For this reason, Paul also says that believers were "baptized into His death." This does not mean that every believer literally died on the cross. Rather, it means that Christ's death has become the death that counts for them. What Christ accomplished is no longer merely an event that took place in history; it has become the foundation of their new standing before God.

It is important to understand that Paul's emphasis is on union with Christ, not on the ritual itself. Water baptism is an important act of obedience to the Lord's command and a public declaration of faith. However, it is not the water that unites a person with Christ. What Paul describes here is the spiritual reality that takes place when someone truly believes in the Lord Jesus Christ.

This truth sheds light on Paul's statement in the previous verse that we have "died to sin." We did not become dead to sin through our own efforts or because we decided to change our lives. It became possible because God united us with Christ, and His death became the basis of our new standing. Every spiritual blessing that a believer receives flows from his union with the Lord Jesus.

This also shows that becoming a believer is far more than adopting a new religion or following a set of rules. The gospel does not merely teach a better way of living. It proclaims that a genuine spiritual union with Christ has taken place. Because of this, the believer's life can no longer be understood apart from the life, death, and saving work of his Savior.

In our day, there is a danger of viewing faith as merely an outward identity. A person may be baptized in water, be active in church, or participate in various religious activities, yet still not truly belong to Christ. On the other hand, the true believer has a spiritual union with Christ, and outward obedience becomes the fruit of that reality.

At this point, Paul simply wants to establish one essential truth: a true believer does not merely believe about Christ but has been united by God with Him. This union with Christ is the foundation of the believer's new standing before God. Therefore, every spiritual blessing the believer receives does not come from his own works but from his relationship with Jesus Christ.

CONCLUSION:

Paul reminds us that those who believe in Jesus Christ possess far more than an outward identity as Christians. God has united them with Christ, and His death has become the foundation of their new standing before Him.

This truth explains why continuing in sin can never be the normal pattern of a believer's life. His life is no longer separate from Christ but is firmly rooted in what his Savior has accomplished.

Therefore, let us not place our confidence in outward rituals or in our own efforts. Instead, let us trust in Jesus Christ, through whom we have been brought near to God and given a new relationship with Him. As we grow in our understanding of this union with Christ, we will also grow in our understanding of the new life God has prepared for His children.

In the end, belonging to Christ is far more than a change of religion or behavior. It is a genuine spiritual union with His death, which becomes the foundation of every spiritual blessing God graciously gives to those who believe in Him.
