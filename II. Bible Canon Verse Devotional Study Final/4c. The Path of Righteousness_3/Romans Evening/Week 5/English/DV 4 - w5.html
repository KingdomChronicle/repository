Daily Devotional Verses



Romans 5:18-21

NKJV (New King James Version)

The Superiority of God's Grace

18 Therefore, as through one man’s offense judgment came to all men, resulting in condemnation, even so through one Man’s righteous act the free gift came to all men, resulting in justification of life. 19 For as by one man’s disobedience many were made sinners, so also by one Man’s obedience many will be made righteous. 20 Moreover the law entered that the offense might abound. But where sin abounded, grace abounded much more, 21 so that as sin reigned in death, even so grace might reign through righteousness to eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord.

GUIDE REFLECTION:

Where Sin Increased, Grace Increased Even More

In the previous part of the passage, Paul explained that when the Law came, human sin became even more evident as open rebellion against God. The Law was not given to save people but to reveal how deeply they needed the grace of God. Yet Paul's message does not end there. After exposing the extent of sin, he immediately points to an even greater truth.

"But where sin abounded, grace abounded much more."

The word "but" marks a significant turning point in Paul's argument. If the Law revealed the increase of sin, God now reveals the even greater abundance of His grace. There has never been a moment when sin proved more powerful than the grace of God.

This does not mean that sin is insignificant or should not be taken seriously. On the contrary, the Law makes its seriousness even more apparent. Every transgression demonstrates humanity's continual rebellion against the holy will of God. Yet as the magnitude of sin becomes more evident, the greatness of God's grace shines even more brightly.

God's grace is not merely kindness or sympathy for our weakness. It is His free and sovereign act of saving those who are completely unable to save themselves. It is not a reward for good people but a gift given to sinners who come to Him through Jesus Christ.

Therefore, when Paul says that grace "abounded much more," he is declaring that the grace of God is infinitely greater than the extent of human sin. Sin can never exhaust the grace of God. No amount of our guilt can prevent God from saving anyone who truly repents and places their faith in Christ.

Neither does this mean that the more a person continues in sin, the more they are blessed by God. Grace does not celebrate sin. Rather, grace reveals the immeasurable love and mercy of God toward those who have no righteousness of their own. Grace is God's answer to our desperate condition, not permission to remain in sin.

This is why there is no room for boasting in the life of a believer. If our salvation is entirely the result of God's grace, then we have nothing to boast about in ourselves. Everything we possess—our forgiveness, our justification, and our hope of eternal life—is the result of His gracious favor.

At the same time, this truth offers hope to hearts weighed down by their past. Some people believe they have committed too many sins to ever be forgiven by God. But Paul reminds us that God's grace is far greater than the number of our transgressions. What our own efforts could never remove, God freely provides through Jesus Christ.

Nevertheless, this grace does not diminish God's holiness. God remains perfectly holy and righteous. The reason He can forgive is because Christ has already paid the penalty for sin. Therefore, the grace of God can never be separated from what the Lord Jesus accomplished on the cross.

As we grow in our understanding of the abundance of God's grace, we also learn to trust Him more deeply and to live with grateful hearts. A heart that has truly experienced grace does not look for excuses to continue in sin. Instead, it longs to honor the God who saved it through His everlasting grace.

CONCLUSION:

Paul shows that although the Law made sin more evident, it was never God's final word. Where sin increased, His grace increased even more.

Our hope does not rest on how small our sins are but on the greatness of God's grace revealed in Jesus Christ. His grace is sufficient to forgive, justify, and give new life to everyone who comes to Him in faith.

Therefore, let us not live in pride or despair. Instead, let us humbly come before God, trust in His grace, and live as people transformed by His everlasting favor.

In the end, the more clearly we see the extent of our sin, the more clearly we recognize that the grace of God is never exhausted. Through Jesus Christ, His gracious favor is always greater than all our transgressions.
