Daily Devotional Verses



Romans 5:18-21

NKJV (New King James Version)

The Superiority of God's Grace

18 Therefore, as through one man’s offense judgment came to all men, resulting in condemnation, even so through one Man’s righteous act the free gift came to all men, resulting in justification of life. 19 For as by one man’s disobedience many were made sinners, so also by one Man’s obedience many will be made righteous. 20 Moreover the law entered that the offense might abound. But where sin abounded, grace abounded much more, 21 so that as sin reigned in death, even so grace might reign through righteousness to eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord.

GUIDE REFLECTION:

Why the Law Was Given

In the previous passage, Paul showed that Adam's disobedience brought sin into humanity, while the perfect obedience of Jesus Christ became the basis for the justification of those who believe in Him. Now, an important question naturally arises: If salvation is by grace and not by obedience to the Law, then what was the purpose of the Law that God gave?

"Moreover the law entered that the offense might abound;"

Paul is not saying that sin only began when God gave the Law. Sin had already existed since Adam's act of disobedience. The entire human race was born under the power of sin. But when God gave the Law through Moses, something new took place in humanity's relationship with sin.

The Law provided a clear revelation of God's will. The sins that people had previously committed according to their sinful nature were now exposed as direct violations of God's clearly revealed commands. The Law did not change the human heart; rather, it revealed just how deep humanity's rebellion against God truly was.

It is in this sense that Paul said "the offense might abound." This does not mean that the Law is the cause of sin or that the Law itself became evil. The Law is holy, righteous, and good. The problem is not the Law but the sinful human heart. When people are confronted with God's clear commands, their desire to rebel against their Creator becomes even more evident.

This is also why the Law was never given as the way of salvation. It was not given to remove sin or to make people righteous. Instead, it reveals humanity's true condition before God. The Law is like a mirror that clearly shows us our uncleanness, yet it has no power to cleanse us.

In fact, the more a person understands God's commandments, the more they recognize the extent of their own failure. Every command serves as a reminder that God's holiness is perfect, while humanity continually falls short in obedience. Rather than giving us a reason to boast in ourselves, the Law exposes how desperately we need the mercy of God.

This is the condition of every human being. We often think that being a good person is enough for God to accept us. But when our lives are measured by the light of His holy Law, we quickly discover that not one of us has kept it perfectly. We do not merely stumble from time to time; we continually fall short of God's holy standard.

As a result, every reason for boasting is removed. The Law gives us no basis for trusting in our own righteousness. Instead, it leads us to the humble realization that we cannot save ourselves. The more clearly we see our sin, the more deeply we understand our need for the grace that comes from God alone.

God's purpose was never to leave humanity in despair. By exposing our sin through the Law, He was preparing us to see an even greater truth. Once we clearly recognize the depth of our disobedience, our hearts become ready to understand how abundant God's grace is toward sinners.

CONCLUSION:

God gave the Law not to save humanity but to expose our true condition. In the light of the Law, it becomes unmistakably clear that every person is a sinner and is unable to become righteous through personal obedience.

When we acknowledge this truth, our confidence in ourselves disappears, and we learn to depend on the grace of God. The Law is not our Savior; it is the one that points us to our need for a Savior.

However, this does not mean that the Law has lost its value in the lives of those who believe in Christ. Although it is not the basis of our salvation, it remains the revelation of God's holy will. Those who have been saved by grace no longer obey the Law in order to be saved, but because they have already been saved. Through the Holy Spirit, God guides and empowers His children to live lives of holiness and righteousness according to His will.

Therefore, let us never make the Law the foundation of our hope. Instead, let it lead us to Christ, who alone is able to grant true justification and life. And as those who have already received His grace, let the Word of God also serve as our guide in our daily walk so that we may increasingly display a life that is pleasing to Him.

In the end, the Law was given to expose the depth of our disobedience, not to become the means of our salvation. Yet for those who have already been saved by Christ, the Law remains the revelation of God's will and is used by the Holy Spirit to shape us into lives of obedience. The more clearly we see our sin, the more deeply we understand the abundance of God's grace and the power He gives us to live for His glory.
