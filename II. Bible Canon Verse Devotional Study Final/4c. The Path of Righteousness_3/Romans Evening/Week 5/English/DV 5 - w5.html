Daily Devotional Verses



Romans 5:18-21

NKJV (New King James Version)

The Superiority of God's Grace

18 Therefore, as through one man’s offense judgment came to all men, resulting in condemnation, even so through one Man’s righteous act the free gift came to all men, resulting in justification of life. 19 For as by one man’s disobedience many were made sinners, so also by one Man’s obedience many will be made righteous. 20 Moreover the law entered that the offense might abound. But where sin abounded, grace abounded much more, 21 so that as sin reigned in death, even so grace might reign through righteousness to eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord.

GUIDE REFLECTION:

The Reign of Sin Through Death

In the previous verse, Paul showed that where sin increased, the grace of God increased even more. He did not deny the seriousness of sin, but declared that God’s grace is far greater than the extent of our disobedience. Now, he describes two opposing reigns—the reign of sin and the reign of grace. He begins by describing the first reign.

“so that as sin reigned in death,”

Paul does not describe sin as a simple mistake or an occasional wrong action. He describes it as a ruling power. The word “reigned” speaks of dominion, authority, and prevailing power. From the moment sin entered through Adam’s disobedience, the whole human race came under its rule.

This reign is revealed through death. Paul is not referring only to physical death, although that is clearly one of the consequences of sin. Death also portrays humanity’s separation from God, the ruin of His original purpose for mankind, and humanity’s inability to save itself from the consequences of sin.

Throughout human history, no one has escaped this reality. Every person is born, grows old, and dies. Despite the advancement of knowledge, science, and technology, humanity remains unable to defeat the power of death. This reminds us that the root of humanity’s problem is far deeper than disease, poverty, or social difficulties. Sin is the true ruling power, and death is the continuing evidence of its reign.

Moreover, sin also reigns in the human heart. In our own strength, we cannot completely turn away from sin or live according to the holy will of God. Even when we know what is right, we often do the opposite. This shows that people do not merely commit sin; they are under the power of sin.

This is why good intentions, discipline, or personal effort are not enough to solve humanity’s deepest problem. We may be able to change some outward habits, but we cannot free ourselves from the power that rules our sinful nature. A slave cannot set himself free while he remains under the authority of his master.

This truth humbles us. We often think we can fix our lives if only we try hard enough. But Paul shows that the problem is not merely a lack of effort. The problem is the reign of sin, which brings death and reveals our inability to save ourselves.

Nevertheless, Paul did not write these words to leave us without hope. Instead, he wants us to see the depth of our need. Until we understand how powerful the reign of sin is, we will never fully understand how great the salvation God has provided in Jesus Christ truly is.

The reign of sin may appear powerful, but it is not the final chapter in the story of redemption. A greater King has come, and Paul will declare a greater reign in the next part of the verse.

CONCLUSION:

Sin is not merely an action that must be avoided; it is a power that rules over humanity and produces death. Because of this, no one can free themselves through personal effort or goodness alone.

When we acknowledge this truth, our confidence in ourselves disappears, and we learn to depend on God. Recognizing the depth of our problem prepares us to understand more fully the greatness of His salvation.

Therefore, let us not underestimate the power of sin, but neither should we lose hope. God has prepared a greater reign that is able to bring an end to the power of sin and death.

In the end, sin reigned through death and revealed humanity’s inability to save itself. But this truth prepares us to see the far greater reign of God’s grace through Jesus Christ.
