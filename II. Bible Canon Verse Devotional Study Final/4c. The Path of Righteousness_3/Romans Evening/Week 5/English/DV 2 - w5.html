Daily Devotional Verses



Romans 5:18-21

NKJV (New King James Version)

The Superiority of God's Grace

18 Therefore, as through one man’s offense judgment came to all men, resulting in condemnation, even so through one Man’s righteous act the free gift came to all men, resulting in justification of life. 19 For as by one man’s disobedience many were made sinners, so also by one Man’s obedience many will be made righteous. 20 Moreover the law entered that the offense might abound. But where sin abounded, grace abounded much more, 21 so that as sin reigned in death, even so grace might reign through righteousness to eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord.

GUIDE REFLECTION:

Adam's Disobedience and Christ's Obedience

In the previous verse, Paul presented the contrasting results of what Adam did and what Jesus Christ accomplished. One brought condemnation and death, while the other brought justification and life. In this verse, he deepens the comparison by focusing on the very root of these contrasting outcomes—the disobedience of one man and the obedience of another Man.

“For as by one man’s disobedience many were made sinners, so also by one Man’s obedience many will be made righteous.”

Paul is not merely presenting two different results but also two different responses to God. Adam disobeyed God's clear command. He knew what God had forbidden, yet he still chose to follow his own will rather than submit to the word of the Lord. His disobedience became the means by which sin entered humanity.

Because Adam was the representative of the entire human race, the consequences of his disobedience were not limited to himself. His transgression placed all mankind in the condition of being sinners. From him, the whole human race was born with a sinful nature and separated from God. This is why no one is naturally righteous before God.

On the other hand, Paul points to the obedience of one Man—Jesus Christ. If the history of humanity began with one act of disobedience, the history of redemption rests upon the perfect obedience of Christ. His entire life was marked by complete submission to the Father's will. He never turned aside from anything God commanded but fulfilled everything with perfect faithfulness.

The supreme expression of this obedience was seen in His willing sacrifice of Himself on the cross. Though He was without sin, He humbly embraced the path of suffering and death in order to fulfill God's purpose of saving sinners. His obedience did not end with good teachings or a holy life alone; it extended to the offering of His own life for our sake.

Because of Christ's perfect obedience, many will be made righteous. This does not mean that we become righteous because we are able to imitate His obedience. Our righteousness rests entirely on what Christ has done for us. His perfect obedience became the basis upon which God declares righteous everyone who believes in Him.

This shows that salvation is not the result of our own obedience but of Christ's obedience. We often think that God will accept us more if we are good enough or if our good deeds outweigh our failures. But Paul reminds us that our hope does not begin with what we have done but with what Christ has already accomplished.

This truth also exposes the true condition of our hearts. Like Adam, we naturally desire to follow our own will rather than the will of God. Even when we know His commands, we often choose our own desires instead. This reveals that humanity's problem is not merely a lack of knowledge but a heart that is naturally opposed to God.

Therefore, we have nothing to boast about before God. If our salvation depended on our own obedience, not one of us would have any hope. But the good news is that there is One who was perfectly obedient on our behalf. What we could never accomplish, Jesus Christ fulfilled completely.

This truth does not encourage us to become careless in the way we live. Rather, the more we understand Christ's obedience, the more we are inspired to live in obedience as a response to the grace we have received.

We do not obey in order to be saved; we obey because we have been saved through His perfect obedience.

CONCLUSION:

Adam's disobedience brought the entire human race under the power of sin. But the perfect obedience of Jesus Christ became the basis for the justification of all who believe in Him.

Our hope does not rest on our ability to be perfectly obedient. It rests on Christ's perfect obedience, which extended even to His death on the cross to fulfill the Father's will for our salvation.

Therefore, instead of trusting in our own righteousness, let us place our trust in Jesus Christ. And in response to His grace, let us strive to live each day with hearts that are willing to obey God.

In the end, the disobedience of one man brought many into sin, but the perfect obedience of the Lord Jesus Christ opened the way for all who believe in Him to be justified and receive life.
