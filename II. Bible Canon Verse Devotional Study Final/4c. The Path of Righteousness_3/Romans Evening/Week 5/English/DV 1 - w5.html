Daily Devotional Verses



Romans 5:18-21

NKJV (New King James Version)

The Superiority of God's Grace

18 Therefore, as through one man’s offense judgment came to all men, resulting in condemnation, even so through one Man’s righteous act the free gift came to all men, resulting in justification of life. 19 For as by one man’s disobedience many were made sinners, so also by one Man’s obedience many will be made righteous. 20 Moreover the law entered that the offense might abound. But where sin abounded, grace abounded much more, 21 so that as sin reigned in death, even so grace might reign through righteousness to eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord.

GUIDE REFLECTION:

Condemnation and Justification

In the earlier part of his letter, Paul carefully explained how sin entered the world through one man, and through sin came death. From Adam, all humanity came under the power of sin and the righteous judgment of God. In this verse, Paul summarizes the great contrast between what Adam did and what Jesus Christ accomplished.

“Therefore, as through one man’s offense judgment came to all men, resulting in condemnation, even so through one Man’s righteous act the free gift came to all men, resulting in justification of life.”

Paul places before us two men and two actions. Adam’s disobedience brought condemnation, while Jesus Christ’s righteous act brings justification and life. Not only are their actions different, but the results they produced for those they represented are completely opposite.

Condemnation is God's righteous judgment against sin. When Adam disobeyed God's clear command, he did not merely commit a personal mistake. As the first man and the representative of humanity, his disobedience brought all his descendants under the condition of sin, death, and judgment. Sin entered human history, and no one born from Adam has escaped its consequences.

Yet Paul's argument does not end with condemnation. He presents the “righteous act” of one Man—Jesus Christ. This refers to His perfect work as the Savior, culminating in the offering of Himself on the cross. If Adam's act brought judgment, Christ's act became the righteous basis upon which sinners may be justified and receive life.

Justification does not mean that God ignored sin. It is God's righteous declaration that the sinner who believes in Christ is accepted as righteous in His sight. This is possible not because of human goodness, but because sin was fully dealt with and paid for through the work of Jesus Christ.

In this way, God's grace does not operate against His justice. God did not have to set aside His holiness in order to save sinners. At the cross, both the weight of His judgment against sin and the greatness of His love for sinners were displayed. Justification is a gift of grace, but it is a gift given through the righteous work of Christ.

The statement that Christ's work brings justification and life “to all” does not mean that every person is automatically saved regardless of his response to Christ. In the previous verse, Paul made it clear that the abundance of grace and the gift of righteousness are received. Justification and life are granted to all who come to Jesus Christ and receive Him through faith.

This reveals the true condition of humanity. By nature, we want to believe that we can correct ourselves, make up for our failures, or become worthy of God's acceptance through good works. But if our condition is condemnation, simply improving our behavior is not enough. A person under judgment does not merely need a fresh start; he needs to be justified.

We have no righteousness of our own to overturn God's judgment. Our good works cannot erase our sin, and our efforts cannot restore the life that was lost through disobedience. If we are left to our own ability, we will remain under condemnation. This is why salvation cannot originate from man. It must come from God as the free gift of His grace.

At the same time, this verse offers genuine hope to anyone who feels the weight of his sin. Condemnation does not have to be the final word for the one who comes to Christ. In Him, there is justification for the guilty and life for those who were once under death. We do not need to hide our sin or prove ourselves worthy first. We are invited to come in repentance and faith to the only righteous act that is sufficient for us to be accepted by God.

Therefore, the proper response is not confidence in our own goodness but humble acceptance of God's grace. The believer no longer has to live as though his acceptance before God depends on the adequacy of his own efforts. His hope rests upon what Christ has accomplished, and from that assurance flows a life of gratitude, obedience, and trust in God.

In this verse, Paul presents the opposite outcomes of two actions: Adam's disobedience brought condemnation, but Christ's righteous act brings justification and life.

CONCLUSION:

Adam's sin placed humanity under God's righteous condemnation. But through the righteous act of Jesus Christ, God has provided justification and life for those who receive Him by faith.

Our salvation does not depend on our ability to correct ourselves. It rests upon the perfect and sufficient work of Christ. In Him, the guilty sinner can be justified, and the one who was once under death can receive life.

Therefore, let us not trust in our own righteousness or attempt to conceal our true condition. Instead, let us come to Jesus Christ with repentance and faith, and receive the gift that only the grace of God can provide.

In the end, the condemnation that came through Adam's disobedience does not have to remain the final verdict upon us, for through the righteous act of Jesus Christ, God has provided justification and life for all who believe in Him.
