Daily Devotional Verses



Romans 5:18-21

NKJV (New King James Version)

The Superiority of God's Grace

18 Therefore, as through one man’s offense judgment came to all men, resulting in condemnation, even so through one Man’s righteous act the free gift came to all men, resulting in justification of life. 19 For as by one man’s disobedience many were made sinners, so also by one Man’s obedience many will be made righteous. 20 Moreover the law entered that the offense might abound. But where sin abounded, grace abounded much more, 21 so that as sin reigned in death, even so grace might reign through righteousness to eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord.

GUIDE REFLECTION:

The Reign of Grace Leading to Eternal Life

In the previous part of the passage, Paul presented the sobering reality that sin reigns through death. Ever since sin entered humanity through Adam, all people have come under its power. But that is not the end of the gospel message. After describing the reign of sin, Paul now reveals a far greater reign—the reign of God's grace.

“even so grace might reign through righteousness to eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord.”

If sin is portrayed as a reigning power, so also is the grace of God. Yet there is a profound difference between their reigns. The reign of sin brings death, whereas the reign of grace brings life. This shows that God's final word for those who believe in Christ is not death but life.

Neither does grace reign by overlooking sin. God did not set aside His holiness or His justice in order to save mankind. Rather, Paul says that grace reigns “through righteousness.” This means that God's grace operates in a way that is perfectly consistent with His righteousness.

Justification is God's declaration that the sinner who believes in Jesus Christ is accepted as righteous in His sight. This is not because the person has become perfect, but because the righteousness of Christ is the basis of his acceptance before God. In this way, God's grace and justice are not in conflict. At the cross of Christ, the holy judgment against sin was fully satisfied, and grace is freely bestowed upon all who believe.

The result of this reign is “eternal life.” This is more than endless existence. Eternal life is the new life that flows from being reconciled to God. It is a life that begins now in the believer's relationship with Christ and will be fully experienced in His everlasting kingdom.

It is also important to notice that all of this is possible only “through Jesus Christ our Lord.” He is the only way through whom grace reigns. There is no other name, no other Savior, and no other way by which a person can be justified and receive eternal life. God's entire plan of salvation is centered on Christ.

This is why every believer has a firm hope. He no longer lives under the condemnation of sin but under the reign of God's grace. This does not mean that he no longer struggles against sin, but it does mean that sin no longer has the final authority to condemn him or separate him from God. The grace of God now reigns over his new standing in Christ.

This truth should produce deep gratitude and worship. Salvation is not the result of our own efforts but of the grace of God. Eternal life is not a reward for our goodness but a gift that we have received through Jesus Christ. Therefore, every day of our lives should be a response of thanksgiving for the grace we have received.

At the same time, the reign of grace does not give us permission to become complacent toward sin. The grace that saved us is the same grace that calls us to live for God. This is the truth that Paul will explain even further in the next chapter, where he answers the mistaken idea that we may continue in sin so that grace may abound.

As Paul concludes this section of Romans, he clearly presents the great contrast between Adam and Christ. Through one act of disobedience came sin, condemnation, and death. But through the righteous act and perfect obedience of Jesus Christ came justification, the reign of grace, and eternal life for all who believe in Him.

CONCLUSION:

Sin once reigned through death, but it is no longer the ruling power in the lives of those who are in Christ. Through His grace, God grants justification and eternal life to all who believe in His Son.

Our assurance does not rest upon our own righteousness but upon the finished work of Jesus Christ. Because of Him, the grace of God reigns instead of sin, and life triumphs instead of death.

Therefore, let us live each day with gratitude, trust, and obedience to our Lord Jesus Christ. The grace that saved us is the same grace that continues to guide us until we fully experience eternal life in His presence.

In the end, the great story of Romans 5:12–21 concludes not with the reign of sin but with the reign of God's grace. Through Jesus Christ, justification leads to eternal life, and the grace of God has the final word for all who believe in Him.
