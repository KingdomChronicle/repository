Daily Devotional Verses



Romans 6:12-14

NKJV (New King James Version)

Living Under Grace

12 Therefore do not let sin reign in your mortal body, that you should obey it in its lusts. 13 And do not present your members as instruments of unrighteousness to sin, but present yourselves to God as being alive from the dead, and your members as instruments of righteousness to God. 14 For sin shall not have dominion over you, for you are not under law but under grace.

GUIDE REFLECTION:

Under Grace, Not Under Law

In Romans 6:12-14, Paul calls believers not to let sin reign in their lives, but to present themselves to God as being alive from the dead. In the closing words of verse 14, he gives the crucial reason they can live free from sin's dominion:

“For you are not under law but under grace.”

The word “for” is important. Paul does not simply command believers not to let sin reign and then leave them to rely on their own strength. Something fundamental has changed in the position of the person who is in Christ: he is no longer “under law” but “under grace.” That change becomes the foundation of a new way of life.

We have already seen that being “not under law” does not mean that God's law is evil or that holiness and obedience no longer matter. The law is holy, just, and good. The problem lies with sinful humanity. The law reveals what is right and exposes our disobedience, but it does not have the power to change the sinful heart or free us from the dominion of sin.

Paul says the same thing elsewhere:

“Therefore by the deeds of the law no flesh will be justified in His sight, for by the law is the knowledge of sin.” — Romans 3:20

We have also seen that the law is like a mirror that reveals our true condition. When a mirror shows us that our face is dirty, it serves an important purpose because it exposes the problem, but it cannot wash the dirt away. In the same way, the law reveals our sin, but it cannot do what is necessary to justify us or give us new life.

For this reason, the believer no longer comes before God as though obedience to the law were the basis of his righteousness or acceptance before Him. He does not have to earn God's acceptance through his own obedience. His standing before God is now rooted in what Jesus Christ has done.

This helps us understand what it means to be “under grace.” Grace is more than forgiveness for the sins we have committed. It is a new position in which the believer no longer depends on his own ability to make himself acceptable to God, but rests in what God has done for him in Christ.

We see the heart of this grace in the words of Jesus:

“Come to Me, all you who labor and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.” — Matthew 11:28

Jesus did not say that we must first fix ourselves until we become worthy to come to Him. His invitation is given precisely to those who labor and are heavy laden. We come to God in our need, and we receive from Christ what we could never provide for ourselves.

But as we have already seen, living under grace does not mean that we are free to serve sin. The grace that forgives us also brings us into a new relationship with God. Obedience is no longer a means of earning His acceptance, but the response of someone who has already been accepted in Christ. We were not saved so that we could freely serve sin, but so that we could freely live for God.

This matters deeply in everyday life. Without realizing it, we may begin to live as though we constantly have to prove our worth to God. When our spiritual life seems to be going well, we may feel that God is closer to us; but when we fail, we may think we first have to improve ourselves before we can draw near to Him again. In this way, our assurance begins to rest on how well we are obeying rather than on the grace of God.

But our access to God rests on Christ. This does not make sin insignificant; when we fail, we must confess and repent of it. Yet we do not have to hide from God until we feel worthy enough to return. We can come to Him, confess our sin, and once again place our trust in His grace.

We see this same invitation in Hebrews:

“Let us therefore come boldly to the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy and find grace to help in time of need.” — Hebrews 4:16

This is what life under grace looks like: not becoming comfortable with sin, but continually coming to God. When we are weak, we come to Him for help; when we sin, we come to Him in repentance; when we are tempted, we come to Him for strength. And when we stand firm, we recognize that it is God's grace, not our own ability, that sustains us.

Grace, then, teaches us humility while also giving us assurance. We are humbled because we have no righteousness of our own in which to boast, yet we have confidence because our relationship with God does not depend on the changing measure of our success, but on what Christ has done.

So we do not have to fall into either of two wrong paths:

  • becoming complacent about sin
  • or trying to make ourselves acceptable to God through our own works.

Grace is not permission to sin, but neither are we required to earn God's acceptance. We can obey as people who have already been saved, accepted, and given new life in Christ.

CONCLUSION:

Romans 6:14 reveals a great change in the believer's position: “you are not under law but under grace.” Our salvation and acceptance before God are not rooted in our own righteousness, but in what Jesus Christ has done. This grace is not permission to sin; instead, it sets us free to live for God. Our obedience, then, is a response to His grace, not a payment we make in order to earn His acceptance.

When we experience victory, we do not need to trust in our own righteousness; when we fail, we do not have to make ourselves worthy before coming back to God. In both circumstances, we look to Christ and depend on His grace.

In the end, life under grace is not a life that is free to serve sin, but a life that is free to draw near to God and obey Him. We are not accepted because our obedience is sufficient; we are accepted because Christ is sufficient. And from that assurance, we can live not to earn grace, but as people who already live under it.
