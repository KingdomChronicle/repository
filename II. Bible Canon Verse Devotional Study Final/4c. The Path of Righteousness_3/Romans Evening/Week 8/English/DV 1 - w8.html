Daily Devotional Verses



Romans 6:12-14

NKJV (New King James Version)

Living Under Grace

12 Therefore do not let sin reign in your mortal body, that you should obey it in its lusts. 13 And do not present your members as instruments of unrighteousness to sin, but present yourselves to God as being alive from the dead, and your members as instruments of righteousness to God. 14 For sin shall not have dominion over you, for you are not under law but under grace.

GUIDE REFLECTION:

Do Not Give Sin the Right to Rule

In the verses before this passage, Paul explained what God has accomplished for those who are united with Christ. The old self was crucified with Him, the believer's former bondage to sin was broken, and he is now called to consider himself dead to sin but alive to God in Christ Jesus. With this new identity in view, Paul now gives a practical command concerning how believers should live each day.

“Therefore do not let sin reign in your mortal body, that you should obey it in its lusts.”

The word “reign” carries an important meaning. Paul presents sin as a ruler attempting to take control and issue commands. He is not simply warning against isolated sinful actions. The deeper issue is authority—who has the right to rule, whose voice we follow, and what we allow to influence the direction of our lives. Therefore, when Paul tells believers not to let sin reign, he is reminding them that sin must no longer be treated as their master.

This does not mean that believers will never experience sin or temptation again. In fact, Paul's command makes it clear that the struggle remains. If there were no sinful desires, temptations, or opportunities to give in, there would be no need for this warning. The believer has been released from the former rule of sin, but he still lives in a world where sin is present and where sinful desires can attempt to draw him away from obedience to God.

Jesus also spoke about what happens when a person continually places himself under sin's control.

“Most assuredly, I say to you, whoever commits sin is a slave of sin.” — John 8:34

Jesus makes it clear that sin is never something we can safely entertain without consequences. It may appear to offer pleasure, freedom, or satisfaction, but its ultimate purpose is to bring a person into bondage. The more someone continually gives in to sin, the more deeply that person can become controlled by it. This is why Paul's warning matters. The sin that once ruled us must not be welcomed back as though it still possesses the authority to govern those who now belong to Christ.

Paul specifically refers to “your mortal body.” Our bodies belong to this present earthly life and have not yet experienced the fullness of the redemption that will come. We experience weakness, exhaustion, hunger, pain, fear, physical desires, and other limitations. These things can become opportunities that sin attempts to use against us. The body itself is not sinful, but its desires can be used as channels through which sin seeks to influence our choices and actions.

This brings us to the next part of Paul's instruction: “that you should obey it in its lusts.” Experiencing a desire does not mean that we are required to follow it. Being tempted does not mean that we have already surrendered.

A sinful desire may feel powerful, but its intensity does not give it the authority to rule over us.

The believer can face temptation knowing that sin is no longer his master and therefore does not have to be obeyed.

This truth is easy to forget when we are in the middle of a difficult struggle. When temptation becomes intense, we can begin to think that surrender is inevitable.

If we have battled the same sinful pattern for many years, we may begin to think, “This is simply who I am,” or “I have no power to resist this.” When we think this way, we unintentionally give temptation an authority that God has not given it. We begin to act as though every desire we experience must determine our behavior.

Paul does not describe the Christian life in this way. He does not claim that resisting sin will always be easy, nor does he say that every sinful desire will immediately disappear. Instead, his command rests on the truth that our position in Christ has already changed. Since the believer is no longer a slave to sin, he is no longer obligated to obey the commands of his former master. Sin may still make its demands, but those demands no longer have to control our decisions.

Scripture gives us a strong encouragement concerning temptation. Paul told the Corinthians:

“God is faithful, who will not allow you to be tempted beyond what you are able, but with the temptation will also make the way of escape, that you may be able to bear it.” — 1 Corinthians 10:13

This promise does not suggest that temptation is weak or insignificant. Instead, it reminds us that our struggle takes place under the faithfulness of God. We do not have to assume that every temptation is impossible to resist.

There are times when we can refuse, walk away, pray for God's help, seek wise support, and choose obedience instead of giving in. Sometimes this means making the same decision repeatedly, day after day. But we are not fighting as people who have no freedom. Through Christ, we have been given a new position from which we can resist sin.

That is why we need to honestly identify the areas where we may be allowing sin to gain control. It may appear...

in anger that we continually defend and justify,

in desires that we secretly continue to nurture,

in envy that we are unwilling to surrender,

in pride that we refuse to acknowledge,

or in habits and practices we know do not please God but still continue to make room for.

We cannot claim that we want to overcome sin while deliberately continuing to follow the desires that lead us toward it.

Paul's command calls us to take sin seriously while holding firmly to the hope we have in Christ. Sin must be taken seriously because it continually seeks an opportunity to rule.

Yet there is also great hope because the person who is united with Christ is no longer required to live under sin's authority. When temptation appears, we can recognize what is really happening:

a former master attempting to reclaim authority over a life that no longer belongs to it.

Living out Romans 6:12 requires continual awareness of what is happening within our hearts, thoughts, and desires. We do not have to wait for temptation to disappear before choosing obedience. The moment we recognize sin trying to gain control, we can remember who we are in Christ and refuse to give it authority over us. Sin may continue to attract and tempt us, but its desires are no longer commands that we are obligated to follow.

CONCLUSION:

In Romans 6:12, Paul brings the truth of our new life in Christ directly into the everyday battle against sin. Because the believer has died to sin and is now alive to God, he must not allow sin to reclaim its former position as ruler of his life.

This freedom does not mean that temptation will disappear or that sinful desires will never return. Their attraction can still be strong. But temptation is not our master, and a sinful desire is not an order that must be obeyed. Because we are united with Christ, we no longer have to live as though surrendering to sin is our only option.

Therefore, when temptation comes, do not judge your spiritual condition simply by how strong the temptation feels. Instead, remember whose you are and what Christ has already accomplished for you. Do not surrender the throne of your life to a sin that no longer possesses the right to rule over someone who has been redeemed and united with Christ.

In the end, sin may continue to call, tempt, and demand our obedience, but we no longer need to acknowledge it as our king. Because we belong to Christ, we can face every temptation with this truth: sin no longer has the right to reign over our lives.
