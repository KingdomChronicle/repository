Daily Devotional Verses



Romans 6:12-14

NKJV (New King James Version)

Living Under Grace

12 Therefore do not let sin reign in your mortal body, that you should obey it in its lusts. 13 And do not present your members as instruments of unrighteousness to sin, but present yourselves to God as being alive from the dead, and your members as instruments of righteousness to God. 14 For sin shall not have dominion over you, for you are not under law but under grace.

GUIDE REFLECTION:

Sin Shall Not Have Dominion Over You

In the preceding verses, Paul commands believers not to let sin reign in their lives, but instead to present themselves to God. This shows us that the struggle against sin is real, but it is not a hopeless battle. Paul now gives the basis for their obedience:

“For sin shall not have dominion over you,”

The word “For” is important. Paul does not simply tell believers to resist sin and then leave them to depend on their own strength. The command to live differently is grounded in what God has already done and in the new position believers now have in Christ: sin shall not have dominion over them.

The picture of “dominion” is also important. Paul portrays sin as a king or master that once ruled over a person's life. Before Christ, the problem was not merely that a person occasionally did what was wrong; he lived under the power of sin. But through union with Christ, his position has changed. The power that once ruled over him no longer has the same claim over his life.

This does not mean that the presence of sin has disappeared. Temptation, weakness, and old habits and desires are still present. But there is a great difference between the presence of sin and its dominion. Sin may still wage war against us, but it is no longer the rightful master of the person who is in Christ.

We can see the importance of this freedom in what Jesus said:

So if the Son sets you free, you will be free indeed. — John 8:36

The freedom Christ gives is more than a change in a few sinful habits; it is freedom from our former slavery to sin. Our hope in the struggle, then, does not rest on the strength of our determination, but on Christ, who has set us free.

This is especially important when we repeatedly fail in the face of the same temptation. It is easy to begin thinking, “Maybe this is simply who I am.” But although sin must be acknowledged, repented of, and forsaken, it is not the highest truth about us. The deepest truth about a believer is not how powerful his temptation may feel, but to whom he now belongs.

We see the same assurance in what John says about the forgiveness found in God:

“But if we confess our sins to God, we can be assured that God will forgive us and cleanse us from all unrighteousness because He is faithful and just.” — 1 John 1:9

When a believer fails, he can come to God, confess his sin, receive His forgiveness, and rise again to continue the struggle. God's grace is not a reason to take sin lightly; it is the reason a person who truly repents does not have to lose hope.

There are two wrong ways of thinking that we need to avoid. Failure may lead us to say,

“I cannot resist sin, so I might as well give up.”

On the other hand, victory may lead us to think,

“I am strong now, so I can defeat sin on my own.”

Both responses take our eyes off Christ. The right kind of confidence says: I am weak, and my struggle is real, but I am no longer in my former condition because God has done something for me in Christ.

Freedom from the dominion of sin also does not mean that there will never be another failure. The Christian life involves growth, struggle, repentance, and getting back up again. But through it all, the believer is no longer under sin's former dominion.

When we understand this, it changes the way we face temptation. We do not have to approach the struggle assuming that defeat is inevitable. We can draw near to God, avoid situations that lead us toward sin, seek help, use His Word, and take practical steps of obedience. This is not a reason to become complacent; it is a reason to fight with courage and perseverance.

When we fail, genuine repentance gives us not only an opportunity to ask for forgiveness but also an opportunity to learn. We can look back and consider how we fell, what choices brought us closer to sin, and what consequences followed. Trust may have been broken, someone may have been hurt, time or money may have been wasted, or responsibilities may have been neglected. God's forgiveness does not make these consequences insignificant; He can use them to teach us not to treat sin lightly and to become more watchful.

👉 So when we fail, we can ask, “Lord, what do I need to learn from this? Where was I weak? What did I allow to draw me closer to temptation? What do I need to change so that I will not fall so easily in the same way again?”

If we repeatedly struggle with the same temptation, it is important to learn from our past failures. We can identify the situations and habits that commonly draw us toward it and make changes early, before we find ourselves following the same path again.

Past failure does not have to become a reason to give up; it can become a lesson that helps us prepare more wisely for the next struggle. By God's grace, we can rise again with greater wisdom, watchfulness, and dependence on Him.

CONCLUSION:

In Romans 6:14a, Paul gives assurance to believers who are struggling against sin: “For sin shall not have dominion over you,” He is not saying that temptation or the possibility of failure will disappear. What he is declaring is something deeper—the former dominion of sin has been broken in the life of the person who has been united with Christ.

Therefore, we do not have to lose hope when the struggle is intense, nor should we place our confidence in ourselves when we experience victory. Our hope and victory are rooted in what God has done in Christ.

When you fail, come to God in repentance and rise again. When you are tempted, do not assume that you have no choice but to obey sin. And when you experience victory, do not think that your own strength is what set you free. Sin may still be present as an enemy, but it no longer has dominion over you. Therefore, you can fight with hope, because in Christ the former dominion of sin over your life has been broken.
