Daily Devotional Verses



Romans 6:12-14

NKJV (New King James Version)

Living Under Grace

12 Therefore do not let sin reign in your mortal body, that you should obey it in its lusts. 13 And do not present your members as instruments of unrighteousness to sin, but present yourselves to God as being alive from the dead, and your members as instruments of righteousness to God. 14 For sin shall not have dominion over you, for you are not under law but under grace.

GUIDE REFLECTION:

The Body as an Instrument of Righteousness

In the first part of the verse, Paul commands believers not to place any part of their bodies at the service of sin as instruments of unrighteousness. He then calls them to present themselves to God as those who have been brought from death to life. Now he shows us how that surrender is expressed in practical terms:

the very life and body that were once available to sin can now be offered to God as instruments of righteousness.

“And your members as instruments of righteousness to God.”

The word “instruments” is important. Paul is not simply telling us to stop using our bodies for what is wrong. His call is also positive:

the life we present to God can now be placed at His service. Our bodies, strength, time, abilities, opportunities, and the blessings entrusted to us can be used for what is right and in accordance with His will.

Paul describes our members as “instruments of righteousness.” An instrument is placed at the disposal of the one who uses it and serves the purpose for which it is used.

  • The same hands can hurt or help.
  • The same mouth can lie or speak the truth.
  • The same eyes can look at what is wrong or notice someone who needs help.
  • The same mind can be used to plan for personal gain or to think about how to serve others.

So the question is not only what we have, but how we use it and whom we are using it for.

We also see this principle in what Jesus said about the good works of His followers:

“In the same way, let your light shine before others, that they may see your good deeds and glorify your Father in heaven.” — Matthew 5:16

A believer does not live a good life in order to draw attention to himself. The purpose is for God's goodness to be seen through his life and for God Himself to be honored. When we use our bodies and abilities to do what is right, ordinary life becomes a place where God's light can shine before the people around us.

This is deeply practical. Faith does not exist only in our minds or in the moments when we pray and worship. We use our bodies in nearly everything we do each day.

  • With our mouths, we can encourage someone who is discouraged, tell the truth when it would be easier to lie, apologize when we have done wrong, or speak about the goodness of God.
  • With our hands, we can work honestly, help those in need, and serve without expecting anything in return.
  • We can use our time to listen to someone who needs companionship, our abilities to help others, and our strength to faithfully carry out the responsibilities entrusted to us.

Scripture gives us the same reminder about our everyday work:

“Whatever you do, work at it with all your heart, as working for the Lord, not for human masters.” — Colossians 3:23

This shows us that being an instrument of righteousness is not limited to activities we commonly think of as spiritual. A believer can serve God at work, at home, at school, in relationships with others, and even through simple responsibilities that no one else sees. When what we do is surrendered to God and carried out according to His will, even the ordinary parts of life can become opportunities for obedience and service to Him.

But there is another important way to understand what it means to present ourselves to God. Some believers may say, “I'm not a pastor. I'm not good at talking about the Bible. I'm not a missionary. What can I really contribute to the work of God's Kingdom?” Because of this, they may begin to think that active service belongs only to certain people while everyone else simply watches from the sidelines.

But that is not how the Bible describes believers. Not everyone has been given the same role or ability, but every true believer is part of one body.

“Now you are the body of Christ, and each one of you is a part of it.” — 1 Corinthians 12:27

What a beautiful picture. A body is not made up of only one part. The foot does not need to become a hand in order to be valuable, and the ear does not need to become an eye in order to be useful. Each part has its own function, but when they work together, the whole body is able to do what it was meant to do.

The same is true of the body of Christ. A person who serves visibly at the front is not more important than someone who quietly serves behind the scenes. Not everyone is called to preach from a pulpit, become a missionary in a distant place, or lead a large ministry. But that does not mean that other believers have no part in God's work.

Paul himself explained that there are different gifts and different ways of serving:

“For just as each of us has one body with many members, and these members do not all have the same function, so in Christ we, though many, form one body, and each member belongs to all the others. We have different gifts, according to the grace given to each of us, so let us use them.” — Romans 12:4-6

As the passage continues, Paul gives different examples: serving, teaching, encouraging, giving, leading, and showing mercy. This reminds us that God's work does not depend on only one kind of ability. Many different parts are needed for the work as a whole to move forward.

God may not be calling you to become a missionary who travels to different places, but He may have given you the ability to help meet the needs of those who do. You may not be the preacher, but perhaps God has entrusted you with the ability to give so that others can be sent, fed, supported, and equipped, and so that the broader work of God's Kingdom on earth can continue.

Your ability may be in management and organization, finance, administration, teaching, music, writing, technology, hospitality, caring for those in need, encouragement, or quiet service that often goes unnoticed. A skilled administrator can help a ministry operate effectively. Someone knowledgeable in finance can help ensure that resources are managed faithfully. A gifted teacher can help others understand the truth. Someone with the ability to give can take part in sending and supporting workers. A person who knows how to listen and encourage can be used by God to strengthen a brother or sister who is struggling.

The important question is not, “Can I do what someone else is doing?” but rather,

“What has God placed in my hands, and how can I offer it to Him?”

👉 We do not need to be like another part of the body of Christ in order to have value in His work. What matters is that we are faithful with the part God has entrusted to us.

Not every act of service is seen by many people, but that does not make it unimportant. In a body, many vital parts cannot be seen from the outside, yet the body could not function properly without them. The same is true of God's work. Some people serve in visible roles while others serve behind the scenes, but when each person is faithful with what has been entrusted to them, together they share in the same work.

Nor do we need to wait until we have extraordinary abilities before we can be useful. Paul's call is not, “First, acquire more talents.” His call is to place before God what we already have. The important question is not how many abilities we possess, but whether we are willing to surrender them and use them according to His purpose.

At the same time, serving within the body of Christ is not a substitute for personally doing good. We cannot say, “I give to God's work, so I no longer need to show kindness to the person in front of me.” Nor is it enough simply to do good personally while refusing to take part in the broader work God may be entrusting to us.

These two belong together. Our eyes, mouths, hands, feet, time, strength, money, knowledge, talents, professions, and opportunities can all become instruments of righteousness. They can be used to serve the person beside us today, and they can also be used to support the broader work of God's Kingdom.

So presenting our members to God means more than saying,

“Lord, don't let me do what is wrong.”

We can also say,

“Lord, here is my life. What have You placed in my hands that You can use for what is right, for the good of others, and for the work of Your Kingdom that You desire to advance?”

Doing good and serving are not ways to earn God's acceptance. The person Paul is speaking about is already someone who has been brought from death to life in Christ. Good works are the fruit of that new life, not the price we pay to receive it. We do not present ourselves to persuade God to save us. We present the life He has already saved so that it may be used according to His good purpose.

When we begin to see our lives this way, even ordinary things take on new meaning.

  • Our bodies are not merely for our own comfort.
  • Our abilities are not merely for our own success.
  • Our work is not merely a way to earn money.
  • Our money is not merely for spending on ourselves.
  • Our talents are not merely for gaining recognition.

These are things God has entrusted to us, and we can willingly present them to Him to be used for what is right.

There is another beautiful picture of this transformation. The very things we may once have used in ways that harmed us can now be used for good. Money that may once have been spent on vices, luxuries, or things that damaged our bodies and lives can now be used to help meet the needs of our families, assist those in need, and even support the work of God's Kingdom. A resource that may once have served our bondage can now become an instrument of service.

The same is true of our skills and abilities. A mind once used only for personal ambition can now be used to plan and help a ministry. Financial knowledge can be used for careful and faithful stewardship of resources. Talents in management, technology, writing, music, teaching, communication, or other fields can be offered to support the broader work of God's Kingdom.

The transformation God has brought about is seen not only in the things we no longer do, but also in the new purpose given to things we once used for ourselves or for sin.

The money we once spent on something that harmed us may now be used to support the work of His Kingdom. The time we once spent on meaningless pursuits may now be devoted to serving. The talent we once used only to elevate ourselves may now be offered to help others and bring glory to God.

👉 The question is not only, “What do I need to stop doing?” but also, “What has God entrusted to me that I can now use for what is right and for His work?”

We may not all be the ones who are sent, but we can take part in sending. We may not all be the ones who preach, but we can help the Word reach others. We may not all be leaders, but each of us can use our gifts so that the body of Christ can serve together.

In this way, Paul's call to present “your members as instruments of righteousness to God” becomes deeply practical. A life that was once available to self and sin can now be made available to God. As individuals, we can use our bodies to do what is right each day. And as members of the body of Christ, we can use the gifts and resources entrusted to us to support the work of His Kingdom.

CONCLUSION:

The question is not whether we can do what someone else is doing. The question is:

“What has God placed in my hands, and am I willing to offer it to Him?”

What we have may be a mouth that can encourage, hands that can serve, time we can give, the ability to teach or manage, a talent for music, knowledge of finance, resources we can share, or simply a presence that can help someone in need. Our roles do not have to be the same for each of them to matter.

So today, let us ask God:

“Lord, how do You want to use the life, abilities, and things You have entrusted to me—to do what is right for the people around me and to serve as part of the body of Christ?”

In the end, we are not only people who have been saved from sin. We are also members of the body of Christ and His Kingdom, given life, abilities, and opportunities to serve. Whatever God has placed in our hands, we can willingly present it to Him as an instrument of righteousness and as part of His work.
