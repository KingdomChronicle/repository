Daily Devotional Verses



Romans 6:12-14

NKJV (New King James Version)

Living Under Grace

12 Therefore do not let sin reign in your mortal body, that you should obey it in its lusts. 13 And do not present your members as instruments of unrighteousness to sin, but present yourselves to God as being alive from the dead, and your members as instruments of righteousness to God. 14 For sin shall not have dominion over you, for you are not under law but under grace.

GUIDE REFLECTION:

Do Not Offer Yourself to Sin

In the previous verse, Paul commanded believers not to allow sin to reign in their mortal bodies so that they would obey its desires. Sin may continue to tempt us, but it is no longer the master that those who are united with Christ are obligated to obey. Now Paul makes this command more specific and practical. Refusing to let sin reign is seen in what we do with our very bodies and the abilities God has given us.

“Do not present any part of yourselves to sin as an instrument for doing evil.”

The picture Paul gives is very clear. The parts of our bodies can become “instruments” used for a certain purpose. The word can also describe a tool or a weapon placed into someone's hands to accomplish a particular goal. An instrument becomes useful depending on who uses it and what purpose it serves.

Therefore, the question this verse places before us is not only whether we experience sinful desires, but whether we allow sin to use our bodies to accomplish what it wants.

This is important because sin does not remain only as an inward desire. It seeks expression through our lives.

  • Anger looks for a mouth that will speak harmful words.
  • Envy looks for a mind that will constantly compare itself with others.
  • Lust looks for eyes that will continue looking at what is wrong.
  • Greed looks for hands that will take or accumulate only for self-interest.

When we give our abilities to these desires, the sin within us gains the tools it needs to become outward action.

This is also why Jesus taught that we must not treat lightly the things that become opportunities for sin. He said:

“If your right eye causes you to sin, tear it out and throw it away.” — Matthew 5:29

Jesus was not teaching literal self-harm as a solution to sin. His point was the seriousness and determination required when dealing with anything that leads us into sin. We should not protect an opportunity for sin and then wonder why we continue falling into it. Rejecting sin includes refusing to give it the things it can use against us.

This is especially important in everyday life because we usually do not fall into sin suddenly.

  1. There are often small choices we make first.
  2. We choose to remain in thoughts we know are wrong.
  3. We continue looking at things that feed sinful desires.
  4. We listen to conversations that strengthen anger or envy.
  5. We allow habits that have already led us into sin to remain in our lives.

In this way, even before sin becomes a visible action, we may already be giving it the instruments it needs to work through us.

That is why Paul's command calls for active rejection. It is not enough to say,

“I do not want to sin,”

if we continue placing ourselves under the influence of things that strengthen sin.

There are times when following God means we must avoid, stop, leave, or remove something we know sin is using to tempt us. This is not legalism or an attempt to save ourselves through personal discipline. It is taking seriously an enemy that seeks to use anything we make available to it.

Paul gives the same warning in another part of his letter to the Romans:

“Make no provision for the flesh, to fulfill its lusts.” — Romans 13:14

The principle is clear:

Do not prepare an opportunity for sin to use.

If we know that a certain situation, activity, conversation, image, place, or habit repeatedly weakens us, it is not wisdom to keep returning to it while hoping that our strength will be enough when temptation comes. The battle against sin does not begin only when temptation reaches its strongest point. Often, it begins earlier with the decision not to give sin the instrument it wants to use.

At the same time, we must not fall into the mistaken idea that the body itself is the problem. Our eyes, hands, mouths, minds, strength, and other abilities are not evil. The issue is whose purpose we choose to serve with them.

  • The mouth that can lie is also the mouth that can speak truth.
  • The hands that can do harm can also serve others.
  • The mind that can feed envy and lust can also be focused on things that are true and worthy.

The problem is not having a body; the danger is allowing our bodies to be used by sin.

This brings a personal challenge to every believer. It is easy to talk about sin in general terms, but Paul brings the issue into the specific areas of our lives.

  • What are we doing with our eyes?
  • What are we allowing into our minds through what we listen to?
  • What do we say when we are angry?
  • Where do we allow our feet to take us?
  • What do our hands do when no one is watching?
  • How do we use our time, strength, and abilities?

Faith is not only expressed through what we believe or say; it is also revealed through how we use our bodies.

This command also does not mean that believers will never fail. There may be times when we still give ourselves to sinful actions and later repent. But failure is not a reason to accept that continually being used by sin is normal. Instead, failure should lead us to recognize where we have been careless, seek God's forgiveness, and become more watchful against the opportunities sin uses to tempt us.

Paul teaches us that the fight against sin involves real and practical choices. We do not have to give sin our bodies, time, abilities, and opportunities so that it can accomplish evil. Because our former slavery to sin has been broken through our union with Christ, we can reject its demands and guard the areas of our lives that it seeks to use.

CONCLUSION:

In Romans 6:13, Paul shows that refusing to let sin reign is not merely an inward desire. It is revealed through concrete decisions about our bodies and abilities. We should not allow any part of ourselves to become an instrument that sin uses for evil.

Therefore, we must examine not only the obvious sins we commit but also the opportunities we give to sin.

Are there things we continue to look at, listen to, think about, say, or do that become instruments strengthening sin in our lives?

A serious battle against sin requires that we also take seriously the ways sin attempts to gain influence over us.

When we recognize these areas, we should not justify them or continue playing with them. Instead, we must remember that we no longer have to give ourselves to our former master. Our bodies and abilities do not have to become instruments that sin uses to accomplish its desires.

In the end, sin looks for instruments it can use, but we do not have to surrender ourselves to its power. Because of Christ, we can reject sin's demands and refuse to use our bodies for things that oppose God's will.
