Book 10

Chapter 1. Probation.

But in the morning, after sunrise, I Clement, and Niceta and Aquila, along with Peter, came to the apartment in which my father and mother were sleeping; and finding them still asleep, we sat down before the door, when Peter addressed us in such terms as these: Listen to me, most beloved fellow-servants: I know that you have a great affection for your father; therefore I am afraid that you will urge him too soon to take upon himself the yoke of religion, while he is not yet prepared for it; and to this he may perhaps consent, through his affection for you. But this is not to be depended on; for what is done for the sake of men is not worthy of approbation, and soon falls to pieces. Therefore it seems to me, that you should permit him to live for a year according to his own judgment; and during that time he may travel with us, and while we are instructing others he may hear with simplicity; and as he hears, if he has any right purpose of acknowledging the truth, he will himself request that he may take up the yoke of religion; or if he do not please to take it, he may remain a friend. For those who do not take it up heartily, when they begin not to be able to bear it, not only cast off that which they had taken up, but by way of excuse, as it were, for their weakness, they begin to speak evil of the way of religion, and to malign those whom they have not been able to follow or to imitate.

Chapter 2. A Difficulty.

To this Niceta answered: My lord Peter, I say nothing against your right and good counsels; but I wish to say one thing, that thereby I may learn something that I do not know. What if my father should die within the year during which you recommend that he should be put off? He will go down to hell helpless, and so be tormented forever. Then said Peter: I embrace your kindly purpose towards your father, and I forgive you in respect of things of which you are ignorant. For do you suppose that, if any one is thought to have lived righteously, he shall immediately be saved? Do you not think that he must be examined by Him who knows the secrets of men, as to how he has lived righteously, whether perchance according to the rule of the Gentiles, obeying their institutions and laws; or for the sake of the friendship of men; or merely from custom, or any other cause; or from necessity, and not on account of righteousness itself, and for the sake of God? For those who have lived righteously, for the sake of God alone and His righteousness, they shall come to eternal rest, and shall receive the perpetuity of the heavenly kingdom. For salvation is not attained by force, but by liberty; and not through the favour of men, but by the faith of God. Then, besides, you ought to consider that God is prescient, and knows whether this man is one of His. But if He knows that he is not, what shall we do with respect to those things which have been determined by Him from the beginning? But wherein I can, I give counsel: when he is awake, and we sit down together, then do you, as if you wished to learn something, ask a question about those matters which it is fitting for him to learn; and while we speak to one another, he will gain instruction. But yet wait first to see if he himself ask anything; for if he do so, the occasion of discourse will be the fitter. But if he do not ask anything, let us by turns put questions to one another, wishing to learn something, as I have said. Such is my judgment, state what is yours.

Chapter 3. A Suggestion.

And when we had commended his right counsel, I Clement said: In all things, the end for the most part looks back upon the beginning, and the issue of things is similar to their commencement. I hope, therefore, with respect to our father also, since God by your means has given a good beginning, that He will bestow also an ending suitable to the beginning, and worthy of Himself. However, I make this suggestion, that if, as you have said, we begin to speak, in presence of my father, as if for the purpose of discussing some subject, or learning something from one another, you, my lord Peter, ought not to occupy the place of one who has anything to learn; for if he see this, he will rather be offended. For he is convinced that you fully know all things, as indeed you do. How then will it be, if he see you pretending ignorance? This, as I have said, will rather hurt him, being ignorant of your design. But if we brothers, while we converse among ourselves, are in any doubt, let a fitting solution be given by you to our inquiry. For if he see even you hesitating and doubting, then truly he will think that no one has knowledge of the truth.

Chapter 4. Free Inquiry.

To this Peter answered: Let us not concern ourselves about this; and if indeed it is fitting that he enter the gate of life, God will afford a fitting opportunity; and there shall be a beginning from God, and not from man. And therefore, as I have said, let him journey with us, and hear our discussions; but because I saw you in haste, therefore I said that opportunity must be sought; and when God shall give it, do you comply with my advice in what I shall say. While we were thus talking, a boy came to tell us that our father was now awake; and when we were intending to go in to him, he himself came to us, and saluting us with a kiss, after we had sat down again, he said: Is it permitted to one to ask a question, if he wishes it; or is silence enforced, after the manner of the Pythagoreans? Then said Peter: We do not compel those who come to us either to keep silence continually, or to ask questions; but we leave them free to do as they will knowing that he who is anxious about his salvation, if he feels pain in any part of his soul, does not suffer it to be silent. But he who neglects his salvation, no advantage is conferred upon him if he is compelled to ask, excepting this only, that he may seem to be earnest and diligent. Wherefore, if you wish to get any information, ask on.

Chapter 5. Good and Evil.

Then the old man said: There is a saying very prevalent among the Greek philosophers, to the effect that there is in reality neither good nor evil in the life of man; but that men call things good or evil as they appear to them, prejudiced by the use and custom of life. For not even murder is really an evil, because it sets the soul free from the bonds of the flesh. Further, they say that even just judges put to death those who commit crimes; but if they knew homicide to be an evil, just men would not do that. Neither do they say that adultery is an evil; for if the husband does not know, or does not care, there is, they say, no evil in it. But neither, say they is theft an evil; for it takes away what one does not possess from another who has it. And, indeed, it ought to be taken freely and openly; but in that it is done secretly, that is rather a reproof of his inhumanity from whom it is secretly taken. For all men ought to have the common use of all things that are in this world; but through injustice one says that this is his, and another that that is his, and so division is caused among men. In short, a certain man, the wisest among the Greeks, knowing that these things are so, says that friends should have all things common. Now, in all things unquestionably wives are included. He says also that, as the air and the sunshine cannot be divided, so neither ought other things to be divided, which are given in this world to all to be possessed in common, but should be so possessed. But I wished to say this, because I am desirous to turn to well-doing, and I cannot act well unless I first learn what is good; and if I can understand that, I shall thereby perceive what is evil, that is, opposite to good.

Chapter 6. Peter's Authority.

But I should like that one of you, and not Peter, should answer what I have said; for it is not fitting to take words and instruction at his hand, with questions; but when he gives a deliverance on any subject, that should be held without answering again. And therefore let us keep him as an umpire; so that if at any time our discussion does not come to an issue, he may declare what seems good to him, and so give an undoubted end to doubtful matters. And now therefore I could believe, content with his sole opinion, if he expressed any opinion; and this is what I shall do at last. Yet I wish first to see if it is possible by discussion to find what is sought. My wish therefore is, that Clement should begin first, and should show if there is any good or evil in substance or in actions.

Chapter 7. Clement's Argument.

To this I answered: Since indeed you wish to learn from me if there is any good or evil in nature or in act, or whether it is not rather that men, prejudiced by custom, think some things to be good, and others to be evil, forasmuch as they have made a division among themselves of common things, which ought, as you say, to be as common as the air and the sunshine; I think that I ought not to bring before you any statements from any other quarter than from those studies in which you are well versed, and which you support, so that what I say you will receive without hesitation. You assign certain boundaries of all the elements and the heavenly bodies, and these, you say, meet in some without hurt, as in marriages; but in others they are hurtfully united, as in adulteries. And you say that some things are general to all, but other things do not belong to all, and are not general. But not to make a long discussion, I shall speak briefly of the matter. The earth which is dry is in need of the addition and admixture of water, that it may be able to produce fruits, without which man cannot live: this is therefore a legitimate conjunction. On the contrary if the cold of hoar-frost be mixed with the earth, or heat with the water, a conjunction of this sort produces corruption; and this, in such things, is adultery.

Chapter 8. Admitted Evils.

Then my father answered: But as the harmfulness of an inharmonious conjunction of elements or stars is immediately betrayed, so ought also adultery to be immediately shown that it is an evil. Then I: First tell me this, whether, as you yourself have confessed, evils are produced from incongruous and inharmonious mixture; and then after that we shall inquire into the other matter. Then my father said: The nature of things is as you say, my son. Then I answered: Since, then, you wish to learn of these things, see how many things there are which no one doubts to be evils. Do you think that a fever, a fire, sedition, the fall of a house, murder, holds, racks, pains, mournings, and such like, are evils? Then said my father: It is true, my son, that these things are evil, and very evil; or, at all events, whoever denies that they are evil, let him suffer them!

Chapter 9. Existence of Evil on Astrological Principles.

Then I answered: Since, therefore, I have to deal with one who is skilled in astrological science, I shall treat the matter with you according to that science, that, taking my method from those things with which you are familiar, you may the more readily acquiesce. Listen now, therefore: you confess that those things which we have mentioned are evils, such as fevers, conflagrations, and such like. Now these, according to you, are said to be produced by malignant stars, such as the humid Saturn and the hot Mars; but things contrary to these are produced by benignant stars, such as the temperate Jupiter and the humid Venus. Is it not so? My father answered: It is so, my son; and it cannot be otherwise. Then said I: Since you say, therefore, that good things are produced by good stars — by Jupiter and Venus, for example — let us see what is the product where any one of the evil stars is mixed with the good, and let us understand that that is evil. For you lay it down that Venus makes marriages, and if she have Jupiter in her configuration she makes the marriages chaste; but if Jupiter be not regarding, and Mars be present, then you pronounce that the marriages are corrupted by adultery. Then said my father: It is even so. Then I answered: Therefore adultery is an evil, seeing that it is committed through the admixture of evil stars; and, to state it in a word, all things that you say that the good stars suffer from the mixture of evil stars, are undoubtedly to be pronounced to be evil. Those stars, therefore, by whose admixture we have said that fevers, configurations, and other such like evils are produced — those, according to you, work also murders, adulteries, thefts, and also produce haughty and stolid men.

Chapter 10. How to Make Progress.

Then my father said: Truly you have shown briefly and incomparably that there are evils in actions; but still I should wish to learn this, how God justly judges those who sin, as you say, if Genesis compels them to sin? Then I answered: I am afraid to speak anything to you, my father, because it becomes me to hold you in all honour, else I have an answer to give you, if it were becoming. Then says my father: Speak what occurs to you, my son; for it is not you, but the method of inquiry, that does the wrong, as a modest woman to an incontinent man, if she is indignant for her safety and her honour. Then I answered: If we do not hold by the principles that we have acknowledged and confessed, but if those things which have been defined are always loosened by forgetfulness, we shall seem to be weaving Penelope's web, undoing what we have done. And therefore we ought either not to acquiesce too easily, before we have diligently examined the doctrine propounded; or if we have once acquiesced, and the proposition has been agreed to, then we ought to keep by what has been once determined, that we may go on with our inquiries respecting other matters. And my father said: You say well, my son; and I know why you say this: it is because in the discussion yesterday on natural causes, you showed that some malignant power, transferring itself into the order of the stars, excites the lusts of men, provoking them in various ways to sin, yet not compelling or producing sins. To this I answered: It is well that you remember it; and yet, though you do remember it, you have fallen into error. Then said my father: Pardon me, my son; for I have not yet much practice in these things: for indeed your discourses yesterday, by their truth, shut me up to agree with you; yet in my consciousness there are, as it were, some remains of fevers, which for a little hold me back from faith, as from health. For I am distracted, because I know that many things, yea, almost all things, have befallen me according to Genesis .

Chapter 11. Test of Astrology.

Then I answered: I shall therefore tell you, my father, what is the nature of mathematics, and do you act according to what I tell you. Go to a mathematician, and tell him first that such and such evils have befallen you at such a time, and that you wish to learn of him whence, or how, or through what stars they have befallen you. He will no doubt answer you that a malignant Mars or Saturn has ruled your times, or that some one of them has been periodic; or that some one has regarded you diametrically, or in conjunction, or centrally; or some such answer will he give, adding that in all these some one was not in harmony with the malignant one, or was invisible, or was in the figure, or was beyond the division, or was eclipsed, or was not in contact, or was among the dark stars; and many other like things will he answer, according to his own reasons, and will condescend upon particulars. After him go to another mathematician, and tell him the opposite, that such and such good happened to you at that time, mentioning to him the same time, and ask him from what parts of your Genesis this good has come to you, and take care, as I said, that the times are the same with those about which you asked concerning evils. And when you have deceived him concerning the times, see what figures he will invent for you, by which to show that good things ought to have befallen you at those very times. For it is impossible for those treating of the Genesis of men not to find in every quarter, as they call it, of the heavenly bodies, some stars favourably placed, and some unfavourably; for the circle is equally complete in every part, according to mathematics, admitting of diverse and various causes, from which they can take occasion of saying whatever they please.

Chapter 12. Astrology Baffled by Free-Will.

For, as usually happens when men see unfavourable dreams, and can make nothing certain out of them, when any event occurs, then they adapt what they saw in the dream to what has occurred; so also is mathematics. For before anything happens, nothing is declared with certainty; but after something has happened, they gather the causes of the event. And thus often, when they have been at fault, and the thing has fallen out otherwise, they take the blame to themselves, saying that it was such and such a star which opposed, and that they did not see it; not knowing that their error does not proceed from their unskilfulness in their art, but from the inconsistency of the whole system. For they do not know what those things are which we indeed desire to do, but in regard to which we do not indulge our desires. But we who have learned the reason of this mystery know the cause, since, having freedom of will, we sometimes oppose our desires, and sometimes yield to them. And therefore the issue of human doings is uncertain, because it depends upon freedom of will. For a mathematician can indeed indicate the desire which a malignant power produces; but whether the acting or the issue of this desire shall be fulfilled or not, no one can know before the accomplishment of the thing, because it depends upon freedom of will. And this is why ignorant astrologers have invented to themselves the talk about climacterics as their refuge in uncertainties, as we showed fully yesterday.

Chapter 13. People Admitted.

If you have anything that you wish to say to this, say on. Then my father: Nothing can be more true, my son, than what you have stated. And while we were thus speaking among ourselves, some one informed us that a great multitude of people were standing outside, having assembled for the purpose of hearing. Then Peter ordered them to be admitted, for the place was large and convenient. And when they had come in, Peter said to us: If any one of you wishes, let him address the people, and discourse concerning idolatry. To whom I Clement answered: Your great benignity and gentleness and patience towards all encourages us, so that we dare speak in your presence, and ask what we please; and therefore, as I said, the gentleness of your disposition invites and encourages all to undertake the precepts of saving doctrine. This I never saw before in any one else, but in you only, with whom there is neither envy nor indignation. Or what do you think?

Chapter 14. No Man Has Universal Knowledge.

Then Peter said: These things come not only from envy or indignation; but sometimes there is a bashfulness in some persons, lest haply they may not be able to answer fully the questions that may be proposed, and so they avoid the discovery of their want of skill. But no one ought to be ashamed of this, because there is no man who ought to profess that he knows all things; for there is only One who knows all things, even He who also made all things. For if our Master declared that He knew not the day and the hour whose signs even He foretold, and referred the whole to the Father, how shall we account it disgraceful to confess that we are ignorant of some things, since in this we have the example of our Master? But this only we profess, that we know those things which we have learned from the true Prophet; and that those things have been delivered to us by the true Prophet, which He judged to be sufficient for human knowledge.

Chapter 15. Clement's Disclosure.

Then I Clement went on to speak thus: At Tripolis, when you were disputing against the Gentiles, my lord Peter, I greatly wondered at you, that although you were instructed by your father according to the fashion of the Hebrews and in observances of your own law, and were never polluted by the studies of Greek learning, you argued so magnificently and so incomparably; and that you even touched upon some things concerning the histories of the gods, which are usually declaimed in the theatres. But as I perceived that their fables and blasphemies are not so well known to you, I shall discourse upon these in your hearing, repeating them from the very beginning, if it please you. Then says Peter: Say on; you do well to assist my preaching. Then said I: I shall speak, therefore, because you order me, not by way of teaching you, but of making public what foolish opinions the Gentiles entertain of the gods.

Chapter 16. Would that All God's People Were Prophets.

But when I was about to speak, Niceta, biting his lip, beckoned to me to be silent. And when Peter saw him, he said: Why would you repress his liberal disposition and noble nature, that you would have him be silent for my honour, which is nothing? Or do you not know, that if all nations, after they have heard from me the preaching of the truth, and have believed, would betake themselves to teaching, they would gain the greater glory for me, if indeed you think me desirous of glory? For what so glorious as to prepare disciples for Christ, not who shall be silent, and shall be saved alone, but who shall speak what they have learned, and shall do good to others? I wish indeed that both you, Niceta, and you, beloved Aquila, would aid me in preaching the word of God, and the rather because those things in which the Gentiles err are well known to you; and not you only, but all who hear me, I wish, as I have said, so to hear and to learn, that they may be able also to teach: for the world needs many helpers, by whom men may be recalled from error. When he had spoken thus, he said to me: Go on then, Clement, with what you have begun.

Chapter 17. Gentile Cosmogony.

And I immediately rejoined: Seeing that when you were disputing at Tripolis, as I said, you discoursed much concerning the gods of the Gentiles profitably and convincingly, I desire to set forth in your presence the ridiculous legends concerning their origin, both that you may not be unacquainted with the falsehood of this vain superstition, and that the hearers who are present may know the disgraceful character of their error. The wise men, then, who are among the Gentiles, say that first of all things was chaos; that this, through a long time solidifying its outer parts, made bounds to itself and a sort of foundation, being gathered, as it were, into the manner and form of a huge egg, within which, in the course of a long time, as within the shell of the egg, there was cherished and vivified a certain animal; and that afterwards, that huge globe being broken, there came forth a certain kind of man of double sex, which they call masculo-feminine. This they called Phanetas, from appearing, because when it appeared, they say, then also light shone forth. And from this, they say that there were produced substance, prudence, motion, and coition, and from these the heavens and the earth were made. From the heaven they say that six males were produced, whom they call Titans; and in like manner, from the earth six females, whom they called Titanides. And these are the names of the males who sprang from the heaven: Oceanus, Cœus, Crios, Hyperion, Iapetus, Chronos, who among us is called Saturn. In like manner, the names of the females who sprang from the earth are these: Theia, Rhea, Themis, Mnemosyne, Tethys, Hebe.

Chapter 18. Family of Saturn.

Of all these, the first-born of the heaven took to wife the first-born of earth; the second the second, and in like manner all the rest. The first male, therefore, who had married the first female, was on her account drawn downwards; but the second female rose upwards, by reason of him to whom she was married; and so each doing in their order, remained in those places which fell to their share by the nuptial lot. From their intercourse they assert that innumerable others sprang. But of these six males, the one who is called Saturn received in marriage Rhea, and having been warned by a certain oracle that he who should be born of her should be more powerful than himself, and should drive him from his kingdom, he determined to devour all the sons that should be born to him. First, then, there is born to him a son called Aides, who among us is called Orcus; and him, for the reason we have just stated, he took and devoured. After him he begot a second son, called Neptune; and him he devoured in like manner. Last of all, he begot him whom they call Jupiter; but him his mother Rhea pitying, by stratagem withdrew from his father when he was about to devour him. And first, indeed, that the crying of the child might not be noticed, she made certain Corybantes strike cymbals and drums, that by the deafening sound the crying of the infant might not be heard.

Chapter 19. Their Destinies.

But when he understood from the lessening of her belly that her child was born, he demanded it, that he might devour it; then Rhea presented him with a large stone, and told him that that was what she had brought forth. And he took it, and swallowed it; and the stone, when it was devoured, pushed and drove forth those sons whom he had formerly swallowed. Therefore Orcus, coming forth first, descended, and occupies the lower, that is, the infernal regions. The second, being above him — he whom they call Neptune — is thrust forth upon the waters. The third, who survived by the artifice of his mother Rhea, she put upon a she-goat and sent into heaven.

Chapter 20. Doings of Jupiter.

But enough of the old wife's fables and genealogy of the Gentiles; for it were endless if I should set forth all the generations of those whom they call gods, and their wicked doings. But by way of example, omitting the rest, I shall detail the wicked deeds of him only whom they hold to be the greatest and the chief, and whom they call Jupiter. For they say that he possesses heaven, as being superior to the rest; and he, as soon as he grew up, married his own sister, whom they call Juno, in which truly he at once becomes like a beast. Juno bears Vulcan; but, as they relate, Jupiter was not his father. However, by Jupiter himself she became mother of Medea; and Jupiter having received a response that one who should be born of her should be more powerful than himself, and should expel him from his kingdom, took her and devoured her. Again Jupiter produced Minerva from his brain, and Bacchus from his thigh. After this, when he had fallen in love with Thetis, they say that Prometheus informed him that, if he lay with her, he who should be born of her should be more powerful than his father; and for fear of this, he gave her in marriage to one Peleus. Subsequently he had intercourse with Persephone, who was his own daughter by Ceres and by her he begot Dionysius, who was torn in pieces by the Titans. But calling to mind, it is said, that perhaps his own father Saturn might beget another son, who might be more powerful than himself, and might expel him from the kingdom, he went to war with his father, along with his brothers the Titans; and having beaten them, he at last threw his father into prison, and cut off his genitals, and threw them into the sea. But the blood which flowed from the wound, being mixed with the waves, and turned into foam by the constant churning, produced her whom they call Aphrodite, and whom with us they call Venus. From his intercourse with her who was thus his own sister, they say that this same Jupiter begot Cypris, who, they say, was the mother of Cupid.

Chapter 21. A Black Catalogue.

Thus much of his incests; I shall now speak of his adulteries. He defiled Europa, the wife of Oceanus, of whom was born Dodonæus; Helen, the wife of Pandion, of whom Musæus; Eurynome, the wife of Asopus, of whom Ogygias; Hermione, the wife of Oceanus, of whom the Graces, Thalia, Euphrosyne, Aglaia; Themis, his own sister, of whom the Hours, Eurynomia, Dice, Irene; Themisto, the daughter of Inachus, of whom Arcas; Idæa, the daughter of Minos, of whom Asterion; Phœnissa, the daughter of Alphion, of whom Endymion; Io, the daughter of Inachus, of whom Epaphus; Hippodamia and Isione, daughters of Danaus, of whom Hippodamia was the wife of Olenus, and Isione of Orchomenus or Chryses; Carme, the daughter of Phœnix, of whom was born Britomartis, who was an attendant of Diana; Callisto, the daughter of Lycaon, of whom Orcas; Lybee, the daughter of Munantius, of whom Belus; Latona, of whom Apollo and Diana; Leandia, the daughter of Eurymedon, of whom Coron; Lysithea, the daughter of Evenus, of whom Helenus; Hippodamia, the daughter of Bellerophon, of whom Sarpedon; Megaclite, the daughter of Macarius, of whom Thebe and Locrus; Niobe, the daughter of Phoroneus, of whom Argus and Pelasgus; Olympias, the daughter of Neoptolemus, of whom Alexander; Pyrrha, the daughter of Prometheus, of whom Helmetheus; Protogenia and Pandora, daughters of Deucalion, of whom he begot Æthelius, and Dorus, and Melera, and Pandorus; Thaicrucia, the daughter of Proteus, of whom was born Nympheus; Salamis, the daughter of Asopus, of whom Saracon; Taygete, Electra, Maia, Plutide, daughters of Atlas, of whom respectively he begot Lacedæmon, Dardanus, Mercury, and Tantalus; Phthia, the daughter of Phoroneus, of whom he begot Achæus; Chonia, the daughter of Aramnus, of whom he begot Lacon; Chalcea, a nymph, of whom was born Olympus; Charidia, a nymph, of whom Alcanus; Chloris, who was the wife of Ampycus, of whom Mopsus was born; Cotonia, the daughter of Lesbus, of whom Polymedes; Hippodamia, the daughter of Anicetus; Chrysogenia, the daughter of Peneus, of whom was born Thissæus.

Chapter 22. Vile Transformation of Jupiter.

There are also innumerable adulteries of his, of which no offspring was the result, which it were tedious to enumerate. But among those whom we have mentioned, he violated some being transformed, like a magician. In short, he seduced Antiope, the daughter of Nycteus, when turned into a satyr, and of her were born Amphion and Zethus; Alcmene, when changed into her husband Amphitryon, and of her was born Hercules; Ægina, the daughter of Asopus, when changed into an eagle, of whom Æacus was born. So also he defiled Ganymede, the son of Dardanus, being changed into an eagle; Manthea, the daughter of Phocus, when changed into a bear, of whom was born Arctos; Danæ, the daughter of Acrisius, being changed into gold, of whom Perseus; Europa, the daughter of Phœnix, changed into a bull, of whom were born Minos, Rhadamanthus, and Sarpedon; Eurymedusa, the daughter of Achelaus, being changed into an ant, of whom Myrmidon; Thalia, the nymph, being changed into a vulture, of whom were born the Palisci, in Sicily; Imandra, the daughter of Geneanus, at Rhodes, being changed into a shower; Cassiopeia, being changed into her husband Phœnix, and of her was born Anchinos; Leda, the daughter of Thestius, being changed into a swan, of whom was born Helen; and again the same, being changed into a star, and of her were born Castor and Pollux; Lamia, being changed into a lapwing; Mnemosyne, being changed into a shepherd, of whom were born the nine Muses; Nemesis, being changed into a goose; the Cadmian Semele, being changed into fire, and of her was born Dionysius. By his own daughter Ceres he begot Persephone, whom also herself he defiled, being changed into a dragon.

Chapter 23. Why a God?

He also committed adultery with Europa, the wife of his own uncle Oceanus, and with her sister Eurynome, and punished their father; and he committed adultery with Plute, the daughter of his own son Atlas, and condemned Tantalus, whom she bore to him. Of Larisse, the daughter of Orchomenus, he begot Tityon, whom also he consigned to punishment. He carried off Dia, the wife of his own son Ixion, and subjected him to perpetual punishment; and almost all the sons who sprang from his adulteries he put to violent deaths; and indeed the sepulchres of almost all of them are well known. Yea, the sepulchre of this parricide himself, who destroyed his uncles and defiled their wives, who committed whoredom with his sisters, this magician of many transformations, is shown among the Cretans, who, although they know and acknowledge his horrid and incestuous deeds, and tell them to all, yet are not ashamed to confess him to be a god. Whence it seems to me to be wonderful, yea, exceeding wonderful, how he who exceeds all men in wickedness and crimes, has received that holy and good name which is above every name, being called the father of gods and men; unless perhaps he who rejoices in the evils of men has persuaded unhappy souls to confer honour above all others upon him whom he saw to excel all others in crimes, in order that he might allure all to the imitation of his evil deeds.

Chapter 24. Folly of Polytheism.

But also the sepulchres of his sons, who are regarded among these the Gentiles as gods, are openly pointed out, one in one place, and another in another: that of Mercury at Hermopolis; that of the Cyprian Venus at Cyprus; that of Mars in Thrace; that of Bacchus at Thebes, where he is said to have been torn in pieces; that of Hercules at Tyre, where he was burnt with fire; that of Æsculapius in Epidaurus. And all these are spoken of, not only as men who have died, but as wicked men who have been punished for their crimes; and yet they are adored as gods by foolish men.

Chapter 25. Dead Men Deified.

But if they choose to argue, and affirm that these are rather the places of their birth than of their burial or death, the former and ancient doings shall be convicted from those at hand and still recent, since we have shown that they worship those whom they themselves confess to have been men, and to have died, or rather to have been punished; as the Syrians worship Adonis, and the Egyptians Osiris; the Trojans, Hector; Achilles is worshipped at Leuconesus, Patroclus at Pontus, Alexander the Macedonian at Rhodes; and many others are worshipped, one in one place and another in another, whom they do not doubt to have been dead men. Whence it follows that their predecessors also, falling into a like error, conferred divine honour upon dead men, who perhaps had had some power or some skill, and especially if they had stupefied stolid men by magical phantasies.

Chapter 26. Metamorphoses.

Hence there has now been added, that the poets also adorn the falsehoods of error by elegance of words, and by sweetness of speech persuade that mortals have been made immortal; yea more, they say that men are changed into stars, and trees, and animals, and flowers, and birds, and fountains, and rivers. And but that it might seem to be a waste of words, I could even enumerate almost all the stars, and trees, and fountains, and rivers, which they assert to have been made of men; yet, by way of example, I shall mention at least one of each class. They say that Andromeda, the daughter of Cepheus, was turned into a star; Daphne, the daughter of the river Lado, into a tree; Hyacinthus, beloved of Apollo, into a flower; Callisto into the constellation which they call Arctos; Progne and Philomela, with Tereus, into birds; that Thysbe in Cilicia was dissolved into a fountain; and Pyramus, at the same place, into a river. And they assert that almost all the stars, trees, fountains, and rivers, flowers, animals, and birds, were at one time human beings.

Chapter 27. Inconsistency of Polytheists.

But Peter, when he heard this, said: According to them, then, before men were changed into stars, and the other things which you mention, the heaven was without stars, and the earth without trees and animals; and there were neither fountains, nor rivers, nor birds. And without these, how did those men themselves live, who afterwards were changed into them, since it is evident that, without these things, men could not live upon the earth? Then I answered: But they are not even able to observe the worship of their own gods consistently; for every one of those whom they worship has something dedicated to himself, from which his worshippers ought to abstain: as they say the olive is dedicated to Minerva, the she-goat to Jupiter, seeds to Ceres, wine to Bacchus, water to Osiris, the ram to Hammon, the stag to Diana, the fish and the dove to the demon of the Syrians, fire to Vulcan; and to each one, as I have said, is there something specially consecrated, from which the worshippers are bound to abstain, for the honour of those to whom they are consecrated. But were one abstaining from one thing, and another from another, by doing honor to one of the gods, they incur the anger of all the rest; and therefore, if they would conciliate them all, they must abstain from all things for the honour of all, so that, being self-condemned by a just sentence before the day of judgment, they should perish by a most wretched death through starvation.

Chapter 28. Buttresses of Gentilism.

But let us return to our purpose. What reason is there, yea, rather, what madness possesses the minds of men, that they worship and adore as a god, a man whom they not only know to be impious, wicked, profane — I mean Jupiter — incestuous, a parricide, an adulterer, but even proclaim him publicly as such in their songs in the theatres? Or if by means of these deeds he has deserved to be a god, then also, when they hear of any murderers, adulterers, parricides, incestuous persons, they ought to worship them also as gods. But I cannot understand why they venerate in him what they execrate in others. Then Peter answered: Since you say that you cannot understand it, learn of me why they venerate wickedness in him. In the first place, it is that, when they themselves do like deeds, they may know that they shall be acceptable to him, inasmuch as they have but imitated him in his wickedness. In the second place, because the ancients have left these things skilfully composed in their writings, and elegantly engrafted in their verses. And now, by the aid of youthful education, since the knowledge of these things adheres to their tender and simple minds, it cannot without difficulty be torn from them and cast away.

Chapter 29. Allegories.

When Peter had said this, Niceta answered: Do not suppose, my lord Peter, but that the learned men of the Gentiles have certain plausible arguments, by which they support those things which seem to be blameworthy and disgraceful. And this I state, not as wishing to confirm their error (for far be it from me that such a thing should ever come into my thought); but yet I know that there are among the more intelligent of them certain defences, by which they are accustomed to support and color over those things which seem to be absurd. And if it please you that I should state some of them — for I am to some extent acquainted with them — I shall do as you order me. And when Peter had given him leave, Niceta proceeded as follows.

Chapter 30. Cosmogony of Orpheus.

All the literature among the Greeks which is written on the subject of the origin of antiquity, is based upon many authorities, but especially two, Orpheus and Hesiod. Now their writings are divided into two parts, in respect of their meaning — that is the literal and the allegorical; and the vulgar crowd has flocked to the literal, but all the eloquence of the philosophers and learned men is expended in admiration of the allegorical. It is Orpheus, then, who says that at first there was chaos, eternal, unbounded, unproduced, and that from it all things were made. He says that this chaos was neither darkness nor light, neither moist nor dry, neither hot nor cold, but that it was all things mixed together, and was always one unformed mass; yet that at length, as it were after the manner of a huge egg, it brought forth and produced from itself a certain double form, which had been wrought through immense periods of time, and which they call masculo-feminine, a form concrete from the contrary admixture of such diversity; and that this is the principle of all things, which came of pure matter, and which, coming forth, effected a separation of the four elements, and made heaven of the two elements which are first, fire and air, and earth of the others, earth and water; and of these he says that all things now are born and produced by a mutual participation of them. So far Orpheus.

Chapter 31. Hesiod's Cosmogony.

But to this Hesiod adds, that after chaos the heaven and the earth were made immediately, from which he says that those eleven were produced (and sometimes also he speaks of them as twelve) of whom he makes six males and five females. And these are the names that he gives to the males: Oceanus, Cœus, Crius, Hyperion, Iapetus, Chronos, who is also called Saturn. Also the names of the females are: Theia, Rhea, Themis, Mnemosyne, Tethys. And these names they thus interpret allegorically. They say that the number is eleven or twelve: that the first is nature itself, which also they would have to be called Rhea, from Flowing; and they say that the other ten are her accidents, which also they call qualities; yet they add a twelfth, namely Chronos, who with us is called Saturn, and him they take to be time. Therefore they assert that Saturn and Rhea are time and matter; and these, when they are mixed with moisture and dryness, heat and cold, produce all things.

Chapter 32. Allegorical Interpretation.

She therefore (Rhea, or nature), it is said, produced, as it were, a certain bubble which had been collecting for a long time; and it being gradually collected from the spirit which was in the waters, swelled, and being for some time driven over the surface of matter, from which it had come forth as from a womb, and being hardened by the rigour of cold, and always increasing by additions of ice, at length was broken off and sunk into the deep, and drawn by its own weight, went down to the infernal regions; and because it became invisible it was called Aides, and is also named Orcus or Pluto. And since it was sunk from the top to the bottom, it gave place to the moist element to flow together; and the grosser part, which is the earth, was laid bare by the retirement of the waters. They say, therefore, that this freedom of the waters, which was formerly restrained by the presence of the bubble, was called Neptune after the bubble attained the lowest place. After this, when the cold element had been sucked down to the lower regions by the concretion of the icy bubble, and the dry and the moist element had been separated, there being now no hindrance, the warm element rushed by its force and lightness to the upper regions of the air, being borne up by wind and storm. This storm, therefore, which in Greek is called καταιγίς, they called ægis — that is, a she-goat; and the fire which ascended to the upper regions they called Jupiter; wherefore they say that he ascended to Olympus riding on a she-goat.

Chapter 33. Allegory of Jupiter, Etc.

Now this Jupiter the Greeks would have to be called from his living, or giving life, but our people from his giving succour. They say, therefore, that this is the living substance, which, placed in the upper regions, and drawing all things to itself by the influence of heat, as by the convolution of the brain, and arranging them by the moderation of a certain tempering, is said from his head to have produced wisdom, whom they call Minerva, who was called ᾿Αθήνη by the Greeks on account of her immortality; who, because the father of all created all things by his wisdom, is also said to have been produced from his head, and from the principal place of all, and is represented as having formed and adorned the whole world by the regulated admixture of the elements. Therefore the forms which were impressed upon matter, that the world might be made, because they are constrained by the force of heat, are said to be held together by the energy of Jupiter. And since there are enough of these, and they do not need anything new to be added to them, but each thing is repaired by the produce of its own seed, the hands of Saturn are said to be bound by Jupiter; because, as I have said, time now produces from matter nothing new: but the warmth of seeds restores all things according to their kinds; and no birth of Rhea — that is, no increase of flowing matter — ascends further. And therefore they call that first division of the elements the mutilation of Saturn, because he cannot any more produce a world.

Chapter 34. Other Allegories.

And of Venus they give forth an allegory to this effect. When, say they, the sea was put under the air, and when the brightness of the heavens shone more pleasantly, being reflected from the waters, the loveliness of things, which appeared fairer from the waters, was called Venus; and she, it, being united with the air as with her, its, own brother, so as to produce beauty, which might be the object of desire, is said to have given birth to Cupid. In this way, therefore, as we have said, they teach that Chronos, who is Saturn, is allegorically time; Rhea is matter; Aides — that is, Orcus — is the depth of the infernal regions; Neptune is water; Jupiter is air — that is, the element of heat; Venus is the loveliness of things; Cupid is desire, which is in all things, and by which posterity is propagated, or even the reason of things, which gives delight when wisely looked into. Hera — that is, Juno — is said to be that middle air which descends from heaven to earth. To Diana, whom they call Proserpine, they hand over the air below. They say that Apollo is the Sun himself, which goes round the heaven; that Mercury is speech, by which a reason is rendered for everything; that Mars is unrestrained fire, which consumes all things. But not to delay you by enumerating everything, those who have the more abstruse intelligence concerning such things think that they give fair and just reasons, by applying this sort of allegory to every one of their objects of worship.

Chapter 35. Uselessness of These Allegories.

When Niceta had thus spoken, Aquila answered: Whoever he was that was the author and inventor of these things, he seems to me to have been very impious, since he covered over those things which seem to be pleasant and seemly, and made the ritual of his superstition to consist in base and shameful observances, since those things which are written according to the letter are manifestly unseemly and base; and the whole observance of their religion consists in these, that by such crimes and impieties they may teach men to imitate their gods whom they worship. For in these allegories what profit can there be to them? For although they are framed so as to be decent, yet no use is derived from them for worship, nor for amendment of morals.

Chapter 36. The Allegories an Afterthought.

Whence it is the more evident that prudent men, when they saw that the common superstition was so disgraceful, so base, and yet they had not learned any way of correcting it, or any knowledge, endeavoured with what arguments and interpretations they could to veil unseemly things under seemly speech, and not, as they say, to conceal seemly reasons under unseemly fables. For if this were the case, surely their statues and their pictures would never be made with representations of their vices and crimes. The swan, which committed adultery with Leda, would not be represented, nor the bull which committed adultery with Europa; nor would they turn into a thousand monstrous shapes, him whom they think better than all. And assuredly, if the great and wise men who are among them knew that all this is fiction and not truth, would not they charge with impiety and sacrilege those who should exhibit a picture or carve an image of this sort, to the injury of the gods? In short, let them present a king of their own time in the form of an ox, or a goose, or an ant, or a vulture, and let them write the name of their king upon it, and set up such a statue or figure in a public place, and they will soon be made to feel the wrong of their deed, and the greatness of its punishment.

Chapter 37. Like Gods, Like Worshippers.

But since those things rather are true which the public baseness testifies, and concealments have been sought and fabricated by prudent men to excuse them by seemly speeches, therefore are they not only not prohibited, but even in the very mysteries figures are produced of Saturn devouring his sons, and of the boy hidden by the cymbals and drums of the Corybantes; and with respect to the mutilation of Saturn, what better proof of its truth could there be, than that even his worshippers are mutilated, by a like miserable fate, in honour of their god? Since then these things are manifestly seen, who shall be found of so little sense, yea, of such stolidity, that he does not perceive that those things are true concerning the unfortunate gods, which their more unfortunate worshippers attest by the wounding and mutilation of their bodies?

Chapter 38. Writings of the Poets.

But if, as they say, these things, so creditably and piously done, are dispensed by so discreditable and impious a ritual, assuredly he is sacrilegious, whoever either gave forth these things at first, or persists in fulfilling them, now that they have unhappily been given forth. And what shall we say of the books of the poets? Ought not they, if they have debased the honourable and pious deeds of the gods with base fables, to be immediately cast away and thrown into the fire, that they may not persuade the still tender age of boys that Jupiter himself, the chief of the gods, was a parricide towards his parents, incestuous towards his sisters and his daughters, and even impure towards boys; that Venus and Mars were adulterers, and all those things which have been spoken of above? What do you think of this matter, my lord Peter?

Chapter 39. All for the Best.

Then he answered: Be sure, beloved Aquila, that all things are done by the good providence of God, that the cause which was to be contrary to the truth should not only be infirm and weak, but also base. For if the assertion of error had been stronger and more truth-like, any one who had been deceived by it would not easily return to the path of truth. If even now, when so many wicked and disgraceful things are related concerning the gods of the Gentiles, scarce any one forsakes the base error, how much more if there had been in it anything seemly and truth-like? For the mind is with difficulty transferred from those things with which it has been imbued in early youth; and on this account, as I said, it has been effected by divine providence, that the substance of error should be both weak and base. But all other things also divine providence dispenses fitly and advantageously, although the method of the divine dispensation, as good, and the best possible, is not clear to us who are ignorant of the causes of things.

Chapter 40. Further Information Sought.

When Peter had thus said, I Clement asked Niceta that he would explain to us, for the sake of instruction, some things concerning the allegories of the Gentiles, which he had carefully studied; for, said I, it is useful that when we dispute with the Gentiles, we should not be unacquainted with these things. Then said Niceta: If my lord Peter permits me, I can do as you ask me. Then said Peter: Today I have given you leave to speak in opposition to the Gentiles, as you know. And Niceta said: Tell me then, Clement, what you would have me speak about. And I said to him: Inform us how the Gentiles represent matters concerning the supper of the gods, which they had at the marriage of Peleus and Thetis. What do they make of the shepherd Paris, and what of less Juno, Minerva, and Venus, between whom he acted as judge? What of Mercury? And what of the apple, and the other things which follow in order?

Chapter 41. Explanation of Mythology.

Then Niceta: The affair of the supper of the gods stands in this wise. They say that the banquet is the world, that the order of the gods sitting at table is the position of the heavenly bodies. Those whom Hesiod calls the first children of heaven and earth, of whom six were males and six females, they refer to the number of the twelve signs, which go round all the world. They say that the dishes of the banquet are the reasons and causes of things, sweet and desirable, which in the shape of inferences from the positions of the signs and the courses of the stars, explain how the world is ruled and governed. Yet they say these things exist after the free manner of a banquet, inasmuch as the mind of every one has the option whether he shall taste anything of this sort of knowledge, or whether he shall refrain; and as in a banquet no one is compelled, but every one is at liberty to eat, so also the manner of philosophizing depends upon the choice of the will. They say that discord is the lust of the flesh, which rises up against the purpose of the mind, and hinders the desire of philosophizing; and therefore they say that the time was that in which the marriage was celebrated. Thus they make Peleus and the nymph Thetis to be the dry and the moist element, by the admixture of which the substance of bodies is composed. They hold that Mercury is speech, by which instruction is conveyed to the mind; that Juno is chastity, Minerva courage, Venus lust, Paris the understanding. If therefore, say they, it happens that there is in a man a barbarous and uncultivated understanding, and ignorant of right judgment, he will despise chastity and courage, and will give the prize, which is the apple, to lust; and thereby, ruin and destruction will come not only upon himself, but also upon his countrymen and the whole race. These things, therefore, it is in their power to compose from whatever matter they please; yet they can be adapted to every man; because if any one has a pastoral and rustic and uncultivated understanding, and does not wish to be instructed, when the heat of his body shall make suggestions concerning the pleasure of lust, straightway he despises the virtues of studies and the blessings of knowledge, and turns his mind to bodily pleasures. And hence it is that implacable wars arise, cities are destroyed, countries fall, even as Paris, by the abduction of Helen, armed the Greeks and the barbarians to their mutual destruction.

Chapter 42. Interpretation of Scripture.

Then Peter, commending his statement, said: Ingenious men, as I perceive, take many verisimilitudes from the things which they read; and therefore great care is to be taken, that when the law of God is read, it be not read according to the understanding of our own mind. For there are many sayings in the divine Scriptures which can be drawn to that sense which every one has preconceived for himself; and this ought not to be done. For you ought not to seek a foreign and extraneous sense, which you have brought from without, which you may confirm from the authority of the Scriptures, but to take the sense of truth from the Scriptures themselves; and therefore it behooves you to learn the meaning of the Scriptures from him who keeps it according to the truth handed down to him from his fathers, so that he can authoritatively declare what he has rightly received. But when one has received an entire and firm rule of truth from the Scriptures, it will not be improper if he contribute to the establishment of true doctrine anything from common education and from liberal studies, which, it may be, he has attached himself to in his boyhood; yet so that, when he has learned the truth, he renounce falsehood and pretence.

Chapter 43. A Word of Exhortation.

And when he had said this, he looked to our father, and said: You therefore, old man, if indeed you care for your soul's safety, that when you desire to be separated from the body, it may, in consequence of this short conversion, find eternal rest, ask about whatever you please, and seek counsel, that you may be able to cast off any doubt that remains in you. For even to young men the time of life is uncertain; but to old men it is not even uncertain, for there is no doubt that there is but little time remaining to them. And therefore both young and old ought to be very earnest about their conversion and repentance, and to be taken up with the adornment of their souls for the future with the worthiest ornaments, such as the doctrines of truth, the grace of chastity, the splendour of righteousness, the fairness of piety, and all other things with which it becomes a reasonable mind to be adorned. Then, besides, they should break off from unseemly and unbelieving companions, and keep company with the faithful, and frequent those assemblies in which subjects are handled relating to chastity, righteousness and piety; to pray to God always heartily, and to ask of Him those things which ought to be asked of God; to give thanks to Him; to repent truly of their past doings; in some measure also, if possible, by deeds of mercy towards the poor, to help their penitence: for by these means pardon will be more easily bestowed, and mercy will be sooner shown to the merciful.

Chapter 44. Earnestness.

But if he who comes to repentance is of more advanced age, he ought the more to give thanks to God, because, having received the knowledge of the truth, after all the violence of carnal lust has been broken, there awaits him no fight of contest, by which to repress the pleasures of the body rising against the mind. It remains, therefore, that he be exercised in the learning of the truth, and in works of mercy, that he may bring forth fruits worthy of repentance; and that he do not suppose that the proof of conversion is shown by length of time, but by strength of devotion and of purpose. For minds are manifest to God; and He does not take account of times, but of hearts. For He approves if any one, on hearing the preaching of the truth, does not delay, nor spend time in negligence, but immediately, and if I may say so, in the same moment, abhorring the past, begins to desire things to come, and burns with love of the heavenly kingdom.

Chapter 45. All Ought to Repent.

Wherefore, let no one of you longer dissemble nor look backwards, but willingly approach to the Gospel of the kingdom of God. Let not the poor man say, When I shall become rich, then I shall be converted. God does not ask money of you, but a merciful heart and a pious mind. Nor let the rich man delay his conversion by reason of worldly care, while he thinks how he may dispose the abundance of his fruits; nor say within himself, 'What shall I do? Where shall I bestow my fruits?' Nor say to his soul, 'You have much goods laid up for many years; feast and rejoice.' For it shall be said to him, 'You fool, this night your soul shall be taken from you, and whose shall those things be which you have provided?' Therefore let every age, every sex, every condition, haste to repentance, that they may obtain eternal life. Let the young be thankful that they put their necks under the yoke of discipline in the very violence of their desires. The old also are themselves praise-worthy, because they change for the fear of God, the custom of a long time in which they have been unhappily occupied.

Chapter 46. The Sure Word of Prophecy.

Let no one therefore put off. Let no one delay. For what occasion is there for delaying to do well? Or are you afraid, lest, when you have done well, you do not find the reward as you supposed? And what loss will you sustain if you do well without reward? Would not conscience alone be sufficient in this? But if you find as you anticipate, shall you not receive great things for small, and eternal for temporal? But I say this for the sake of the unbelieving. For the things which we preach are as we preach them; because they cannot be otherwise, since they have been promised by the prophetic word.

Chapter 47. A Faithful Saying, and Worthy of All Acceptation.

But if any one desires to learn exactly the truth of our preaching, let him come to hear, and let him ascertain what the true Prophet is; and then at length all doubtfulness will cease to him, unless with obstinate mind he resist those things which he finds to be true. For there are some whose only object it is to gain the victory in any way whatever, and who seek praise for this rather than their salvation. These ought not to have a single word addressed to them, lest both the noble word suffer injury, and condemn to eternal death him who is guilty of the wrong done to it. For what is there in respect of which any one ought to oppose our preaching? Or in respect of which the word of our preaching is found to be contrary to the belief of what is true and honourable? It says that the God the Father, the Creator of all, is to be honoured, as also His Son, who alone knows Him and His will, and who alone is to be believed concerning all things which He has enjoined. For He alone is the law and the Lawgiver, and the righteous Judge, whose law decrees that God, the Lord of all, is to be honoured by a sober, chaste, just, and merciful life, and that all hope is to be placed in Him alone.

Chapter 48. Errors of the Philosophers.

But some one will say that precepts of this sort are given by the philosophers also. Nothing of the kind: for they do indeed give commandments concerning justice and sobriety, but they are ignorant that God is the recompenser of good and evil deeds; and therefore their laws and precepts only shun a public accuser, but cannot purify the conscience. For why should one fear to sin in secret, who does not know that there is a witness and a judge of secret things? Besides, the philosophers in their precepts add that even the gods, who are demons, are to be honoured; and this alone, even if in other respects they seemed worthy of approbation, is sufficient to convict them of the most dreadful impiety, and condemn them by their own sentence, since they declare indeed that there is one God, yet command that many be worshipped, by way of humouring human error. But also the philosophers say that God is not angry, not knowing what they say. For anger is evil, when it disturbs the mind, so that it loses right counsel. But that anger which punishes the wicked does not bring disturbance to the mind; but it is one and the same affection, so to speak, which assigned rewards to the good and punishment to the evil; for if He should bestow blessings upon the good and the evil, and confer equal rewards upon the pious and the impious, He would appear to be unjust rather than good.

Chapter 49. God's Long-Suffering.

But you say, Neither ought God to do evil. You say truly; nor does He. But those who have been created by Him, while they do not believe that they are to be judged, indulging their pleasures, have fallen away from piety and righteousness. But you will say, If it is right to punish the wicked, they ought to be punished immediately when they do wickedly. You indeed do well to make haste; but He who is eternal, and from whom nothing is secret, inasmuch as He is without end, in the same proportion is His patience extended, and He regards not the swiftness of vengeance, but the causes of salvation. For He is not so much pleased with the death as with the conversion of a sinner. Ezekiel 18:33 Therefore, in short, He has bestowed upon men holy baptism, to which, if any one makes haste to come, and for the future remains without stain, all his sins are thenceforth blotted out, which were committed in the time of his ignorance.

Chapter 50. Philosophers Not Benefactors of Men.

For what have the philosophers contributed to the life of man, by saying that God is not angry with men? Only to teach them to have no fear of any punishment or judgment, and thereby to take away all restraint from sinners. Or what have they benefited the human race, who have said that there is no God, but that all things happen by chance and accident? What but that men, hearing this, and thinking that there is no judge, no guardian of things, are driven headlong, without fear of any one, to every deed which either rage, or avarice, or lust may dictate. For they truly have much benefited the life of man who have said that nothing can be done apart from Genesis; that is, that every one, ascribing the cause of his sin to Genesis, might in the midst of his crimes declare himself innocent, while he does not wash out his guilt by repentance, but doubles it by laying the blame upon fate. And what shall I say of those philosophers who have maintained that the gods are to be worshipped, and such gods as were described to you a little while ago? What else was this but to decree that vices, crimes, and base deeds should be worshipped? I am ashamed of you, and I pity you, if you have not yet discovered that these things were unworthy of belief, and impious, and execrable, or if, having discovered and ascertained them to be evil, you have nevertheless worshipped them as if they were good, yea, even the best.

Chapter 51. Christ the True Prophet.

Then, besides, of what sort is that which some of the philosophers have presumed to speak even concerning God, though they are mortal, and can only speak by opinion concerning invisible things, or concerning the origin of the world, since they were not present when it was made, or concerning the end of it, or concerning the treatment and judgment of souls in the infernal regions, forgetting that it belongs indeed to a reasonable man to know things present and visible, but that it is the part of prophetic prescience alone to know things past, and things future, and things invisible? These things, therefore, are not to be gathered from conjectures and opinions, in which men are greatly deceived, but from faith in prophetic truth, as this doctrine of ours is. For we speak nothing of ourselves, nor announce things gathered by human judgment; for this were to deceive our hearers. But we preach the things which have been committed and revealed to us by the true Prophet. And concerning His prophetic prescience and power, if any one, as I have said, wishes to receive clear proofs, let him come instantly and be alert to hear, and we shall give evident proofs by which he shall seem not only to hear the power of prophetic prescience with his ears, but even to see it with his eyes and handle it with his hand; and when he has entertained a sure faith concerning Him, he will without any labour take upon him the yoke of righteousness and piety; Matthew 11:30 and so great sweetness will he perceive in it, that not only will he not find fault with any labour being in it, but will even desire something further to be added and imposed upon him.

Chapter 52. Appion and Anubion.

And when he had said this, and more to the same purpose, and had cured some who were present who were infirm and possessed of demons, he dismissed the crowds, while they gave thanks and praised God, charging them to come to the same place on the following days also for the sake of hearing. And when we were together at home, and were preparing to eat, one entering told us that Appion Pleistonices, with Anubion, were lately come from Antioch, and were lodging with Simon. Then my father, when he heard this, rejoiced, and said to Peter: If you permit me, I should like to go and salute Appion and Anubion, for they are great friends of mine; and perhaps I shall be able to persuade Anubion to dispute with Clement on the subject of Genesis . Then Peter said: I consent; and I commend you, because you respect your friends. But consider how all things occur to you according to your wish by God's providence; for, behold, not only have the objects of proper affection been restored to you by the appointment of God, but also the presence of your friends is arranged for you. Then said my father: Truly I consider that it is so as you say. And when he had said this, he went away to Anubion.

Chapter 53. A Transformation.

But we, sitting with Peter the whole night, asking questions, and learning of him on many subjects, remained awake through very delight in his teaching and the sweetness of his words; and when it was daybreak, Peter, looking at me and my brothers, said: I wonder what has befallen your father. And while he was speaking my father came in, and found Peter speaking to us about him. And when he had saluted he began to apologize, and to explain the reason why he had remained abroad. But we, looking at him, were horrified; for we saw on him the face of Simon, yet we heard the voice of our father. And when we shrank from him, and cursed him, my father was astonished at our treating him so harshly and barbarously. Yet Peter was the only one who saw his natural countenance; and he said to us: Why do you curse your father? And we, along with our mother, answered him: He appears to us to be Simon, though he has our father's voice. Then Peter: You indeed know only his voice, which has not been changed by the sorceries; but to me also his face, which to others appears changed by Simon's art, is known to be that of your father Faustinianus. And looking at my father, he said: The cause of the dismay of your wife and your sons is this — the appearance of your countenance does not seem to be as it was, but the face of the detestable Simon appears in you.

Chapter 54. Excitement in Antioch.

And while he was thus speaking, one of those returned who had gone before to Antioch, and said to Peter: I wish you to know, my lord Peter, that Simon at Antioch, doing many signs and prodigies in public, has inculcated upon the people nothing but what tends to excite hatred against you, calling you a magician, a sorcerer, a murderer; and to such an extent has he stirred up hatred against you, that they greatly desire, if they can find you anywhere, even to devour your flesh. And therefore we who were sent before, seeing the city greatly moved against you, met together in secret, and considered what ought to be done.

Chapter 55. A Stratagem.

And when we saw no way of getting out of the difficulty, there came Cornelius the centurion, being sent by Cæsar to the president of Cæsarea on public business. Him we sent for alone, and told him the reason why we were sorrowful, and entreated him that, if he could do anything, he should help us. Then he most readily promised that he would straightway put him to flight, if only we would aid his plans. And when we promised that we would be active in doing everything, he said, 'Cæsar has ordered sorcerers to be sought out and destroyed in the city of Rome and through the provinces, and a great number of them have been already destroyed. I shall therefore give out, through my friends, that I have come to apprehend that magician, and that I am sent by Cæsar for this purpose, that he may be punished with the rest of his fraternity. Let your people, therefore, who are with him in disguise, intimate to him, as if they had heard it from some quarter, that I am sent to apprehend him; and when he hears this, he is sure to take to flight. Or if you think of anything better, tell me. Why need I say more?' It was so done by those of ours who were with him, disguised for the purpose of acting as spies on him. And when Simon learned that this had come upon him, he received the information as a great kindness conferred upon him by them, and took to flight. He therefore departed from Antioch, and, as we have heard, came hither with Athenodorus.

Chapter 56. Simon's Design in the Transformation.

All we, therefore, who went before you, considered that in the meantime you should not go up to Antioch, till we see if the hatred of you which he has sown among the people be in any degree lessened by his departure. When he who had come from Antioch had imparted this information, Peter, looking to our father, said, Faustinianus, your countenance has been transformed by Simon Magus, as is evident; for he, thinking that he was being sought for by Cæsar for punishment, has fled in terror, and has placed his own countenance upon you, if haply you might be apprehended instead of him, and put to death, that so he might cause sorrow to your sons. But my father, when he heard this, crying out, said with tears: You have judged rightly, O Peter: for Anubion also, who is very friendly with me, began to inform me in a certain mysterious way of his plots; but unhappily I did not believe him, because I had done him no harm.

Chapter 57. Great Grief.

And when all of us, along with my father, were agitated with sorrow and weeping, meantime Anubion came to us, intimating to us that Simon had fled during the night, making for Judæa. But seeing our father lamenting and bewailing himself, and saying, Wretch that I am, not to believe when I heard that he is a magician! What has befallen wretched me, that on one day, being recognised by my wife and my sons, I have not been able to rejoice with them, but have been rolled back to the former miseries which I endured in my wandering!— but my mother, tearing her dishevelled hair, bewailed much more bitterly — we also, confounded at the change of our father's countenance, were, as it were, thunderstruck and beside ourselves, and could not understand what was the matter. But Anubion, seeing us all thus afflicted, stood like one dumb. Then Peter, looking at us his sons, said: Believe me that this is your very father; wherefore also I charge you that you respect him as your father. For God will afford some opportunity on which he shall be able to put off the countenance of Simon, and to recover the manifest figure of your father — that is, his own.

Chapter 58. How It All Happened.

Then, turning to my father, he said: I gave you leave to salute Appion and Anubion, who, you said, were your friends from boyhood, but not that you should speak with Simon. Then my father said: I confess I have sinned. Then said Anubion: I also with him beg and entreat of you to pardon the old man — good and noble man as he is. He was unhappily seduced and imposed upon by the magician in question; for I will tell you how the thing was done. When he came to salute us, it happened that at that very time we were standing around him, hearing him tell that he intended to flee away that night, for that he had heard that some persons had come even to this city of Laodicea to apprehend him by command of the emperor, but that he wished to turn all their rage against this Faustinianus, who has lately come hither. And he said to us: 'Only you make him sup with us, and I shall compound a certain ointment, with which, when he has supped, he shall anoint his face, and from that time he shall seem to all to have my countenance. But you first anoint your faces with the juice of a certain herb, that you may not be deceived as to the change of his countenance, so that to all except you he shall seem to be Simon.'

Chapter 59. A Scene of Mourning.

And when he said this, I said to him, 'And what advantage will you gain from this deed?' Then Simon said: 'In the first place, that those who are seeking me may lay hold on him, and so give over the search for me. But if he be punished by Cæsar, that his sons may have much sorrow, who forsook me, and fled to Peter, and are now his assistants.' Now I confess to you, Peter, what is true. I did not dare then tell Faustinianus; but neither did Simon give us opportunity of speaking with him in private, and disclosing to him fully Simon's design. Meantime, about the middle of the night, Simon has fled away, making for Judæa. And Athenodorus and Appion have gone to convoy him; but I pretended bodily indisposition, that I might remain at home, and make him return quickly to you, if haply he may in any way be concealed with you, lest, being seized by those who are in quest of Simon, he be brought before Cæsar, and perish without cause. And now, in my anxiety about him, I have come to see him, and to return before those who have gone to convoy Simon come back. And turning to us, Anubion said: I, Anubion, indeed see the true countenance of your father, because I was previously anointed by Simon himself, as I have told you, that the real face of Faustinianus might appear to my eyes; whence I am astonished and wonder at the art of Simon Magus, because you standing here do not recognise your father. And while my father and mother, and all of us, wept for the things which had befallen, Anubion, moved with compassion, also wept.

Chapter 60. A Counterplot.

Then Peter, moved with compassion, promised that he would restore the face of our father, saying to him: Listen, Faustinianus: As soon as the error of your transformed countenance shall have conferred some advantage on us, and shall have subserved the designs which we have in view, then I shall restore to you the true form of your countenance; on condition, however, that you first dispatch what I shall command you. And when my father promised that he would with all his might fulfil everything that he might charge him with, provided only that he might recover his own countenance, Peter thus began: You have heard with your own ears, that one of those who had been sent before has returned from Antioch, and told us how Simon, while he was there, stirred up the multitudes against me, and inflamed the whole city into hatred of me, declaring that I am a magician, and a murderer, and a deceiver, so that they are eager, if they see me, even to eat my flesh. Do therefore what I tell you: leave Clement with me, and go before us to Antioch, with your wife, and your sons Faustus and Faustinus. And I shall also send others with you, whom I think fit, who shall observe whatsoever I command them.

Chapter 61. A Mine Dug.

When therefore you come with them to Antioch, as you will be thought to be Simon, stand in a public place, and proclaim your repentance, and say: 'I Simon declare to you, and confess that all that I said concerning Peter was false: for he is neither a seducer, nor a magician, nor a murderer, nor any of the things that I spoke against him; but I said all these things under the instigation of madness. I therefore entreat you, even I myself, who erewhile gave you causes of hatred against him, that you think no such thing concerning him. But lay aside your hatred; cease from your indignation; because he is truly sent by God for the salvation of the world — a disciple and apostle of the true Prophet. Wherefore I advise, exhort, and charge you that you hear him, and believe him when he preaches to you the truth, lest haply, if you despise him, your very city suddenly perish. But I will tell you why I now make this confession to you. This night an angel of God rebuked me for my wickedness, and scourged me terribly, because I was an enemy to the herald of the truth. Therefore I entreat you, that even if I myself should ever again come to you, and attempt to say anything against Peter, you will not receive nor believe me. For I confess to you, I was a magician, a seducer, a deceiver; but I repent, for it is possible by repentance to blot out former evil deeds.'

Chapter 62. A Case of Conscience.

When Peter made this intimation to my father, he answered: I know what you wish; do not trouble yourself further: for I understand and know what I am to undertake when I come to the place. And Peter gave him further instruction, saying: When therefore you come to the place, and see the people turned by your discourse, and laying aside their hatred, and returning to their longing for me, send and tell me, and I shall come immediately; and when I come, I shall without delay set you free from this strange countenance, and restore to you your own, which is known to all your friends. And having said this, he ordered my brothers to go with him, and at the same time our mother Matthidia, and some of our friends. But my mother refused to go along with him, and said: It seems as if I should be an adulteress if I were to associate with the countenance of Simon; but if I be compelled to go along with him, it is at all events impossible that I can lie in the same bed with him; but I do not know if I can consent even to go with him. And when she stoutly refused, Anubion began to exhort her, saying: Believe me and Peter. But does not even his voice persuade you that he is your husband Faustinianus, whom truly I love not less than you do? And, in short, I also myself shall come with you. And when Anubion had said this, my mother promised that she would go with him.

Chapter 63. A Pious Fraud.

Then said I: God arranges our affairs to our liking; for we have with us Anubion an astrologer, with whom, if we come to Antioch, we shall dispute with all earnestness on the subject of Genesis . And when our father had set out, after the middle of the night, with those whom Peter had ordered to accompany him, and with Anubion; in the morning, before Peter went to the discussion, those men returned who had convoyed Simon, namely Appion and Athenodorus, and came to us inquiring after my father. But Peter, when he was informed of their coming, ordered them to enter. And when they were seated, they asked, Where is Faustinianus? Peter answered: We do not know; for since the evening that he went to you, no one of his friends has seen him. But yesterday morning Simon came inquiring for him; and because we gave him no answer, I know not what he meant, but he said that he was Faustinianus. But when nobody believed him, he went and lamented, and threatened that he would destroy himself; and afterwards he went away towards the sea.

Chapter 64. A Competition in Lying.

When Appion heard this, and those who were with him, they raised a great howling, saying: Why have you done this? Why did you not receive him? And when Athenodorus was going to tell me that it was my father Faustinianus himself, Appion prevented him, and said: We have learned from some one that he has gone with Simon, and that at the entreaty of Faustinianus himself, being unwilling to see his sons, because they are Jews. When therefore we heard this, we came to inquire after him here; but since he is not here, it appears that he must have spoken truly who told us that he has gone with Simon. This, therefore, we tell you. But I Clement, when I understood the designs of Peter, that he wished to make them suppose that the old man would be required at their hands, so that they might be afraid and flee away, I began to aid his design, and said to Appion: Listen, dear Appion: what we believe to be good, we wish to deliver to our father also; but if he will not receive it, but rather, as you say, flees away through abhorrence of us — it may perhaps be harsh to say so — we care nothing about him. And when I had said this, they departed, cursing my cruelty, and followed the track of Simon, as we learned on the following day.

Chapter 65. Success of the Plot.

Meantime, while Peter was daily, according to his custom, teaching the people, and working many miracles and cures, after ten days came one of our people from Antioch, sent by my father, informing us how my father stood in public, accusing Simon, whose face indeed he seemed to wear, and extolling Peter with unmeasured praises, and commending him to all the people, and making them long for him, so that all were changed by his speech, and longed to see him; and that many had come to love Peter so much, that they raged against my father in his character of Simon, and thought of laying hands on him, because he had done such wrong to Peter! Wherefore, said he, make haste, lest haply he be murdered; for he sent me with speed to you, being in great fear, to ask you to come without delay, that you may find him alive, and also that you may appear at the favourable moment, when the city is growing in affection towards you. He also told us how, as soon as my father entered the city of Antioch, the whole people were gathered to him, supposing him to be Simon; and he began to make public confession to them all, according to what the restoration of the people demanded: for all, as many as came, both noble and common, both rich and poor, hoping that some prodigies would be wrought by him in his usual way, he addressed thus:—

Chapter 66. Truth Told by Lying Lips.

It is long that the divine patience bears with me, Simon the most unhappy of men; for whatever you have wondered at in me was done, not by means of truth, but by the lies and tricks of demons, that I might subvert your faith and condemn my own soul. I confess that all things that I said about Peter were lies; for he never was either a magician or a murderer, but has been sent by God for the salvation of you all; and if from this hour you think that he is to be despised, be assured that your very city may suddenly be destroyed. But, you will ask, what is the reason that I make this confession to you of my own accord? I was vehemently rebuked by an angel of God this night, and most severely scourged, because I was his enemy. I therefore entreat you, that if from this hour even I myself shall ever open my mouth against him, you will drive me from your sight; for that foul demon, who is an enemy to the salvation of men, speaks against him through my mouth, that you may not attain to life by his means. For what miracle could the magic art show you through me? I made brazen dogs bark, and statues move, men change their appearances, and suddenly vanish from men's sight; and for these things you ought to have cursed the magic art, which bound your souls with devilish fetters, that I might show you a vain miracle, that you might not believe Peter, who cures the sick in the name of Him by whom he is sent, and expels demons, and gives sight to the blind, and restores health to the palsied, and raises the dead.

Chapter 67. Faustinianus is Himself Again.

Whilst he made these and similar statements, the people began to curse him, and to weep and lament because they had sinned against Peter, believing him to be a magician or wicked man. But the same day, at evening, Faustinianus had his own face restored to him, and the appearance of Simon Magus left him. Now Simon, hearing that his face on Faustinianus had contributed to the glory of Peter, came in haste to anticipate Peter, and intending to cause by his art that his likeness should be taken from Faustinianus, when Christ had already accomplished this according to the word of His apostle. But Niceta and Aquila, seeing their father's face restored after the necessary proclamation, gave thanks to God, and would not allow him to address the people any more.

Chapter 68. Peter's Entry into Antioch.

But Simon began, though secretly, to go among his friends and acquaintances, and to malign Peter more than before. Then all spat in his face, and drove him from the city, saying: You will be chargeable with your own death, if you think of coming hither again, speaking against Peter. These things being known at Laodicea, Peter ordered the people to meet on the following day; and having ordained one of those who followed him as bishop over them, and others as presbyters, and having baptized multitudes, and restored to health all who were troubled with sicknesses or demons, he stayed there three days longer; and all things being properly arranged, he bade them farewell, and set out from Laodicea, being much longed for by the people of Antioch. And the whole city began to hear, through Niceta and Aquila, that Peter was coming. Then all the people of the city of Antioch, hearing of Peter's arrival, went to meet him, and almost all the old men and the nobles came with ashes sprinkled on their heads, in this way testifying their repentance, because they had listened to the magician Simon, in opposition to his preaching.

Chapter 69. Peter's Thanksgiving.

Stating these and such like things, they bring to him those distressed with sicknesses, and tormented with demons, paralytics also, and those suffering diverse perils; and there was an infinite number of sick people collected. And when Peter saw that they not only repented of the evil thoughts they had entertained of him through means of Simon, but also that they showed so entire faith in God, that they believed that all who suffered from every sort of ailment could be healed by him, he spread out his hands towards heaven, pouring out prayers with tears, and gave thanks to God, saying: I bless you, O Father, worthy of all praise, who hast deigned to fulfil every word and promise of Your Son, that every creature may know that You alone are God in heaven and in earth.

Chapter 70. Miracles.

With such sayings, he went up on a height, and ordered all the multitude of sick people to be ranged before him, and addressed them all in these words: As you see me to be a man like to yourselves, do not suppose that you can recover your health from me, but through Him who, coming down from heaven, has shown to those who believe in Him a perfect medicine for body and soul. Hence let all this people be witnesses to your declaration, that with your whole heart you believe in the Lord Jesus Christ, that they may know that themselves also may be saved by Him. And when all the multitude of the sick with one voice cried out that He is the true God whom Peter preaches, suddenly an overpowering light of the grace of God appeared in the midst of the people; and the paralytics being cured, began to run to Peter's feet, the blind to shout on the recovery of their sight, the lame to give thanks on regaining the power of walking, the sick to rejoice in restored health; some even who were barely alive, being already without consciousness or the power of speech, were raised up; and all the lunatics, and those possessed of demons, were set free.

Chapter 71. Success.

So great grace of His power did the Holy Spirit show on that day, that all, from the least to the greatest, with one voice confessed the Lord; and not to delay you with many words, within seven days, more than ten thousand men, believing in God, were baptized and consecrated by sanctification: so that Theophilus, who was more exalted than all the men of power in that city, with all eagerness of desire consecrated the great palace of his house under the name of a church, and a chair was placed in it for the Apostle Peter by all the people; and the whole multitude assembling daily to hear the word, believed in the healthful doctrine which was avouched by the efficacy of cures.

Chapter 72. Happy Ending.

Then I Clement, with my brothers and our mother, spoke to our father, asking him whether any remnants of unbelief remained in him. And he said: Come, and you shall see, in the presence of Peter, what an increase of faith has grown in me. Then Faustinianus approached, and fell down at Peter's feet, saying: The seeds of your word, which the field of my mind has received, are now sprung up, and have so advanced to fruitful maturity, that nothing is wanting but that you separate me from the chaff by that spiritual reaping-hook of yours, and place me in the garner of the Lord, making me partaker of the divine table. Then Peter, with all alacrity grasping his hand, presented him to me Clement, and my brothers, saying: As God has restored your sons to you, their father, so also your sons restore their father to God. And he proclaimed a fast to all the people, and on the next Lord's day he baptized him; and in the midst of the people, taking occasion from his conversion, he related all his fortunes, so that the whole city received him as an angel, and paid him no less honour than they did to the apostle. And these things being known, Peter ordered the people to meet on the following day; and having ordained one of his followers as bishop, and others as presbyters, he baptized also a great number of people, and restored to health all who had been distressed with sicknesses.

Aklat 10

Kabanata 1. Pagsubok.

Sa umaga, pagkasilang ng araw, kaming sina Clemente, Niceta, at Aquila, kasama si Pedro, ay nagtungo sa silid na tinutulugan ng aking ama at ina. Nang matagpuan naming natutulog pa sila, umupo kami sa harap ng pintuan. Sinabi sa amin ni Pedro: Makinig kayo sa akin, pinakamamahal kong mga kapwa-lingkod. Alam kong napakalaki ng pagmamahal ninyo sa inyong ama; kaya nangangamba akong baka madaliin ninyo siyang pasanin ang pamatok ng relihiyon samantalang hindi pa siya handa, at baka pumayag lamang siya dahil sa pagmamahal niya sa inyo. Ngunit hindi dapat umasa sa gayong pagsang-ayon, sapagkat ang ginagawa alang-alang lamang sa tao ay hindi karapat-dapat purihin at madaling gumuho. Kaya sa palagay ko'y dapat ninyo siyang hayaang mamuhay sa loob ng isang taon ayon sa sarili niyang paghatol. Sa panahong iyon maaari siyang maglakbay kasama natin at, habang nagtuturo tayo sa iba, makinig siya nang payak at walang pamimilit. Kung habang nakikinig ay tunay niyang nanaising kilalanin ang katotohanan, siya mismo ang hihiling na pasanin ang pamatok ng relihiyon; ngunit kung ayaw niya, maaari pa rin siyang manatiling kaibigan. Sapagkat ang mga hindi taos-pusong tumatanggap nito, kapag hindi na nila kayang dalhin ang kanilang pinasan, ay hindi lamang nagtatakwil sa kanilang tinanggap; upang bigyang-katwiran ang sarili nilang kahinaan, nagsisimula pa silang magsalita laban sa landas ng relihiyon at manira sa mga hindi nila kayang sundan o tularan.

Kabanata 2. Isang Suliranin.

Sumagot si Niceta: Panginoon kong Pedro, wala akong tutol sa matuwid at mabuti mong payo; ngunit nais kong magtanong ng isang bagay upang matutuhan ko ang hindi ko alam. Paano kung mamatay ang aking ama sa loob ng taong ipinapayo mong ipagpaliban muna siya? Bababa siyang walang magawa sa impiyerno at pahihirapan magpakailanman. Sinabi ni Pedro: Nauunawaan ko ang mabuti mong hangarin para sa iyong ama, at pinatatawad kita sa mga bagay na hindi mo pa nalalaman. Inaakala mo ba na kapag ang isang tao ay waring namuhay nang matuwid ay agad na siyang maliligtas? Hindi ba siya susuriin Niya na nakaaalam ng mga lihim ng tao upang makita kung bakit siya namuhay nang matuwid—kung ayon lamang sa tuntunin ng mga Hentil at pagsunod sa kanilang mga kaugalian at batas; dahil sa pakikisama sa mga tao; dahil lamang sa nakasanayan; dahil sa ibang dahilan o pangangailangan; at hindi dahil sa katuwiran mismo at alang-alang sa Diyos? Yaong mga namuhay nang matuwid alang-alang lamang sa Diyos at sa Kanyang katuwiran ang darating sa walang-hanggang kapahingahan at tatanggap ng walang-hanggang kaharian sa langit. Sapagkat ang kaligtasan ay hindi nakakamit sa pamimilit kundi sa kalayaan; hindi sa pabor ng tao kundi sa pananampalataya sa Diyos. Bukod dito, dapat mong tandaan na alam ng Diyos nang pauna kung ang taong ito ay kabilang sa Kanya. Kung alam Niyang hindi, ano ang magagawa natin tungkol sa mga bagay na Kanyang itinakda mula pa sa pasimula? Ngunit sa abot ng aking maipapayo, ganito: kapag gising na siya at magkakasama tayong nakaupo, magtanong ka na para bang may nais kang matutuhan tungkol sa mga bagay na nararapat niyang malaman; habang nag-uusap tayo, makatatanggap siya ng pagtuturo. Gayunman, tingnan muna natin kung siya mismo ang magtatanong, sapagkat kung gayon ay magiging higit na angkop ang pagkakataon. Kung hindi siya magtanong, magpalitan tayo ng mga tanong na para bang nais nating matuto sa isa't isa. Iyan ang aking pasiya; sabihin mo naman ang sa iyo.

Kabanata 3. Isang Mungkahi.

Nang papurihan namin ang matuwid niyang payo, sinabi ko, ako si Clemente: Sa maraming bagay, ang wakas ay karaniwang tumutugon sa pasimula, at ang kinalabasan ay kahawig ng pinagmulan nito. Kaya tungkol sa aming ama, umaasa akong yamang sa pamamagitan mo ay binigyan siya ng Diyos ng mabuting pasimula, bibigyan din Siya ng wakas na naaayon doon at karapat-dapat sa Kanya. Gayunman, may mungkahi ako. Kung, gaya ng sinabi mo, magsisimula tayong mag-usap sa harap ng aking ama na para bang tinatalakay lamang natin ang isang paksa o may nais matutuhan sa isa't isa, hindi mo dapat kunin, panginoon kong Pedro, ang papel ng isang taong may kailangang matutuhan; sapagkat kapag nakita niya iyon ay baka lalo siyang mabagabag. Naniniwala siyang lubos mong nalalaman ang lahat ng mga bagay na kailangan, gaya nga ng totoo. Ano ang iisipin niya kung makita ka niyang nagkukunwaring mangmang? Sa halip na makatulong, baka makasama iyon sa kanya dahil hindi niya alam ang ating layunin. Ngunit kung kaming magkakapatid, habang nag-uusap, ay magkaroon ng alinlangan, ikaw ang magbigay ng angkop na sagot. Sapagkat kung makita ka rin niyang nag-aatubili at nag-aalinlangan, iisipin niyang walang sinumang nakaaalam ng katotohanan.

Kabanata 4. Malayang Pagsisiyasat.

Sumagot si Pedro: Huwag natin itong labis na alalahanin. Kung nararapat siyang pumasok sa pintuan ng buhay, magbibigay ang Diyos ng angkop na pagkakataon; ang pasimula ay magmumula sa Diyos, hindi sa tao. Kaya, gaya ng sinabi ko, hayaan siyang maglakbay kasama natin at makinig sa ating mga talakayan. Dahil nakita kong nagmamadali kayo, sinabi kong kailangan lamang hintayin ang pagkakataon; at kapag ibinigay iyon ng Diyos, sundin ninyo ang payong ibibigay ko. Habang nag-uusap kami, may batang dumating at nagsabing gising na ang aming ama. Nang papasok sana kami sa kanya, siya mismo ang lumabas, binati kami ng halik, at nang makaupo kaming muli ay nagtanong: Pinahihintulutan ba rito ang isang tao na magtanong kung nais niya, o ipinag-uutos ang katahimikan gaya ng sa mga Pythagoreo? Sinabi ni Pedro: Hindi namin pinipilit ang mga lumalapit sa amin na laging manahimik, ni pinipilit silang magtanong. Malaya silang kumilos ayon sa kanilang nais. Alam naming ang taong nagmamalasakit sa kanyang kaligtasan, kapag may nararamdamang sugat o kirot sa alinmang bahagi ng kanyang kaluluwa, ay hindi kayang manatiling tahimik. Ngunit sa taong nagpapabaya sa kanyang kaligtasan, walang pakinabang kung pipilitin siyang magtanong, maliban marahil sa magmukhang masigasig at masipag. Kaya kung may nais kang malaman, magtanong ka.

Kabanata 5. Mabuti at Masama.

Pagkatapos ay sinabi ng matandang lalaki: Laganap sa mga pilosopong Griyego ang pahayag na sa tunay na kalagayan ay walang mabuti o masama sa buhay ng tao; tinatawag lamang ng mga tao na mabuti o masama ang mga bagay ayon sa tingin nila, sapagkat hinubog sila ng gamit at kaugalian ng buhay. Sinasabi nilang maging ang pagpatay ay hindi likas na masama, sapagkat pinalalaya lamang nito ang kaluluwa mula sa pagkakagapos sa laman. Dagdag pa nila, maging ang matutuwid na hukom ay pumapatay sa mga gumagawa ng krimen; kung talagang masama ang pagpatay, hindi sana iyon ginagawa ng matutuwid. Hindi rin daw masama ang pangangalunya, sapagkat kung hindi alam o hindi pinapansin ng asawa, wala raw kasamaan doon. Hindi rin masama ang pagnanakaw, ayon sa kanila, sapagkat kinukuha lamang nito mula sa isang may taglay ng isang bagay ang wala naman sa iba. Sa katunayan, anila, dapat sanang malaya at lantad ang pagkuha; kung palihim itong ginagawa, ang lihim na paraan ay pagsaway lamang sa kawalan ng pagkamakatao ng taong ayaw magbahagi. Sapagkat dapat gamitin ng lahat ng tao nang sama-sama ang lahat ng bagay sa sanlibutang ito; dahil lamang sa kawalang-katarungan ay sinasabi ng isa, “Akin ito,” at ng isa pa, “Akin naman iyan,” kaya nagkakaroon ng paghahati-hati sa mga tao. Sa madaling salita, may isang taong itinuturing na pinakamarunong sa mga Griyego na nagsabing dapat maging pangkaraniwan sa magkakaibigan ang lahat ng bagay. At sa lahat ng bagay, walang alinlangang kabilang pati ang mga asawa. Sinabi rin niya na kung paanong hindi maaaring hatiin ang hangin at liwanag ng araw, hindi rin dapat paghati-hatian ang iba pang mga bagay na ibinigay sa sanlibutan upang ariin ng lahat nang sama-sama. Sinabi ko ito sapagkat nais kong bumaling sa mabuting pamumuhay; ngunit hindi ako makagagawa ng mabuti hangga't hindi ko muna nalalaman kung ano ang mabuti. Kapag naunawaan ko iyon, mauunawaan ko rin kung ano ang masama, samakatuwid, yaong salungat sa mabuti.

Kabanata 6. Awtoridad ni Pedro.

Ngunit nais kong isa sa inyo, at hindi si Pedro, ang sumagot sa sinabi ko. Hindi angkop na hingan siya ng mga paliwanag sa pamamagitan ng tanong at sagot; kapag nagbibigay siya ng pasiya tungkol sa isang bagay, dapat iyon tanggapin nang walang pagtutol. Kaya panatilihin natin siyang tagahatol: kung hindi natin malutas ang isang talakayan, siya ang magsabi kung ano ang matuwid at sa gayon ay wakasan nang tiyak ang mga bagay na nananatiling may alinlangan. Kung ngayon pa lamang ay magbigay siya ng pasiya, masisiyahan na ako at maniniwala; at sa huli ay ganoon din ang gagawin ko. Ngunit nais ko munang makita kung matatagpuan natin sa pamamagitan ng talakayan ang hinahanap natin. Kaya nais kong si Clemente ang maunang magsalita at ipakita kung may tunay bang mabuti o masama sa kalikasan o sa mga gawa.

Kabanata 7. Pangangatwiran ni Clemente.

Sumagot ako: Yamang nais mong malaman mula sa akin kung may mabuti o masama sa kalikasan o sa kilos, o kung iniisip lamang ng mga tao na mabuti ang ilan at masama ang iba dahil sa kaugaliang naghati-hati sa mga bagay na dapat sana, ayon sa iyo, ay pangkaraniwan gaya ng hangin at sikat ng araw, palagay ko'y dapat kong gamitin ang mismong mga aral na kilala at tinatanggap mo upang walang dahilan para pag-alinlanganan mo ang aking sasabihin. Nagtatakda kayo ng ilang hangganan sa mga elemento at sa mga bagay sa langit. Sinasabi ninyong ang ilan ay nagtatagpo nang walang pinsala, gaya ng isang wastong pag-aasawa, ngunit ang iba ay nagsasanib nang nakapipinsala, gaya ng pangangalunya. Sinasabi rin ninyong may mga bagay na pangkalahatan sa lahat at may iba namang hindi. Upang hindi pahabain ang usapan, ganito ang maikling halimbawa: ang tuyong lupa ay nangangailangan ng pagdagdag at paghahalo ng tubig upang makapagbunga ng mga pananim na kailangan ng tao upang mabuhay. Kaya ito ay wastong pagsasanib. Ngunit kung ang lamig ng hamog na nagyelo ang ihalo sa lupa, o ang init sa tubig, ang gayong pagsasanib ay nagbubunga ng pagkasira; sa mga bagay na iyon, iyan ang maihahambing sa pangangalunya.

Kabanata 8. Mga Kasamaang Inamin.

Sumagot ang aking ama: Ngunit kung paanong agad na nakikita ang pinsala ng hindi magkatugmang pagsasanib ng mga elemento o mga bituin, dapat ding maipakita agad na masama ang pangangalunya. Sinabi ko: Sabihin mo muna kung inaamin mo, gaya ng sinabi mo na, na nagmumula ang masasamang bunga sa hindi angkop at hindi magkatugmang paghahalo; pagkatapos ay susuriin natin ang isa pang bagay. Sinabi ng aking ama: Gayon nga ang kalikasan ng mga bagay, anak ko. Sumagot ako: Kung gayon, yamang nais mong malaman ang tungkol dito, tingnan mo kung gaano karaming bagay ang walang sinumang nag-aalinlangang masama. Itinuturing mo bang masama ang lagnat, sunog, kaguluhan, pagbagsak ng bahay, pagpatay, pagkakagapos, pagpapahirap, sakit, pagdadalamhati, at mga katulad nito? Sinabi ng aking ama: Totoo, anak ko, masama ang mga bagay na ito—napakasama. At kung may tumatangging masama ang mga iyon, siya na ang makaranas ng mga iyon!

Kabanata 9. Pag-iral ng Kasamaan Ayon sa Astrolohiya.

Sumagot ako: Yamang ang kausap ko ay bihasa sa astrolohiya, gagamitin ko ang mismong aral na iyan upang higit mong madaling tanggapin ang pangangatwiran. Inaamin mong masama ang mga nabanggit natin—mga lagnat, sunog, at mga katulad nito. Ayon sa inyong aral, ang mga ito ay ibinubunga ng masasamang bituin, gaya ng malamig at mamasa-masang Saturno at ng mainit na Marte; samantalang ang mga salungat na mabubuting bagay ay iniuugnay sa mabubuting bituin gaya ng katamtamang Jupiter at mamasa-masang Venus. Hindi ba? Sumagot ang aking ama: Gayon nga, anak ko; hindi maaaring iba. Sinabi ko: Kung sinasabi mong ang mabubuting bagay ay nagmumula sa mabubuting bituin—halimbawa, Jupiter at Venus—tingnan natin kung ano ang bunga kapag nahalo sa mabuti ang isa sa masasamang bituin, at kilalanin nating masama ang gayong bunga. Sinasabi ninyong si Venus ang gumagawa ng pag-aasawa, at kapag kasama niya si Jupiter sa pagkakaayos ay nagiging malinis ang pag-aasawa; ngunit kapag hindi nakatingin si Jupiter at naroon si Marte, sinasabi ninyong nasisira ang pag-aasawa sa pangangalunya. Sinabi ng aking ama: Gayon nga. Sumagot ako: Kung gayon, masama ang pangangalunya, sapagkat ayon sa inyong sariling aral ay nagmumula ito sa paghahalo ng masasamang bituin. Sa madaling salita, lahat ng masamang epekto na sinasabi ninyong idinudulot ng masasamang bituin kapag nakihalo sa mabubuti ay dapat tawaging masama. Kaya ang mga bituing sinasabi ninyong nagdudulot ng mga lagnat, masasamang pagkakaayos, at iba pang kapahamakan ay siya ring, ayon sa inyo, nagdudulot ng pagpatay, pangangalunya, pagnanakaw, at gumagawa ng mga taong palalo at matigas ang isip.

Kabanata 10. Paano Sumulong.

Sinabi ng aking ama: Tunay na maikli ngunit napakahusay mong naipakitang may kasamaan sa mga gawa. Gayunman, nais ko pang malaman kung paano makatarungang hinahatulan ng Diyos ang mga nagkakasala kung, gaya ng sinasabi ko, pinipilit sila ng genesis na magkasala. Sumagot ako: Natatakot akong magsalita laban sa iyo, ama ko, sapagkat nararapat kitang parangalan; kung hindi lamang dahil dito ay may maibibigay akong sagot. Sinabi niya: Sabihin mo ang nasa isip mo, anak. Hindi ikaw ang gumagawa ng pagkakasala kundi ang paraan ng pagsisiyasat, gaya ng isang mahinhing babaeng nagagalit sa isang taong mahalay upang ipagtanggol ang sarili at ang kanyang dangal. Sumagot ako: Kung hindi natin panghahawakan ang mga simulaing kinilala at inamin na natin, at kung palagi nating kinakalas dahil sa pagkalimot ang mga bagay na napagpasiyahan na, magiging gaya tayo ng paghabi ni Penelope—ginigiba ang ginawa na natin. Kaya dapat tayong alinman sa huwag agad sumang-ayon bago masusing masuri ang aral, o, kapag minsan na tayong sumang-ayon at napagtibay na ang isang pahayag, panghawakan natin iyon upang makapagpatuloy tayo sa ibang mga tanong. Sinabi ng aking ama: Matuwid ang sinasabi mo, anak. Alam ko rin kung bakit mo ito sinasabi. Sa talakayan kahapon tungkol sa mga sanhi sa kalikasan, ipinakita mong may isang masamang kapangyarihang nakikialam sa kaayusan ng mga bituin at pumupukaw sa mga pagnanasa ng tao, inuudyukan sila sa iba't ibang paraan tungo sa kasalanan ngunit hindi sila pinipilit ni siya mismo ang gumagawa ng kasalanan. Sumagot ako: Mabuti at naaalala mo iyon; ngunit kahit naaalala mo, nagkamali ka pa rin ngayon. Sinabi ng aking ama: Patawarin mo ako, anak, sapagkat hindi pa ako sanay sa mga bagay na ito. Dahil sa katotohanan ng mga sinabi ninyo kahapon, napilitan akong sumang-ayon; ngunit sa aking kamalayan ay tila may natitira pang bakas ng lagnat na pansamantalang humahadlang sa akin sa pananampalataya, gaya ng karamdaman na humahadlang sa kalusugan. Nalilito ako sapagkat alam kong napakaraming bagay—halos lahat—ang nangyari sa akin ayon sa genesis.

Kabanata 11. Pagsubok sa Astrolohiya.

Sumagot ako: Sasabihin ko sa iyo, ama, kung ano talaga ang kalagayan ng tinatawag na matematika o astrolohiya, at gawin mo ang ipapayo ko. Pumunta ka sa isang astrologo at sabihin mong ganito at gayong masasamang bagay ang nangyari sa iyo sa isang tiyak na panahon, at nais mong malaman kung saan, paano, o sa pamamagitan ng anong mga bituin nangyari ang mga iyon. Walang alinlangang sasabihin niyang isang masamang Marte o Saturno ang namahala sa mga panahong iyon, o naging pana-panahon ang isa sa kanila; o na may isang bituing nakaharap sa iyo nang tuwiran, nasa pagsasanib, nasa gitna, o sa iba pang gayong posisyon. Idaragdag pa niyang may isang bagay na hindi kasuwato ng masamang bituin, o hindi nakikita, nasa isang anyo, lampas sa isang hati, nasa eklipse, hindi nakadikit, o kasama ng madidilim na bituin; at marami pang katulad na paliwanag ang ibibigay niya ayon sa sarili niyang sistema, hanggang sa maliliit na detalye. Pagkatapos ay pumunta ka sa ibang astrologo at sabihin mo ang kabaligtaran: na ganito at gayong mabuting bagay ang nangyari sa iyo sa mismong panahong iyon. Itanong mo kung saang bahagi ng iyong genesis nagmula ang mabuting iyon, at tiyaking pareho ang mga petsa at oras na ginamit mo nang magtanong tungkol sa mga kasamaan. Kapag nalinlang mo siya tungkol sa pangyayari ngunit hindi sa oras, tingnan mo kung anong mga pagkakaayos ng bituin ang iimbentuhin niya upang patunayang dapat ngang nangyari sa iyo ang mga mabubuting bagay sa mismong panahong iyon. Sapagkat sa bawat bahagi ng bilog ng langit ay lagi silang makakikita ng ilang bituing kanais-nais ang posisyon at ilang hindi kanais-nais. Dahil ang bilog, ayon sa kanilang matematika, ay ganap sa bawat bahagi at nagpapahintulot sa maraming magkakaibang dahilan, lagi silang makakukuha roon ng pagkakataong sabihin ang anumang nais nilang sabihin.

Kabanata 12. Nabigo ang Astrolohiya Dahil sa Malayang Kalooban.

Kung paanong kapag may mga taong nakakita ng hindi kanais-nais na panaginip at wala silang matiyak na kahulugan, saka lamang nila inaangkop ang panaginip sa pangyayaring naganap matapos mangyari ang isang bagay, gayon din ang astrolohiya. Bago mangyari ang isang bagay ay walang tiyak na ipinahahayag; kapag nangyari na, saka lamang nila tinitipon ang mga sinasabing sanhi nito. Kaya kapag nagkamali sila at naging iba ang kinalabasan, sinisisi nila ang sarili nilang pagbasa at sinasabing may ganito o gayong bituing sumalungat na hindi nila nakita. Hindi nila nalalaman na ang kamalian ay hindi lamang kakulangan ng husay kundi ang hindi pagkakatugma ng buong sistema. Hindi nila nalalaman ang mga bagay na nais nating gawin ngunit pinipili nating huwag sundin ang pagnanasa. Tayo na nakaunawa sa dahilan ng hiwagang ito ay alam kung bakit: sapagkat may kalayaan ng kalooban, kung kaya minsan ay sinasalungat natin ang ating mga pagnanasa at minsan ay sumusunod tayo sa mga iyon. Dahil dito, hindi tiyak ang magiging bunga ng mga kilos ng tao sapagkat nakasalalay iyon sa malayang pagpapasya. Maaaring ipahiwatig ng isang astrologo ang pagnanasang inuudyok ng isang masamang kapangyarihan; ngunit kung ang pagnanasang iyon ay isasagawa at magbubunga nga, walang sinumang makaaalam bago mangyari, sapagkat nakasalalay iyon sa kalayaan ng kalooban. Kaya nag-imbento ang mga mangmang na astrologo ng usapin tungkol sa mga climacteric bilang takbuhan nila sa mga kawalang-katiyakan, gaya ng lubos nating ipinaliwanag kahapon.

Kabanata 13. Pinapasok ang mga Tao.

Kung mayroon ka pang nais sabihin laban dito, sabihin mo. Sinabi ng aking ama: Wala nang higit pang totoo kaysa sa sinabi mo, anak ko. Habang nag-uusap kami, may nagsabing napakaraming tao ang nakatayo sa labas at nagtipon upang makinig. Ipinapasok sila ni Pedro sapagkat malaki at angkop ang lugar. Nang makapasok sila, sinabi ni Pedro sa amin: Kung may sinuman sa inyo na nais magsalita sa mga tao, magsalita siya tungkol sa pagsamba sa mga diyus-diyosan. Sumagot ako, si Clemente: Ang napakalaking kabaitan, kahinahunan, at pagtitiis mo sa lahat ang nagbibigay sa amin ng lakas ng loob na magsalita sa iyong harapan at magtanong ng anumang nais namin. Dahil dito, ang kahinahunan ng iyong ugali ay nag-aanyaya at naghihikayat sa lahat na yakapin ang mga aral ng doktrinang nagliligtas. Hindi ko pa ito nakita sa iba kundi sa iyo lamang, na walang inggit ni galit. Ano ang masasabi mo rito?

Kabanata 14. Walang Taong May Ganap na Kaalaman.

Sinabi ni Pedro: Hindi lamang inggit o galit ang dahilan kung bakit umiiwas ang ilan. Kung minsan ay nahihiya ang isang tao dahil baka hindi niya ganap na masagot ang mga tanong at mabunyag ang kakulangan ng kanyang kaalaman. Ngunit walang dapat ikahiya rito, sapagkat walang taong nararapat magpanggap na alam niya ang lahat. Iisa lamang ang nakaaalam ng lahat—Siya ring gumawa ng lahat. Kung ang ating Guro mismo ay nagpahayag na hindi Niya alam ang araw at oras na ang mga tanda ay Siya rin ang nagpahayag, at iniukol ang kaalamang iyon sa Ama, bakit natin ituturing na kahihiyan ang pag-aming hindi natin alam ang ilang bagay, yamang mayroon tayong halimbawa ng ating Guro? Ito lamang ang aming ipinahahayag: alam namin ang mga bagay na natutuhan namin mula sa tunay na Propeta, at ibinigay Niya sa amin ang mga bagay na Kanyang hinatulang sapat para sa kaalaman ng tao.

Kabanata 15. Pahayag ni Clemente.

Pagkatapos ay nagsalita ako, si Clemente: Noong nasa Tripolis tayo at nakikipagtalo ka laban sa mga Hentil, panginoon kong Pedro, labis akong namangha sa iyo. Bagaman tinuruan ka ng iyong ama ayon sa paraan ng mga Hebreo at sa mga pagsunod sa sarili ninyong kautusan, at hindi ka kailanman nahaluan ng pag-aaral ng karunungang Griyego, napakaganda at walang kapantay ng iyong pangangatwiran. Nabanggit mo pa ang ilang bagay tungkol sa mga kasaysayan ng kanilang mga diyos na karaniwang itinatanghal sa mga teatro. Ngunit napansin kong hindi mo lubos na kilala ang lahat ng kanilang mga pabula at pamumusong. Kung mamarapatin mo, ilalahad ko ang mga iyon sa iyong pandinig mula sa pasimula. Sinabi ni Pedro: Magpatuloy ka; mabuti ang ginagawa mong pagtulong sa aking pangangaral. Sinabi ko: Kung gayon ay magsasalita ako dahil iniutos mo, hindi upang turuan ka, kundi upang ilantad sa lahat kung gaano kahangal ang mga palagay ng mga Hentil tungkol sa kanilang mga diyos.

Kabanata 16. Nawa’y Maging mga Propeta ang Buong Bayan ng Diyos.

Nang magsasalita na ako, kinagat ni Niceta ang kanyang labi at sinenyasan akong manahimik. Nang makita iyon ni Pedro, sinabi niya: Bakit mo pipigilan ang kanyang bukas-palad na disposisyon at marangal na kalooban, na para bang dapat siyang manahimik alang-alang sa aking karangalan, na wala namang halaga sa sarili nito? Hindi mo ba nalalaman na kung ang lahat ng mga bansa, matapos marinig sa akin ang pangangaral ng katotohanan at sumampalataya, ay sila ring magsimulang magturo, lalo pa nilang mapararangalan ang aking gawain—kung inaakala mong naghahangad ako ng karangalan? Ano ang higit na marangal kaysa maghanda ng mga alagad para kay Cristo na hindi lamang mananahimik at maliligtas nang nag-iisa, kundi magsasalita tungkol sa natutuhan nila at gagawa ng mabuti sa iba? Nais kong kapwa ikaw, Niceta, at ikaw, minamahal na Aquila, ay tumulong sa akin sa pangangaral ng salita ng Diyos, lalo na't kilalang-kilala ninyo ang mga bagay na pinagmumulan ng kamalian ng mga Hentil. At hindi lamang kayo—nais kong ang lahat ng nakikinig sa akin ay makinig at matuto nang gayon kalalim na sila rin ay makapagturo. Sapagkat kailangan ng sanlibutan ang maraming katulong upang maibalik ang mga tao mula sa kamalian. Pagkatapos ay sinabi niya sa akin: Magpatuloy ka, Clemente, sa sinimulan mo.

Kabanata 17. Kosmogoniya ng mga Hentil.

Agad akong nagpatuloy: Gaya ng sinabi ko, nang makipagtalo ka sa Tripolis ay marami kang kapaki-pakinabang at makapangyarihang sinabi tungkol sa mga diyos ng mga Hentil. Kaya nais kong ilahad sa iyong harapan ang katawa-tawang mga alamat tungkol sa kanilang pinagmulan, upang kapwa hindi mo ikamangha ang kasinungalingan ng walang-kabuluhang pamahiing ito at upang malaman ng mga nakikinig kung gaano kahiya-hiya ang kanilang kamalian. Sinasabi ng mga marurunong sa mga Hentil na sa pasimula ng lahat ay may kaguluhan o chaos. Sa napakahabang panahon, tumigas ang mga panlabas na bahagi nito at nakagawa ng mga hangganan at waring pundasyon para sa sarili, na parang isang napakalaking itlog. Sa loob nito, gaya sa balat ng itlog, sa napakahabang panahon ay inalagaan at nabuhay ang isang nilalang. Nang mabasag ang malaking bilog na iyon, lumabas ang isang anyong taong may dalawang kasarian, na tinatawag nilang lalaki-babae. Tinawag nila itong Phanetas, mula sa “paglitaw,” sapagkat ayon sa kanila, nang lumitaw ito ay saka rin sumilang ang liwanag. Mula rito, anila, nagmula ang sangkap, karunungan, pagkilos, at pagsasama; at mula sa mga iyon ay nabuo ang langit at lupa. Mula sa langit, sinasabi nilang nagmula ang anim na lalaki na tinatawag na mga Titan; mula naman sa lupa ay anim na babae na tinatawag na mga Titanide. Ang mga lalaking sinasabing nagmula sa langit ay sina Oceanus, Cœus, Crios, Hyperion, Iapetus, at Chronos, na tinatawag nating Saturno. Ang mga babaeng sinasabing nagmula sa lupa ay sina Theia, Rhea, Themis, Mnemosyne, Tethys, at Hebe.

Kabanata 18. Pamilya ni Saturno.

Sa mga ito, ang panganay na lalaki ng langit ay kumuha bilang asawa ng panganay na babae ng lupa; ang ikalawang lalaki ay sa ikalawang babae, at gayon din ang iba. Dahil sa asawang babae, ang unang lalaki ay waring hinila pababa; ang ikalawang babae naman ay waring umakyat dahil sa lalaking kanyang napangasawa. Sa ganitong paraan, ang bawat isa ayon sa kanilang pagkakasunud-sunod ay nanatili sa bahaging napunta sa kanila sa pamamagitan ng pag-aasawa. Mula sa kanilang pagsasama, sinasabi nilang napakarami pang iba ang isinilang. Sa anim na lalaking ito, si Saturno ang kumuha kay Rhea bilang asawa. Nang mabalaan siya ng isang orakulo na ang magiging anak nila ay magiging higit na makapangyarihan kaysa sa kanya at magtataboy sa kanya mula sa kanyang kaharian, nagpasiya siyang lamunin ang bawat anak na lalaki na isisilang. Una nilang anak si Aides, na tinatawag nating Orcus; at dahil sa nabanggit na dahilan, kinuha at nilamon siya ni Saturno. Sumunod ang ikalawang anak na si Neptune, at nilamon din niya ito. Sa huli ay ipinanganak ang tinatawag nilang Jupiter; ngunit naawa ang kanyang inang si Rhea at sa pamamagitan ng panlilinlang ay itinago siya sa ama nang ito'y handa nang lumamon sa kanya. Upang hindi marinig ang iyak ng sanggol, pinatunog niya sa mga Corybante ang mga simbalo at tambol upang matabunan ng malakas na ingay ang tinig ng bata.

Kabanata 19. Kanilang mga Kapalaran.

Nang mapansin ni Saturno sa pagliit ng tiyan ni Rhea na naipanganak na ang bata, hiningi niya ito upang lamunin. Ngunit isang malaking bato ang iniharap ni Rhea at sinabi niyang iyon ang kanyang iniluwal. Kinuha at nilamon iyon ni Saturno. Nang malunok ang bato, itinulak at inilabas nito ang mga anak na dati niyang nilamon. Kaya si Orcus, na unang lumabas, ay bumaba at kumuha ng pinakamababang dako, samakatuwid ang mga rehiyon sa ilalim ng lupa. Ang ikalawa, si Neptune, ay itinulak sa ibabaw ng mga tubig. Ang ikatlo, si Jupiter, na nakaligtas dahil sa panlilinlang ng kanyang inang si Rhea, ay inilagay nito sa isang kambing at ipinadala sa langit.

Kabanata 20. Mga Gawa ni Jupiter.

Sapat na muna ang mga kuwentong pambabae at salaysay ng lahi ng mga diyos ng mga Hentil; walang katapusan kung ilalahad ko ang lahat ng salinlahi at masasamang gawa ng mga tinatawag nilang diyos. Bilang halimbawa, iiwan ko ang iba at isasalaysay ko lamang ang masasamang gawa ng itinuturing nilang pinakadakila at pinuno—si Jupiter. Sinasabi nilang kanya ang langit dahil nakahihigit siya sa iba. Nang lumaki siya, pinakasalan niya ang sarili niyang kapatid na si Juno, at sa gawaing iyon ay agad niyang ipinakitang para siyang hayop. Ipinanganak ni Juno si Vulcan, ngunit ayon sa kuwento ay hindi si Jupiter ang ama. Gayunman, kay Jupiter ay naging ina rin siya ni Medea. Nang makarinig si Jupiter ng orakulo na ang magiging anak ni Medea ay magiging higit na makapangyarihan sa kanya at magtataboy sa kanya sa kaharian, kinuha at nilamon niya si Medea. Pagkatapos ay sinasabing iniluwal ni Jupiter si Minerva mula sa kanyang utak at si Bacchus mula sa kanyang hita. Nang umibig siya kay Thetis, sinabi raw ni Prometheus na ang magiging anak niya rito ay magiging higit na makapangyarihan sa ama; dahil dito, sa takot ay ibinigay niya si Thetis bilang asawa kay Peleus. Pagkatapos ay nakipagtalik siya kay Persephone, na sarili niyang anak kay Ceres, at mula rito ay isinilang si Dionysius, na piniraso ng mga Titan. Nang maisip niyang maaaring magkaroon pa ng anak ang kanyang amang si Saturno na higit na makapangyarihan at magtaboy sa kanya sa kaharian, nakidigma siya sa ama kasama ang kanyang mga kapatid na Titan. Nang matalo nila si Saturno, ikinulong niya ito, pinutol ang ari nito, at itinapon sa dagat. Ang dugong dumaloy mula sa sugat ay nahalo sa mga alon at naging bula sa patuloy na pag-ikot ng dagat; mula rito, anila, ipinanganak si Aphrodite, na tinatawag nating Venus. Mula sa pakikipagtalik ni Jupiter sa kanya, na ayon sa gayong salaysay ay sarili rin niyang kapatid, isinilang si Cypris, na sinasabing ina ni Cupid.

Kabanata 21. Isang Maitim na Talaan.

Iyan ang ilan sa kanyang mga pakikipagtalik sa sariling kamag-anak; ngayon naman ay ilalahad ko ang kanyang mga pangangalunya. Dinungisan niya si Europa, asawa ni Oceanus, at isinilang kay Jupiter si Dodonæus; si Helen, asawa ni Pandion, na nagkaanak kay Musæus; si Eurynome, asawa ni Asopus, na nagkaanak kay Ogygias; si Hermione, asawa ni Oceanus, na nagsilang sa mga Gracia—sina Thalia, Euphrosyne, at Aglaia; si Themis, sarili niyang kapatid, na nagsilang sa mga Hora—sina Eurynomia, Dice, at Irene; si Themisto, anak ni Inachus, na nagkaanak kay Arcas; si Idæa, anak ni Minos, na nagkaanak kay Asterion; si Phœnissa, anak ni Alphion, na nagkaanak kay Endymion; si Io, anak ni Inachus, na nagkaanak kay Epaphus; sina Hippodamia at Isione, mga anak ni Danaus, na ang una ay asawa ni Olenus at ang ikalawa ay asawa ni Orchomenus o Chryses; si Carme, anak ni Phœnix, na nagsilang kay Britomartis, kasama ni Diana; si Callisto, anak ni Lycaon, na nagkaanak kay Orcas; si Lybee, anak ni Munantius, na nagkaanak kay Belus; si Latona, na nagsilang kina Apollo at Diana; si Leandia, anak ni Eurymedon, na nagkaanak kay Coron; si Lysithea, anak ni Evenus, na nagkaanak kay Helenus; si Hippodamia, anak ni Bellerophon, na nagkaanak kay Sarpedon; si Megaclite, anak ni Macarius, na nagkaanak kina Thebe at Locrus; si Niobe, anak ni Phoroneus, na nagkaanak kina Argus at Pelasgus; si Olympias, anak ni Neoptolemus, na nagkaanak kay Alexander; si Pyrrha, anak ni Prometheus, na nagkaanak kay Helmetheus; sina Protogenia at Pandora, mga anak ni Deucalion, na nagkaanak kay Æthelius, Dorus, Melera, at Pandorus; si Thaicrucia, anak ni Proteus, na nagsilang kay Nympheus; si Salamis, anak ni Asopus, na nagkaanak kay Saracon; sina Taygete, Electra, Maia, at Plutide, mga anak ni Atlas, na ayon sa pagkakasunod ay nagkaanak kina Lacedæmon, Dardanus, Mercury, at Tantalus; si Phthia, anak ni Phoroneus, na nagkaanak kay Achæus; si Chonia, anak ni Aramnus, na nagkaanak kay Lacon; si Chalcea, isang nimpa, na nagsilang kay Olympus; si Charidia, isang nimpa, na nagkaanak kay Alcanus; si Chloris, asawa ni Ampycus, na nagsilang kay Mopsus; si Cotonia, anak ni Lesbus, na nagkaanak kay Polymedes; si Hippodamia, anak ni Anicetus; at si Chrysogenia, anak ni Peneus, na nagsilang kay Thissæus.

Kabanata 22. Mahalay na mga Pagbabagong-Anyo ni Jupiter.

May napakarami pa raw siyang pangangalunya na walang naging anak, at nakapapagod isa-isahin ang lahat. Ngunit sa ilan sa mga nabanggit, gumamit pa siya ng pagbabagong-anyo na parang salamangkero. Dinaya niya si Antiope, anak ni Nycteus, nang mag-anyong satyr, at isinilang sina Amphion at Zethus; si Alcmene, nang mag-anyong asawa nitong si Amphitryon, at isinilang si Hercules; si Ægina, anak ni Asopus, nang mag-anyong agila, at isinilang si Æacus. Sa anyong agila rin niya dinungisan si Ganymede, anak ni Dardanus; si Manthea, anak ni Phocus, nang mag-anyong oso, at isinilang si Arctos; si Danæ, anak ni Acrisius, nang mag-anyong ginto, at isinilang si Perseus; si Europa, anak ni Phœnix, nang mag-anyong toro, at isinilang sina Minos, Rhadamanthus, at Sarpedon; si Eurymedusa, anak ni Achelaus, nang mag-anyong langgam, at isinilang si Myrmidon; si Thalia, isang nimpa, nang mag-anyong buwitre, at isinilang ang mga Palisci sa Sicilia; si Imandra, anak ni Geneanus sa Rhodes, nang mag-anyong ulan; si Cassiopeia, nang mag-anyong asawa nitong si Phœnix, at isinilang si Anchinos; si Leda, anak ni Thestius, nang mag-anyong sisne, at isinilang si Helen; at muli sa parehong babae, nang mag-anyong bituin, at isinilang sina Castor at Pollux; si Lamia, nang mag-anyong lapwing; si Mnemosyne, nang mag-anyong pastol, at isinilang ang siyam na Musa; si Nemesis, nang mag-anyong gansa; at si Semele na Cadmian, nang mag-anyong apoy, at isinilang si Dionysius. Sa sarili niyang anak na si Ceres ay naging ama siya ni Persephone, at si Persephone mismo ay dinungisan din niya nang mag-anyong dragon.

Kabanata 23. Bakit Isang Diyos?

Nangalunya rin siya kay Europa, asawa ng sarili niyang tiyuhing si Oceanus, at sa kapatid nitong si Eurynome, at pinarusahan pa ang kanilang ama. Nangalunya siya kay Plute, anak ng sarili niyang anak na si Atlas, at pagkatapos ay hinatulan si Tantalus na isinilang nito sa kanya. Kay Larisse, anak ni Orchomenus, naging ama siya ni Tityon, na kanya ring pinarusahan. Inagaw niya si Dia, asawa ng sarili niyang anak na si Ixion, at pagkatapos ay ipinailalim si Ixion sa walang-hanggang kaparusahan. Halos lahat ng mga anak na isinilang mula sa kanyang mga pangangalunya ay inilagay niya sa mararahas na kamatayan; at kilalang-kilala pa ang mga libingan ng marami sa kanila. Oo, maging ang libingan ng mismong mamamatay-sa-magulang na ito—na sumira sa kanyang mga tiyuhin, dumungis sa kanilang mga asawa, nakipagtalik sa sarili niyang mga kapatid, at gumamit ng napakaraming mahikang pagbabagong-anyo—ay itinuturo sa Creta. Gayunman, bagaman alam at hayagang isinasalaysay ng mga Cretano ang kanyang karumal-dumal at incestuosong mga gawa, hindi sila nahihiyang tawagin siyang diyos. Dahil dito, lubha akong namamangha kung paanong ang taong higit sa lahat sa kasamaan at krimen ay nakatanggap ng banal at mabuting pangalang higit sa lahat ng pangalan, at tinawag na ama ng mga diyos at mga tao. Marahil yaong nalulugod sa kasamaan ng tao ang humikayat sa mga sawimpalad na kaluluwa na bigyan ng pinakamataas na karangalan ang nakita niyang higit sa lahat sa krimen, upang mahikayat ang lahat na tularan ang masasamang gawa nito.

Kabanata 24. Kahangalan ng Politeismo.

Maging ang mga libingan ng kanyang mga anak na itinuturing ng mga Hentil na mga diyos ay hayagang itinuturo sa iba't ibang lugar: ang kay Mercury sa Hermopolis; ang kay Venus ng Cyprus sa Cyprus; ang kay Mars sa Thrace; ang kay Bacchus sa Thebes, kung saan sinasabing piniraso siya; ang kay Hercules sa Tiro, kung saan siya sinunog; at ang kay Æsculapius sa Epidaurus. Ang lahat ng mga ito ay inilalarawan hindi lamang bilang mga taong namatay kundi bilang masasamang taong pinarusahan dahil sa kanilang mga krimen; gayunman, sinasamba pa rin sila bilang mga diyos ng mga hangal na tao.

Kabanata 25. Mga Patay na Taong Ginawang Diyos.

Kung makikipagtalo sila at sasabihing ang mga lugar na ito ay mga lugar ng kanilang kapanganakan at hindi ng kanilang libing o kamatayan, mapatutunayan pa rin ang mga sinaunang gawa mula sa mas malapit at kamakailang mga halimbawa. Sapagkat naipakita nating sinasamba nila yaong mga taong sila mismo ang umaaming dating mga tao at namatay—o sa halip, pinarusahan: sinasamba ng mga taga-Siria si Adonis; ng mga Ehipsiyo si Osiris; ng mga taga-Troya si Hector; si Achilles ay sinasamba sa Leuconesus, si Patroclus sa Pontus, at si Alejandro ng Macedonia sa Rhodes. Marami pang iba ang sinasamba sa iba't ibang lugar, bagaman walang alinlangan sa kanilang sarili na ang mga iyon ay mga taong namatay. Kaya malinaw na ang kanilang mga ninuno, nahulog sa parehong kamalian, ay nagbigay rin ng banal na karangalan sa mga patay na tao na marahil ay nagkaroon lamang ng kapangyarihan o natatanging kakayahan—lalo na kung napahanga nila ang mga mapurol ang isip sa pamamagitan ng mahikang mga panlilinlang.

Kabanata 26. Mga Pagbabagong-Anyo.

Dagdag pa rito, pinalalamutian ng mga makata ang mga kasinungalingan ng kamalian sa pamamagitan ng kagandahan at tamis ng kanilang pananalita. Hinihikayat nilang maniwala ang mga tao na ang mga mortal ay ginawang imortal; higit pa, sinasabi nilang ang mga tao ay naging mga bituin, puno, hayop, bulaklak, ibon, bukal, at ilog. Kung hindi lamang sayang sa mga salita ay maaari kong isa-isahin halos lahat ng bituin, puno, bukal, at ilog na sinasabi nilang dating mga tao. Ngunit bilang halimbawa ay babanggit ako ng tig-isa sa bawat uri. Sinasabi nilang si Andromeda, anak ni Cepheus, ay naging isang bituin; si Daphne, anak ng ilog na Lado, ay naging isang puno; si Hyacinthus, minamahal ni Apollo, ay naging isang bulaklak; si Callisto ay naging konstelasyong tinatawag nilang Arctos; sina Progne at Philomela, kasama si Tereus, ay naging mga ibon; si Thysbe sa Cilicia ay natunaw at naging isang bukal; at si Pyramus sa parehong lugar ay naging isang ilog. Sinasabi pa nilang halos lahat ng mga bituin, puno, bukal, ilog, bulaklak, hayop, at ibon ay dating mga tao.

Kabanata 27. Hindi Pagkakatugma ng mga Politeista.

Nang marinig ito ni Pedro, sinabi niya: Kung gayon, ayon sa kanila, bago gawing mga bituin ang mga tao at bago magkaroon ng iba pang mga pagbabagong binanggit mo, walang mga bituin ang langit, walang mga puno at hayop ang lupa, at wala ring mga bukal, ilog, o ibon. Kung wala ang mga ito, paano nabuhay ang mismong mga taong pagkatapos ay sinasabing naging mga iyon, gayong maliwanag na hindi mabubuhay ang tao sa lupa kung wala ang mga gayong bagay? Sumagot ako: Ngunit hindi man lamang nila kayang isagawa nang magkakatugma ang pagsamba sa sarili nilang mga diyos. Bawat diyos na sinasamba nila ay may isang bagay na inialay sa kanya at dapat iwasan ng kanyang mga mananamba: ang olibo kay Minerva, ang babaing kambing kay Jupiter, mga binhi kay Ceres, alak kay Bacchus, tubig kay Osiris, lalaking tupa kay Hammon, usa kay Diana, isda at kalapati sa demonyo ng mga taga-Siria, at apoy kay Vulcan. Sa bawat isa ay may isang bagay na sadyang inihandog, kaya dapat iwasan iyon ng mga mananamba bilang paggalang sa diyos na kinauukulan. Ngunit kung iiwas ang isa sa isang bagay at ang isa naman sa iba, sa pagpaparangal nila sa isang diyos ay mapupukaw nila ang galit ng lahat ng iba. Kaya kung nais nilang bigyang-kasiyahan ang lahat, kailangan nilang umiwas sa lahat ng bagay bilang parangal sa lahat—at sa gayon, bago pa dumating ang araw ng paghuhukom, hahatulan na nila ang kanilang sarili at mamamatay sa pinakakaawa-awang paraan sa gutom.

Kabanata 28. Mga Sandigan ng Paganismo.

Ngunit bumalik tayo sa paksa. Anong katwiran—o sa halip, anong kabaliwan—ang sumasakop sa isip ng mga tao upang sambahin at igalang bilang diyos ang isang taong alam nilang di-banal, masama, lapastangan—si Jupiter—incestuoso, mamamatay-sa-magulang, at mangangalunya, at hayagan pa nilang inaawit ang mga bagay na ito tungkol sa kanya sa mga teatro? Kung naging diyos siya dahil sa mga gayong gawa, kung gayon tuwing makaririnig sila ng isang mamamatay-tao, mangangalunya, mamamatay-sa-magulang, o taong nakikipagtalik sa sariling kamag-anak, dapat din nilang sambahin ang mga iyon bilang mga diyos. Hindi ko maunawaan kung bakit pinararangalan nila kay Jupiter ang mismong kinamumuhian nila sa ibang tao. Sumagot si Pedro: Yamang sinasabi mong hindi mo nauunawaan, alamin mo sa akin kung bakit nila pinararangalan ang kasamaan sa kanya. Una, upang kapag ginagawa nila mismo ang gayong mga gawa ay maisip nilang magiging kalugud-lugod sila sa kanya sapagkat tinularan lamang nila ang kanyang kasamaan. Ikalawa, sapagkat ang mga sinaunang tao ay iniwan ang mga kuwentong ito na mahusay ang pagkakasulat at eleganteng nakatanim sa kanilang mga tula. Dahil sa edukasyong ibinibigay sa kabataan, kumakapit ang kaalaman sa mga bagay na ito sa malambot at payak nilang isip, kaya napakahirap na itong bunutin at itapon.

Kabanata 29. Mga Alegorya.

Nang masabi ito ni Pedro, sumagot si Niceta: Huwag mong isipin, panginoon kong Pedro, na walang mga tila makatwirang argumento ang mga edukadong Hentil upang ipagtanggol ang mga bagay na waring kahiya-hiya at kasuklam-suklam. Hindi ko ito sinasabi upang patibayin ang kanilang kamalian—malayo nawa iyon sa aking isip—ngunit alam kong may ilang mas matatalino sa kanila na gumagamit ng mga paliwanag upang suportahan at pagandahin ang mga tila walang katuturan. Kung mamarapatin mong ilahad ko ang ilan sa mga iyon, sapagkat medyo pamilyar ako sa kanila, gagawin ko ayon sa iyong utos. Nang pahintulutan siya ni Pedro, nagpatuloy si Niceta.

Kabanata 30. Kosmogoniya ni Orpheus.

Ang panitikang Griyego tungkol sa pinagmulan ng mga sinaunang bagay ay umaasa sa maraming awtoridad, ngunit lalo na sa dalawa—sina Orpheus at Hesiod. Ang kanilang mga sulat ay may dalawang paraan ng pag-unawa: literal at alegorikal. Ang karaniwang mga tao ay kumakapit sa literal, samantalang ginagamit ng mga pilosopo at mga edukado ang buong husay ng kanilang pananalita upang hangaan ang alegorikal. Sinasabi ni Orpheus na sa pasimula ay may chaos—walang hanggan, walang hangganan, hindi nilikha—at mula rito ginawa ang lahat. Ayon sa kanya, ang chaos na ito ay hindi dilim ni liwanag, hindi basa ni tuyo, hindi mainit ni malamig, kundi lahat ng bagay na magkakahalo at isang walang hugis na masa. Sa wakas, gaya ng isang napakalaking itlog, iniluwal nito mula sa sarili ang isang dobleng anyong nabuo sa napakahabang panahon, ang tinatawag nilang lalaki-babae, isang anyong binubuo ng pagsasanib ng magkasalungat na mga katangian. Ito raw ang pasimula ng lahat, nagmula sa dalisay na sangkap; at nang lumabas ito, pinaghiwalay nito ang apat na elemento. Mula sa dalawang una—apoy at hangin—ginawa ang langit; mula sa iba—lupa at tubig—ginawa ang lupa. Mula sa pakikibahagi ng mga elementong ito sa isa't isa, anila, nagmumula at nabubuo ang lahat ng bagay. Iyan ang paliwanag ni Orpheus.

Kabanata 31. Kosmogoniya ni Hesiod.

Idinagdag naman ni Hesiod na pagkatapos ng chaos ay agad na nabuo ang langit at lupa, at mula sa mga ito ay nagmula ang labing-isa—at kung minsan ay labindalawa—na binubuo ng anim na lalaki at limang babae. Ang mga lalaki ay sina Oceanus, Cœus, Crius, Hyperion, Iapetus, at Chronos, na tinatawag ding Saturno. Ang mga babae ay sina Theia, Rhea, Themis, Mnemosyne, at Tethys. Ganito raw ang alegorikal na kahulugan ng mga pangalan. Sinasabi nilang labing-isa o labindalawa ang bilang: ang una ay ang kalikasan mismo, na tinatawag din nilang Rhea mula sa “pagdaloy”; ang iba pang sampu ay mga katangian o aksidente nito. Nagdaragdag pa sila ng ikalabindalawa, si Chronos, na tinatawag nating Saturno at itinuturing nilang panahon. Kaya ang Saturno at Rhea, ayon sa kanila, ay panahon at sangkap; at kapag nahalo ang mga ito sa basa at tuyo, init at lamig, nalilikha ang lahat ng bagay.

Kabanata 32. Alegorikal na Pagpapakahulugan.

Sinasabi nilang si Rhea, o kalikasan, ay lumikha ng waring isang bula na matagal na naipon. Unti-unti itong nabuo mula sa espiritung nasa tubig, namaga, at sa loob ng isang panahon ay dinala sa ibabaw ng sangkap na waring sinapupunan nito. Pinatigas ito ng matinding lamig at patuloy na lumaki dahil sa mga dagdag na yelo. Sa wakas ay naputol ito, lumubog sa kailaliman, at dahil sa sarili nitong bigat ay bumaba sa mga rehiyon sa ilalim ng lupa. Dahil hindi na ito nakikita, tinawag itong Aides, na tinatawag ding Orcus o Pluto. Nang lumubog ito mula sa itaas hanggang sa ibaba, nabigyan ng puwang ang tubig na magsama-sama; at nang umurong ang tubig ay lumitaw ang mas mabigat na bahagi, ang lupa. Kaya ang paglaya ng tubig na dati raw pinipigilan ng presensiya ng bula ay tinawag nilang Neptune matapos maabot ng bula ang pinakamababang lugar. Pagkatapos, nang hilahin pababa ng nabuo at nagyelong bula ang malamig na elemento at mapaghiwalay ang tuyo at basa, wala nang hadlang kaya ang mainit na elemento, dahil sa lakas at gaan nito, ay mabilis na umakyat sa mataas na bahagi ng hangin, dinadala ng hangin at bagyo. Ang bagyong ito, na sa Griyego ay tinatawag na καταιγίς, ay tinawag nilang ægis—isang babaing kambing; at ang apoy na umakyat sa itaas ay tinawag nilang Jupiter. Kaya sinasabi nilang umakyat siya sa Olympus na nakasakay sa isang kambing.

Kabanata 33. Alegorya ni Jupiter, at Iba Pa.

Nais ng mga Griyego na ang pangalang Jupiter ay iugnay sa pamumuhay o pagbibigay-buhay, samantalang sa atin ay sa pagbibigay-tulong. Sinasabi nilang siya ang buhay na sangkap na nasa matataas na rehiyon at humihila sa lahat patungo sa sarili sa impluwensiya ng init, gaya ng pagkakabaluktot ng utak, at nagsasaayos sa mga iyon sa pamamagitan ng wastong pagtitimpla. Kaya sinasabing mula sa kanyang ulo ay lumabas ang karunungan na tinatawag nilang Minerva, na tinawag ng mga Griyego na ᾿Αθήνη dahil sa kawalang-kamatayan. Yamang nilikha ng ama ng lahat ang lahat ng bagay sa pamamagitan ng kanyang karunungan, sinasabi ring mula sa kanyang ulo at mula sa pangunahing bahagi ng lahat lumabas si Minerva, at na sa pamamagitan ng maayos na paghahalo ng mga elemento ay hinubog at pinalamutian niya ang buong sanlibutan. Kaya ang mga anyong inukit sa sangkap upang mabuo ang sanlibutan, dahil pinananatili ng lakas ng init, ay sinasabing pinagsasama-sama ng kapangyarihan ni Jupiter. At yamang sapat na ang mga anyong ito at wala nang kailangang bagong idagdag sa kanila, kundi ang bawat bagay ay napapanibago sa pamamagitan ng sarili nitong binhi, sinasabing iginapos ni Jupiter ang mga kamay ni Saturno; sapagkat ang panahon ay hindi na gumagawa ng anumang bagong bagay mula sa sangkap, ngunit ang init ng mga binhi ang nagpapanibago sa lahat ayon sa kani-kanilang uri. Wala nang bagong kapanganakan ni Rhea—walang dagdag na umaakyat mula sa dumadaloy na sangkap. Kaya tinatawag nilang pagputol kay Saturno ang unang paghahating ito ng mga elemento, sapagkat hindi na siya makalilikha ng isa pang sanlibutan.

Kabanata 34. Iba Pang mga Alegorya.

Ganito naman ang alegorya nila tungkol kay Venus. Nang, ayon sa kanila, mailagay ang dagat sa ilalim ng hangin at ang liwanag ng langit ay lalong gumanda dahil sa pagkakasilaw nito sa tubig, ang kagandahan ng mga bagay na waring higit na kaaya-aya mula sa tubig ay tinawag na Venus. Nang ang kagandahang ito ay makiisa sa hangin, na waring sarili nitong kapatid, upang makalikha ng kagandahang magiging bagay ng pagnanasa, sinabing nanganak ito kay Cupid. Sa ganitong paraan, itinuturo nilang alegorikal na si Chronos o Saturno ay panahon; si Rhea ay sangkap; si Aides o Orcus ay ang kalaliman sa ilalim ng lupa; si Neptune ay tubig; si Jupiter ay hangin, samakatuwid ang mainit na elemento; si Venus ay kagandahan ng mga bagay; at si Cupid ay pagnanasa na nasa lahat ng bagay at siyang nagpapalaganap ng mga salinlahi, o maging ang katwiran ng mga bagay na nagbibigay ng kasiyahan kapag sinusuri nang may karunungan. Si Hera o Juno ay sinasabing gitnang hangin na bumababa mula sa langit patungo sa lupa. Kay Diana, na tinatawag nilang Proserpine, ibinibigay nila ang hangin sa ibaba. Sinasabi nilang si Apollo ang araw mismo na umiikot sa langit; si Mercury ay pananalita, na sa pamamagitan nito ay naibibigay ang katwiran para sa bawat bagay; at si Mars ay walang-pigil na apoy na tumutupok sa lahat. Upang hindi kayo antalahin sa mahabang pagbilang, yaong mga nag-aakalang may mas malalim silang pagkaunawa sa mga bagay na ito ay nag-aakalang nakapagbibigay sila ng maganda at makatarungang paliwanag sa paglalapat ng ganitong uri ng alegorya sa bawat bagay na sinasamba nila.

Kabanata 35. Kawalang-Pakinabang ng mga Alegoryang Ito.

Nang masabi ito ni Niceta, sumagot si Aquila: Sinuman ang may-akda at imbentor ng mga bagay na ito, sa palagay ko'y napakalapastangan niya. Tinakpan niya ang mga bagay na tila kaaya-aya at marangal at ginawang binubuo ang ritwal ng kanilang pamahiin ng mababa at kahiya-hiyang mga gawain. Sapagkat ang mga nakasulat ayon sa literal na kahulugan ay hayagang di-marangal at kahiya-hiya; at ang buong pagsasagawa ng kanilang relihiyon ay nagiging pagtuturo sa mga tao na tularan sa mga krimen at kawalang-galang ang mga diyos na kanilang sinasamba. Ano naman ang pakinabang ng mga alegoryang ito? Kahit inayos ang mga iyon upang magmukhang marangal, wala naman silang naitutulong sa pagsamba o sa pagtutuwid ng asal.

Kabanata 36. Ang mga Alegorya ay Huling Paliwanag Lamang.

Dahil dito ay lalo pang maliwanag na nang makita ng matatalinong tao kung gaano kahiya-hiya at mababa ang karaniwang pamahiin, at yamang wala silang natutuhang paraan upang ituwid ito o tunay na kaalaman tungkol dito, sinikap nilang takpan sa pamamagitan ng anumang argumento at paliwanag ang mga di-marangal na bagay sa marangal na pananalita. Hindi, gaya ng kanilang sinasabi, na nagtago ang mga unang may-akda ng marangal na katwiran sa ilalim ng kahiya-hiyang mga pabula. Kung gayon nga ang nangyari, tiyak na hindi sana nila ginawa ang kanilang mga estatwa at larawan na may mga paglalarawan ng mga bisyo at krimen. Hindi sana inilalarawan ang sisneng nangalunya kay Leda, ni ang torong nangalunya kay Europa; ni gagawing libu-libong dambuhalang anyo ang itinuturing nilang higit sa lahat. At kung talagang alam ng mga dakila at marurunong sa kanila na pawang kathang-isip lamang ang mga ito, hindi ba nila ituturing na lapastangan at mapanirang-puri sa mga diyos ang sinumang gumawa ng gayong larawan o inukit na anyo? Bilang pagsubok, ipakita nila ang isang hari sa sarili nilang panahon sa anyo ng baka, gansa, langgam, o buwitre; isulat nila ang pangalan ng hari rito at itayo ang gayong estatwa sa isang pampublikong lugar. Mabilis nilang malalaman kung gaano kalaking insulto ang ginawa nila at kung gaano kabigat ang parusa nito.

Kabanata 37. Kung Gaya ng mga Diyos, Gaya Rin ang mga Mananamba.

Ngunit yamang ang mga karumal-dumal na kuwentong pinatutunayan ng pampublikong pagsamba ang higit na tunay, at ang mga pagtatakip lamang ang inimbento ng mga matatalino upang bigyang-mukhang marangal ang mga iyon, hindi lamang hindi ipinagbabawal ang mga gayong bagay kundi inilalabas pa sa mismong mga misteryo ang mga larawan ni Saturnong nilalamon ang kanyang mga anak at ng batang itinago sa ingay ng mga simbalo at tambol ng mga Corybante. At tungkol sa pagkaputol kay Saturno, ano pang mas matibay na patunay ang maaari nilang ibigay kaysa sa katotohanang maging ang kanyang mga mananamba ay pinuputulan din sa gayunding kaawa-awang kapalaran bilang parangal sa kanilang diyos? Yamang hayagang nakikita ang mga ito, sino ang magiging gayon kawalang-isip at katigas upang hindi maunawaang ang mga bagay na isinasalaysay tungkol sa mga kaawa-awang diyos ay pinatutunayan ng higit pang kaawa-awang mga mananamba sa mismong sugat at pagputol sa kanilang mga katawan?

Kabanata 38. Mga Sulat ng mga Makata.

Ngunit kung, gaya ng sinasabi nila, ang mga kagalang-galang at banal na katotohanang ito ay isinasagawa sa pamamagitan ng gayong kahiya-hiya at lapastangang ritwal, tiyak na lapastangan ang sinumang unang naglabas ng mga ritwal na iyon o patuloy na nagsasagawa ng mga iyon ngayong naipalaganap na. At ano ang sasabihin natin tungkol sa mga aklat ng mga makata? Kung tunay na pinababa nila ang marangal at banal na gawa ng mga diyos sa pamamagitan ng mabababang pabula, hindi ba dapat agad sunugin ang mga aklat na iyon upang hindi mahikayat ang murang isip ng mga bata na maniwalang si Jupiter mismo, pinuno ng mga diyos, ay mamamatay-sa-magulang, nakikipagtalik sa sariling mga kapatid at anak na babae, at marumi maging sa mga batang lalaki; na sina Venus at Mars ay mga mangangalunya; at lahat ng iba pang bagay na nabanggit na natin? Ano ang palagay mo rito, panginoon kong Pedro?

Kabanata 39. Lahat ay Para sa Pinakamabuti.

Sumagot siya: Makatitiyak ka, minamahal na Aquila, na ang lahat ng bagay ay inaayos ng mabuting providensiya ng Diyos upang ang adhikaing sasalungat sa katotohanan ay hindi lamang mahina kundi mababa rin. Sapagkat kung naging higit na malakas at waring mas makatotohanan ang pahayag ng kamalian, ang nalinlang nito ay hindi madaling makababalik sa landas ng katotohanan. Kung ngayon pa lamang, kahit napakaraming masama at kahiya-hiyang bagay ang isinasalaysay tungkol sa mga diyos ng mga Hentil, kakaunti pa rin ang tumatalikod sa mababang kamalian, gaano pa kaya kung may anumang marangal at waring totoo rito? Mahirap ilipat ang isip mula sa mga bagay na naitanim dito sa murang kabataan. Kaya, gaya ng sinabi ko, ipinahintulot ng banal na providensiya na ang nilalaman ng kamalian ay kapwa mahina at mababa. At ang lahat ng iba pang bagay ay maayos at kapaki-pakinabang ding pinangangasiwaan ng banal na providensiya, bagaman hindi malinaw sa atin—na hindi nakaaalam sa mga sanhi ng mga bagay—kung paano ang mabuti at pinakamahusay na paraan ng banal na pamamahala.

Kabanata 40. Humingi ng Karagdagang Paliwanag.

Pagkatapos sabihin ito ni Pedro, hiniling ko kay Niceta na alang-alang sa aming pagkatuto ay ipaliwanag niya ang ilang bagay tungkol sa mga alegorya ng mga Hentil na masusi niyang pinag-aralan. Sapagkat, sabi ko, kapaki-pakinabang na kapag nakikipagtalo tayo sa mga Hentil ay hindi tayo mangmang sa mga bagay na ito. Sinabi ni Niceta: Kung pahihintulutan ako ng panginoon kong Pedro, magagawa ko ang hinihiling mo. Sinabi ni Pedro: Ngayong araw ay binigyan ko kayo ng pahintulot na magsalita laban sa mga Hentil, gaya ng alam ninyo. Sinabi ni Niceta: Sabihin mo kung gayon, Clemente, ano ang nais mong ipaliwanag ko. Sinabi ko: Ipaliwanag mo kung paano inuunawa ng mga Hentil ang piging ng mga diyos sa kasal nina Peleus at Thetis. Ano raw ang kahulugan ng pastol na si Paris, at nina Juno, Minerva, at Venus na sa kanila siya naging tagahatol? Ano ang kahulugan ni Mercury? Ano ang mansanas, at ang iba pang kasunod na mga bagay?

Kabanata 41. Paliwanag sa Mitolohiya.

Sinabi ni Niceta: Ganito nila ipinapaliwanag ang piging ng mga diyos. Sinasabi nilang ang piging ay ang sanlibutan at ang pagkakaayos ng mga diyos sa hapag ay ang posisyon ng mga bagay sa langit. Yaong tinatawag ni Hesiod na mga unang anak ng langit at lupa—anim na lalaki at anim na babae—ay iniuugnay nila sa labindalawang tanda na umiikot sa buong sanlibutan. Ang mga putahe sa piging ay ang mga katwiran at sanhi ng mga bagay, matatamis at kanais-nais, na sa anyo ng mga hinuha mula sa mga posisyon ng mga tanda at galaw ng mga bituin ay nagpapaliwanag kung paano pinamamahalaan ang sanlibutan. Gayunman, sinasabi nilang ang mga bagay na ito ay malaya gaya ng isang piging, sapagkat nasa pagpili ng isip ng bawat tao kung titikim siya sa ganitong uri ng kaalaman o iiwas. Kung paanong sa piging walang pinipilit at malaya ang bawat isa kung kakain, gayundin ang pamimilosopo ay nakasalalay sa pagpili ng kalooban. Ang alitan, ayon sa kanila, ay ang pita ng laman na sumasalungat sa layunin ng isip at humahadlang sa pagnanais na mamilosopo; kaya sinasabi nilang kasal ang pagkakataong iyon. Ginagawa nilang sina Peleus at nimpa na si Thetis ang tuyo at basang elemento na sa paghahalo ay bumubuo sa sangkap ng mga katawan. Si Mercury ay pananalita na naghahatid ng pagtuturo sa isip; si Juno ay kalinisan; si Minerva ay katapangan; si Venus ay pita; at si Paris ay pag-unawa. Kaya kung ang pag-unawa ng isang tao ay barbaro, hindi nahubog, at walang wastong paghatol, hahamakin niya ang kalinisan at katapangan at ibibigay ang gantimpala—ang mansanas—sa pita. Dahil dito, ang kapahamakan ay darating hindi lamang sa kanya kundi sa kanyang mga kababayan at buong lahi. Maaari nilang buuin ang gayong alegorya mula sa anumang materyal na gustuhin nila, ngunit maaari rin itong iangkop sa bawat tao. Kung ang isang tao ay may isip na parang pastol—payak, di-nahubog, at ayaw maturuan—kapag iminungkahi ng init ng katawan ang kaluguran ng pita, agad niyang hahamakin ang mga birtud ng pag-aaral at ang mga pagpapala ng kaalaman at ibabaling ang isip sa mga kaluguran ng katawan. Mula rito nagmumula ang mga di-mapigil na digmaan, pagkawasak ng mga lungsod, at pagbagsak ng mga bansa, kung paanong sa pagdukot kay Helen ay inarmasan ni Paris ang mga Griyego at barbaro tungo sa kapwa nila pagkawasak.

Kabanata 42. Pagpapakahulugan sa Kasulatan.

Pinuri ni Pedro ang kanyang sinabi at nagwika: Napapansin kong ang mga taong maparaan ang isip ay nakakukuha ng maraming waring makatwirang kahulugan mula sa mga bagay na binabasa nila. Kaya kailangang maging lubhang maingat na kapag binabasa ang kautusan ng Diyos ay huwag itong basahin ayon lamang sa nauna nang pagkaunawa ng sarili nating isip. Maraming pahayag sa banal na Kasulatan ang maaaring hilahin patungo sa kahulugang nauna nang inakala ng isang tao, ngunit hindi dapat gawin iyon. Hindi mo dapat hanapin ang isang banyaga at panlabas na kahulugang dinala mo mula sa labas upang saka mo lamang patunayan sa pamamagitan ng awtoridad ng Kasulatan. Sa halip, kunin mo sa mismong Kasulatan ang kahulugan ng katotohanan. Kaya nararapat mong matutuhan ang kahulugan ng Kasulatan mula sa taong nag-iingat nito ayon sa katotohanang ipinasa sa kanya mula sa kanyang mga ninuno, upang maipahayag niya nang may awtoridad ang wastong tinanggap niya. Ngunit kapag natanggap na ng isang tao mula sa Kasulatan ang buo at matatag na tuntunin ng katotohanan, hindi masama kung gamitin niya sa pagpapatibay ng tunay na aral ang anumang natutuhan niya mula sa karaniwang edukasyon at malalayang pag-aaral na maaaring pinagkaabalahan niya mula pagkabata—ngunit sa kundisyong, kapag natutuhan na niya ang katotohanan, itakwil niya ang kasinungalingan at pagkukunwari.

Kabanata 43. Isang Salita ng Panghihikayat.

Pagkasabi nito, tumingin siya sa aming ama at nagsabi: Ikaw kung gayon, matanda, kung tunay mong pinahahalagahan ang kaligtasan ng iyong kaluluwa—upang kapag dumating ang paghihiwalay nito sa katawan, dahil sa maikling pagbabagong-loob na ito ay matagpuan nito ang walang-hanggang kapahingahan—magtanong ka ng anumang nais mo at humingi ng payo, upang maalis mo ang anumang pag-aalinlangang natitira sa iyo. Sapagkat maging sa kabataan ay hindi tiyak ang haba ng buhay; ngunit sa matatanda ay hindi na ito isang bagay na di-tiyak, sapagkat walang alinlangang kaunting panahon na lamang ang natitira sa kanila. Kaya kapwa bata at matanda ay dapat maging lubhang masigasig sa pagbabagong-loob at pagsisisi, at paglaanan ng buong pansin ang pagpapalamuti sa kanilang mga kaluluwa para sa hinaharap ng mga pinakamahalagang palamuti: mga aral ng katotohanan, biyaya ng kalinisan, ningning ng katuwiran, kagandahan ng kabanalan, at lahat ng iba pang bagay na nararapat ipalamuti sa isang makatwirang isip. Bukod dito, dapat silang humiwalay sa di-angkop at di-sumasampalatayang mga kasamahan, makisama sa mga tapat, at palaging dumalo sa mga pagtitipon kung saan tinatalakay ang kalinisan, katuwiran, at kabanalan. Dapat silang laging manalangin sa Diyos nang buong puso at hilingin sa Kanya ang mga bagay na nararapat hilingin sa Diyos; magpasalamat sa Kanya; tunay na magsisi sa mga nagawa nila noon; at, hangga't maaari, tulungan ang kanilang pagsisisi sa pamamagitan ng mga gawa ng awa para sa mga dukha. Sa ganitong paraan ay higit na madaling ipagkakaloob ang kapatawaran at higit na mabilis na maipakikita ang awa sa mga maawain.

Kabanata 44. Kasigasigan.

Ngunit kung ang lumalapit sa pagsisisi ay matanda na, lalo siyang dapat magpasalamat sa Diyos; sapagkat matapos niyang matanggap ang kaalaman ng katotohanan at mahina na ang tindi ng pita ng laman, hindi na naghihintay sa kanya ang gayunding matinding pakikipaglaban upang supilin ang mga kaluguran ng katawan na sumasalungat sa isip. Ang natitira sa kanya ay magsanay sa pagkatuto ng katotohanan at sa mga gawa ng awa upang magbunga siya ng mga bungang karapat-dapat sa pagsisisi. Huwag niyang isipin na ang patunay ng pagbabagong-loob ay nasa haba ng panahon, kundi nasa lakas ng debosyon at layunin. Sapagkat lantad sa Diyos ang mga isip; hindi Niya binibilang ang mga panahon kundi tinitingnan ang mga puso. Kinalulugdan Niya ang taong, sa sandaling marinig ang pangangaral ng katotohanan, ay hindi nagpapaliban o nag-aaksaya ng panahon sa kapabayaan, kundi agad—na para bang sa mismong sandaling iyon—ay kinamumuhian ang nakaraan, nagsisimulang hangarin ang mga bagay na darating, at nag-aalab sa pag-ibig sa kaharian ng langit.

Kabanata 45. Lahat ay Dapat Magsisi.

Kaya huwag nang magkunwari ang sinuman sa inyo o tumingin pabalik, kundi kusang lumapit sa Ebanghelyo ng kaharian ng Diyos. Huwag sabihin ng dukha, “Kapag yumaman na ako, saka ako magbabago.” Hindi salapi ang hinihingi sa iyo ng Diyos kundi maawaing puso at maka-Diyos na isip. Huwag ding ipagpaliban ng mayaman ang kanyang pagbabagong-loob dahil sa mga alalahanin ng sanlibutan habang iniisip kung paano niya ilalagay ang kasaganaan ng kanyang mga ani. Huwag niyang sabihin sa sarili, “Ano ang gagawin ko? Saan ko ilalagay ang aking mga ani?” Ni huwag niyang sabihin sa kanyang kaluluwa, “Marami kang mabuting bagay na nakaimbak para sa maraming taon; magpakasaya ka at magdiwang.” Sapagkat sasabihin sa kanya, “Hangal, ngayong gabi ay kukunin sa iyo ang iyong kaluluwa; kanino mapupunta ang mga bagay na inihanda mo?” Kaya ang bawat edad, bawat kasarian, at bawat kalagayan ay magmadali sa pagsisisi upang makamtan ang buhay na walang hanggan. Magpasalamat ang mga kabataan na ipinapasailalim nila ang kanilang mga leeg sa pamatok ng disiplina sa gitna mismo ng tindi ng kanilang mga pagnanasa. Ang matatanda naman ay karapat-dapat ding purihin sapagkat, dahil sa takot sa Diyos, binabago nila ang matagal na kaugaliang matagal nilang ikinabuhay sa kapahamakan.

Kabanata 46. Ang Tiyak na Salita ng Propesiya.

Kaya huwag nang ipagpaliban ng sinuman. Huwag nang maghintay. Ano ang dahilan upang ipagpaliban ang paggawa ng mabuti? Natatakot ka bang matapos kang gumawa ng mabuti ay hindi mo matanggap ang gantimpalang inaasahan mo? Ano naman ang mawawala sa iyo kung gumawa ka ng mabuti kahit walang gantimpala? Hindi ba sapat na pakinabang na ang isang malinis na budhi? Ngunit kung matagpuan mong totoo ang iyong inaasahan, hindi ba tatanggap ka ng malalaki kapalit ng maliliit, at walang hanggan kapalit ng pansamantala? Sinasabi ko lamang ito alang-alang sa mga hindi sumasampalataya. Sapagkat ang mga bagay na ipinangangaral namin ay tunay na gaya ng ipinangangaral namin; hindi maaaring maging iba, yamang ipinangako ang mga iyon sa pamamagitan ng makahulang salita.

Kabanata 47. Isang Tapat na Salita at Karapat-dapat Tanggapin ng Lahat.

Ngunit kung may nagnanais na matutuhan nang ganap ang katotohanan ng aming pangangaral, lumapit siya upang makinig at alamin kung sino ang tunay na Propeta. Pagkatapos nito ay mawawala sa kanya ang lahat ng pag-aalinlangan, maliban kung sadyang matigas ang kanyang isip at nilalabanan niya ang mga bagay na natagpuan niyang totoo. Sapagkat may ilan na ang tanging layunin ay manalo sa anumang paraan at higit na naghahanap ng papuri kaysa ng sariling kaligtasan. Sa mga gayon ay hindi dapat mag-aksaya kahit isang salita, upang hindi kapwa mapinsala ang marangal na salita at mahulog sa hatol ng walang-hanggang kamatayan ang taong lumalapastangan dito. Ano ba ang mayroon sa aming pangangaral na dapat salungatin? Sa anong bagay ba nasasalungat ng aming salita ang tunay at marangal na paniniwala? Itinuturo nito na dapat parangalan ang Diyos Ama, ang Maylalang ng lahat, gayundin ang Kanyang Anak, na Siya lamang ang lubos na nakakikilala sa Kanya at sa Kanyang kalooban at Siya lamang ang dapat paniwalaan tungkol sa lahat ng Kanyang iniutos. Sapagkat Siya lamang ang kautusan at Tagapagbigay ng Kautusan, at ang matuwid na Hukom. Itinatakda ng Kanyang kautusan na ang Diyos, Panginoon ng lahat, ay parangalan sa pamamagitan ng mahinahon, malinis, matuwid, at maawaing pamumuhay, at na ang lahat ng pag-asa ay ilagak sa Kanya lamang.

Kabanata 48. Mga Kamalian ng mga Pilosopo.

Ngunit may magsasabing nagbibigay rin ang mga pilosopo ng mga gayong utos. Hindi gayon. Totoong nagbibigay sila ng mga tuntunin tungkol sa katuwiran at pagpipigil, ngunit hindi nila nalalaman na ang Diyos ang gumaganti sa mabuti at masasamang gawa. Kaya ang kanilang mga batas at tuntunin ay nakaiiwas lamang sa hayagang tagapagparatang, ngunit hindi nakapaglilinis ng budhi. Bakit matatakot ang isang tao na magkasala nang lihim kung hindi niya alam na may Saksi at Hukom ng mga lihim na bagay? Bukod dito, idinadagdag ng mga pilosopo sa kanilang mga utos na dapat parangalan maging ang mga diyos na sa katotohanan ay mga demonyo. Kahit tila karapat-dapat silang purihin sa ibang bagay, sapat na ang isang ito upang patunayang nahulog sila sa pinakakakila-kilabot na kawalang-kabanalan at hatulan sila ng sarili nilang pasiya: ipinahahayag nilang iisa ang Diyos ngunit iniuutos na sambahin ang marami upang pagbigyan ang kamalian ng tao. Sinasabi rin ng mga pilosopo na hindi nagagalit ang Diyos, nang hindi nila nauunawaan ang kanilang sinasabi. Masama ang galit kapag ginugulo nito ang isip at inaalis ang wastong paghatol. Ngunit ang galit na nagpaparusa sa masama ay hindi kaguluhan ng isip; ito ay, sa isang diwa, ang mismong matuwid na disposisyong nagbibigay ng gantimpala sa mabuti at kaparusahan sa masama. Sapagkat kung magkakaloob Siya ng magkakaparehong pagpapala sa mabuti at masama at pantay na gantimpala sa maka-Diyos at di-maka-Diyos, magmumukha Siyang di-makatarungan sa halip na mabuti.

Kabanata 49. Mahabang Pagtitiis ng Diyos.

Ngunit sasabihin mo, “Hindi rin dapat gumawa ng masama ang Diyos.” Tama ang sinasabi mo; at hindi Siya gumagawa ng masama. Subalit ang mga nilalang Niya, dahil hindi sila naniniwalang hahatulan sila, ay nagpapakasasa sa kanilang mga kaluguran at lumalayo sa kabanalan at katuwiran. Ngunit sasabihin mo, “Kung matuwid na parusahan ang masasama, dapat silang parusahan agad kapag gumagawa sila ng masama.” Mabuti ang pagmamadali mo; ngunit Siya ay walang hanggan, at walang nalilihim sa Kanya. Yamang walang wakas ang Kanyang pag-iral, gayon din kalawak ang Kanyang pagtitiis. Hindi Niya pangunahing hinahanap ang bilis ng paghihiganti kundi ang mga daan tungo sa kaligtasan. Sapagkat higit Siyang nalulugod sa pagbabagong-loob ng makasalanan kaysa sa pagkamatay nito. Ezekiel 18:33 Kaya ipinagkaloob Niya sa mga tao ang banal na bautismo. Kung ang isang tao ay nagmamadaling lumapit dito at mula noon ay nananatiling walang dungis, ang lahat ng kasalanang nagawa niya sa panahon ng kanyang kamangmangan ay napapawi.

Kabanata 50. Ang mga Pilosopo ay Hindi mga Tagapagbigay-Kabutihan sa Tao.

Ano ang naiambag ng mga pilosopo sa buhay ng tao sa pagsasabing hindi nagagalit ang Diyos sa mga tao? Wala kundi turuan silang huwag matakot sa anumang kaparusahan o paghuhukom, at sa gayon ay alisin ang pagpipigil sa mga makasalanan. Ano naman ang pakinabang na naidulot sa sangkatauhan ng mga nagsabing walang Diyos at ang lahat ay nangyayari lamang sa pagkakataon at aksidente? Wala kundi ang ganito: kapag narinig iyon ng mga tao at inakalang walang Hukom at walang Tagapag-ingat ng mga bagay, sumasalpok sila nang walang takot sa bawat gawaing idinidikta ng galit, kasakiman, o pita. Malaki nga raw ang naitulong sa buhay ng tao ng mga nagsabing walang nangyayari hiwalay sa genesis! Sa gayon, maibibintang ng bawat isa sa genesis ang sanhi ng kanyang kasalanan at, sa gitna ng kanyang mga krimen, maipahahayag niyang inosente siya; hindi niya hinuhugasan ang kanyang sala sa pamamagitan ng pagsisisi kundi dinodoble pa iyon sa pagsisi sa kapalaran. At ano ang masasabi ko tungkol sa mga pilosopong nagtuturo na dapat sambahin ang mga diyos—ang mga diyos na katatapos lamang ilarawan sa inyo? Ano pa iyon kundi utos na sambahin ang mga bisyo, krimen, at mababang gawa? Nahihiya ako para sa iyo at nahahabag ako sa iyo kung hindi mo pa natuklasang ang mga bagay na ito ay hindi karapat-dapat paniwalaan, di-maka-Diyos, at kasuklam-suklam; o kung natuklasan mo nang masama ang mga iyon ngunit sinamba mo pa rin na para bang mabuti—oo, pinakamabuti.

Kabanata 51. Si Cristo ang Tunay na Propeta.

Bukod dito, anong uri ng kapangahasan ang ginawa ng ilang pilosopo nang magsalita sila tungkol mismo sa Diyos, gayong sila ay mga mortal at tungkol sa mga bagay na di-nakikita ay opinyon lamang ang kaya nilang ibigay? Paano sila makapagsasalita tungkol sa pinagmulan ng sanlibutan gayong wala sila nang ito'y likhain, o tungkol sa wakas nito, o tungkol sa magiging kalagayan at paghuhukom sa mga kaluluwa sa mga rehiyon sa ilalim ng lupa? Nakalimutan nilang ang makatwirang tao ay makaaalam ng mga bagay na kasalukuyan at nakikita, ngunit ang kaalaman sa nakaraan, hinaharap, at di-nakikita ay nauukol lamang sa makahulang pagkaalam nang pauna. Kaya ang mga bagay na ito ay hindi dapat tipunin mula sa mga haka at opinyon, kung saan madalas na nalilinlang ang tao, kundi mula sa pananampalataya sa makahulang katotohanan, gaya ng aming aral. Wala kaming sinasalita mula sa aming sarili, ni nagpapahayag kami ng mga bagay na hinango lamang sa paghatol ng tao; sapagkat panlilinlang iyon sa mga nakikinig. Ipinangangaral namin ang mga bagay na ipinagkatiwala at inihayag sa amin ng tunay na Propeta. Tungkol sa Kanyang makahulang kaalaman at kapangyarihan, kung may sinumang nais tumanggap ng malinaw na mga patunay, lumapit siya agad at maging handang makinig. Magbibigay kami ng gayong maliwanag na mga patunay na para bang hindi lamang niya maririnig sa tainga ang kapangyarihan ng makahulang kaalaman kundi makikita niya iyon ng kanyang mga mata at mahahawakan ng kanyang kamay. At kapag nagkaroon siya ng tiyak na pananampalataya sa Kanya, walang kahirapang papasanin niya ang pamatok ng katuwiran at kabanalan; Matthew 11:30 at gayon katamis ang matatagpuan niya rito na hindi lamang niya hindi irereklamo ang anumang hirap, kundi nanaisin pa niyang may maidagdag na tungkuling ipasan sa kanya.

Kabanata 52. Sina Appion at Anubion.

Pagkasabi nito at ng marami pang katulad nito, pinagaling niya ang ilan sa mga naroroon na may karamdaman at sinasapian ng mga demonyo. Pagkatapos ay pinauwi niya ang karamihan habang nagpapasalamat at nagpupuri sila sa Diyos, at inutusan silang bumalik sa parehong lugar sa mga susunod na araw upang makinig. Nang magkakasama na kami sa bahay at naghahandang kumain, may pumasok at nagsabing kararating lamang mula sa Antioquia nina Appion Pleistonices at Anubion at nanunuluyan sila kay Simon. Nang marinig ito ng aking ama, natuwa siya at sinabi kay Pedro: Kung pahihintulutan mo ako, nais kong puntahan at batiin sina Appion at Anubion, sapagkat matalik ko silang mga kaibigan. Marahil ay mahihikayat ko rin si Anubion na makipagtalakayan kay Clemente tungkol sa genesis. Sinabi ni Pedro: Pinahihintulutan kita, at pinupuri kita dahil iginagalang mo ang iyong mga kaibigan. Ngunit tingnan mo kung paanong sa providensiya ng Diyos ay dumarating sa iyo ang lahat ng bagay ayon sa iyong nais. Narito, hindi lamang ibinalik sa iyo sa pagtatakda ng Diyos ang mga taong nararapat mong mahalin, kundi inayos din Niya para sa iyo ang pagdating ng iyong mga kaibigan. Sinabi ng aking ama: Tunay ngang nakikita kong gayon ang nangyayari. Pagkasabi nito, umalis siya upang puntahan si Anubion.

Kabanata 53. Isang Pagbabago ng Anyo.

Ngunit kami, na kasama ni Pedro, ay nanatiling gising buong gabi dahil sa labis na kasiyahan sa kanyang pagtuturo at sa tamis ng kanyang mga salita, habang nagtatanong sa kanya at natututo tungkol sa maraming bagay. Nang magbukang-liwayway, tumingin si Pedro sa akin at sa aking mga kapatid at sinabi: Nagtataka ako kung ano ang nangyari sa inyong ama. Habang sinasabi niya ito, dumating ang aking ama at nadatnan si Pedro na pinag-uusapan siya sa amin. Binati niya kami at nagsimulang humingi ng paumanhin at ipaliwanag kung bakit siya nanatili sa labas. Ngunit nang tumingin kami sa kanya ay natakot kami, sapagkat mukha ni Simon ang nakita namin sa kanya ngunit tinig ng aming ama ang narinig namin. Nang umurong kami at sumpain siya, labis na nagtaka ang aming ama sa mabagsik at parang di-makataong pakikitungo namin sa kanya. Si Pedro lamang ang nakakakita sa kanyang tunay na mukha at sinabi niya sa amin: Bakit ninyo sinusumpa ang inyong ama? Sumagot kami, kasama ang aming ina: Si Simon ang nakikita namin, bagaman tinig ng aming ama ang naririnig namin. Sinabi ni Pedro: Tinig lamang niya ang nakikilala ninyo, sapagkat hindi iyon nabago ng mahika. Ngunit sa akin, ang kanyang mukha—na sa iba ay waring binago ng sining ni Simon—ay nakikilala rin bilang mukha ng inyong amang si Faustinianus. At tumingin siya sa aking ama at sinabi: Ito ang dahilan ng takot ng iyong asawa at mga anak: hindi na nila nakikitang gaya ng dati ang iyong mukha; sa halip, ang kasuklam-suklam na mukha ni Simon ang nakikita nila sa iyo.

Kabanata 54. Pagkakagulo sa Antioquia.

Habang sinasabi niya ito, bumalik ang isa sa mga naunang ipinadala sa Antioquia at nagsabi kay Pedro: Nais kong malaman mo, panginoon kong Pedro, na si Simon sa Antioquia, habang gumagawa ng maraming tanda at kababalaghan sa harap ng madla, ay walang itinatanim sa mga tao kundi mga bagay na pumupukaw ng poot laban sa iyo. Tinatawag ka niyang salamangkero, mangkukulam, at mamamatay-tao. Gayon na lamang ang poot na pinukaw niya laban sa iyo na sabik silang, kung matagpuan ka nila, ay halos kainin pati ang iyong laman. Kaya kaming mga naunang ipinadala, nang makita naming lubhang nabalisa ang lungsod laban sa iyo, ay nagtipon nang lihim upang pag-isipan kung ano ang dapat gawin.

Kabanata 55. Isang Pakana.

Nang wala kaming makitang paraan upang malutas ang suliranin, dumating si Cornelio na senturyon, na isinugo ni Cesar sa gobernador ng Cesarea para sa isang pampublikong tungkulin. Ipinatawag namin siya nang nag-iisa, sinabi sa kanya kung bakit kami nalulungkot, at nakiusap na tulungan kami kung may magagawa siya. Agad niyang ipinangakong mapatatakas niya si Simon kung tutulungan namin ang kanyang plano. Nang sabihin naming gagawin namin ang lahat ng aming makakaya, sinabi niya: “Iniutos ni Cesar na hanapin at patayin ang mga salamangkero sa lungsod ng Roma at sa mga lalawigan, at marami na sa kanila ang napatay. Kaya ipapakalat ko sa pamamagitan ng aking mga kaibigan na naparito ako upang dakpin ang salamangkero na iyan at na isinugo ako ni Cesar para sa layuning ito upang parusahan siyang kasama ng iba niyang kasamahan. Yaong mga tao ninyo na kasama niya nang palihim ay dapat magpahiwatig sa kanya na para bang narinig nila mula sa ibang dako na isinugo ako upang dakpin siya. Kapag narinig niya ito, tiyak na tatakas siya. O kung may mas mabuti kayong naiisip, sabihin ninyo.” Hindi na kailangang pahabain: ginawa iyon ng mga kasama naming nakabalatkayo upang tiktikan siya. Nang malaman ni Simon ang balita, tinanggap niya iyon na para bang napakalaking kabutihang ginawa sa kanya ng mga nagsabi at agad siyang tumakas. Kaya nilisan niya ang Antioquia at, ayon sa narinig namin, pumunta rito kasama si Athenodorus.

Kabanata 56. Ang Balak ni Simon sa Pagbabagong-Anyo.

Kaya kaming lahat na nauna sa iyo ay nagpasiya na pansamantala ay huwag ka munang umakyat sa Antioquia hanggang makita namin kung mababawasan man lamang ang poot na itinanim niya sa mga tao dahil sa kanyang pag-alis. Nang matapos ng taong mula sa Antioquia ang ulat na ito, tumingin si Pedro sa aming ama at sinabi: Faustinianus, maliwanag na binago ni Simon Magus ang anyo ng iyong mukha. Dahil inakala niyang hinahanap siya ni Cesar upang parusahan, tumakas siya sa takot at inilagay sa iyo ang sarili niyang mukha upang kung sakaling ikaw ang madakip kapalit niya at mapatay, magdulot siya ng matinding dalamhati sa iyong mga anak. Nang marinig ito ng aking ama, sumigaw siya at lumuluha: Tama ang iyong paghatol, O Pedro. Sapagkat si Anubion, na napakalapit kong kaibigan, ay nagsimulang magpahiwatig sa akin sa isang mahiwagang paraan tungkol sa kanyang mga balak; ngunit sa kasamaang-palad ay hindi ko siya pinaniwalaan sapagkat wala naman akong ginawang masama sa kanya.

Kabanata 57. Matinding Dalamhati.

Nang marinig ito, kaming lahat ay napuno ng matinding dalamhati. Umiyak ang aming ama at sinabi: Kaawa-awa ako! Bakit hindi ko pinaniwalaan ang salamangkero? Sapagkat matapos kong makilala ang aking asawa at mga anak sa loob lamang ng isang araw, muli akong ibinalik sa dati kong mga kapahamakan. Ang aming ina naman ay umiiyak at pinupunit ang kanyang buhok. Kami ring mga anak niya ay natigilan at halos hindi makapagsalita dahil sa di-inaasahang pagbabagong nangyari sa mukha ng aming ama. Maging si Anubion, nang dumating, ay natigilan sa nakita niya. Ngunit sinabi ni Pedro sa amin: Kayong mga anak, maniwala kayo na ito ang inyong ama at igalang ninyo siya gaya ng dati. Huwag kayong mawalan ng pag-asa, sapagkat ang Diyos, na nagpahintulot na mangyari ito, ay magbibigay din ng pagkakataong maalis sa kanya ang mukha ni Simon at maibalik ang sarili niyang anyo.

Kabanata 58. Kung Paano Nangyari ang Lahat.

Pagkatapos ay sinabi ni Pedro sa aking ama: Pinahintulutan kitang puntahan at batiin sina Appion at Anubion, sapagkat mga kaibigan mo sila; ngunit hindi kita pinahintulutang makipag-usap kay Simon. Sumagot ang aking ama: Ako ang nagkasala at ako ang may kasalanan. Ngunit hindi ko inakalang may gayong masamang balak siya laban sa akin. Pagkatapos ay lumapit si Anubion at sinabi kay Pedro: Ako rin ay nakikiusap at nagsusumamo sa iyo na patawarin mo ang matandang ito, na mabuti at marangal na tao. Sa kasamaang-palad ay nilinlang at dinaya siya ng salamangkero. Sasabihin ko sa iyo kung paano ito ginawa. Nang dumating siya upang batiin kami, nagkataong nakatayo kaming nakapaligid kay Simon at narinig naming sinabi niyang balak niyang tumakas nang gabing iyon, sapagkat nabalitaan niyang may ilang taong dumating maging sa lungsod na ito ng Laodicea upang dakpin siya ayon sa utos ng emperador; ngunit nais niyang ibaling ang buong galit ng mga iyon laban kay Faustinianus, na kararating lamang dito. Sinabi niya sa amin: “Ang kailangan lamang ninyong gawin ay anyayahan siyang kumain kasama natin. Kapag nakakain na siya, gagawa ako ng isang pamahid na ipapahid niya sa kanyang mukha; mula noon, sa paningin ng lahat ay magiging kamukha niya ako. Ngunit pahiran muna ninyo ang sarili ninyong mukha ng katas ng isang halamang-gamot upang hindi kayo malinlang sa pagbabago ng kanyang mukha, at sa gayon, sa lahat maliban sa inyo ay magmumukha siyang si Simon.”

Kabanata 59. Isang Tagpo ng Pagdadalamhati.

Nang sabihin niya ito, tinanong ko siya, “Ano ang mapapala mo sa gawaing ito?” Sumagot si Simon: “Una, yaong mga naghahanap sa akin ay siya ang dadakpin at titigil na sa paghahanap sa akin. At kung parusahan siya ni Cesar, labis na magdadalamhati ang kanyang mga anak na tumalikod sa akin, tumakas patungo kay Pedro, at ngayo'y mga katulong nito.” Inaamin ko sa iyo, Pedro, ang katotohanan. Hindi ako nangahas noon na sabihin ito kay Faustinianus; at hindi rin kami binigyan ni Simon ng pagkakataong makausap siya nang lihim at lubos na maihayag sa kanya ang kanyang balak. Samantala, bandang hatinggabi ay tumakas si Simon patungong Judea. Sumama sina Athenodorus at Appion upang ihatid siya. Nagkunwari naman akong may karamdaman upang manatili sa bahay at maibalik agad sa inyo ang matandang ito, kung sakaling maitatago ninyo siya upang hindi siya madakip ng mga naghahanap kay Simon, madala kay Cesar, at mapahamak nang walang kasalanan. Kaya dahil sa pag-aalala ko sa kanya ay naparito ako upang makita siya, at balak kong bumalik bago makabalik ang mga naghatid kay Simon. Pagkatapos ay tumingin si Anubion sa amin at nagsabi: Ako, si Anubion, ay nakikita ang tunay na mukha ng inyong ama sapagkat, gaya ng sinabi ko, ako mismo ay naunang pinahiran ni Simon upang makita ng aking mga mata ang tunay na anyo ni Faustinianus. Kaya lubha akong namamangha sa sining ni Simon Magus, sapagkat kayong nakatayo rito ay hindi nakikilala ang sarili ninyong ama. Habang ang aming ama at ina, at kaming lahat, ay umiiyak dahil sa nangyari, naantig din si Anubion at umiyak dahil sa habag.

Kabanata 60. Isang Kontra-Pakana.

Pagkatapos ay nahabag si Pedro at ipinangakong ibabalik niya ang mukha ng aming ama. Sinabi niya rito: Makinig ka, Faustinianus. Kapag ang maling anyong ito ng iyong mukha ay nakapagbigay na sa atin ng pakinabang at nakatulong na sa mga layuning nais nating maisakatuparan, ibabalik ko sa iyo ang tunay mong mukha—ngunit kailangan mo munang gawin ang ipag-uutos ko. Nang mangako ang aking ama na buong lakas niyang gagawin ang lahat ng ipagagawa sa kanya, basta maibalik lamang ang sarili niyang mukha, nagsimula si Pedro: Narinig mo mismo na bumalik mula sa Antioquia ang isa sa mga nauna nating ipinadala at iniulat kung paanong pinukaw ni Simon ang mga tao laban sa akin at pinuno ang buong lungsod ng poot sa akin, na sinasabing ako ay salamangkero, mamamatay-tao, at manlilinlang; anupa't kung makita nila ako ay sabik nilang lapain maging ang aking laman. Kaya gawin mo ang sasabihin ko. Iwan mo rito si Clemente sa akin, at mauna ka sa amin patungong Antioquia kasama ang iyong asawa at ang mga anak mong sina Faustus at Faustinus. Magpapadala rin ako kasama ninyo ng iba pang mga taong inaakala kong angkop, na siyang magbabantay sa lahat ng iuutos ko.

Kabanata 61. Isang Minang Hinukay.

Pagdating ninyo sa Antioquia, yamang iisipin ng mga tao na ikaw si Simon, tumayo ka sa isang pampublikong lugar at ipahayag ang iyong pagsisisi. Sabihin mo: “Ako, si Simon, ay nagpapahayag at umaamin sa inyo na ang lahat ng sinabi ko tungkol kay Pedro ay kasinungalingan. Hindi siya manlilinlang, ni salamangkero, ni mamamatay-tao, ni alinman sa masasamang bagay na sinabi ko laban sa kanya; sinabi ko ang lahat ng iyon dahil sa udyok ng kabaliwan. Kaya ako mismo, na siyang nagbigay sa inyo ng dahilan upang mapoot sa kanya, ay nakikiusap sa inyong huwag ninyong isipin ang gayong mga bagay tungkol sa kanya. Alisin ninyo ang inyong poot; tigilan ninyo ang inyong galit; sapagkat tunay siyang isinugo ng Diyos para sa kaligtasan ng sanlibutan—isang alagad at apostol ng tunay na Propeta. Kaya ipinapayo, hinihikayat, at iniuutos ko sa inyong pakinggan siya at paniwalaan kapag ipinangangaral niya sa inyo ang katotohanan, upang hindi, kung hahamakin ninyo siya, ay biglang mapahamak ang mismong lungsod ninyo. Ngunit sasabihin ko sa inyo kung bakit ko ginagawa ngayon ang pag-aming ito. Nitong gabi ay sinaway ako ng isang anghel ng Diyos dahil sa aking kasamaan at matindi akong hinagupit sapagkat naging kaaway ako ng tagapagbalita ng katotohanan. Kaya nakikiusap ako sa inyo: kung ako mismo ay muling dumating sa inyo at tangkaing magsalita ng anumang laban kay Pedro, huwag ninyo akong tanggapin ni paniwalaan. Sapagkat inaamin ko sa inyo: ako ay salamangkero, manlilinlang, at mandaraya; ngunit nagsisisi ako, sapagkat sa pamamagitan ng pagsisisi ay maaaring mapawi ang mga dating masasamang gawa.”

Kabanata 62. Isang Usapin ng Budhi.

Nang sabihin ito ni Pedro sa aking ama, sumagot siya: Nauunawaan ko ang nais mong mangyari; huwag ka nang mag-alala, sapagkat alam ko kung ano ang dapat kong gawin pagdating ko roon. Nagbigay pa si Pedro ng karagdagang tagubilin: Kapag nakarating ka roon at nakita mong nabago ng iyong pananalita ang mga tao, na iniwan na nila ang kanilang poot at bumalik na ang kanilang pananabik na makita ako, magpadala ka ng mensahe sa akin at agad akong pupunta. Pagdating ko ay hindi ako magpapaliban na palayain ka mula sa kakaibang mukhang ito at ibalik sa iyo ang sarili mong mukha na kilala ng lahat ng iyong mga kaibigan. Pagkasabi nito, inutusan niya ang aking mga kapatid na sumama sa kanya, gayundin ang aming inang si Matthidia at ilan sa aming mga kaibigan. Ngunit tumanggi ang aking ina at sinabi: Pakiramdam ko'y para akong mangangalunya kung sasama ako sa anyo ni Simon. Kung pilitin man akong sumama sa kanya, tiyak na hindi ko kayang matulog sa iisang higaan kasama niya; at hindi ko rin alam kung kaya kong pumayag kahit sa paglalakbay kasama niya. Nang mahigpit siyang tumanggi, nagsimulang manghikayat si Anubion: Maniwala ka sa akin at kay Pedro. Hindi ba maging ang kanyang tinig ay nagpapatunay sa iyo na siya ang asawa mong si Faustinianus, na tunay kong minamahal nang hindi mas mababa sa pagmamahal mo sa kanya? At sa madaling salita, ako mismo ay sasama rin sa inyo. Dahil dito, pumayag ang aming ina na sumama.

Kabanata 63. Isang Maka-Diyos na Panlilinlang.

Pagkatapos ay sinabi ko: Inaayos ng Diyos ang ating mga gawain ayon sa ating ninanais. Narito, kasama natin si Anubion na isang astrologo; kapag nakarating tayo sa Antioquia, maaari tayong taimtim na makipagtalakayan sa kanya tungkol sa genesis. Pagkalipas ng hatinggabi, umalis ang aming ama kasama ang mga inutusan ni Pedro na sumama sa kanya at kasama si Anubion. Kinaumagahan, bago lumabas si Pedro para sa talakayan, bumalik ang mga naghatid kay Simon—sina Appion at Athenodorus—at dumating sa amin upang hanapin ang aking ama. Nang ipaalam kay Pedro ang kanilang pagdating, inutusan niya silang pumasok. Nang makaupo sila ay nagtanong: Nasaan si Faustinianus? Sumagot si Pedro: Hindi namin alam. Mula noong gabing pumunta siya sa inyo ay wala ni isa sa kanyang mga kaibigan ang nakakita sa kanya. Ngunit kahapon ng umaga ay dumating si Simon upang hanapin siya. Nang hindi namin siya masagot, hindi ko alam kung ano ang ibig niyang mangyari, ngunit sinabi niyang siya raw si Faustinianus. Walang naniwala sa kanya, kaya umiyak siya at nagbanta na papatayin ang sarili; pagkatapos ay umalis patungo sa dagat.

Kabanata 64. Paligsahan sa Pagsisinungaling.

Nang marinig ito ni Appion at ng mga kasama niya, nagsimula silang humagulgol at nagsabi: Bakit ninyo ginawa iyon? Bakit hindi ninyo siya tinanggap? Habang sasabihin sana ni Athenodorus sa akin na siya nga ang aking amang si Faustinianus, pinigilan siya ni Appion at nagsabi: Nalaman namin mula sa isang tao na sumama siya kay Simon at, sa pakiusap mismo ni Faustinianus, ayaw niyang makita ang kanyang mga anak sapagkat mga Judio sila. Nang marinig namin iyon ay naparito kami upang hanapin siya. Ngunit yamang wala siya rito, waring totoo ang sinabi ng nagbigay sa amin ng balita. Iyan ang nais naming sabihin sa inyo. Ngunit ako, si Clemente, nang maunawaan ko ang layunin ni Pedro—na nais niyang isipin nilang hahanapin sa kanila ang matandang lalaki upang matakot sila at tumakas—ay tumulong sa kanyang plano at sinabi kay Appion: Makinig ka, mahal kong Appion. Ang pinaniniwalaan naming mabuti ay nais naming maibigay din sa aming ama; ngunit kung ayaw niya itong tanggapin at sa halip, gaya ng sinasabi mo, ay tumatakas dahil sa pagkasuklam sa amin—maaaring mabagsik sabihin—wala rin kaming pakialam sa kanya. Pagkasabi ko nito, umalis sila habang isinusumpa ang aking kalupitan, at ayon sa nalaman namin kinabukasan ay sinundan nila ang landas ni Simon.

Kabanata 65. Tagumpay ng Pakana.

Samantala, habang araw-araw na nagtuturo si Pedro ayon sa kanyang kaugalian at gumagawa ng maraming himala at pagpapagaling, makalipas ang sampung araw ay dumating mula sa Antioquia ang isa sa ating mga tao na isinugo ng aking ama. Iniulat niya kung paanong tumayo sa harap ng madla ang aking ama, na waring suot ang mukha ni Simon, at inakusahan si Simon habang labis namang pinupuri si Pedro at inirerekomenda siya sa buong bayan. Dahil sa kanyang pananalita ay nagbago silang lahat at nanabik na makita si Pedro. May marami pa ngang umibig kay Pedro nang gayon na lamang na nagalit sila sa aking ama sa pag-aakalang siya si Simon, at binalak siyang dakpin dahil sa napakalaking pinsalang ginawa niya kay Pedro. Kaya, sabi ng sugo, magmadali kayo, baka siya ay mapatay. Isinugo niya ako nang mabilis sa inyo dahil sa matindi niyang takot, upang hilinging pumunta kayo agad habang buhay pa siya, at upang makarating kayo sa tamang pagkakataon habang lumalago ang pagmamahal ng lungsod sa inyo. Isinalaysay din niya na sa sandaling pumasok ang aking ama sa Antioquia ay nagtipon sa kanya ang buong bayan sa pag-aakalang siya si Simon. Nagsimula siyang magpahayag sa kanilang lahat nang hayagan ayon sa hinihingi ng pagpapanumbalik ng mga tao; sapagkat ang lahat ng dumating—marangal at karaniwan, mayaman at dukha—ay umaasang gagawa siya ng mga kababalaghan gaya ng dati. Ganito niya sila kinausap:—

Kabanata 66. Katotohanang Sinabi ng mga Bibig na Nagsisinungaling.

Matagal nang nagtitiis sa akin ang banal na pagtitiyaga—ako, si Simon, ang pinakakaawa-awa sa mga tao. Sapagkat ang lahat ng inyong pinagtakhan sa akin ay ginawa hindi sa pamamagitan ng katotohanan kundi sa pamamagitan ng kasinungalingan at mga daya ng mga demonyo, upang gibain ko ang inyong pananampalataya at hatulan ang sarili kong kaluluwa. Inaamin ko na ang lahat ng sinabi ko tungkol kay Pedro ay kasinungalingan. Hindi siya kailanman naging salamangkero o mamamatay-tao; sa halip, isinugo siya ng Diyos para sa kaligtasan ninyong lahat. Kung mula sa oras na ito ay iisipin pa rin ninyong dapat siyang hamakin, makatitiyak kayong maaaring biglang mapahamak ang mismong lungsod ninyo. Ngunit magtatanong kayo: bakit kusang-loob kong ginagawa ngayon sa inyo ang pag-aming ito? Nitong gabi ay matindi akong sinaway ng isang anghel ng Diyos at lubhang hinagupit sapagkat naging kaaway niya ako. Kaya nakikiusap ako sa inyo: kung mula ngayon ay ako mismo ang magbukas muli ng aking bibig laban sa kanya, itaboy ninyo ako mula sa inyong paningin. Sapagkat ang maruming demonyong iyon, na kaaway ng kaligtasan ng tao, ang nagsasalita laban sa kanya sa pamamagitan ng aking bibig upang hindi ninyo makamtan ang buhay sa pamamagitan niya. Anong himala ba ang naipakita sa inyo ng mahika sa pamamagitan ko? Pinatahol ko ang mga asong tanso, pinakilos ang mga estatwa, binago ang anyo ng mga tao, at bigla silang pinaglaho sa paningin ng iba. Dahil sa mga bagay na ito ay dapat sana ninyong isinumpa ang mahika, na nagtali sa inyong mga kaluluwa ng mga tanikala ng diyablo upang maipakita ko sa inyo ang walang-kabuluhang kababalaghan at hindi ninyo paniwalaan si Pedro—siya na nagpapagaling ng mga maysakit sa pangalan Niya na nagsugo sa kanya, nagpapalayas ng mga demonyo, nagbibigay ng paningin sa mga bulag, nagpapanumbalik ng kalusugan sa mga paralitiko, at bumubuhay ng mga patay.

Kabanata 67. Muling Naging Sarili si Faustinianus.

Habang ginagawa niya ang mga ito at nagsasabi ng mga katulad na bagay, nagsimulang sumpain siya ng mga tao at umiyak at managhoy dahil nagkasala sila laban kay Pedro sa paniniwalang ito ay salamangkero o masamang tao. Ngunit nang gabing iyon ay naibalik kay Faustinianus ang sarili niyang mukha at nawala sa kanya ang anyo ni Simon Magus. Nang malaman ni Simon na ang kanyang mukha sa katawan ni Faustinianus ay naging daan upang maparangalan si Pedro, nagmadali siyang maunahan si Pedro at balak sa pamamagitan ng kanyang sining na alisin kay Faustinianus ang kanyang wangis—ngunit nagawa na iyon ni Cristo ayon sa salita ng Kanyang apostol. Nang makita nina Niceta at Aquila na naibalik ang mukha ng kanilang ama matapos niyang magawa ang kinakailangang pagpapahayag, nagpasalamat sila sa Diyos at hindi na nila siya pinahintulutang muling humarap sa mga tao.

Kabanata 68. Pagpasok ni Pedro sa Antioquia.

Ngunit nagsimulang lihim na maglibot si Simon sa kanyang mga kaibigan at kakilala at lalo pang manira kay Pedro. Dinuraan siya ng lahat sa mukha at itinaboy sa lungsod, na sinasabi: Ikaw mismo ang mananagot sa sarili mong kamatayan kung iisipin mong bumalik dito at muling magsalita laban kay Pedro. Nang malaman ang mga bagay na ito sa Laodicea, inutusan ni Pedro ang mga tao na magtipon kinabukasan. Nagtalaga siya ng isa sa kanyang mga tagasunod bilang obispo sa kanila at ng iba bilang mga presbitero; binautismuhan niya ang napakarami at pinagaling ang lahat ng pinahihirapan ng mga karamdaman o mga demonyo. Nanatili pa siya roon nang tatlong araw; at nang maayos na ang lahat, nagpaalam siya sa kanila at umalis mula sa Laodicea, habang sabik na sabik sa kanyang pagdating ang mga tao ng Antioquia. Sa pamamagitan nina Niceta at Aquila ay nagsimulang marinig ng buong lungsod na paparating si Pedro. Kaya nang marinig ng buong bayan ng Antioquia ang kanyang pagdating, lumabas sila upang salubungin siya. Halos lahat ng matatanda at mararangal ay dumating na may abo sa kanilang mga ulo bilang tanda ng kanilang pagsisisi sapagkat nakinig sila sa salamangkero na si Simon laban sa pangangaral ni Pedro.

Kabanata 69. Pasasalamat ni Pedro.

Habang sinasabi nila ang mga ito at iba pang katulad na bagay, dinala nila sa kanya ang mga pinahihirapan ng mga karamdaman, mga sinasapian ng mga demonyo, mga paralitiko, at mga dumaranas ng sari-saring kapahamakan; napakaraming maysakit ang nagtipon. Nang makita ni Pedro na hindi lamang sila nagsisisi sa masasamang akalang tinanggap nila laban sa kanya dahil kay Simon, kundi nagpapakita rin sila ng lubos na pananampalataya sa Diyos—na naniniwalang ang lahat ng may anumang karamdaman ay maaaring gumaling sa pamamagitan niya—iniunat niya ang kanyang mga kamay sa langit at, habang lumuluha sa panalangin, nagpasalamat sa Diyos: Pinagpapala Kita, O Ama, na karapat-dapat sa lahat ng papuri, sapagkat minarapat Mong tuparin ang bawat salita at pangako ng Iyong Anak, upang malaman ng bawat nilalang na Ikaw lamang ang Diyos sa langit at sa lupa.

Kabanata 70. Mga Himala.

Pagkasabi ng mga ito, umakyat siya sa isang mataas na lugar at inutusan ang buong karamihan ng mga maysakit na pumuwesto sa harap niya. Kinausap niya silang lahat: Nakikita ninyong ako ay isang taong katulad din ninyo. Kaya huwag ninyong isipin na sa akin magmumula ang inyong paggaling, kundi sa Kanya na bumaba mula sa langit at ipinakita sa mga naniniwala sa Kanya ang ganap na gamot para sa katawan at kaluluwa. Kaya maging saksi ang buong bayang ito sa inyong pagpapahayag na buong puso kayong sumasampalataya sa Panginoong Jesu-Cristo, upang malaman din nila na maaari silang maligtas sa pamamagitan Niya. Nang sabay-sabay na sumigaw ang buong karamihan ng mga maysakit na Siya ang tunay na Diyos na ipinangangaral ni Pedro, biglang lumitaw sa gitna ng mga tao ang isang makapangyarihang liwanag ng biyaya ng Diyos. Gumaling ang mga paralitiko at tumakbo sa paanan ni Pedro; ang mga bulag ay sumigaw nang manumbalik ang kanilang paningin; ang mga pilay ay nagpasalamat nang muli silang makalakad; ang mga maysakit ay nagalak sa pagbabalik ng kanilang kalusugan. Maging ang ilang halos wala nang buhay, nawalan na ng malay o kakayahang magsalita, ay ibinangon; at ang lahat ng mga baliw at sinasapian ng mga demonyo ay pinalaya.

Kabanata 71. Tagumpay.

Gayon kadakila ang biyaya ng Kanyang kapangyarihang ipinakita ng Banal na Espiritu sa araw na iyon na ang lahat, mula sa pinakamababa hanggang sa pinakadakila, ay sabay-sabay na nagpahayag sa Panginoon. Upang hindi kayo antalahin sa maraming salita: sa loob ng pitong araw, mahigit sampung libong lalaki ang sumampalataya sa Diyos, nabautismuhan, at itinalaga sa pamamagitan ng pagpapabanal. Kaya si Theophilus, na higit na mataas kaysa sa lahat ng makapangyarihang tao sa lungsod na iyon, ay buong pananabik na inilaan ang malaking palasyo ng kanyang bahay sa pangalan ng isang iglesya. Naglagay doon ang buong bayan ng isang upuan para kay Apostol Pedro; at ang buong karamihan, na araw-araw na nagtitipon upang makinig sa salita, ay sumampalataya sa aral na nagbibigay-kaligtasan na pinatotohanan ng bisa ng mga pagpapagaling.

Kabanata 72. Maligayang Wakas.

Pagkatapos ay kinausap namin—ako si Clemente, kasama ang aking mga kapatid at aming ina—ang aming ama at tinanong kung may natitira pa sa kanya na anumang bakas ng kawalan ng pananampalataya. Sinabi niya: Halina kayo at makikita ninyo sa harap ni Pedro kung gaano kalaki ang paglago ng pananampalataya sa akin. Lumapit si Faustinianus at nagpatirapa sa paanan ni Pedro: Ang mga binhi ng iyong salita na tinanggap ng bukirin ng aking isip ay sumibol na at lumago hanggang sa mabungang kapanahunan. Wala nang kulang kundi ang ihiwalay mo ako sa ipa sa pamamagitan ng iyong espirituwal na karit at ilagay sa kamalig ng Panginoon, upang maging kabahagi ako sa banal na hapag. Agad na hinawakan ni Pedro ang kanyang kamay nang buong galak at iniharap siya sa akin at sa aking mga kapatid, na sinasabi: Kung paanong ibinalik ng Diyos sa iyo, na ama, ang iyong mga anak, gayon din ibinabalik ngayon ng iyong mga anak ang kanilang ama sa Diyos. Pagkatapos ay nagpahayag siya ng pag-aayuno para sa buong bayan, at sa sumunod na Araw ng Panginoon ay binautismuhan niya ang aking ama. Sa gitna ng mga tao, ginamit niya ang pagbabagong-loob nito bilang pagkakataon upang isalaysay ang lahat ng kanyang mga sinapit, kaya tinanggap siya ng buong lungsod na parang isang anghel at pinarangalan nang hindi gaanong mababa kaysa sa apostol. At nang malaman ang mga bagay na ito, inutusan ni Pedro ang mga tao na magtipon kinabukasan; nagtalaga siya ng isa sa kanyang mga tagasunod bilang obispo at ng iba bilang mga presbitero, binautismuhan din ang napakaraming tao, at pinagaling ang lahat ng mga pinahihirapan ng mga karamdaman.
