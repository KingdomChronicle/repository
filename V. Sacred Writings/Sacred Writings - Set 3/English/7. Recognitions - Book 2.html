Chapter 1. Power of Habit.

When the day dawned which had been fixed for the discussion with Simon, Peter, rising at the first cock-crowing, aroused us also: for we were sleeping in the same apartment, thirteen of us in all; of whom, next to Peter, Zacchæus was first, then Sophonius, Joseph and Michæas, Eliesdrus, Phineas, Lazarus, and Elisæus: after these I (Clement) and Nicodemus; then Niceta and Aquila, who had formerly been disciples of Simon, and were converted to the faith of Christ under the teaching of Zacchæus. Of the women there was no one present. As the evening light was still lasting, we all sat down; and Peter, seeing that we were awake, and that we were giving attention to him, having saluted us, immediately began to speak, as follows:—

I confess, brethren, that I wonder at the power of human nature, which I see to be fit and suited to every call upon it. This, however, it occurs to me to say of what I have found by experience, that when the middle of the night is passed, I awake of my own accord, and sleep does not come to me again. This happens to me for this reason, that I have formed the habit of recalling to memory the words of my Lord, which I heard from Himself; and for the longing I have towards them, I constrain my mind and my thoughts to be roused, that, awaking to them, and recalling and arranging them one by one, I may retain them in my memory. From this, therefore, while I desire to cherish the sayings of the Lord with all delight in my heart, the habit of waking has come upon me, even if there be nothing that I wish to think of. Thus, in some unaccountable way, when any custom is established, the old custom is changed, provided indeed you do not force it above measure, but as far as the measure of nature admits. For it is not possible to be altogether without sleep; otherwise night would not have been made for rest.

Chapter 2. Curtailment of Sleep.

Then I, when I heard this, said: You have very well said, O Peter; for one custom is superseded by another. For when I was at sea, I was at first distressed, and all my system was disordered, so that I felt as if I had been beaten, and could not bear the tossing and tumult of the sea; but after a few days, when I had got accustomed to it, I began to bear it tolerably, so that I was glad to take food immediately in the morning along with the sailors, whereas before it was not my custom to eat anything before the seventh hour. Now, therefore, simply from the custom which I then acquired, hunger reminds me about that time at which I used to eat with the sailors; which, however, I hope to get rid of, when once another custom shall have been formed. I believe, therefore, that you also have acquired the habit of wakefulness, as you state; and you have wished at a fitting time to explain this to us, that we also may not grudge to throw off and dispense with some portion of our sleep, that we may be able to take in the precepts of the living doctrine. For when the food is digested, and the mind is under the influence of the silence of night, those things which are seasonably taught abide in it.

Chapter 3. Need of Caution.

Then Peter, being pleased to hear that I understood the purport of his preface, that he had delivered it for our advantage; and commending me, doubtless for the purpose of encouraging, and stimulating me, began to deliver the following discourse: It seems to me to be seasonable and necessary to have some discussion relating to those things that are near at hand; that is, concerning Simon. For I should wish to know of what character and of what conduct he is. Wherefore, if any one of you has any knowledge of him, let him not fail to inform me; for it is of consequence to know these things beforehand. For if we have it in charge, that when we enter into a city we should first learn who in it is worthy, Matthew 10:11 that we may eat with him, how much more is it proper for us to ascertain who or what sort of man he is to whom the words of immortality are to be committed! For we ought to be careful, yea, extremely careful, that we cast not our pearls before swine. Matthew 7:6

Chapter 4. Prudence in Dealing with Opponents.

But for other reasons also it is of importance that I should have some knowledge of this man. For if I know that in those things concerning which it cannot be doubted that they are good, he is faultless and irreproachable — that is to say, if he is sober, merciful, upright, gentle, and humane, which no one doubts to be good qualities, — then it will seem to be fitting, that upon him who possesses these good virtues, that which is lacking of faith and knowledge should be conferred; and so his life, which is in other respects worthy of approbation, should be amended in those points in which it shall appear to be imperfect. But if he remains wrapped up and polluted in those sins which are manifestly such, it does not become me to speak to him at all of the more secret and sacred things of divine knowledge, but rather to protest and confront him, that he cease from sin, and cleanse his actions from vice. But if he insinuate himself, and lead us on to speak what he, while he acts improperly, ought not to hear, it will be our part to parry him cautiously. For not to answer him at all does not seem proper, for the sake of the hearers, lest haply they may think that we decline the contest through want of ability to answer him, and so their faith may be injured through their misunderstanding of our purpose.

Chapter 5. Simon Magus, a Formidable Antagonist.

When Peter had thus spoken to us, Niceta asks permission to say something to him; and Peter having granted permission, he says: With your pardon, I beseech you, my lord Peter, to hear me, who am very anxious for you, and who am afraid lest, in the contest which you have in hand with Simon, you should seem to be overmatched. For it very frequently happens that he who defends the truth does not gain the victory, since the hearers are either prejudiced, or have no great interest in the better cause. But over and above all this, Simon himself is a most vehement orator, trained in the dialectic art, and in the meshes of syllogisms; and what is worse than all, he is greatly skilled in the magic art. And therefore I fear, lest haply, being so strongly fortified on every side, he shall be thought to be defending the truth, while he is alleging falsehoods, in the presence of those who do not know him. For neither should we ourselves have been able to escape from him, and to be converted to the Lord, had it not been that, while we were his assistants, and the sharers of his errors, we had ascertained that he was a deceiver and a magician.

Chapter 6. Simon Magus: His Wickedness.

When Niceta had thus spoken, Aquila also, asking that he might be permitted to speak, proceeded in manner following: Receive, I entreat you, most excellent Peter, the assurance of my love towards you; for indeed I also am extremely anxious on your account. And do not blame us in this, for indeed to be concerned for any one comes of affection; whereas to be indifferent is no less than hatred. But I call God to witness that I feel for you, not as knowing you to be weaker in debate — for indeed I was never present at any dispute in which you were engaged — but because I well know the impieties of this man, I think of your reputation, and at the same time the souls of the hearers, and above all, the interests of the truth itself. For this magician is vehement towards all things that he wishes, and wicked above measure. For in all things we know him well, since from boyhood we have been assistants and ministers of his wickedness; and had not the love of God rescued us from him, we should even now be engaged in the same evil deeds with him. But a certain inborn love towards God rendered his wickedness hateful to us, and the worship of God attractive to us. Whence I think also that it was the work of Divine Providence, that we, being first made his associates, should take knowledge in what manner or by what art he effects the prodigies which he seems to work. For who is there that would not be astonished at the wonderful things which he does? Who would not think that he was a god come down from heaven for the salvation of men? For myself, I confess, if I had not known him intimately, and had taken part in his doings, I would easily have been carried away with him. Whence it was no great thing for us to be separated from his society, knowing as we did that he depends upon magic arts and wicked devices. But if you also yourself wish to know all about him — who, what, and whence he is, and how he contrives what he does — then listen.

Chapter 7. Simon Magus: His History.

This Simon's father was Antonius, and his mother Rachel. By nation he is a Samaritan, from a village of the Gettones; by profession a magician yet exceedingly well trained in the Greek literature; desirous of glory, and boasting above all the human race, so that he wishes himself to be believed to be an exalted power, which is above God the Creator, and to be thought to be the Christ, and to be called the Standing One. And he uses this name as implying that he can never be dissolved, asserting that his flesh is so compacted by the power of his divinity, that it can endure to eternity. Hence, therefore, he is called the Standing One, as though he cannot fall by any corruption.

Chapter 8. Simon Magus: His History.

For after that John the Baptist was killed, as you yourself also know, when Dositheus had broached his heresy, with thirty other chief disciples, and one woman, who was called Luna — whence also these thirty appear to have been appointed with reference to the number of the days, according to the course of the moon — this Simon ambitious of evil glory, as we have said, goes to Dositheus, and pretending friendship, entreats him, that if any one of those thirty should die, he should straightway substitute him in room of the dead: for it was contrary to their rule either to exceed the fixed number, or to admit any one who was unknown, or not yet proved; whence also the rest, desiring to become worthy of the place and number, are eager in every way to please, according to the institutions of their sect each one of those who aspire after admittance into the number, hoping that he may be deemed worthy to be put into the place of the deceased, when, as we have said, any one dies. Therefore Dositheus, being greatly urged by this man, introduced Simon when a vacancy occurred among the number.

Chapter 9. Simon Magus: His Profession.

But not long after he fell in love with that woman whom they call Luna; and he confided all things to us as his friends: how he was a magician, and how he loved Luna, and how, being desirous of glory, he was unwilling to enjoy her ingloriously, but that he was waiting patiently till he could enjoy her honourably; yet so if we also would conspire with him towards the accomplishment of his desires. And he promised that, as a reward of this service, he would cause us to be invested with the highest honours, and we should be believed by men to be gods; 'Only, however, on condition,' says he, 'that you confer the chief place upon me, Simon, who by magic art am able to show many signs and prodigies, by means of which either my glory or our sect may be established. For I am able to render myself invisible to those who wish to lay hold of me, and again to be visible when I am willing to be seen. If I wish to flee, I can dig through the mountains, and pass through rocks as if they were clay. If I should throw myself headlong from a lofty mountain, I should be borne unhurt to the earth, as if I were held up; when bound, I can loose myself, and bind those who had bound me; being shut up in prison, I can make the barriers open of their own accord; I can render statues animated, so that those who see suppose that they are men. I can make new trees suddenly spring up, and produce sprouts at once. I can throw myself into the fire, and not be burnt; I can change my countenance, so that I cannot be recognised; but I can show people that I have two faces. I shall change myself into a sheep or a goat; I shall make a beard to grow upon little boys; I shall ascend by flight into the air; I shall exhibit abundance of gold, and shall make and unmake kings. I shall be worshipped as God; I shall have divine honours publicly assigned to me, so that an image of me shall be set up, and I shall be worshipped and adored as God. And what need of more words? Whatever I wish, that I shall be able to do. For already I have achieved many things by way of experiment. In short,' says he, 'once when my mother Rachel ordered me to go to the field to reap, and I saw a sickle lying, I ordered it to go and reap; and it reaped ten times more than the others. Lately, I produced many new sprouts from the earth, and made them bear leaves and produce fruit in a moment; and the nearest mountain I successfully bored through.'

Chapter 10. Simon Magus: His Deception.

But when he spoke thus of the production of sprouts and the perforation of the mountain, I was confounded on this account, because he wished to deceive even us, in whom he seemed to place confidence; for we knew that those things had been from the days of our fathers, which he represented as having been done by himself lately. We then, although we heard these atrocities from him, and worse than these, yet we followed up his crimes, and suffered others to be deceived by him, telling also many lies on his behalf; and this before he did any of the things which he had promised, so that while as yet he had done nothing, he was by some thought to be God.

Chapter 11. Simon Magus, at the Head of the Sect of Dositheus.

Meantime, at the outset, as soon as he was reckoned among the thirty disciples of Dositheus, he began to depreciate Dositheus himself, saying that he did not teach purely or perfectly, and that this was the result not of ill intention, but of ignorance. But Dositheus, when he perceived that Simon was depreciating him, fearing lest his reputation among men might be obscured (for he himself was supposed to be the Standing One), moved with rage, when they met as usual at the school, seized a rod, and began to beat Simon; but suddenly the rod seemed to pass through his body, as if it had been smoke. On which Dositheus, being astonished, says to him, 'Tell me if you are the Standing One, that I may adore you.' And when Simon answered that he was, then Dositheus, perceiving that he himself was not the Standing One, fell down and worshipped him, and gave up his own place as chief to Simon, ordering all the rank of thirty men to obey him; himself taking the inferior place which Simon formerly occupied. Not long after this he died.

Chapter 12. Simon Magus and Luna.

Therefore, after the death of Dositheus Simon took Luna to himself; and with her he still goes about, as you see, deceiving multitudes, and asserting that he himself is a certain power which is above God the Creator, while Luna, who is with him, has been brought down from the higher heavens, and that she is Wisdom, the mother of all things, for whom, says he, the Greeks and barbarians contending, were able in some measure to see an image of her; but of herself, as she is, as the dweller with the first and only God, they were wholly ignorant. Propounding these and other things of the same sort, he has deceived many. But I ought also to state this, which I remember that I myself saw. Once, when this Luna of his was in a certain tower, a great multitude had assembled to see her, and were standing around the tower on all sides; but she was seen by all the people to lean forward, and to look out through all the windows of that tower. Many other wonderful things he did and does; so that men, being astonished at them, think that he himself is the great God.

Chapter 13. Simon Magus: Secret of His Magic.

Now when Niceta and I once asked him to explain to us how these things could be effected by magic art, and what was the nature of that thing, Simon began thus to explain it to us as his associates. 'I have,' said he, 'made the soul of a boy, unsullied and violently slain, and invoked by unutterable adjurations, to assist me; and by it all is done that I command.' 'But,' said I, 'is it possible for a soul to do these things?' He answered: 'I would have you know this, that the soul of man holds the next place after God, when once it is set free from the darkness of his body. And immediately it acquires prescience: wherefore it is invoked for necromancy.' Then I answered: 'Why, then, do not the souls of persons who are slain take vengeance on their slayers?' 'Do you not remember,' said he, 'that I told you, that when it goes out of the body it acquires knowledge of the future?' 'I remember,' said I. 'Well, then,' said he, 'as soon as it goes out of the body, it immediately knows that there is a judgment to come, and that every one shall suffer punishment for those evils that he has done; and therefore they are unwilling to take vengeance on their slayers, because they themselves are enduring torments for their own evil deeds which they had done here, and they know that severer punishments await them in the judgment. Moreover, they are not permitted by the angels who preside over them to go out, or to do anything.' 'Then,' I replied, 'if the angels do not permit them to come hither, or to do what they please, how can the souls obey the magician who invokes them?' 'It is not,' said he, 'that they grant indulgence to the souls that are willing to come: but when the presiding angels are adjured by one greater than themselves, they have the excuse of our violence who adjure them, to permit the souls which we invoke to go out: for they do not sin who suffer violence, but we who impose necessity upon them.' Thereupon Niceta, not able longer to refrain, hastily answered, as indeed I also was about to do, only I wished first to get information from him on several points; but, as I said, Niceta, anticipating me, said: 'And do you not fear the day of judgment, who do violence to angels, and invoke souls, and deceive men, and bargain for divine honour to yourself from men? And how do you persuade us that there shall be no judgment, as some of the Jews confess, and that souls are not immortal, as many suppose, though you see them with your very eyes, and receive from them assurance of the divine judgment.'

Chapter 14. Simon Magus, Professes to Be God.

At those sayings of his Simon grew pale; but after a little, recollecting himself, he thus answered: 'Do not think that I am a man of your race. I am neither magician, nor lover of Luna, nor son of Antonius. For before my mother Rachel and he came together, she, still a virgin, conceived me, while it was in my power to be either small or great, and to appear as a man among men. Therefore I have chosen you first as my friends, for the purpose of trying you, that I may place you first in my heavenly and unspeakable places when I shall have proved you. Therefore I have pretended to be a man, that I might more clearly ascertain if you cherish entire affection towards me.' But when I heard that, judging him indeed to be a wretch, yet wondering at his impudence; and blushing for him, and at the same time fearing lest he should attempt some evil against us, I beckoned to Niceta to feign for a little along with me, and said to him: 'Be not angry with us, corruptible men, O you incorruptible God, but rather accept our affection, and our mind willing to know who God is; for we did not till now know who you are, nor did we perceive that you are he whom we were seeking.'

Chapter 15. Simon Magus, Professed to Have Made a Boy of Air.

As we spoke these and such like words with looks suited to the occasion, this most vain fellow believed that we were deceived; and being thereby the more elated, he added also this: 'I shall now be propitious to you, for the affection which you bear towards me as God; for you loved me while you did not know me, and were seeking me in ignorance. But I would not have you doubt that this is truly to be God, when one is able to become small or great as he pleases; for I am able to appear to man in whatever manner I please. Now, then, I shall begin to unfold to you what is true. Once on a time, I, by my power, turning air into water, and water again into blood, and solidifying it into flesh, formed a new human creature — a boy — and produced a much nobler work than God the Creator. For He created a man from the earth, but I from air — a far more difficult matter; and again I unmade him and restored him to air, but not until I had placed his picture and image in my bed-chamber, as a proof and memorial of my work.' Then we understood that he spoke concerning that boy, whose soul, after he had been slain by violence, he made use of for those services which he required.

Chapter 16. Simon Magus: Hopelessness of His Case.

But Peter, hearing these things, said with tears: Greatly do I wonder at the infinite patience of God, and, on the other hand, at the audacity of human rashness in some. For what further reason can be found to persuade Simon that God judges the unrighteous, since he persuades himself that he employs the obedience of souls for the service of his crimes? But, in truth, he is deluded by demons. Yet, although he is sure by these very things that souls are immortal, and are judged for the deeds which they have done, and although he thinks that he really sees those things which we believe by faith; though, as I said, he is deluded by demons, yet he thinks that he sees the very substance of the soul. How shall such a man, I say, be brought to confess either that he acts wickedly while he occupies such an evil position, or that he is to be judged for those things which he has done, who, knowing the judgment of God, despises it, and shows himself an enemy to God, and dares commit such horrid things? Wherefore it is certain, my brethren, that some oppose the truth and religion of God, not because it appears to them that reason can by no means stand with faith, but because they are either involved in excess of wickedness, or prevented by their own evils, or elated by the swelling of their heart, so that they do not even believe those things which they think that they see with their own eyes.

Chapter 17. Men Enemies to God.

But, inasmuch as inborn affection towards God the Creator seemed to suffice for salvation to those who loved Him, the enemy studies to pervert this affection in men, and to render them hostile and ungrateful to their Creator. For I call heaven and earth to witness, that if God permitted the enemy to rage as much as he desires, all men should have perished long ere now; but for His mercy's sake God does not allow him. But if men would turn their affection towards God, all would doubtless be saved, even if for some faults they might seem to be corrected for righteousness. But now the most of men have been made enemies of God, whose hearts the wicked one has entered, and has turned aside towards himself the affection which God the Creator had implanted in them, that they might have it towards Him. But of the rest, who seemed for a time to be watchful, the enemy, appearing in a phantasy of glory and splendour, and promising them certain great and mighty things, has caused their mind and heart to wander away from God; yet it is for some just reason that he is permitted to accomplish these things.

Chapter 18. Responsibility of Men.

To this Aquila answered: How, then, are men in fault, if the wicked one, transforming himself into the brightness of light, 2 Corinthians 11:14 promises to men greater things than the Creator Himself does? Then Peter answered: I think, says he that nothing is more unjust than this; and now listen while I tell you how unjust it is. If your son, whom you have trained and nourished with all care, and brought to man's estate, should be ungrateful to you, and should leave you and go to another, whom perhaps he may have seen to be richer, and should show to him the honour which he owed to you, and, through hope of greater profit, should deny his birth, and refuse you your paternal rights, would this seem to you right or wicked? Then Aquila answered: It is manifest to all that it would be wicked. Then Peter said: If you say that this would be wicked among men, how much more so is it in the case of God, who, above all men, is worthy of honour from men; whose benefits we not only enjoy, but by whose means and power it is that we began to be when we were not, and whom, if we please, we shall obtain from Him to be for ever in blessedness! In order, therefore, that the unfaithful may be distinguished from the faithful, and the pious from the impious, it has been permitted to the wicked one to use those arts by which the affections of every one towards the true Father may be proved. But if there were in truth some strange God, were it right to leave our own God, who created us, and who is our Father and our Maker, and to pass over to another? God forbid. said Aquila. Then said Peter: How, then, shall we say that the wicked one is the cause of our sin, when this is done by permission of God, that those may be proved and condemned in the day of judgment, who, allured by greater promises, have abandoned their duty towards their true Father and Creator; while those who have kept the faith and the love of their own Father, even with poverty, if so it has befallen, and with tribulation, may enjoy heavenly gifts and immortal dignities in His kingdom. But we shall expound these things more carefully at another time. Meantime I desire to know what Simon did after this.

Chapter 19. Disputation Begun.

And Niceta answered: When he perceived that we had found him out, having spoken to one another concerning his crimes, we left him, and came to Zacchæus, telling him those same things which we have now told to you. But he, receiving us most kindly, and instructing us concerning the faith of our Lord Jesus Christ, enrolled us in the number of the faithful. When Niceta had done speaking, Zacchæus, who had gone out a little before, entered, saying, It is time, O Peter, that you proceed to the disputation; for a great crowd, collected in the court of the house, is awaiting you, in the midst of whom stands Simon, supported by many attendants. Then Peter, when he heard this, ordering me to withdraw for the sake of prayer (for I had not yet been washed from the sins which I had committed in ignorance), said to the rest, Brethren, let us pray that God, for His unspeakable mercy through His Christ, would help me going out on behalf of the salvation of men who have been created by Him. Having said this, and having prayed, he went forth to the court of the house, in which a great multitude of people were assembled; and when he saw them all looking intently on him in profound silence, and Simon the Magician standing in the midst of them like a standard-bearer, he began in manner following.

Chapter 20. The Kingdom of God and His Righteousness.

Peace be to all of you who are prepared to give your right hands to truth: for whosoever are obedient to it seem indeed themselves to confer some favour upon God; whereas they do themselves obtain from Him the gift of His greatest bounty, walking in His paths of righteousness. Wherefore the first duty of all is to inquire into the righteousness of God and His kingdom; Matthew 6:33 His righteousness, that we may be taught to act rightly; His kingdom, that we may know what is the reward appointed for labour and patience; in which kingdom there is indeed a bestowal of eternal good things upon the good, but upon those who have acted contrary to the will of God, a worthy infliction of penalties in proportion to the doings of every one. It becomes you, therefore, while you are here — that is, while you are in the present life — to ascertain the will of God, while there is opportunity also of doing it. For if any one, before he amends his doings, wishes to investigate concerning things which he cannot discover, such investigation will be foolish and ineffectual. For the time is short, and the judgment of God shall be occupied with deeds, not questions. Therefore before all things let us inquire into this, what or in what manner we must act that we may merit to obtain eternal life.

Chapter 21. Righteousness the Way to the Kingdom.

For if we occupy the short time of this life with vain and useless questions, we shall without doubt go into the presence of God empty and void of good works, when, as I have said, our works shall be brought into judgment. For everything has its own time and place. This is the place, this the time of works; the world to come, that of recompenses. That we may not therefore be entangled, by changing the order of places and times, let us inquire, in the first place, what is the righteousness of God; so that, like persons going to set out on a journey, we may be filled with good works as with abundant provision, so that we may be able to come to the kingdom of God, as to a very great city. For to those who think aright, God is manifest even by the operations of the world which He has made, using the evidence of His creation; Romans 1:20 and therefore, since there ought to be no doubt about God, we have now to inquire only about His righteousness and His kingdom. But if our mind suggest to us to make any inquiry concerning secret and hidden things before we inquire into the works of righteousness, we ought to render to ourselves a reason, because if acting well we shall merit to obtain salvation: then, going to God chaste and clean, we shall be filled with the Holy Spirit, and shall know all things that are secret and hidden, without any cavilling of questions; whereas now, even if any one should spend the whole of his life in inquiring into these things, he not only shall not be able to find them, but shall involve himself in greater errors, because he did not first enter through the way of righteousness, and strive to reach the haven of life.

Chapter 22. Righteousness; What It is.

And therefore I advise that His righteousness be first inquired into, that, pursuing our journey through it, and placed in the way of truth, we may be able to find the true Prophet, running not with swiftness of foot, but with goodness of works, and that, enjoying His guidance, we may be under no danger of mistaking the way. For if under His guidance we shall merit to enter that city to which we desire to come, all things concerning which we now inquire we shall see with our eyes, being made, as it were, heirs of all things. Understand, therefore, that the way is this course of our life; the travellers are those who do good works; the gate is the true Prophet, of whom we speak; the city is the kingdom in which dwells the Almighty Father, whom only those can see who are of pure heart. Matthew 5:8 Let us not then think the labour of this journey hard, because at the end of it there shall be rest. For the true Prophet Himself also from the beginning of the world, through the course of time, hastens to rest. For He is present with us at all times; and if at any time it is necessary, He appears and corrects us, that He may bring to eternal life those who obey Him. Therefore this is my judgment, as also it is the pleasure of the true Prophet, that inquiry should first be made concerning righteousness, by those especially who profess that they know God. If therefore any one has anything to propose which he thinks better, let him speak; and when he has spoken, let him hear, but with patience and quietness: for in order to this at the first, by way of salutation, I prayed for peace to you all.

Chapter 23. Simon Refuses Peace.

To this Simon answered: We have no need of your peace; for if there be peace and concord, we shall not be able to make any advance towards the discovery of truth. For robbers and debauchees have peace among themselves, and every wickedness agrees with itself; and if we have met with this view, that for the sake of peace we should give assent to all that is said, we shall confer no benefit upon the hearers; but, on the contrary, we shall impose upon them, and shall depart friends. Wherefore, do not invoke peace, but rather battle, which is the mother of peace; and if you can, exterminate errors. And do not seek for friendship obtained by unfair admissions; for this I would have you know, above all, that when two fight with each other, then there will be peace when one has been defeated and has fallen. And therefore fight as best you can, and do not expect peace without war, which is impossible; or if it can be attained, show us how.

Chapter 24. Peter's Explanation.

To this Peter answered: Hear with all attention, O men, what we say. Let us suppose that this world is a great plain, and that from two states, whose kings are at variance with each other, two generals were sent to fight: and suppose the general of the good king gave this counsel, that both armies should without bloodshed submit to the authority of the better king, whereby all should be safe without danger; but that the opposite general should say, No, but we must fight; that not he who is worthy, but who is stronger, may reign, with those who shall escape;— which, I ask you, would you rather choose? I doubt not but that you would give your hands to the better king, with the safety of all. And I do not now wish, as Simon says that I do, that assent should be given, for the sake of peace, to those things that are spoken amiss but that truth be sought for with quietness and order.

Chapter 25. Principles on Which the Discussion Should Be Conducted.

For some, in the contest of disputations, when they perceive that their error is confuted, immediately begin, for the sake of making good their retreat, to create a disturbance, and to stir up strifes, that it may not be manifest to all that they are defeated; and therefore I frequently entreat that the investigation of the matter in dispute may be conducted with all patience and quietness, so that if perchance anything seem to be not rightly spoken, it may be allowed to go back over it, and explain it more distinctly. For sometimes a thing may be spoken in one way and heard in another, while it is either advanced too obscurely, or not attended to with sufficient care; and on this account I desire that our conversation should be conducted patiently, so that neither should the one snatch it away from the other, nor should the unseasonable speech of one contradicting interrupt the speech of the other; and that we should not cherish the desire of finding fault, but that we should be allowed, as I have said, to go over again what has not been clearly enough spoken, that by fairest examination the knowledge of the truth may become clearer. For we ought to know, that if any one is conquered by the truth, it is not he that is conquered, but the ignorance which is in him, which is the worst of all demons; so that he who can drive it out receives the palm of salvation. For it is our purpose to benefit the hearers, not that we may conquer badly, but that we may be well conquered for the acknowledgment of the truth. For if our speech be actuated by the desire of seeking the truth, even although we shall speak anything imperfectly through human frailty, God in His unspeakable goodness will fill up secretly in the understandings of the hearers those things that are lacking. For He is righteous; and according to the purpose of every one, He enables some to find easily what they seek, while to others He renders even that obscure which is before their eyes. Since, then, the way of God is the way of peace, let us with peace seek the things which are God's. If any one has anything to advance in answer to this, let him do so; but if there is no one who wishes to answer, I shall begin to speak, and I myself shall bring forward what another may object to me, and shall refute it.

Chapter 26. Simon's Interruption.

When therefore Peter had begun to continue his discourse, Simon, interrupting his speech, said: Why do you hasten to speak whatever you please? I understand your tricks. You wish to bring forward those matters whose explanation you have well studied, that you may appear to the ignorant crowd to be speaking well; but I shall not allow you this subterfuge. Now therefore, since you promise, as a brave man, to answer to all that any one chooses to bring forward, be pleased to answer me in the first place. Then Peter said: I am ready, only provided that our discussion may be with peace. Then Simon said: Do not you see, O simpleton, that in pleading for peace you act in opposition to your Master, and that what you propose is not suitable to him who promises that he will overthrow ignorance? Or, if you are right in asking peace from the audience, then your Master was wrong in saying, 'I have not come to send peace on earth, but a sword.' Matthew 10:34 For either you say well, and he not well; or else, if your Master said well, then you not at all well: for you do not understand that your statement is contrary to his, whose disciple you profess yourself to be.

Chapter 27. Questions and Answers.

Then Peter: Neither He who sent me did amiss in sending a sword upon the earth, nor do I act contrary to Him in asking peace of the hearers. But you both unskilfully and rashly find fault with what you do not understand: for you have heard that the Master came not to send peace on earth; but that He also said, 'Blessed are the peace-makers, for they shall be called the very sons of God,' Matthew 5:9 you have not heard. Wherefore my sentiments are not different from those of the Master when I recommend peace, to the keepers of which He assigned blessedness. Then Simon said: In your desire to answer for your Master, O Peter, you have brought a much more serious charge against him, if he himself came not to make peace, yet enjoined upon others to keep it. Where, then, is the consistency of that other saying of his, 'it is enough for the disciple that he be as his master?' Matthew 10:25

Chapter 28. Consistency of Christ's Teaching.

To this Peter answered: Our Master, who was the true Prophet, and ever mindful of Himself, neither contradicted Himself, nor enjoined upon us anything different from what Himself practised. For whereas He said, 'I am not come to send peace on earth, but a sword; and henceforth you shall see father separated from son, son from father, husband from wife and wife from husband, mother from daughter and daughter from mother, brother from brother, father-in-law from daughter-in-law, friend from friend,' all these contain the doctrine of peace; and I will tell you how. At the beginning of His preaching, as wishing to invite and lead all to salvation, and induce them to bear patiently labours and trials, He blessed the poor, and promised that they should obtain the kingdom of heaven for their endurance of poverty, in order that under the influence of such a hope they might bear with equanimity the weight of poverty, despising covetousness; for covetousness is one, and the greatest, of most pernicious sins. But He promised also that the hungry and the thirsty should be satisfied with the eternal blessings of righteousness, in order that they might bear poverty patiently, and not be led by it to undertake any unrighteous work. In like manner, also, He said that the pure in heart are blessed, and that thereby they should see God, in order that every one desiring so great a good might keep himself from evil and polluted thoughts.

Chapter 29. Peace and Strife.

Thus, therefore, our Master, inviting His disciples to patience, impressed upon them that the blessing of peace was also to be preserved with the labour of patience. But, on the other hand, He mourned over those who lived in riches and luxury, who bestowed nothing upon the poor; proving that they must render an account, because they did not pity their neighbours, even when they were in poverty, whom they ought to love as themselves. And by such sayings as these He brought some indeed to obey Him, but others He rendered hostile. The believers therefore, and the obedient, He charges to have peace among themselves. and says to them, 'Blessed are the peacemakers, for they shall be called the very sons of God.' Matthew 5:9 But to those who not only did not believe, but set themselves in opposition to His doctrine, He proclaims the war of the word and of confutation, and says that 'henceforth you shall see son separated from father, and husband from wife, and daughter from mother, and brother from brother, and daughter-in-law from mother-in-law, and a man's foes shall be they of his own house.' For in every house, when there begins to be a difference between believer and unbeliever, there is necessarily a contest: the unbelievers, on the one hand, fighting against the faith; and the believers on the other, confuting the old error and the vices of sins in them.

Chapter 30. Peace to the Sons of Peace.

In like manner, also, during the last period of His teaching, He wages war against the scribes and Pharisees, charging them with evil deeds and unsound doctrine, and with hiding the key of knowledge which they had handed down to them from Moses, by which the gate of the heavenly kingdom might be opened. But when our Master sent us forth to preach, He commanded us, that into whatsoever city or house we should enter, we should say, 'Peace be to this house.' 'And if,' said He, 'a son of peace be there, your peace shall come upon him; but if there be not, your peace shall return to you.' Also that, going out from that house or city, we should shake off upon them the very dust which adhered to our feet. 'But it shall be more tolerable for the land of Sodom and Gomorrha in the day of judgment than for that city or house.' This indeed He commanded to be done at length, if first the word of truth be preached in the city or house, whereby they who receive the faith of the truth may become sons of peace and sons of God; and those who will not receive it may be convicted as enemies of peace and of God.

Chapter 31. Peace and War.

Thus, therefore, we, observing the commands of our Master, first offer peace to our hearers, that the way of salvation may be known without any tumult. But if any one do not receive the words of peace, nor acquiesce in the truth, we know how to direct against him the war of the word, and to rebuke him sharply by confuting his ignorance and charging home upon him his sins. Therefore of necessity we offer peace, that if any one is a son of peace, our peace may come upon him; but from him who makes himself an enemy of peace, our peace shall return to ourselves. We do not therefore, as you say, propose peace by agreement with the wicked, for indeed we should straightway have given you the right hand; but only in order that, through our discussing quietly and patiently, it might be more easily ascertained by the hearers which is the true speech. But if you differ and disagree with yourself, how shall you stand? He must of necessity fall who is divided in himself; 'for every kingdom divided against itself shall not stand.' Matthew 12:25 If you have anything to say to this, say on.

Chapter 32. Simon's Challenge.

Then said Simon: I am astonished at your folly. For you so propound the words of your Master, as if it were held to be certain concerning him that he is a prophet; while I can very easily prove that he often contradicted himself. In short, I shall refute you from those words which you have yourself brought forward. For you say, that he said that every kingdom or every city divided in itself shall not stand; and elsewhere you say, that he said that he would send a sword, that he might separate those who are in one house, so that son shall be divided from father, daughter from mother, brother from brother; so that if there be five in one house, three shall be divided against two, and two against three. Luke 12:51-53 If, then, everything that is divided falls, he who makes divisions furnishes causes of falling; and if he is such, assuredly he is wicked. Answer this if you can.

Chapter 33. Authority.

Then Peter: Do not rashly take exception, O Simon, against the things which you do not understand. In the first place, I shall answer your assertion, that I set forth the words of my Master, and from them resolve matters about which there is still doubt. Our Lord, when He sent us apostles to preach, enjoined us to teach all nations Matthew 28:19-20 the things which were committed to us. We cannot therefore speak those things as they were spoken by Himself. For our commission is not to speak, but to teach those things, and from them to show how every one of them rests upon truth. Nor, again, are we permitted to speak anything of our own. For we are sent; and of necessity he who is sent delivers the message as he has been ordered, and sets forth the will of the sender. For if I should speak anything different from what He who sent me enjoined me, I should be a false apostle, not saying what I am commanded to say, but what seems good to myself. Whoever does this, evidently wishes to show himself to be better than he is by whom he is sent, and without doubt is a traitor. If, on the contrary, he keeps by the things that he is commanded, and brings forward most clear assertions of them, it will appear that he is accomplishing the work of an apostle; and it is by striving to fulfil this that I displease you. Blame me not, therefore, because I bring forward the words of Him who sent me. But if there is anything in them that is not fairly spoken, you have liberty to confute me; but this can in no way be done, for He is a prophet, and cannot be contrary to Himself. But if you do not think that He is a prophet, let this be first inquired into.

Chapter 34. Order of Proof.

Then said Simon: I have no need to learn this from you, but how these things agree with one another. For if he shall be shown to be inconsistent, he shall be proved at the same time not to be a prophet. Then says Peter: But if I first show Him to be a prophet, it will follow that what seems to be inconsistency is not such. For no one can be proved to be a prophet merely by consistency, because it is possible for many to attain this; but if consistency does not make a prophet, much more inconsistency does not. Because, therefore, there are many things which to some seem inconsistent, which yet have consistency in them on a more profound investigation; as also other things which seem to have consistency, but which, being more carefully discussed, are found to be inconsistent; for this reason I do not think there is any better way to judge of these things than to ascertain in the first instance whether He be a prophet who has spoken those things which appear to be inconsistent. For it is evident that, if He be found a prophet, those things which seem to be contradictory must have consistency, but are misunderstood. Concerning these things, therefore, proofs will be properly demanded. For we apostles are sent to expound the sayings and affirm the judgments of Him who has sent us; but we are not commissioned to say anything of our own, but to unfold the truth, as I have said, of His words.

Chapter 35. How Error Cannot Stand with Truth.

Then Simon said: Instruct us, therefore, how it can be consistent that he who causes divisions, which divisions cause those who are divided to fall, can either seem to be good, or to have come for the salvation of men. Then Peter said: I will tell you how our Master said that every kingdom and every house divided against itself cannot stand; and whereas He Himself did this, see how it makes for salvation. By the word of truth He certainly divides the kingdom of the world, which is founded in error, and every home in it, that error may fall, and truth may reign. But if it happen to any house, that error, being introduced by any one, divides the truth, then, where error has gained a footing, it is certain that truth cannot stand. Then Simon said: But it is uncertain whether your master divides error or truth. Then Peter: That belongs to another question; but if you are agreed that everything which is divided falls, it remains that I show, if only you will hear in peace, that our Jesus has divided and dispelled error by teaching truth.

Chapter 36. Altercation.

Then said Simon: Do not repeat again and again your talk of peace, but expound briefly what it is that you think or believe. Peter answered: Why are you afraid of hearing frequently of peace? For do you not know that peace is the perfection of law? For wars and disputes spring from sins; and where there is no sin, there is peace of soul; but where there is peace, truth is found in disputations, righteousness in works. Then Simon: You seem to me not to be able to profess what you think. Then Peter: I shall speak, but according to my own judgment, not under constraint of your tricks. For I desire that what is salutary and profitable be brought to the knowledge of all and therefore I shall not delay to state it as briefly as possible. There is one God; and He is the creator of the world, a righteous judge, rendering to every one at some time or other according to his deeds. But now for the assertion of these things I know that countless thousands of words can be called forth.

Chapter 37. Simon's Subtlety.

Then Simon said: I admire, indeed, the quickness of your wit, yet I do not embrace the error of your faith. For you have wisely foreseen that you may be contradicted; and you have even politely confessed, that for the assertion of these things countless thousands of words will be called forth, for no one agrees with the profession of your faith. In short, as to there being one God, and the world being His work, who can receive this doctrine? Neither, I think, any one of the Pagans, even if he be an unlearned man, and certainly no one of the philosophers; but not even the rudest and most wretched of the Jews, nor I myself, who am well acquainted with their law. Then Peter said: Put aside the opinions of those who are not here, and tell us face to face what is your own. Then Simon said: I can state what I really think; but this consideration makes me reluctant to do so, that if I say what is neither acceptable to you, nor seems right to this unskilled rabble, you indeed, as confounded, will straightway shut your ears, that they may not be polluted with blasphemy, forsooth, and will take to flight because you cannot find an answer; while the unreasoning populace will assent to you, and embrace you as one teaching those things which are commonly received among them; and will curse me, as professing things new and unheard of, and instilling my error into the minds of others.

Chapter 38. Simon's Creed.

Then Peter: Are not you making use of long preambles, as you accused us of doing, because you have no truth to bring forward? For if you have, begin without circumlocution, if you have so much confidence. And if, indeed, what you say be displeasing to any one of the hearers, he will withdraw; and those who remain shall be compelled by your assertion to approve what is true. Begin, therefore, to expound what seems to you to be right. Then Simon said: I say that there are many gods; but that there is one incomprehensible and unknown to all, and that He is the God of all these gods. Then Peter answered: This God whom you assert to be incomprehensible and unknown to all, can you prove His existence from the Scriptures of the Jews, which are held to be of authority, or from some others of which we are all ignorant, or from the Greek authors, or from your own writings? Certainly you are at liberty to speak from whatever writings you please, yet so that you first show that they are prophetic; for so their authority will be held without question.

Chapter 39. Argument for Polytheism.

Then Simon said: I shall make use of assertions from the law of the Jews only. For it is manifest to all who take interest in religion, that this law is of universal authority, yet that every one receives the understanding of this law according to his own judgment. For it has so been written by Him who created the world, that the faith of things is made to depend upon it. Whence, whether any one wishes to bring forward truth, or any one to bring forward falsehood, no assertion will be received without this law. Inasmuch, therefore, as my knowledge is most fully in accordance with the law, I rightly declared that there are many gods, of whom one is more eminent than the rest, and incomprehensible, even He who is God of gods. But that there are many gods, the law itself informs me. For, in the first place, it says this in the passage where one in the figure of a serpent speaks to Eve, the first woman, 'On the day you eat of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, you shall be as gods,' Genesis 3:5 that is, as those who made man; and after they have tasted of the tree, God Himself testifies, saying to the rest of the gods, 'Behold, Adam has become as one of us;' Genesis 3:22 thus, therefore, it is manifest that there were many gods engaged in the making of man. Also, whereas at the first God said to the other gods, 'Let us make man after our image and likeness;' Genesis 1:26 also His saying, 'Let us drive him out;' Genesis 3:22 and again, 'Come, let us go down, and confound their language;' Genesis 11:7 all these things indicate that there are many gods. But this also is written, 'You shall not curse the gods, nor curse the chief of your people;' Exodus 22:28 and again this writing, 'God alone led them, and there was no strange god with them,' Deuteronomy 32:12 shows that there are many gods. There are also many other testimonies which might be adduced from the law, not only obscure, but plain, by which it is taught that there are many gods. One of these was chosen by lot, that he might be the god of the Jews. But it is not of him that I speak, but of that God who is also his God, whom even the Jews themselves did not know. For he is not their God, but the God of those who know him.

Chapter 40. Peter's Answer.

When Peter had heard this, he answered: Fear nothing, Simon: for, behold, we have neither shut our ears, nor fled; but we answer with words of truth to those things which you have spoken falsely, asserting this first, that there is one God, even the God of the Jews, who is the only God, the Creator of heaven and earth, who is also the God of all those whom you call gods. If, then, I shall show you that none is superior to Him, but that He Himself is above all, you will confess that your error is above all. Then Simon said: Why, indeed, though I should be unwilling to confess it, would not the hearers who stand by charge me with unwillingness to profess the things that are true?

Chapter 41. The Answer, Continued.

Listen, then, says Peter, that you may know, first of all, that even if there are many gods, as you say, they are subject to the God of the Jews, to whom no one is equal, than whom no one can be greater; for it is written that the prophet Moses thus spoke to the Jews: 'The Lord your God is the God of gods, and the Lord of lords, the great God.' Deuteronomy 10:17 Thus, although there are many that are called gods, yet He who is the God of the Jews is alone called the God of gods. For not every one that is called God is necessarily God. Indeed, even Moses is called a god to Pharaoh, Exodus 7:1 and it is certain that he was a man; and judges were called gods, and it is evident that they were mortal. The idols also of the Gentiles are called gods, and we all know that they are not; but this has been inflicted as a punishment on the wicked, that because they would not acknowledge the true God, they should regard as God whatever form or image should occur to them. Because they refused to receive the knowledge of the One who, as I said, is God of all, therefore it is permitted to them to have as gods those who can do nothing for their worshippers. For what can either dead images or living creatures confer upon men, since the power of all things is with One?

Chapter 42. Guardian Angels.

Therefore the name God is applied in three ways: either because he to whom it is given is truly God, or because he is the servant of him who is truly; and for the honour of the sender, that his authority may be full, he that is sent is called by the name of him who sends, as is often done in respect of angels: for when they appear to a man, if he is a wise and intelligent man, he asks the name of him who appears to him, that he may acknowledge at once the honour of the sent, and the authority of the sender. For every nation has an angel, to whom God has committed the government of that nation; and when one of these appears, although he be thought and called God by those over whom he presides, yet, being asked, he does not give such testimony to himself. For the Most High God, who alone holds the power of all things, has divided all the nations of the earth into seventy-two parts, and over these He has appointed angels as princes. But to the one among the archangels who is greatest, was committed the government of those who, before all others, received the worship and knowledge of the Most High God. But holy men also, as we have said, are made gods to the wicked, as having received the power of life and death over them, as we mentioned above with respect to Moses and the judges. Wherefore it is also written concerning them, 'You shall not curse the gods, and you shall not curse the prince of your people.' Exodus 22:28 Thus the princes of the several nations are called gods. But Christ is God of princes, who is Judge of all. Therefore neither angels, nor men, nor any creature, can be truly gods, forasmuch as they are placed under authority, being created and changeable: angels, for they were not, and are; men, for they are mortal; and every creature, for it is capable of dissolution, if only He dissolve it who made it. And therefore He alone is the true God, who not only Himself lives, but also bestows life upon others, which He can also take away when it pleases Him.

Chapter 43. No God But Jehovah.

Wherefore the Scripture exclaims in name of the God of the Jews, saying, 'Behold, behold, seeing that I am God, and there is none else besides me, I will kill, and I will make alive; I will smite, and I will heal; and there is none who can deliver out of my hands.' Deuteronomy 32:39 See therefore how, by some ineffable virtue, the Scripture, opposing the future errors of those who should affirm that either in heaven or on earth there is any other god besides Him who is the God of the Jews, decides thus: 'The Lord your God is one God, in heaven above, and in the earth beneath; and besides Him there is none else.' Deuteronomy 4:39 How, then, have you dared to say that there is any other God besides Him who is the God of the Jews? And again the Scripture says, 'Behold, to the Lord your God belong the heaven, and the heaven of heavens, the earth, and all things that are in them: nevertheless I have chosen your fathers, that I might love them, and you after them.' Deuteronomy 10:14-15 Thus that judgment is supported by the Scripture on every side, that He who created the world is the true and only God.

Chapter 44. The Serpent, the Author of Polytheism.

But even if there be others, as we have said, who are called gods, they are under the power of the God of the Jews; for thus says the Scripture to the Jews, 'The Lord our God, He is God of gods, and Lord of lords.' Deuteronomy 10:17 Him alone the Scripture also commands to be worshipped, saying, 'You shall worship the Lord your God, and Him only shall you serve;' and, 'Hear, O Israel: the Lord your God is one God.' Deuteronomy 6:4 Yea, also the saints, filled with the Spirit of God, and bedewed with the drops of His mercy, cried out, saying, 'Who is like You among the gods? O Lord, who is like You?' And again, 'Who is God, but the Lord; and who is God, but our Lord.' Therefore Moses, when he saw that the people were advancing, by degrees initiated them in the understanding of the monarchy and the faith of one God, as he says in the following words: 'You shall not make mention of the names of other gods;' doubtless remembering with what penalty the serpent was visited, which had first named gods. For it is condemned to feed upon dust, and is judged worthy of such food, for this cause, that it first of all introduced the name of gods into the world. But if you also wish to introduce many gods, see that you partake not the serpent's doom.

Chapter 45. Polytheism Inexcusable.

For be sure of this, that you shall not have us participators in this attempt; nor will we suffer ourselves to be deceived by you. For it will not serve us for an excuse in the judgment, if we say that you deceived us; because neither could it excuse the first woman, that she had unhappily believed the serpent; but she was condemned to death, because she believed badly. For this cause therefore, Moses, also commending the faith of one God to the people, says, 'Take heed to yourself, that you be not seduced from the Lord your God.' Deuteronomy 8:11 Observe that he makes use of the same word which the first woman also made use of in excusing herself, saying that she was seduced; but it profited her nothing. But over and above all this, even if some true prophet should arise, who should perform signs and miracles, but should wish to persuade us to worship other gods besides the God of the Jews, we should never be able to believe him. For so the divine law has taught us, handing down a secret injunction more purely by means of tradition, for thus it says: 'If there arise among you a prophet, or one dreaming a dream, and give you signs or wonders, and these signs or wonders come to pass, and he say to you, Let us go and worship strange gods, whom you know not; you shall not hear the words of that prophet, nor the dream of that dreamer, because proving he has proved you, that he may see if you love the Lord your God.' Deuteronomy 13:1-3

Chapter 46. Christ Acknowledged the God of the Jews.

Wherefore also our Lord, who wrought signs and wonders, preached the God of the Jews; and therefore we are right in believing what He preached. But as for you, even if you were really a prophet, and performed signs and wonders, as you promise to do, if you were to announce other gods besides Him who is the true God, it would be manifest that you were raised up as a trial to the people of God; and therefore you can by no means be believed. For He alone is the true God, who is the God of the Jews; and for this reason our Lord Jesus Christ did not teach them that they must inquire after God, for Him they knew well already, but that they must seek His kingdom and righteousness, Matthew 6:33 which the scribes and Pharisees, having received the key of knowledge, had not shut in, but shut out. Luke 11:52 For if they had been ignorant of the true God, surely He would never have left the knowledge of this thing, which was the chief of all, and blamed them for small and little things, as for enlarging their fringes, and claiming the uppermost rooms in feasts, and praying standing in the highways, and such like things; which assuredly, in comparison of this great charge, ignorance of God, seem to be small and insignificant matters.

Chapter 47. Simon's Cavil.

To this Simon replied: From the words of your master I shall refute you, because even he introduces to all men a certain God who was unknown. For although both Adam knew the God who was his creator, and the maker of the world; and Enoch knew him, inasmuch as he was translated by him; and Noah, since he was ordered by him to construct the ark; and although Abraham, and Isaac, and Jacob, and Moses, and all, even every people and all nations, know the maker of the world, and confess him to be a God, yet your Jesus, who appeared long after the patriarchs, says: 'No one knows the Son, but the Father; neither knows any one the Father, but the Son, and he to whom the Son has been pleased to reveal Him.' Thus, therefore, even your Jesus confesses that there is another God, incomprehensible and unknown to all.

Chapter 48. Peter's Answer.

Then Peter says: You do not perceive that you are making statements in opposition to yourself. For if our Jesus also knows Him whom you call the unknown God, then He is not known by you alone. Yea, if our Jesus knows Him, then Moses also, who prophesied that Jesus should come, assuredly could not himself be ignorant of Him. For he was a prophet; and he who prophesied of the Son doubtless knew the Father. For if it is in the option of the Son to reveal the Father to whom He will, then the Son, who has been with the Father from the beginning, and through all generations, as He revealed the Father to Moses, so also to the other prophets; but if this be so, it is evident that the Father has not been unknown to any of them. But how could the Father be revealed to you, who do not believe in the Son, since the Father is known to none except him to whom the Son is pleased to reveal Him? But the Son reveals the Father to those who honour the Son as they honour the Father. John 5:23

Chapter 49. The Supreme Light.

Then Simon said: Remember that you said that God has a son, which is doing Him wrong; for how can He have a son, unless He is subject to passions, like men or animals? But on these points there is not time now to show your profound folly, for I hasten to make a statement concerning the immensity of the supreme light; and so now listen. My opinion is, that there is a certain power of immense and ineffable light, whose greatness may be held to be incomprehensible, of which power even the maker of the world is ignorant, and Moses the lawgiver, and Jesus your master.

Chapter 50. Simon's Presumption.

Then Peter: Does it not seem to you to be madness, that any one should take upon himself to assert that there is another God than the God of all; and should say that he supposes there is a certain power, and should presume to affirm this to others, before he himself is sure of what he says? Is any one so rash as to believe your words, of which he sees that you are yourself doubtful, and to admit that there is a certain power unknown to God the Creator, and to Moses, and the prophets, and the law, and even to Jesus our Master, which power is so good, that it will not make itself known to any but to one only, and that one such an one as you! Then, further, if that is a new power, why does it not confer upon us some new sense, in addition to those five which we possess, that by that new sense, bestowed upon us by it, we may be able to receive and understand itself which is new? Or if it cannot bestow such a sense upon us, how has it bestowed it upon you? Or if it has revealed itself to you, why not also to us? But if you of yourself understand things which not even the prophets were able to perceive or understand, come, tell us what each one of us is thinking now; for if there is such a spirit in you that you know those things which are above the heavens, which are unknown to all, and incomprehensible by all, much more easily do you know the thoughts of men upon the earth. But if you cannot know the thoughts of us who are standing here, how can you say that you know those things which, you assert, are known to none?

Chapter 51. The Sixth Sense.

But believe me, that you could never know what light is unless you had received both vision and understanding from light itself; so also in other things. Hence, having received understanding, you are framing in imagination something greater and more sublime, as if dreaming, but deriving all your hints from those five senses, to whose Giver you are unthankful. But be sure of this, that until you find some new sense which is beyond those five which we all enjoy, you cannot assert the existence of a new God. Then Simon answered: Since all things that exist are in accordance with those five senses, that power which is more excellent than all cannot add anything new. Then Peter said: It is false; for there is also a sixth sense, namely that of foreknowledge: for those five senses are capable of knowledge, but the sixth is that of foreknowledge: and this the prophets possessed. How, then, can you know a God who is unknown to all, who do not know the prophetic sense, which is that of prescience? Then Simon began to say: This power of which I speak, incomprehensible and more excellent than all, ay, even than that God who made the world, neither any of the angels has known, nor of the demons, nor of the Jews, nay, nor any creature which subsists by means of God the creator. How, then, could that creator's law teach me that which the creator himself did not know, since neither did the law itself know it, that it might teach it?

Chapter 52. Reductio Ad Absurdum

Then Peter said: I wonder how you have been able to learn more from the law than the law was able to know or to teach; and how you say that you adduce proofs from the law of those things which you are pleased to assert, when you declare that neither the law, nor He who gave the law — that is, the Creator of the world — knows those things of which you speak! But this also I wonder at, how you, who alone know these things, should be standing here now with us all, circumscribed by the limits of this small court. Then Simon, seeing Peter and all the people laughing, said: Do you laugh, Peter, while so great and lofty matters are under discussion? Then said Peter: Be not enraged, Simon, for we are doing no more than keeping our promise: for we are neither shutting our ears, as you said, nor did we take to flight as soon as we heard you propound your unutterable things; but we have not even stirred from the place. For indeed you do not even propound things that have any resemblance to truth, which might to a certain extent frighten us. Yet, at all events, disclose to us the meaning of this saying, how from the law you have learned of a God whom the law itself does not know, and of whom He who gave the law is ignorant. Then Simon said: If you have done laughing, I shall prove it by clear assertions. Then Peter said: Assuredly I shall give over, that I may learn from you how you have learned from the law what neither the law nor the God of the law Himself knows.

Chapter 53. Simon's Blasphemy.

Then says Simon: Listen: it is manifest to all, and ascertained in a manner of which no account can be given, that there is one God, who is better than all, from whom all that is took its beginning; whence also of necessity, all things that are after him are subject to him, as the chief and most excellent of all. When, therefore, I had ascertained that the God who created the world, according to what the law teaches, is in many respects weak, whereas weakness is utterly incompatible with a perfect God, and I saw that he is not perfect, I necessarily concluded that there is another God who is perfect. For this God, as I have said, according to what the writing of the law teaches, is shown to be weak in many things. In the first place, because the man whom he formed was not able to remain such as he had intended him to be; and because he cannot be good who gave a law to the first man, that he should eat of all the trees of paradise, but that he should not touch the tree of knowledge; and if he should eat of it, he should die. For why should he forbid him to eat, and to know what is good and what evil, that, knowing, he might shun the evil and choose the good? But this he did not permit; and because he ate in violation of the commandment, and discovered what is good, and learned for the sake of honour to cover his nakedness (for he perceived it to be unseemly to stand naked before his Creator), he condemns to death him who had learned to do honour to God, and curses the serpent who had shown him these things. But truly, if man was to be injured by this means, why did he place the cause of injury in paradise at all? But if that which he placed in paradise was good, it is not the part of one that is good to restrain another from good.

Chapter 54. How Simon Learned from the Law What the Law Does Not Teach.

Thus then, since he who made man and the world is, according to what the law relates, imperfect, we are given to understand, without doubt, that there is another who is perfect. For it is of necessity that there be one most excellent of all, on whose account also every creature keeps its rank. Whence also I, knowing that it is every way necessary that there be some one more benignant and more powerful than that imperfect God who gave the law, understanding what is perfect from comparison of the imperfect, understood even from the Scripture that God who is not mentioned there. And in this way I was able, O Peter, to learn from the law what the law did not know. But even if the law had not given indications from which it might be gathered that the God who made the world is imperfect, it was still possible for me to infer from those evils which are done in this world, and are not corrected, either that its creator is powerless, if he cannot correct what is done amiss; or else, if he does not wish to remove the evils, that he is himself evil; but if he neither can nor will, that he is neither powerful nor good. And from this it cannot but be concluded that there is another God more excellent and more powerful than all. If you have anything to say to this, say on.

Chapter 55. Simon's Objections Turned Against Himself.

Peter answered: O Simon, they are wont to conceive such absurdities against God who do not read the law with the instruction of masters, but account themselves teachers, and think that they can understand the law, though he has not explained it to them who has learned of the Master. Nevertheless now, that we also may seem to follow the book of the law according to your apprehension of it; inasmuch as you say that the creator of the world is shown to be both impotent and evil, how is it that you do not see that that power of yours, which you say is superior to all, fails and lies under the very same charges? For the very same thing may be said of it, that it is either powerless, since it does not correct those things which here are done amiss; or if it can and will not, it is evil; or if it neither can nor will, then it is both impotent and imperfect. Whence that new power of yours is not only found liable to a similar charge, but even to a worse one, if, in addition to all these things, it is believed to be, when it is not. For He who created the world, His existence is manifest by His very operation in creating the world, as you yourself also confess. But this power which you say that you alone know, affords no indication of itself, by which we might perceive, at least, that it is, and subsists.

Chapter 56. No God Above the Creator.

What kind of conduct, then, would it be that we should forsake God, in whose world we live and enjoy all things necessary for life, and follow I know not whom, from whom we not only obtain no good, but cannot even know that he exists? Nor truly does he exist. For whether you call him light, and brighter than that light which we see, you borrow that very name from the Creator of the world; or whether you say that he is a substance above all, you derive from Him the idea with enlargement of speech. Whether you make mention of mind, or goodness, or life, or whatever else, you borrow the words from Him. Since, then, you have nothing new concerning that power you speak of, not only as regards understanding, but even in respect of naming him, how do you introduce a new God, for whom you cannot even find a new name? For not only is the Creator of the world called a Power, but even the ministers of His glory, and all the heavenly host. Do you not then think it better that we should follow our Creator God, as a Father who trains us and endows us as He knows how? But if, as you say, there be some God more benignant than all, it is certain that he will not be angry with us; or if he be angry, he is evil. For if our God is angry and punishes, He is not evil, but righteous, for He corrects and amends His own sons. But he who has no concern with us, if he shall punish us, how should he be good? Inflicting punishments upon us because we have not been drawn by vain imaginations to forsake our own Father and follow him, how can you assert that he is so good, when he cannot be regarded as even just?

Chapter 57. Simon's Inconsistency.

Then Simon: Do you so far err, Peter, as not to know that our souls were made by that good God, the most excellent of all, but they have been brought down as captives into this world? To this Peter answered: Then he is not unknown by all, as you said a little while ago; and yet how did the good God permit his souls to be taken captive, if he be a power over all? Then Simon said: He sent God the creator to make the world; and he, when he had made it, gave out that himself was God. Then Peter said: Then he is not, as you said, unknown to Him who made the world; nor are souls ignorant of him, if indeed they were stolen away from him. To whom, then, can he be unknown, if both the Creator of the world know him, as having been sent by him; and all souls know him, as having been violently withdrawn from him? Then, further, I wish you would tell us whether he who sent the creator of the world did not know that he would not keep faith? For if he did not know it, then he was not prescient; while if he foreknew it, and suffered it, he is himself guilty of this deed, since he did not prevent it; but if he could not, then he is not omnipotent. But if, knowing it as good, he did not prohibit it, he is found to be better, who presumed to do that which he who sent him did not know to be good.

Chapter 58. Simon's God Unjust.

Then Simon said: He receives those who will come to him, and does them good. Peter answered: But there is nothing new in this; for He whom you acknowledge to be the Creator of the world also does so. Then Simon: But the good God bestows salvation if he is only acknowledged; but the creator of the world demands also that the law be fulfilled. Then said Peter: He saves adulterers and men-slayers, if they know him; but good, and sober, and merciful persons, if they do not know him, in consequence of their having no information concerning him, he does not save! Great and good truly is he whom you proclaim, who is not so much the saviour of the evil, as he is one who shows no mercy to the good. Then Simon: It is truly very difficult for man to know him, as long as he is in the flesh; for blacker than all darkness, and heavier than all clay, is this body with which the soul is surrounded. Then says Peter: That good God of yours demands things which are difficult; but He who is truly God seeks easier things. Let him then, since he is so good, leave us with our Father and Creator; and when once we depart from the body, and leave that darkness that you speak of, we shall more easily know Him; and then the soul shall better understand that God is its Creator, and shall remain with Him, and shall no more be harassed with diverse imaginations; nor shall wish to betake itself to another power, which is known to none but Simon only, and which is of such goodness that no one can come to it, unless he be first guilty of impiety towards his own father! I know not how this power can be called either good or just, which no one can please except by acting impiously towards him by whom he was made!

Chapter 59. The Creator Our Father.

Then Simon: It is not impious for the sake of greater profit and advantage to flee to him who is of richer glory. Then Peter: If, as you say, it is not impious to flee to a stranger, it is at all events much more pious to remain with our own father, even if he be poor. But if you do not think it impious to leave our father, and flee to another, as being better than he; and you do not believe that our Creator will take this amiss; much more the good God will not be angry, because, when we were strangers to him, we have not fled to him, but have remained with our own Creator. Yea, I think he will rather commend us the more for this, that we have kept faith with God our Creator; for he will consider that, if we had been his creatures, we should never have been seduced by the allurements of any other to forsake him. For if any one, allured by richer promises, shall leave his own father and betake himself to a stranger, it may be that he will leave him in his turn, and go to another who shall promise him greater things, and this the rather because he is not his son, since he could leave even him who by nature was his father. Then Simon said: But what if souls are from him, and do not know him, and he is truly their father?

Chapter 60. The Creator the Supreme God.

Then Peter said: You represent him as weak enough. For if, as you say, he is more powerful than all, it can never be believed the weaker wrenched the spoils from the stronger. Luke 11:22 Or if God the Creator was able by violence to bring down souls into this world, how can it be that, when they are separated from the body and freed from the bonds of captivity, the good God shall call them to the sufferance of punishment, on the ground that they, either through his remissness or weakness, were dragged away to this place, and were involved in the body, as in the darkness of ignorance? You seem to me not to know what a father and a God is: but I could tell you both whence souls are, and when and how they were made; but it is not permitted to me now to disclose these things to you, who are in such error in respect of the knowledge of God. Then said Simon: A time will come when you shall be sorry that you did not understand me speaking of the ineffable power. Then said Peter: Give us then, as I have often said, as being yourself a new God, or as having yourself come down from him, some new sense, by means of which we may know that new God of whom you speak; for those five senses, which God our Creator has given us, keep faith to their own Creator, and do not perceive that there is any other God, for so their nature necessitates them.

Chapter 61. Imagination.

To this Simon answered: Apply your mind to those things which I am going to say, and cause it, walking in peaceable paths, to attain to those things which I shall demonstrate. Listen now, therefore. Did you never in thought reach forth your mind into regions or islands situated far away, and remain so fixed in them, that you could not even see the people that were before you, or know where yourself were sitting, by reason of the delightfulness of those things on which you were gazing? And Peter said: It is true, Simon, this has often occurred to me. Then Simon said: In this way now reach forth your sense into heaven, yea above the heaven, and behold that there must be some place beyond the world, or outside the world, in which there is neither heaven nor earth, and where no shadow of these things produces darkness; and consequently, since there are neither bodies in it, nor darkness occasioned by bodies, there must of necessity be immense light; and consider of what sort that light must be, which is never succeeded by darkness. For if the light of this sun fills this whole world, how great do you suppose that bodiless and infinite light to be? So great, doubtless, that this light of the sun would seem to be darkness and not light, in comparison.

Chapter 62. Peter's Experience of Imagination.

When Simon thus spoke, Peter answered: Now listen patiently concerning both these matters, that is, concerning the example of stretching out the senses, and concerning the immensity of light. I know that I myself, O Simon, have sometimes in thought extended my sense, as you say, into regions and islands situated afar off, and have seen them with my mind not less than if it had been with my eyes. When I was at Capernaum, occupied in the taking of fishes, and sat upon a rock, holding in my hand a hook attached to a line, and fitted for deceiving the fishes, I was so absorbed that I did not feel a fish adhering to it while my mind eagerly ran through my beloved Jerusalem, to which I had frequently gone up, waking, for the sake of offerings and prayers. But I was accustomed also to admire this Cæsarea, hearing of it from others, and to long to see it; and I seemed to myself to see it, although I had never been in it; and I thought of it what was suitable to be thought of a great city, its gates, walls, baths, streets, lanes, markets, and the like, in accordance with what I had seen in other cities; and to such an extent was I delighted with the intentness of such inspection, that, as you said, I neither saw one who was present and standing by me, nor knew where myself was sitting. Then said Simon: Now you say well.

Chapter 63. Peter's Reverie.

Then Peter: In short, when I did not perceive, through the occupation of my mind, that I had caught a very large fish which was attached to the hook, and that although it was dragging the hook-line from my hand, my brother Andrew, who was sitting by me, seeing me in a reverie and almost ready to fall, thrusting his elbow into my side as if he would awaken me from sleep, said: 'Do you not see, Peter, what a large fish you have caught? Are you out of your senses, that you are thus in a stupor of astonishment? Tell me, What is the matter with you?' But I was angry with him for a little, because he had withdrawn me from the delight of those things which I was contemplating; then I answered that I was not suffering from any malady, but that I was mentally gazing on the beloved Jerusalem, and at the same time on Cæsarea; and that, while I was indeed with him in the body, in my mind I was wholly carried away there. But he, I know not whence inspired, uttered a hidden and secret word of truth.

Chapter 64. Andrew's Rebuke.

'Give over,' says he, 'O Peter. What is it that you are doing? For those who are beginning to be possessed with a demon, or to be disturbed in their minds, begin in this way. They are first carried away by fancies to some pleasant and delightful things, then they are poured out in vain and fond motions towards things which have no existence. Now this happens from a certain disease of mind, by reason of which they see not the things which are, but long to bring to their sight those which are not. But thus it happens also to those who are suffering phrenzy, and seem to themselves to see many images, because their soul, being torn and withdrawn from its place by excess of cold or of heat, suffers a failure of its natural service. But those also who are in distress through thirst, when they fall asleep, seem to themselves to see rivers and fountains, and to drink; but this befalls them through being distressed by the dryness of the unmoistened body. Wherefore it is certain that this occurs through some ailment either of the soul or body.'

Chapter 65. Fallacy of Imagination.

In short, that you may receive the faith of the matter; concerning Jerusalem, which I had often seen, I told my brother what places and what gatherings of people I had seemed to myself to see. But also concerning Cæsarea, which I had never seen, I nevertheless contended that it was such as I had conceived it in my mind and thought. But when I came hither, and saw nothing at all like to those things which I had seen in phantasy, I blamed myself, and observed distinctly, that I had assigned to it gates, and walls, and buildings from others which I had seen, taking the likeness in reality from others. Nor indeed can any one imagine anything new, and of which no form has ever existed. For even if any one should fashion from his imagination bulls with five heads, he only forms them with five heads out of those which he has seen with one head. And you therefore, now, if truly you seem to yourself to perceive anything with your thought, and to look above the heavens, there is no doubt but that you imagine them from those things which you see, placed as you are upon the earth. But if you think that there is easy access for your mind above the heavens, and that you are able to conceive the things that are there, and to apprehend knowledge of that immense light, I think that for him who can comprehend these things, it were easier to throw his sense, which knows how to ascend there, into the heart and breast of some one of us who stand by, and to tell what thoughts he is cherishing in his breast. If therefore you can declare the thoughts of the heart of any one of us, who is not pre-engaged in your favour, we shall perhaps be able to believe you, that you are able to know those things that are above the heavens, although these are much loftier.

Chapter 66. Existence and Conception.

To this Simon replied: O you who has woven a web of many frivolities, listen now. It is impossible that anything which comes into a man's thoughts should not also subsist in truth and reality. For things that do not subsist, have no appearances; but things that have no appearances, cannot present themselves to our thoughts. Then said Peter: If everything that can come into our thoughts has a subsistence, then, with respect to that place of immensity which you say is outside the world, if one thinks in his heart that it is light, and another that it is darkness, how can one and the same place be both light and darkness, according to their different thoughts concerning it? Then said Simon: Let pass for the present what I have said; and tell us what you suppose to be above the heavens.

Chapter 67. The Law Teaches of Immensity.

Then said Peter: If you believed concerning the true fountain of light, I could instruct you what and of what sort is that which is immense, and should render, not a vain fancy, but a consistent and necessary account of the truth, and should make use, not of sophistical assertions, but testimonies of the law and nature, that you might know that the law especially contains what we ought to believe in regard to immensity. But if the doctrine of immensity is not unknown to the law, then assuredly, nought else can be unknown to it; and therefore it is a false supposition of yours, that there is anything of which the law is not cognisant. Much more shall nothing be unknown to Him who gave the law. Yet I cannot speak anything to you of immensity and of those things which are without limit, unless first you either accept our account of those heavens which are bounded by a certain limit, or else propound your own account of them. But if you cannot understand concerning those which are comprehended within fixed boundaries, much more can you neither know nor learn anything concerning those which are without limit.

Chapter 68. The Visible and the Invisible Heaven.

To this Simon answered: It seems to me to be better to believe simply that God is, and that that heaven which we see is the only heaven in the whole universe. But Peter said: Not so; but it is proper to confess one God who truly is; but that there are heavens, which were made by Him, as also the law says, of which one is the higher, in which also is contained the visible firmament; and that that higher heaven is perpetual and eternal, with those who dwell in it; but that this visible heaven is to be dissolved and to pass away at the end of the world, in order that that heaven which is older and higher may appear after the judgment to the holy and the worthy. To this Simon answered: That these things are so, as you say, may appear to those who believe them; but to him who seeks for reasons of these things, it is impossible that they can be produced from the law, and especially concerning the immensity of light.

Chapter 69. Faith and Reason.

Then Peter: Do not think that we say that these things are only to be received by faith, but also that they are to be asserted by reason. For indeed it is not safe to commit these things to bare faith without reason, since assuredly truth cannot be without reason. And therefore he who has received these things fortified by reason, can never lose them; whereas he who receives them without proofs, by an assent to a simple statement of them, can neither keep them safely, nor is certain if they are true; because he who easily believes, also easily yields. But he who has sought reason for those things which he has believed and received, as though bound by chains of reason itself, can never be torn away or separated from those things which he has believed. And therefore, according as any one is more anxious in demanding a reason, by so much will he be the firmer in preserving his faith.

Chapter 70. Adjournment.

To this Simon replied: It is a great thing which you promise, that the eternity of boundless light can be shown from the law. And when Peter said, I shall show it whenever you please, Simon answered: Since now it is a late hour, I shall stand by you and oppose you tomorrow; and if you can prove that this world was created, and that souls are immortal, you shall have me to assist you in your preaching. When he had said thus, he departed, and was followed by a third part of all the people who had come with him, who were about one thousand men. But the rest with bended knees prostrated themselves before Peter; and he, invoking upon them the name of God, cured some who had demons, healed others who were sick, and so dismissed the people rejoicing, commanding them to come early the next day. But Peter, when the crowds had withdrawn, commanded the table to be spread on the ground, in the open air, in the court where the disputation had been held, and sat down together with those eleven; but I dined reclining with some others who also had made a beginning of hearing the word of God, and were greatly beloved.

Chapter 71. Separation from the Unclean.

But Peter, most benignantly regarding me, lest haply that separation might cause me sorrow, says to me: It is not from pride, O Clement, that I do not eat with those who have not yet been purified; but I fear lest perhaps I should injure myself, and do no good to them. For this I would have you know for certain, that every one who has at any time worshipped idols, and has adored those whom the pagans call gods, or has eaten of the things sacrificed to them, is not without an unclean spirit; for he has become a guest of demons, and has been partaker with that demon of which he has formed the image in his mind, either through fear or love. 1 Corinthians 10:20 And by these means he is not free from an unclean spirit, and therefore needs the purification of baptism, that the unclean spirit may go out of him, which has made its abode in the inmost affections of his soul, and what is worse, gives no indication that it lurks within, for fear it should be exposed and expelled.

Chapter 72. The Remedy.

For these unclean spirits love to dwell in the bodies of men, that they may fulfil their own desires by their service, and, inclining the motions of their souls to those things which they themselves desire, may compel them to obey their own lusts, that they may become wholly vessels of demons. One of whom is this Simon, who is seized with such disease, and cannot now be healed, because he is sick in his will and purpose. Nor does the demon dwell in him against his will; and therefore, if any one would drive it out of him, since it is inseparable from himself, and, so to speak, has now become his very soul, he should seem rather to kill him, and to incur the guilt of manslaughter. Let no one of you therefore be saddened at being separated from eating with us, for every one ought to observe that it is for just so long a time as he pleases. For he who wishes soon to be baptized is separated but for a little time, but he for a longer who wishes to be baptized later. Every one therefore has it in his own power to demand a shorter or a longer time for his repentance; and therefore it lies with you, when you wish it, to come to our table; and not with us, who are not permitted to take food with any one who has not been baptized. It is rather you, therefore, who hinder us from eating with you, if you interpose delays in the way of your purification, and defer your baptism. Having said thus, and having blessed, he took food. And afterwards, when he had given thanks to God, he went into the house and went to bed; and we all did the like, for it was now night.

Aklat 2

Kabanata 1. Kapangyarihan ng Nakasanayan.

Nang sumapit ang araw na itinakda para sa pakikipagtalakayan kay Simon, bumangon si Pedro sa unang tilaok ng manok at ginising din kami. Labintatlo kaming magkakasama sa iisang silid: kasunod ni Pedro ay si Zaqueo, pagkatapos ay sina Sophonius, Jose at Miqueas, Eliesdrus, Phineas, Lazaro, at Eliseo; kasunod nila ay ako, si Clemente, at si Nicodemo; at pagkatapos ay sina Niceta at Aquila, na dating mga alagad ni Simon ngunit napabilang sa pananampalataya kay Cristo sa pamamagitan ng pagtuturo ni Zaqueo. Walang babaeng kasama roon. Habang nananatili pa ang mapusyaw na liwanag bago tuluyang mag-umaga, naupo kaming lahat. Nang makita ni Pedro na gising na kami at nakikinig sa kanya, binati niya kami at agad na nagsalita nang ganito:

Inaamin ko, mga kapatid, na namamangha ako sa kakayahan ng kalikasan ng tao, sapagkat nakikita kong kaya nitong masanay at umangkop sa iba't ibang pangangailangan. Ito ang natutuhan ko sa sarili kong karanasan: kapag lumampas na ang hatinggabi, kusang nagigising ako at hindi na ako muling dinadalaw ng antok. Nangyayari ito sapagkat nakasanayan kong alalahanin ang mga salita ng aking Panginoon na mismong narinig ko mula sa Kanya. Dahil sa pananabik ko sa mga salitang iyon, sinasanay kong maging gising ang aking isip at mga kaisipan upang isa-isang balikan, ayusin, at itanim sa alaala ang Kanyang mga sinabi. Sa kagustuhan kong ingatan nang buong galak sa aking puso ang mga salita ng Panginoon, nabuo sa akin ang kaugaliang magising sa ganoong oras, kahit wala naman akong partikular na bagay na kailangang pag-isipan. Sa paraang mahirap ipaliwanag, kapag matatag na ang isang bagong gawi, napapalitan nito ang dating gawi—basta't hindi pinipilit nang higit sa kayang tanggapin ng kalikasan ng tao. Sapagkat hindi naman maaaring tuluyang mawalan ng tulog ang isang tao; kung maaari iyon, hindi sana nilikha ang gabi bilang panahon ng pahinga.

Kabanata 2. Pagbabawas ng Tulog.

Nang marinig ko iyon, sinabi ko: Tama ang sinabi mo, O Pedro: napapalitan nga ng isang gawi ang isa pa. Nang ako'y nasa dagat, sa simula'y labis akong nahirapan at nagulo ang buong katawan ko. Para akong binugbog, at hindi ko matiis ang pag-ugoy at kaguluhan ng dagat. Ngunit makalipas ang ilang araw, nang masanay na ako, natutuhan ko ring tiisin iyon. Umabot pa nga sa puntong masaya na akong kumain agad sa umaga kasama ng mga mandaragat, kahit dati'y hindi ko ugaling kumain bago ang ikapitong oras. Dahil lamang sa kaugaliang nabuo ko noon, hanggang ngayon ay nagpapaalala sa akin ang gutom pagsapit ng oras na dati kaming kumakain ng mga mandaragat. Umaasa akong mawawala rin ang gawi na iyon kapag napalitan na ito ng ibang gawi. Kaya naniniwala akong ganoon din ang nangyari sa iyo sa pagkagising nang maaga. At tila sa tamang pagkakataon mo ito ipinaliwanag sa amin upang hindi rin kami mangiming bawasan ang bahagi ng aming tulog, nang sa gayon ay higit naming matutuhan ang mga tuntunin ng aral na nagbibigay-buhay. Kapag natunaw na ang pagkain at tahimik ang gabi, mas nananatili sa isip ang mga aral na ibinibigay sa wastong panahon.

Kabanata 3. Pangangailangan ng Pag-iingat.

Natuwa si Pedro nang marinig niyang naunawaan ko ang layunin ng kanyang pambungad at na sinabi niya iyon para sa aming kapakinabangan. Pinuri niya ako—marahil upang pasiglahin at palakasin ang aking loob—at saka nagsimula ng ganitong paliwanag: Sa palagay ko, napapanahon at kailangan nating pag-usapan ang bagay na malapit na nating harapin, samakatuwid ay si Simon. Nais kong malaman kung anong uri siya ng tao at kung paano siya namumuhay. Kaya kung may sinuman sa inyo na may nalalaman tungkol sa kanya, huwag ninyo itong ipagkait sa akin, sapagkat mahalagang malaman muna natin ang mga ito. Kung ipinagbilin sa atin na sa pagpasok sa isang lungsod ay alamin muna natin kung sino roon ang karapat-dapat upang sa kanya tayo tumuloy (Mateo 10:11), lalo pang nararapat na alamin natin kung sino at anong uri ng tao ang pagkakatiwalaan natin ng mga salita tungkol sa kawalang-kamatayan. Kailangan tayong maging maingat—napakaingat—upang hindi natin ihagis ang ating mga perlas sa mga baboy (Mateo 7:6).

Kabanata 4. Maingat na Pakikitungo sa mga Katunggali.

May iba pang dahilan kung bakit mahalagang makilala ko ang taong ito. Kung malalaman kong sa mga bagay na walang alinlangang mabuti ay wala siyang kapintasan—halimbawa, kung siya'y mahinahon, mahabagin, matuwid, mapagkumbaba, at makatao, mga katangiang walang sinumang magdududa na mabuti—kung gayon ay angkop lamang na maibahagi sa isang taong may ganitong mabubuting katangian ang pananampalataya at kaalamang wala pa sa kanya. Sa gayon, ang buhay niyang karapat-dapat namang papurihan sa ibang bagay ay maituwid sa mga bahaging kulang pa. Ngunit kung nananatili siyang nababalot at nadudungisan ng mga kasalanang maliwanag na masama, hindi nararapat na ihayag ko sa kanya ang mas malalalim at banal na bagay tungkol sa kaalaman sa Diyos. Sa halip, dapat ko muna siyang sawayin at harapin tungkol sa kanyang kasalanan, upang tumigil siya rito at linisin ang kanyang mga gawa mula sa kasamaan. Kung susubukan niyang linlangin tayo upang mapilit tayong sabihin ang mga bagay na hindi niya dapat marinig habang nagpapatuloy siya sa maling pamumuhay, tungkulin nating umiwas nang may pag-iingat. Gayunman, hindi rin wari nararapat na lubusan tayong tumangging sumagot, alang-alang sa mga nakikinig. Baka isipin nilang umiiwas tayo sa pagtatalo dahil wala tayong kakayahang sagutin siya, at dahil sa maling pagkaunawa nila sa ating layunin ay mapinsala ang kanilang pananampalataya.

Kabanata 5. Si Simon Magus, Isang Mabigat na Katunggali.

Pagkasabi nito ni Pedro, humingi ng pahintulot si Niceta na magsalita. Nang payagan siya ni Pedro, sinabi niya: Ipagpaumanhin mo, panginoon kong Pedro, ngunit hinihiling kong pakinggan mo ako. Labis akong nag-aalala para sa iyo at nangangamba akong sa pakikipagtunggali mo kay Simon ay magmukhang nalalamangan ka niya. Madalas mangyari na ang nagtatanggol sa katotohanan ay hindi siyang lumilitaw na nananalo, sapagkat maaaring may kinikilingan na ang mga nakikinig o kaya'y hindi sila gaanong interesado sa mas mabuting panig. Higit pa rito, napakahusay magsalita ni Simon. Sanay siya sa sining ng pangangatwiran at sa masalimuot na mga silohismo; at ang mas masahol pa, napakahusay niya sa salamangka. Kaya nangangamba ako na, dahil sa dami ng sandatang hawak niya, baka sa harap ng mga taong hindi siya kilala ay magmukhang siya ang nagtatanggol sa katotohanan kahit kasinungalingan ang kanyang sinasabi. Kami mismo ay marahil hindi sana nakatakas sa kanyang impluwensiya at napabalik-loob sa Panginoon kung, noong kami'y mga kasama niya at kabahagi sa kanyang mga kamalian, ay hindi namin natuklasang siya'y isang mandaraya at salamangkero.

Kabanata 6. Si Simon Magus: Ang Kanyang Kasamaan.

Pagkatapos magsalita ni Niceta, humingi rin ng pahintulot si Aquila at sinabi: Napakabuting Pedro, tanggapin mo, nakikiusap ako, ang katiyakan ng aming pagmamahal sa iyo, sapagkat ako man ay lubhang nag-aalala para sa iyo. Huwag mo kaming sisihin dahil dito. Ang pag-aalala para sa isang tao ay bunga ng pagmamahal; ang walang pakialam ay halos katumbas na rin ng pagkapoot. Saksi ko ang Diyos na hindi ako nag-aalala dahil iniisip kong mahina ka sa pakikipagtalo—sapagkat hindi pa ako nakadalo sa alinmang pakikipagtalo mo—kundi dahil lubos kong nalalaman ang kasamaan ng taong ito. Iniisip ko ang iyong pangalan, ang kaluluwa ng mga nakikinig, at higit sa lahat ang kapakanan ng katotohanan mismo. Napakatindi ng salamangkero na ito sa anumang nais niyang gawin, at sukdulan ang kanyang kasamaan. Kilalang-kilala namin siya, sapagkat mula pagkabata'y naging katuwang at kasangkapan kami ng kanyang kasamaan. Kung hindi kami iniligtas ng pag-ibig ng Diyos mula sa kanya, hanggang ngayon ay ginagawa pa rin namin ang masasamang gawaing iyon. Ngunit may likas na pag-ibig sa Diyos sa loob namin na nagtulak sa aming kasuklaman ang kanyang kasamaan at mahalin ang pagsamba sa Diyos. Kaya iniisip kong bahagi rin iyon ng banal na pamamatnubay: na una kaming naging malalapit sa kanya upang malaman namin kung paano at sa pamamagitan ng anong sining niya nagagawa ang mga kababalaghang wari'y ginagawa niya. Sino ba ang hindi mamamangha sa mga kamangha-manghang bagay na ipinakikita niya? Sino ang hindi mag-aakalang isa siyang diyos na bumaba mula sa langit upang iligtas ang mga tao? Inaamin ko, kung hindi ko siya nakilala nang malapitan at hindi ako nakilahok sa kanyang mga gawain, madali rin sana akong nadala. Kaya hindi mahirap para sa aming humiwalay sa kanya, sapagkat alam naming umaasa siya sa salamangka at masasamang panlilinlang. Kung nais mo ring malaman ang lahat tungkol sa kanya—kung sino siya, ano siya, saan siya nagmula, at kung paano niya ginagawa ang mga bagay na ito—makinig ka.

Kabanata 7. Si Simon Magus: Ang Kanyang Kasaysayan.

Ang ama ni Simon ay si Antonius at ang kanyang ina ay si Rachel. Siya'y isang Samaritano mula sa nayon ng Gettones. Salamangkero ang kanyang hanapbuhay, ngunit napakahusay din niya sa panitikang Griyego. Sabik siya sa kaluwalhatian at higit pa sa sinumang tao ang kanyang pagmamataas. Nais niyang paniwalaan ng mga tao na siya'y isang dakilang kapangyarihan na nakahihigit pa sa Diyos na Manlilikha; nais din niyang ituring na siya ang Cristo at tawaging ang Nakatindig. Ginagamit niya ang pangalang ito upang ipahiwatig na hindi siya kailanman mawawasak. Sinasabi niyang sa bisa ng kanyang pagka-diyos ay napakatatag ng pagkakabuo ng kanyang laman kaya mananatili ito magpakailanman. Kaya tinatawag siyang Nakatindig, na para bang walang anumang pagkabulok ang makapagpapabagsak sa kanya.

Kabanata 8. Si Simon Magus: Ang Kanyang Kasaysayan.

Matapos patayin si Juan Bautista, gaya ng nalalaman mo rin, inilunsad ni Dositheus ang kanyang sariling turo kasama ang tatlumpung pangunahing alagad at isang babaeng tinatawag na Luna. Lumilitaw na ang bilang na tatlumpu ay itinugma sa bilang ng mga araw ayon sa takbo ng buwan. Si Simon naman, na sabik sa masamang uri ng karangalan, gaya ng nasabi na namin, ay lumapit kay Dositheus at nagkunwaring kaibigan niya. Hiniling niyang kung mamatay ang isa sa tatlumpu, agad siyang ipalit sa namatay. Labag kasi sa kanilang tuntunin ang lumampas sa itinakdang bilang, gayundin ang tumanggap ng taong hindi pa nila kilala o nasusubok. Dahil dito, ang iba pang nagnanais mapabilang sa kanilang hanay ay nagsisikap na kalugdan ang mga kasapi, alinsunod sa mga tuntunin ng kanilang sekta, sa pag-asang kapag may namatay ay mapili silang pumalit. Dahil sa matinding pagpupumilit ni Simon, ipinasok siya ni Dositheus nang magkaroon ng bakante sa bilang.

Kabanata 9. Si Simon Magus: Ang Kanyang Ipinahahayag.

Hindi nagtagal, umibig siya sa babaeng tinatawag nilang Luna. Dahil itinuturing niya kaming mga kaibigan, ipinagtapat niya sa amin ang lahat: na siya'y isang salamangkero, na iniibig niya si Luna, at na dahil hangad niya ang karangalan ay ayaw niyang makamtan siya sa paraang magdadala sa kanya ng kahihiyan. Hihintayin daw niya hanggang makamtan niya siya nang may dangal—ngunit kailangan naming makipagsabwatan upang matupad ang kanyang hangarin. Bilang kapalit ng aming tulong, ipinangako niyang bibigyan niya kami ng pinakamataas na mga karangalan at paniwalain ang mga tao na kami'y mga diyos. 'May isa lamang kondisyon,' sabi niya, 'kilalanin ninyo akong si Simon bilang inyong pinuno. Sa pamamagitan ng salamangka, kaya kong magpakita ng maraming tanda at kababalaghan na magpapatatag sa aking kaluwalhatian o sa ating sekta. Kaya kong maging di-nakikita sa mga taong gustong dumakip sa akin, at magpakita muli kapag nais kong makita nila ako. Kung gusto kong tumakas, kaya kong bumutas sa mga bundok at dumaan sa bato na para bang luwad lamang. Kung ihulog ko ang aking sarili mula sa mataas na bundok, bababa akong hindi nasasaktan, na para bang may sumasalo sa akin. Kapag iginapos ako, kaya kong kalagin ang sarili ko at igapos ang mga gumapos sa akin. Kapag ikinulong ako, kaya kong pabuksan nang kusa ang mga harang ng bilangguan. Kaya kong buhayin ang mga rebulto sa paraang iisipin ng nakakakita na tunay silang mga tao. Kaya kong biglang magpatubo ng mga bagong puno at pasibulin agad ang mga ito. Kaya kong ihagis ang sarili ko sa apoy nang hindi nasusunog. Kaya kong baguhin ang aking mukha upang hindi ako makilala, at maipakita pa sa mga tao na dalawa ang aking mukha. Kaya kong mag-anyong tupa o kambing; kaya kong patubuan ng balbas ang maliliit na batang lalaki; kaya kong lumipad sa himpapawid; kaya kong magpakita ng napakaraming ginto at magluklok o magpatalsik ng mga hari. Sasambahin ako bilang Diyos; bibigyan ako ng mga karangalang banal sa harap ng madla, hanggang sa magtayo sila ng larawan ko at sambahin ako bilang Diyos. At bakit ko pa pahahabain? Anuman ang naisin ko, kaya kong gawin. Marami na akong nasubukan. Minsan,' sabi niya, 'nang utusan ako ng aking inang si Rachel na pumunta sa bukid upang mag-ani, may nakita akong karit na nakahandusay. Inutusan ko itong mag-ani, at sampung ulit ang dami ng naani nito kaysa sa iba. Kamakailan lamang, nagpalitaw ako ng maraming bagong usbong mula sa lupa at sa isang iglap ay pinasibol ko ang mga dahon at bunga nito; at matagumpay ko ring binutas ang pinakamalapit na bundok.'

Kabanata 10. Si Simon Magus: Ang Kanyang Panlilinlang.

Nang sabihin niya ang tungkol sa pagpapasibol ng mga halaman at pagbubutas sa bundok, labis akong nabagabag. Nais pala niyang dayain maging kami na inaakala naming pinagkakatiwalaan niya. Alam naming matagal nang umiiral, mula pa sa panahon ng aming mga ninuno, ang mga bagay na ipinagmamalaki niyang siya lamang daw ang gumawa kamakailan. Gayunman, kahit narinig namin mula sa kanya ang mga kasamaang ito at maging ang mas malala pa, nagpatuloy pa rin kaming sumama sa kanyang mga kasalanan at hinayaan naming malinlang niya ang iba. Nagsinungaling pa kami nang maraming ulit para sa kanya, at ginawa namin iyon bago pa niya matupad ang alinman sa kanyang mga ipinangako. Kaya kahit wala pa siyang tunay na nagagawa, may ilan nang naniniwalang siya'y Diyos.

Kabanata 11. Si Simon Magus Bilang Pinuno ng Sekta ni Dositheus.

Samantala, mula nang mapabilang siya sa tatlumpung alagad ni Dositheus, sinimulan niyang siraan si Dositheus. Sinasabi niyang hindi dalisay o ganap ang pagtuturo nito, hindi raw dahil masama ang hangarin kundi dahil kulang sa kaalaman. Nang mapansin ni Dositheus na minamaliit siya ni Simon, natakot siyang mawala ang kanyang reputasyon sa mga tao—sapagkat siya mismo ang inaakalang Nakatindig. Sa galit niya, nang minsang magtagpo sila gaya ng dati sa paaralan, kumuha siya ng pamalo at sinimulang hampasin si Simon. Ngunit biglang tila dumaan lamang sa katawan ni Simon ang pamalo, na para bang usok ang katawan nito. Namangha si Dositheus at sinabi, 'Sabihin mo sa akin kung ikaw nga ang Nakatindig, upang sambahin kita.' Sumagot si Simon na siya nga. Nang mapagtanto ni Dositheus na hindi pala siya ang Nakatindig, nagpatirapa siya at sumamba kay Simon. Ibinigay niya kay Simon ang kanyang puwesto bilang pinuno at inutusan ang buong pangkat ng tatlumpu na sumunod dito. Siya naman ang pumalit sa mababang puwestong dating kinalalagyan ni Simon. Hindi nagtagal pagkatapos nito, namatay si Dositheus.

Kabanata 12. Si Simon Magus at si Luna.

Pagkamatay ni Dositheus, kinuha ni Simon si Luna bilang kasama. Hanggang ngayon ay magkasama silang naglilibot, gaya ng nakikita ninyo, at nililinlang ang maraming tao. Ipinapahayag ni Simon na siya raw ay isang kapangyarihang nakahihigit sa Diyos na Manlilikha. Tungkol naman kay Luna, sinasabi niyang ibinaba siya mula sa mas matataas na langit at siya ang Karunungan, ang ina ng lahat ng bagay. Ayon sa kanya, nang pag-agawan siya ng mga Griyego at mga barbaro, bahagya lamang nilang nakita ang isang larawan niya; ngunit kung sino talaga siya bilang kasama ng una at tanging Diyos, lubos nilang hindi nalaman. Sa pagtuturo ng mga ito at ng iba pang katulad nito, marami siyang nalinlang. May isa pang bagay na dapat kong sabihin, sapagkat ako mismo ang nakakita nito. Minsan, nang naroon si Luna sa isang tore, nagtipon ang napakaraming tao upang makita siya at pumalibot sa tore sa lahat ng panig. Gayunman, nakita ng lahat na sabay-sabay siyang nakadungaw sa bawat bintana ng tore. Marami pang ibang kamangha-manghang bagay ang ginawa at patuloy na ginagawa ni Simon; kaya sa labis na pagkamangha ng mga tao, iniisip nilang siya mismo ang dakilang Diyos.

Kabanata 13. Si Simon Magus: Ang Lihim ng Kanyang Salamangka.

Minsan, nang tanungin namin ni Niceta si Simon kung paano nagagawa sa salamangka ang mga bagay na iyon at kung ano ang likas na katangian ng kapangyarihang ginagamit niya, ipinaliwanag niya ito sa amin bilang kanyang mga kasamahan. 'Ginagamit ko,' sabi niya, 'ang kaluluwa ng isang batang lalaki na walang bahid at pinatay nang marahas. Tinawag ko ito sa pamamagitan ng mga panawagang hindi maaaring bigkasin, at tinutulungan ako nito; sa pamamagitan nito nagagawa ang lahat ng inuutos ko.' Sinabi ko, 'Posible bang magawa ng isang kaluluwa ang mga bagay na iyan?' Sumagot siya: 'Dapat mong malaman na kapag nakalaya na ang kaluluwa ng tao mula sa dilim ng katawan, pumapangalawa ito sa Diyos sa antas. Agad din itong nagkakaroon ng kaalaman tungkol sa darating na mga bagay; kaya ito tinatawag sa necromancy.' Sumagot ako, 'Kung gayon, bakit hindi naghihiganti sa mga pumatay sa kanila ang mga kaluluwa ng mga pinatay?' Sinabi niya, 'Hindi mo ba natatandaang sinabi ko sa iyo na kapag lumabas na ang kaluluwa sa katawan, nalalaman nito ang hinaharap?' Sinabi ko, 'Natatandaan ko.' At sinabi niya, 'Sa sandaling lumabas ito sa katawan, nalalaman nitong may darating na paghuhukom at na bawat isa ay parurusahan dahil sa masasamang ginawa niya. Kaya ayaw nilang maghiganti sa mga pumatay sa kanila, sapagkat sila mismo ay nagdurusa dahil sa masasamang ginawa nila rito at alam nilang mas mabigat pang kaparusahan ang naghihintay sa paghuhukom. Bukod dito, hindi sila pinahihintulutan ng mga anghel na namamahala sa kanila na lumabas o gumawa ng anumang bagay.' Sumagot ako, 'Kung hindi sila pinahihintulutan ng mga anghel na pumunta rito o gawin ang gusto nila, paano nila nasusunod ang salamangkero na tumatawag sa kanila?' Sinabi niya, 'Hindi sa pinapayagan ng mga anghel ang mga kaluluwang gustong pumunta. Ngunit kapag ang mga anghel na namamahala sa kanila ay pinanumpa sa pangalan ng isang higit sa kanila, nagkakaroon sila ng dahilan dahil sa pamimilit nating tumatawag sa kanila upang payagang lumabas ang mga kaluluwang ating tinatawag. Hindi nagkakasala ang pinipilit; tayo ang nagkakasala sapagkat tayo ang naglalagay sa kanila sa ilalim ng gayong pangangailangan.' Hindi na nakapagpigil si Niceta at agad siyang sumagot—gagawin ko rin sana iyon, ngunit gusto ko munang makakuha ng higit pang paliwanag sa ilang bagay. Sinabi niya: 'Hindi mo ba kinatatakutan ang araw ng paghuhukom? Pinipilit mo ang mga anghel, tumatawag ka ng mga kaluluwa, nililinlang mo ang mga tao, at hinihingi mo sa kanila ang karangalang para sa Diyos. Paano mo kami nahihikayat na walang paghuhukom, gaya ng sinasabi ng ilan sa mga Judio, at na hindi imortal ang mga kaluluwa, gaya ng ipinapalagay ng marami, samantalang sinasabi mong nakikita mo mismo ang mga ito at mula sa kanila pa nga ay nakatatanggap ka ng katiyakan tungkol sa banal na paghuhukom?'

Kabanata 14. Ipinahayag ni Simon Magus na Siya ay Diyos.

Namutla si Simon sa sinabi ni Niceta. Ngunit makalipas ang ilang sandali ay nakabawi siya at sumagot: 'Huwag ninyong isipin na ako'y isang taong katulad ninyo. Hindi ako salamangkero, hindi ako mangingibig ni Luna, at hindi ako anak ni Antonius. Bago pa magsama ang aking inang si Rachel at si Antonius, naglihi na siya sa akin habang siya'y dalaga pa. Nasa kapangyarihan kong maging maliit o malaki at magpakita bilang tao sa gitna ng mga tao. Pinili ko muna kayong maging mga kaibigan upang subukin kayo; kapag napatunayan ko kayo, ilalagay ko kayo sa mga dakila at di-maipahayag na dako sa langit. Nagkunwari lamang akong tao upang mas malinaw kong malaman kung buo ang inyong pagmamahal sa akin.' Nang marinig ko iyon, naisip kong tunay siyang kahiya-hiya, ngunit namangha rin ako sa kapal ng kanyang mukha. Nahihiya ako para sa kanya at kasabay nito'y natatakot na baka gumawa siya ng masama laban sa amin. Kaya sinenyasan ko si Niceta na makisabay muna sa pagkukunwari ko, at sinabi ko kay Simon: 'Huwag kang magalit sa amin, O Diyos na hindi nasisira, kaming mga taong nasisira. Tanggapin mo sa halip ang aming pagmamahal at ang pagnanais naming makilala kung sino ang Diyos. Hanggang ngayon ay hindi namin alam kung sino ka, ni hindi namin naunawaang ikaw pala ang aming hinahanap.'

Kabanata 15. Sinabi ni Simon Magus na Nakagawa Siya ng Isang Batang Lalaki mula sa Hangin.

Habang sinasabi namin ang mga salitang iyon at nagkukunwaring naaayon ang aming kilos, naniwala ang hambog na lalaking iyon na nalinlang niya kami. Lalo siyang nagmataas at sinabi: 'Mula ngayon ay magiging mabuti ako sa inyo dahil sa pagmamahal na ipinakita ninyo sa akin bilang Diyos. Minahal ninyo ako kahit hindi ninyo ako kilala, at hinanap ninyo ako nang hindi ninyo nalalaman kung sino ako. Huwag ninyong pagdudahan na tunay na pagka-Diyos ang kakayahang maging maliit o malaki ayon sa sariling kagustuhan, sapagkat kaya kong magpakita sa tao sa anumang anyong nais ko. Ngayon ay sisimulan kong ihayag sa inyo ang katotohanan. Minsan, sa pamamagitan ng aking kapangyarihan, ginawa kong tubig ang hangin, saka ginawa kong dugo ang tubig, at pinatigas ko iyon hanggang maging laman. Sa gayon ay nakabuo ako ng isang bagong nilalang na tao—isang batang lalaki—at nakagawa ako ng gawaing higit na marangal kaysa sa ginawa ng Diyos na Manlilikha. Siya ay lumikha ng tao mula sa lupa, ngunit ako'y lumikha mula sa hangin, na mas mahirap gawin. Pagkatapos ay muli ko siyang winasak at ibinalik sa hangin, ngunit bago ko iyon ginawa ay inilagay ko muna sa aking silid ang larawan at wangis niya bilang katibayan at alaala ng aking ginawa.' Doon namin naunawaan na ang tinutukoy niya ay ang batang lalaking pinatay nang marahas at ang kaluluwa nito ang ginagamit niya sa mga gawaing kailangan niya.

Kabanata 16. Si Simon Magus: Ang Kawalang-Pag-asa ng Kanyang Kalagayan.

Nang marinig ito ni Pedro, lumuluha niyang sinabi: Labis akong namamangha sa walang hanggang pagtitiis ng Diyos, at sa kabilang panig ay sa sukdulang kapangahasan ng ilang tao. Ano pa ba ang maaaring magpatunay kay Simon na hinahatulan ng Diyos ang mga di-matuwid, gayong kumbinsido siyang napapasunod niya ang mga kaluluwa upang paglingkuran ang kanyang mga krimen? Sa katotohanan, mga demonyo ang nanlilinlang sa kanya. Gayunman, dahil sa mga karanasang ito ay kumbinsido siyang imortal ang mga kaluluwa at hinahatulan sila ayon sa kanilang mga gawa. Iniisip pa niyang aktuwal niyang nakikita ang mga bagay na pinaniniwalaan natin sa pananampalataya. Kahit mga demonyo ang nanlilinlang sa kanya, gaya ng sinabi ko, iniisip niyang ang mismong sangkap ng kaluluwa ang nakikita niya. Paano mahihikayat ang gayong tao na amining masama ang kanyang ginagawa o na hahatulan siya dahil sa mga ginawa niya, kung alam na niya ang tungkol sa paghuhukom ng Diyos ngunit hinahamak niya ito, itinuturing ang sarili niyang kaaway ng Diyos, at nangangahas pa ring gumawa ng mga kakila-kilabot na bagay? Kaya maliwanag, mga kapatid, na may mga taong lumalaban sa katotohanan at sa relihiyon ng Diyos hindi dahil iniisip nilang hindi maaaring magkasundo ang katuwiran at pananampalataya, kundi dahil lubha silang nalubog sa kasamaan, hinahadlangan ng sarili nilang masasamang gawa, o namamaga sa pagmamataas ang kanilang puso. Umaabot sila sa puntong ayaw nilang paniwalaan maging ang mga bagay na sa palagay nila'y nakikita na mismo ng kanilang mga mata.

Kabanata 17. Ang mga Taong Naging Kaaway ng Diyos.

Sapagkat wari'y sapat sana para sa kaligtasan ng mga umiibig sa Diyos na Manlilikha ang likas na pagmamahal sa Kanya, sinisikap ng kaaway na baluktutin ang pagmamahal na iyon sa puso ng mga tao at gawin silang mapoot at walang utang-na-loob sa kanilang Manlilikha. Tinatawag kong saksi ang langit at lupa: kung pahihintulutan ng Diyos ang kaaway na magngalit hanggang sa sukdulang nais nito, matagal nang napuksa ang lahat ng tao. Ngunit dahil sa Kanyang awa, hindi iyon pinahihintulutan ng Diyos. Kung ibabaling lamang ng mga tao ang kanilang pagmamahal sa Diyos, tiyak na maliligtas silang lahat, kahit pa kailangan silang ituwid dahil sa ilang pagkukulang alang-alang sa katuwiran. Ngunit ngayon, karamihan sa mga tao ay naging mga kaaway ng Diyos. Pinasok ng masama ang kanilang mga puso at ibinaling sa kanyang sarili ang pagmamahal na itinanim ng Diyos na Manlilikha sa kanila upang para sana sa Kanya iyon. At sa iba namang wari'y naging mapagbantay sa loob ng ilang panahon, nagpakita ang kaaway sa anyo ng kaluwalhatian at kariktan at nangako ng dakila at makapangyarihang mga bagay; dahil dito, inilayo niya sa Diyos ang kanilang isip at puso. Gayunman, may makatarungang dahilan kung bakit pinahihintulutan siyang gawin ang mga bagay na ito.

Kabanata 18. Pananagutan ng mga Tao.

Sumagot si Aquila: Kung gayon, paano nagiging may sala ang mga tao kung ang masama, na nag-aanyong parang maningning na liwanag (2 Corinto 11:14), ay nangangako sa kanila ng mas malalaking bagay kaysa sa ipinapangako mismo ng Manlilikha? Sumagot si Pedro: Sa palagay ko, sabi niya, walang higit na di-makatarungan kaysa rito; pakinggan mo kung bakit. Ipagpalagay mong may anak kang pinalaki at inalagaan nang buong pag-iingat hanggang sa maging ganap na lalaki. Kung magiging walang utang-na-loob siya sa iyo, iiwan ka at pupunta sa ibang taong nakita niyang mas mayaman, ibibigay sa taong iyon ang karangalang dapat sana'y sa iyo, at dahil sa pag-asang makakuha ng mas malaking pakinabang ay itatanggi pa ang sarili niyang pinagmulan at ang karapatan mo bilang ama—ituturing mo ba iyong tama o masama? Sumagot si Aquila: Malinaw sa lahat na masama iyon. Sinabi ni Pedro: Kung masama na iyon sa pagitan ng mga tao, lalo na pagdating sa Diyos, na higit sa lahat ay karapat-dapat tumanggap ng karangalan mula sa tao. Hindi lamang natin tinatamasa ang Kanyang mga biyaya; sa pamamagitan ng Kanyang kapangyarihan ay nagsimula tayong umiral mula sa kawalan, at kung nanaisin natin ay mula rin sa Kanya natin matatanggap ang walang hanggang mapalad na buhay. Kaya upang maihiwalay ang di-tapat sa tapat at ang maka-Diyos sa di-maka-Diyos, pinahintulutan ang masama na gamitin ang mga paraang sumusubok sa pagmamahal ng bawat isa sa tunay na Ama. Ngunit kahit ipagpalagay pang may ibang diyos, magiging tama ba na iwan natin ang sarili nating Diyos, na lumikha sa atin at siyang Ama at Maylikha natin, upang lumipat sa iba? Huwag nawang mangyari. sabi ni Aquila. Sinabi ni Pedro: Kung gayon, paano natin masasabi na ang masama ang siyang dahilan ng ating kasalanan, kung pinahihintulutan ito ng Diyos upang sa araw ng paghuhukom ay mapatunayan at mahatulan ang mga taong, dahil nahikayat ng mas malalaking pangako, tumalikod sa kanilang tungkulin sa tunay nilang Ama at Manlilikha? Samantala, yaong nanatiling tapat at patuloy na umibig sa kanilang sariling Ama, kahit dumaan sila sa kahirapan at pagdurusa, ay makatatanggap ng mga kaloob na makalangit at mga karangalang hindi nasisira sa Kanyang kaharian. Higit pa nating ipaliliwanag ang mga bagay na ito sa ibang pagkakataon. Sa ngayon, nais kong malaman kung ano ang sumunod na ginawa ni Simon.

Kabanata 19. Nagsimula ang Pagtatalo.

Sumagot si Niceta: Nang mapansin niyang nabunyag na namin kung sino talaga siya at napag-usapan namin ang kanyang mga krimen, iniwan namin siya at pumunta kay Zaqueo. Sinabi namin kay Zaqueo ang mga bagay na katulad ng ikinuwento namin ngayon sa iyo. Napakabuti ng pagtanggap niya sa amin, tinuruan niya kami tungkol sa pananampalataya sa ating Panginoong Jesu-Cristo, at isinama niya kami sa bilang ng mga mananampalataya. Pagkatapos magsalita ni Niceta, pumasok si Zaqueo, na sandaling lumabas kanina, at sinabi: Oras na, O Pedro, para pumunta ka sa pagtatalakayan. Maraming tao ang nagtipon sa patyo ng bahay at naghihintay sa iyo. Nakatayo si Simon sa gitna nila at napaliligiran ng maraming tagasunod. Nang marinig ito ni Pedro, inutusan niya akong lumayo muna upang manalangin, sapagkat hindi pa ako nahuhugasan mula sa mga kasalanang nagawa ko dahil sa kawalang-kaalaman. Pagkatapos ay sinabi niya sa iba: Mga kapatid, manalangin tayo na sa Kanyang di-maipahayag na awa sa pamamagitan ng Kanyang Cristo ay tulungan ako ng Diyos habang lumalabas ako alang-alang sa kaligtasan ng mga taong Kanyang nilikha. Pagkatapos niyang sabihin iyon at manalangin, lumabas siya sa patyo ng bahay. Napakaraming tao ang nagkatipon doon. Nang makita niyang nakatingin silang lahat sa kanya nang buong pananahimik at si Simon na Salamangkero ay nakatayo sa gitna nila na parang pinuno ng hukbo, nagsimula siyang magsalita nang ganito:

Kabanata 20. Ang Kaharian ng Diyos at ang Kanyang Katuwiran.

Kapayapaan sa inyong lahat na handang tumanggap sa katotohanan. Ang sumusunod dito ay maaaring magmukhang sila ang gumagawa ng pabor sa Diyos, ngunit sa katunayan sila ang tumatanggap mula sa Kanya ng pinakadakilang biyaya habang lumalakad sila sa mga landas ng Kanyang katuwiran. Kaya ang unang tungkulin nating lahat ay alamin ang katuwiran ng Diyos at ang Kanyang kaharian (Mateo 6:33): ang Kanyang katuwiran, upang matutuhan natin kung paano mamuhay nang tama; at ang Kanyang kaharian, upang malaman natin ang gantimpalang inilaan para sa pagpapagal at pagtitiis. Sa kahariang iyon, walang hanggang mabubuting bagay ang ibibigay sa mabubuti, samantalang ang mga kumilos laban sa kalooban ng Diyos ay tatanggap ng nararapat na parusa ayon sa ginawa ng bawat isa. Kaya habang naririto pa kayo—ibig sabihin, habang nabubuhay pa kayo sa kasalukuyang buhay—dapat ninyong alamin ang kalooban ng Diyos habang may pagkakataon pa kayong isagawa ito. Kung ang isang tao, bago niya ituwid ang kanyang mga gawa, ay gugugol ng panahon sa pagsisiyasat ng mga bagay na hindi naman niya kayang tuklasin, magiging hangal at walang kabuluhan ang gayong pagsisiyasat. Maikli ang panahon, at sa paghuhukom ng Diyos ay mga gawa, hindi mga tanong, ang haharap sa pagsusuri. Kaya higit sa lahat, alamin muna natin kung ano at kung paano tayo dapat mamuhay upang maging karapat-dapat sa buhay na walang hanggan.

Kabanata 21. Ang Katuwiran Bilang Daan Patungo sa Kaharian.

Kung gugugulin natin ang maikling panahon ng buhay na ito sa walang kabuluhan at walang pakinabang na mga tanong, tiyak na haharap tayo sa Diyos na walang dalang mabubuting gawa, sa panahong, gaya ng sinabi ko, ang ating mga gawa ang hahatulan. May tamang panahon at lugar ang bawat bagay. Narito ang lugar at panahon para gumawa; sa daigdig na darating naman ang panahon ng gantimpala. Kaya huwag nating guluhin ang wastong ayos ng mga panahon at lugar. Unahin nating alamin kung ano ang katuwiran ng Diyos. Tulad ng mga taong naghahanda para sa isang paglalakbay, punuin natin ang ating sarili ng mabubuting gawa na para bang saganang baon, upang makarating tayo sa kaharian ng Diyos na tila isang dakilang lungsod. Para sa taong may wastong pag-iisip, nahahayag ang Diyos sa mismong mga gawa ng mundong Kanyang nilikha, sapagkat nagpapatotoo ang Kanyang nilalang tungkol sa Kanya (Roma 1:20). Kaya dahil hindi na dapat pagdudahan ang tungkol sa Diyos, ang kailangan nating saliksikin ngayon ay ang Kanyang katuwiran at kaharian. Kung uudyukan tayo ng ating isip na siyasatin muna ang mga lihim at nakatagong bagay bago ang mga gawa ng katuwiran, dapat nating paalalahanan ang ating sarili na kung mamumuhay tayo nang mabuti ay magiging karapat-dapat tayo sa kaligtasan. Pagkatapos, kapag lumapit tayo sa Diyos na malinis at dalisay, mapupuspos tayo ng Banal na Espiritu at malalaman ang lahat ng lihim at nakatagong bagay nang hindi na kailangan ang walang katapusang pakikipagtalo. Ngunit sa ngayon, kahit gugulin pa ng isang tao ang buong buhay niya sa pagsisiyasat sa mga ito, hindi lamang niya mabibigong matagpuan ang mga sagot; mas lalo pa siyang maliligaw, sapagkat hindi muna siya pumasok sa landas ng katuwiran at nagsikap makarating sa kanlungan ng buhay.

Kabanata 22. Ano ang Katuwiran.

Kaya ipinapayo kong unahin nating saliksikin ang katuwiran ng Diyos. Sa paglalakad natin sa landas na iyon at sa pananatili sa daan ng katotohanan, matatagpuan natin ang tunay na Propeta—hindi sa pamamagitan ng bilis ng ating mga paa kundi sa kabutihan ng ating mga gawa. Sa ilalim ng Kanyang paggabay, hindi tayo manganganib na maligaw. Kung sa patnubay Niya ay maging karapat-dapat tayong makapasok sa lungsod na nais nating marating, makikita natin mismo ang lahat ng bagay na ngayo'y tinatanong natin, na para bang ginawa tayong mga tagapagmana ng lahat. Unawain ninyo, kung gayon, ang larawan: ang landas ay ang takbo ng ating buhay; ang mga manlalakbay ay ang mga gumagawa ng mabuti; ang pintuan ay ang tunay na Propeta na ating tinutukoy; at ang lungsod ay ang kaharian na tinitirhan ng Makapangyarihang Ama, na yaon lamang may malilinis na puso ang makakakita (Mateo 5:8). Huwag nating isipin na napakahirap ng paglalakbay na ito, sapagkat sa dulo nito ay may kapahingahan. Maging ang tunay na Propeta, mula sa pasimula ng daigdig at sa buong takbo ng panahon, ay patuloy na humahantong sa kapahingahan. Kasama natin Siya sa lahat ng panahon; at kapag kailangan, nagpapakita Siya at itinutuwid tayo upang dalhin sa buhay na walang hanggan ang mga sumusunod sa Kanya. Kaya ito ang aking pasiya, at ito rin ang kalooban ng tunay na Propeta: na ang katuwiran muna ang dapat saliksikin, lalo na ng mga nagpapahayag na kilala nila ang Diyos. Kung may sinuman sa inyo na may mas mabuting maimumungkahi, magsalita siya; at pagkatapos niyang magsalita, makinig din siya nang may pagtitiis at katahimikan. Dahil dito, sa simula pa lamang, bilang pagbati, ipinanalangin ko ang kapayapaan para sa inyong lahat.

Kabanata 23. Tinanggihan ni Simon ang Kapayapaan.

Sumagot si Simon: Hindi namin kailangan ang kapayapaan mo. Kung kapayapaan at pagkakasundo lamang ang pairalin, hindi tayo uusad sa pagtuklas ng katotohanan. Maging ang mga magnanakaw at mga taong mahalay ay may kapayapaan sa isa't isa; ang bawat uri ng kasamaan ay maaaring magkasundo sa sarili nito. Kung nagtipon tayo upang tanggapin ang lahat ng sasabihin alang-alang lamang sa kapayapaan, wala tayong maitutulong sa mga nakikinig. Sa halip, lilinlangin natin sila at uuwi tayong magkakaibigan. Kaya huwag kang humingi ng kapayapaan; humingi ka ng labanan, sapagkat mula sa labanan nagmumula ang kapayapaan. Kung kaya mo, lipulin mo ang mga kamalian. Huwag mong hanapin ang pagkakaibigang nabubuo sa di-makatarungang pagpapayag. Ito ang dapat mong malaman higit sa lahat: kapag dalawang tao ang naglalaban, magkakaroon lamang ng kapayapaan kapag natalo at bumagsak ang isa. Kaya lumaban ka nang buong makakaya, at huwag kang umasa sa kapayapaang walang labanan, sapagkat imposible iyon. Kung iniisip mong posible, ipakita mo sa amin kung paano.

Kabanata 24. Paliwanag ni Pedro.

Sumagot si Pedro: Makinig kayong mabuti sa sinasabi namin. Ipagpalagay nating ang mundong ito ay isang malawak na kapatagan, at mula sa dalawang kahariang magkaaway ang mga hari ay nagpadala ng tig-isang heneral upang lumaban. Ipagpalagay ding ipinayo ng heneral ng mabuting hari na kapwa hukbo ay kusang magpasakop, nang walang pagdanak ng dugo, sa mas mabuting hari upang maligtas ang lahat nang walang panganib. Ngunit sinabi ng kabilang heneral: 'Hindi, dapat tayong maglaban, upang hindi ang karapat-dapat kundi ang pinakamalakas ang maghari kasama ng mga makaliligtas.' Alin ang pipiliin ninyo? Wala akong dudang ibibigay ninyo ang inyong pagsang-ayon sa mas mabuting hari upang maligtas ang lahat. At hindi ko hinihiling, gaya ng paratang ni Simon, na alang-alang sa kapayapaan ay sang-ayunan ang mga maling sinasabi. Ang nais ko ay hanapin natin ang katotohanan nang mahinahon at maayos.

Kabanata 25. Mga Prinsipyong Dapat Sundin sa Pagtatalakayan.

May mga tao na, kapag sa isang pagtatalo ay napapansing napabubulaanan ang kanilang kamalian, agad na gumagawa ng kaguluhan at nagpapasimula ng alitan upang maitago sa lahat na sila'y natalo. Kaya palagi kong hinihiling na ang usaping pinagtatalunan ay siyasatin nang may buong pagtitiis at katahimikan. Kung may bagay na waring hindi nasabi nang tama, dapat pahintulutang balikan iyon at ipaliwanag nang mas malinaw. Kung minsan, iba ang ibig sabihin ng nagsasalita at iba ang nauunawaan ng nakikinig—maaaring dahil malabo ang pagkakasabi o dahil hindi ito napakinggan nang sapat na mabuti. Kaya nais kong maging matiisin ang ating usapan: huwag agawin ng isa ang pagkakataon ng iba na magsalita, at huwag putulin ng pagsalungat sa maling oras ang sinasabi ng kausap. Huwag tayong maghanap lamang ng maipipintas. Sa halip, gaya ng sinabi ko, balikan natin ang anumang hindi malinaw upang sa makatarungang pagsusuri ay lalo nating makita ang katotohanan. Dapat nating maunawaan na kapag ang isang tao ay natalo ng katotohanan, hindi siya mismo ang tunay na natalo kundi ang kamangmangan na nasa kanya—ang pinakamasama sa lahat ng mga demonyo. Kaya ang makapagpapalayas nito ay siyang tumatanggap ng gantimpala ng kaligtasan. Ang layunin natin ay makinabang ang mga nakikinig. Hindi natin hangad ang masamang uri ng tagumpay; mas mabuti pang tayo ang matalo nang tama kung iyon ang magdadala sa pagkilala sa katotohanan. Kung ang ating pananalita ay talagang pinakikilos ng pagnanais na hanapin ang katotohanan, kahit may masabi tayong kulang dahil sa kahinaan ng tao, pupunan ng Diyos sa Kanyang di-maipahayag na kabutihan ang kulang sa pagkaunawa ng mga nakikinig. Siya ay matuwid; ayon sa layunin ng bawat isa, may mga taong madali Niyang pinagkakaloobang matagpuan ang hinahanap nila, samantalang sa iba nama'y maging ang nasa harap na ng kanilang mga mata ay nagiging malabo. Dahil ang daan ng Diyos ay daan ng kapayapaan, hanapin natin nang may kapayapaan ang mga bagay na sa Diyos. Kung may nais tumutol dito, magsalita siya. Ngunit kung walang nais sumagot, magsisimula akong magsalita, at ako na rin ang maglalahad ng mga posibleng pagtutol laban sa akin at sasagot sa mga iyon.

Kabanata 26. Pagputol ni Simon sa Pagsasalita.

Nang magsimula nang ipagpatuloy ni Pedro ang kanyang paliwanag, pinutol siya ni Simon at sinabi: Bakit ka nagmamadaling sabihin ang anumang gusto mo? Nauunawaan ko ang pakana mo. Nais mong unahin ang mga paksang napag-aralan mo nang mabuti upang magmukha kang mahusay magsalita sa harap ng mangmang na karamihan. Hindi ko hahayaang gawin mo iyan. Ngayon, yamang ipinagmamalaki mong handa kang sagutin ang anumang ihaharap sa iyo, sagutin mo muna ako. Sinabi ni Pedro: Handa ako, basta't mapayapa ang ating pagtatalakayan. Sumagot si Simon: Hindi mo ba nakikita, hangal, na sa paghingi mo ng kapayapaan ay sumasalungat ka sa sarili mong Guro? Hindi angkop sa isang nangangakong papawiin ang kamangmangan ang hinihingi mong paraan. Kung tama kang humingi ng kapayapaan sa mga nakikinig, mali naman ang iyong Guro nang sabihin niyang, 'Hindi ako naparito upang magdala ng kapayapaan sa lupa kundi ng tabak' (Mateo 10:34). Kaya alinman sa tama ka at mali siya, o kung tama ang iyong Guro, ikaw naman ang mali. Hindi mo nauunawaang salungat sa sinabi niya ang sinasabi mo, gayong ipinahahayag mong alagad ka niya.

Kabanata 27. Mga Tanong at Sagot.

Sinabi ni Pedro: Hindi nagkamali ang nagsugo sa akin nang sabihin Niyang nagdala Siya ng tabak sa lupa, at hindi rin ako sumasalungat sa Kanya sa paghingi ko ng kapayapaan sa mga nakikinig. Padalus-dalos at walang sapat na pagkaunawa ang pagpuna mo sa bagay na hindi mo naiintindihan. Narinig mong sinabi ng Guro na hindi Siya naparito upang magdala ng kapayapaan sa lupa; ngunit tila hindi mo narinig na sinabi rin Niya, 'Mapalad ang mga gumagawa ng kapayapaan, sapagkat sila'y tatawaging mga anak ng Diyos' (Mateo 5:9). Kaya hindi naiiba sa Kanyang kaisipan ang payo kong panatilihin ang kapayapaan, sapagkat Siya mismo ang nagpahayag ng pagpapala sa mga gumagawa nito. Sumagot si Simon: Sa pagtatanggol mo sa iyong Guro, O Pedro, mas mabigat pa ang ibinibintang mo sa kanya. Kung siya mismo ay hindi naparito upang gumawa ng kapayapaan ngunit inutusan naman ang iba na panatilihin ito, paano magiging kaayon niyan ang isa pa niyang sinabi: 'Sapat na sa alagad na maging gaya ng kanyang guro'? (Mateo 10:25)

Kabanata 28. Ang Pagkakatugma ng Turo ni Cristo.

Sumagot si Pedro: Ang aming Guro, na siyang tunay na Propeta at laging tapat sa Kanyang sarili, ay hindi sumalungat sa sarili Niya at hindi Niya kami inutusan ng anumang hindi Niya mismo isinagawa. Nang sabihin Niyang, 'Hindi ako naparito upang magdala ng kapayapaan sa lupa kundi ng tabak; at mula ngayon makikita ninyong mahihiwalay ang ama sa anak at ang anak sa ama, ang asawa sa asawa, ang ina sa anak na babae at ang anak na babae sa ina, ang kapatid sa kapatid, ang biyenan sa manugang, at ang kaibigan sa kaibigan,' ang lahat ng ito ay kaugnay pa rin ng aral tungkol sa kapayapaan. Sasabihin ko sa iyo kung paano. Sa simula ng Kanyang pangangaral, nais Niyang anyayahan at akayin ang lahat sa kaligtasan at turuan silang matiising harapin ang hirap at pagsubok. Kaya ipinahayag Niyang mapalad ang mga dukha at ipinangakong mapapasakanila ang kaharian ng langit dahil sa pagtitiis nila sa kahirapan. Sa pag-asang iyon, matitiis nila nang may kapanatagan ang bigat ng kahirapan at hindi sila matutukso sa kasakiman, na isa sa pinakamabigat at pinakamapaminsalang kasalanan. Ipinangako rin Niyang ang mga nagugutom at nauuhaw ay bubusugin ng walang hanggang mga pagpapala ng katuwiran, upang matiis nila ang kahirapan at hindi mapilitang gumawa ng anumang di-matuwid dahil dito. Gayundin, sinabi Niyang mapalad ang mga may malinis na puso at makikita nila ang Diyos, upang ang sinumang naghahangad ng napakadakilang pagpapalang iyon ay umiwas sa masasama at maruruming pag-iisip.

Kabanata 29. Kapayapaan at Alitan.

Sa ganitong paraan, habang tinuturuan ng aming Guro ang Kanyang mga alagad na magtiis, itinanim din Niya sa kanila na ang pagpapala ng kapayapaan ay kailangang ingatan sa pamamagitan ng pagtitiis. Sa kabilang dako, nahabag Siya at nagpahayag ng kapahamakan tungkol sa mga namumuhay sa kayamanan at luho ngunit walang ibinibigay sa mahihirap. Ipinakita Niyang mananagot sila dahil hindi sila naawa sa kanilang kapwa na naghihirap, gayong dapat nila silang mahalin gaya ng kanilang sarili. Sa mga ganitong salita, ang ilan ay nahikayat Niyang sumunod sa Kanya, samantalang ang iba nama'y naging laban sa Kanya. Inutusan Niya ang mga nananampalataya at sumusunod na mamuhay nang may kapayapaan sa isa't isa, at sinabi: 'Mapalad ang mga gumagawa ng kapayapaan, sapagkat sila'y tatawaging mga anak ng Diyos' (Mateo 5:9). Ngunit laban sa mga hindi lamang tumangging maniwala kundi lumaban pa sa Kanyang aral, ipinahayag Niya ang digmaan ng salita at ng pagpapabulaan. Kaya sinabi Niyang mula noon ay makikita ang anak na hiwalay sa ama, ang asawa sa asawa, ang anak na babae sa ina, ang kapatid sa kapatid, ang manugang na babae sa biyenang babae, at ang mga kaaway ng isang tao ay magiging mga kasambahay niya mismo. Kapag sa isang tahanan ay nagkaroon ng pagkakaiba sa pagitan ng mananampalataya at hindi mananampalataya, hindi maiiwasang magkaroon ng pagtatalo: nilalabanan ng hindi nananampalataya ang pananampalataya, samantalang ang mananampalataya ay sumasalungat sa dating kamalian at sa masasamang gawa na kasama nito.

Kabanata 30. Kapayapaan para sa mga Anak ng Kapayapaan.

Gayundin, sa huling bahagi ng Kanyang pagtuturo, hinarap Niya ang mga eskriba at mga Pariseo. Sinumbatan Niya sila dahil sa kanilang masasamang gawa, maling aral, at sa pagtatago nila ng susi ng kaalaman na ipinamana sa kanila mula kay Moises—ang susi na makapagbubukas sana ng pintuan ng kaharian ng langit. Ngunit nang suguin kami ng aming Guro upang mangaral, iniutos Niyang sa anumang lungsod o bahay na pasukin namin ay sabihin namin: 'Kapayapaan sa bahay na ito.' Sinabi rin Niya: 'Kung may anak ng kapayapaan doon, mananatili sa kanya ang inyong kapayapaan; kung wala, babalik sa inyo ang inyong kapayapaan.' At kapag aalis kami sa bahay o lungsod na iyon, dapat naming ipagpag laban sa kanila maging ang alikabok na dumikit sa aming mga paa. 'Sa araw ng paghuhukom,' sabi Niya, 'mas magaan pa ang sasapitin ng lupain ng Sodoma at Gomorra kaysa sa lungsod o bahay na iyon.' Iniutos lamang itong gawin matapos maipangaral muna sa lungsod o bahay ang salita ng katotohanan. Sa gayon, ang mga tumatanggap sa pananampalataya ng katotohanan ay magiging mga anak ng kapayapaan at mga anak ng Diyos, at ang mga tumatanggi rito ay mapatutunayang mga kaaway ng kapayapaan at ng Diyos.

Kabanata 31. Kapayapaan at Digmaan.

Kaya sa pagsunod namin sa utos ng aming Guro, kapayapaan muna ang inihahandog namin sa mga nakikinig upang makilala nila ang daan ng kaligtasan nang walang kaguluhan. Ngunit kung may tumangging tumanggap sa mga salita ng kapayapaan at hindi sumang-ayon sa katotohanan, alam namin kung paano gamitin laban sa kanya ang digmaan ng salita: mahigpit naming pinabubulaanan ang kanyang kamangmangan at inilalantad ang kanyang mga kasalanan. Kaya kailangan muna naming maghandog ng kapayapaan, upang kung ang isang tao ay anak ng kapayapaan ay dumating sa kanya ang aming kapayapaan; ngunit kung ginagawa niya ang sarili niyang kaaway ng kapayapaan, babalik sa amin ang aming kapayapaan. Hindi namin iniaalok ang kapayapaan sa paraang pakikipagkasundo sa masasama, gaya ng paratang mo—kung ganoon sana, matagal na naming iniabot sa iyo ang aming kamay bilang tanda ng pagsang-ayon. Ang layunin lamang ay upang sa mahinahon at matiising pag-uusap ay mas madaling makita ng mga nakikinig kung alin ang tunay na pananalita. Ngunit kung ikaw mismo ay hindi magkasundo sa sarili mong sinasabi, paano ka mananatiling nakatayo? Ang nahahati laban sa sarili ay tiyak na babagsak, sapagkat 'ang bawat kahariang nahahati laban sa sarili ay hindi mananatili' (Mateo 12:25). Kung may maitutugon ka rito, sabihin mo.

Kabanata 32. Hamon ni Simon.

Sinabi ni Simon: Namamangha ako sa iyong kahangalan. Inihaharap mo ang mga salita ng iyong Guro na para bang tiyak nang propeta siya, samantalang madali kong mapatutunayang madalas siyang sumalungat sa sarili niya. Sa mismong mga salitang binanggit mo kita pabubulaanan. Sinasabi mong sinabi niya na ang bawat kaharian o lungsod na nahahati laban sa sarili ay hindi mananatili. Ngunit sinasabi mo ring sa ibang pagkakataon ay sinabi niyang magdadala siya ng tabak upang paghiwalayin ang mga nasa iisang bahay: anak laban sa ama, anak na babae laban sa ina, kapatid laban sa kapatid; kaya kung lima ang nasa isang bahay, tatlo ang laban sa dalawa at dalawa ang laban sa tatlo (Lucas 12:51–53). Kung ang lahat ng nahahati ay bumabagsak, ang sinumang gumagawa ng paghahati ang nagbibigay ng dahilan ng pagbagsak. At kung ganoon siya, tiyak na masama siya. Sagutin mo iyan kung kaya mo.

Kabanata 33. Awtoridad.

Sinabi ni Pedro: Huwag kang padalus-dalos sa pagpuna, O Simon, sa mga bagay na hindi mo nauunawaan. Una, sasagutin ko ang sinabi mong ginagamit ko ang mga salita ng aking Guro upang lutasin ang mga usaping pinagdududahan pa. Nang suguin kaming mga apostol upang mangaral, iniutos ng aming Panginoon na ituro namin sa lahat ng bansa ang mga bagay na ipinagkatiwala Niya sa amin (Mateo 28:19–20). Hindi namin kailangang ulitin lamang ang mga iyon nang eksakto sa paraan ng pagkakasabi Niya. Ang aming atas ay hindi basta magsalita kundi magturo ng mga bagay na iyon at ipakita kung paano nakasalig sa katotohanan ang bawat isa. Wala rin kaming pahintulot na magsalita ng sarili naming aral. Kami ay isinugo; at ang isinugo ay kailangang maghatid ng mensahe ayon sa iniutos sa kanya at ihayag ang kalooban ng nagsugo. Kung magsasalita ako ng anumang iba sa iniutos ng nagsugo sa akin, magiging huwad akong apostol, sapagkat hindi ko sasabihin ang ipinag-utos kundi ang para sa akin ay mabuti. Ang gumagawa niyan ay maliwanag na gustong ipakitang mas mahusay siya kaysa sa nagsugo sa kanya, at walang duda na isa siyang taksil. Ngunit kung mananatili siya sa ipinag-utos at malinaw na inilalahad ang mga iyon, makikitang ginagawa niya ang gawain ng isang apostol. Dahil nagsisikap akong tuparin ang tungkuling iyan kaya hindi ka nasisiyahan sa akin. Kaya huwag mo akong sisihin dahil inihaharap ko ang mga salita ng nagsugo sa akin. Kung may anumang hindi tama sa mga salitang iyon, malaya kang pabulaanan ako. Ngunit hindi mo iyon magagawa, sapagkat Siya ay isang Propeta at hindi maaaring sumalungat sa sarili Niya. Kung hindi mo Siya kinikilalang Propeta, iyon muna ang dapat nating siyasatin.

Kabanata 34. Wastong Ayos ng Pagpapatunay.

Sinabi ni Simon: Hindi ko kailangang matutuhan mula sa iyo kung propeta siya. Ang nais kong malaman ay kung paano nagkakasundo ang mga sinabi niya. Kung mapatutunayang salungat siya sa sarili, kasabay niyon ay mapatutunayang hindi siya propeta. Sumagot si Pedro: Ngunit kung mauna kong mapatunayan na Siya ay Propeta, susunod na ang anumang waring salungatan ay hindi tunay na salungatan. Hindi mapatutunayang propeta ang isang tao dahil lamang pare-pareho ang kanyang sinasabi, sapagkat marami ang kayang maging pare-pareho sa kanilang pananalita. Kung hindi sapat ang pagkakapare-pareho upang gawing propeta ang isang tao, lalo ring hindi sapat ang waring di-pagkakapare-pareho upang patunayang hindi siya propeta. Maraming bagay ang sa unang tingin ay tila salungat ngunit sa mas malalim na pagsusuri ay nagkakasundo pala. Mayroon din namang wari'y magkakatugma ngunit kapag masusing siniyasat ay lumalabas na hindi. Kaya sa palagay ko, ang pinakamainam na paraan ng paghatol sa mga bagay na ito ay alamin muna kung propeta nga ang nagsalita ng mga pahayag na waring nagkakasalungatan. Kung mapatutunayang Propeta Siya, maliwanag na ang mga waring salungatan ay may tunay na pagkakaisa ngunit mali lamang ang pagkaunawa natin sa mga ito. Kaya nararapat na humingi ng patunay tungkol dito. Kami bilang mga apostol ay isinugo upang ipaliwanag ang mga salita at pagtibayin ang mga pasiya ng nagsugo sa amin. Hindi kami binigyan ng atas na magpahayag ng sarili naming aral, kundi ihayag ang katotohanan ng Kanyang mga salita, gaya ng sinabi ko.

Kabanata 35. Kung Bakit Hindi Makapananatili ang Kamalian Kasama ng Katotohanan.

Sinabi ni Simon: Kung gayon, ipaliwanag mo sa amin kung paano magiging tama na ang isang taong lumilikha ng paghahati—na nagiging dahilan naman ng pagbagsak ng mga nahahati—ay maituring na mabuti o masabing naparito upang iligtas ang mga tao. Sumagot si Pedro: Ipapaliwanag ko. Sinabi ng aming Guro na ang bawat kaharian at bawat bahay na nahahati laban sa sarili ay hindi mananatili. At bagaman Siya rin ang nagdudulot ng isang uri ng paghahati, makikita mong ang paghahating iyon ay para sa kaligtasan. Sa pamamagitan ng salita ng katotohanan, hinahati Niya ang kaharian ng sanlibutan na nakatayo sa kamalian, at gayundin ang bawat tahanang kabilang dito, upang bumagsak ang kamalian at maghari ang katotohanan. Ngunit kung sa isang tahanan ay ang kamalian naman ang pumasok at siyang naghati sa katotohanan, kapag nakapuwesto na ang kamalian doon, tiyak na hindi mananatili ang katotohanan. Sinabi ni Simon: Ngunit hindi pa tiyak kung kamalian o katotohanan ang hinahati ng iyong Guro. Sumagot si Pedro: Ibang tanong na iyan. Ngunit kung sang-ayon kang ang nahahati ay bumabagsak, ang natitira kong patunayan—kung makikinig ka lamang nang mapayapa—ay na sa pagtuturo ng katotohanan ay hinati at pinawi ng ating Jesus ang kamalian.

Kabanata 36. Mainitang Pagtatalo.

Sinabi ni Simon: Huwag mong paulit-ulit banggitin ang kapayapaan. Sabihin mo na lamang nang maikli kung ano talaga ang iniisip at pinaniniwalaan mo. Sumagot si Pedro: Bakit ka natatakot na paulit-ulit marinig ang tungkol sa kapayapaan? Hindi mo ba alam na ang kapayapaan ang ganap na katuparan ng kautusan? Ang mga digmaan at pagtatalo ay nagmumula sa kasalanan. Kung walang kasalanan, may kapayapaan sa kaluluwa; at kung may kapayapaan, matatagpuan ang katotohanan sa pagtatalakayan at ang katuwiran sa mga gawa. Sinabi ni Simon: Para bang hindi mo kayang sabihin nang malinaw kung ano ang pinaniniwalaan mo. Sumagot si Pedro: Magsasalita ako, ngunit ayon sa aking sariling pasiya at hindi dahil pinipilit ako ng mga pakana mo. Nais kong malaman ng lahat ang bagay na makapagliligtas at makabubuti sa kanila, kaya hindi ko ipagpapaliban ang pinakamaikling paglalahad nito. Iisa ang Diyos. Siya ang Manlilikha ng sanlibutan at ang matuwid na Hukom na sa takdang panahon ay gaganti sa bawat isa ayon sa kanyang mga gawa. Ngunit alam kong upang patunayan ang mga pahayag na ito ay maaaring kailanganin ang napakaraming salita.

Kabanata 37. Tusong Pangangatwiran ni Simon.

Sinabi ni Simon: Hinahangaan ko ang bilis ng iyong pag-iisip, ngunit hindi ko tinatanggap ang kamalian ng iyong pananampalataya. Matalino mong inunahan ang magiging pagtutol sa iyo at maayos mo pang inaming kakailanganin ang napakaraming salita upang patunayan ang mga bagay na ito, sapagkat halos walang sumasang-ayon sa ipinahahayag mong pananampalataya. Halimbawa, sino ang tatanggap sa aral na iisa lamang ang Diyos at ang sanlibutan ay gawa Niya? Sa palagay ko'y walang sinuman sa mga pagano, kahit hindi pinag-aralan, at tiyak na wala sa mga pilosopo. Maging ang pinakapayak at pinakakaawa-awang Judio ay hindi; at ako man, na bihasa sa kanilang kautusan, ay hindi rin. Sinabi ni Pedro: Iwan mo muna ang mga palagay ng mga taong wala rito. Sabihin mo nang harapan kung ano ang sarili mong paniniwala. Sumagot si Simon: Kaya kong sabihin kung ano talaga ang iniisip ko, ngunit may dahilan kung bakit nag-aatubili ako. Kung sasabihin ko ang hindi mo matatanggap at hindi rin magmumukhang tama sa mangmang na karamihang ito, ikaw, dahil malilito, ay agad na magtatakip ng iyong mga tainga upang huwag kunwari'y madungisan ng kalapastanganan at tatakas dahil wala kang maisagot. Samantala, ang walang katuwirang karamihan ay kakampi sa iyo at yayakap sa iyo dahil itinuturo mo ang mga bagay na karaniwan na nilang tinatanggap, at susumpain ako dahil nagpapahayag ako ng bago at hindi pa naririnig na mga bagay at nagtatanim umano ng kamalian sa isip ng iba.

Kabanata 38. Ang Paniniwala ni Simon.

Sinabi ni Pedro: Hindi ba't ikaw ngayon ang gumagamit ng mahahabang pambungad na ibinintang mo sa amin, marahil dahil wala kang katotohanang maihaharap? Kung mayroon ka, magsimula ka nang walang paikot-ikot, yamang sinasabi mong may lakas ng loob ka. Kung may sinuman sa mga nakikinig na ayaw sa sasabihin mo, maaari siyang umalis; at ang mga mananatili ay mapipilitang kilalanin ang totoo kung iyon nga ang maipakikita mo. Kaya ipaliwanag mo na kung ano ang sa palagay mo'y tama. Sinabi ni Simon: Sinasabi kong maraming diyos, ngunit may isang hindi kayang unawain at hindi kilala ng lahat, at siya ang Diyos ng lahat ng mga diyos na iyon. Sumagot si Pedro: Ang Diyos na sinasabi mong hindi kayang unawain at hindi kilala ng lahat—mapatutunayan mo ba ang pag-iral Niya mula sa mga Kasulatan ng mga Judio na kinikilalang may awtoridad, o mula sa ibang mga kasulatang hindi namin nalalaman, o mula sa mga manunulat na Griyego, o mula sa sarili mong mga akda? Malaya kang gumamit ng alinmang kasulatan, basta patunayan mo munang propetiko ang mga iyon; sa gayon, magiging walang alinlangan ang kanilang awtoridad.

Kabanata 39. Pangangatwiran para sa Maraming Diyos.

Sinabi ni Simon: Ang kautusan lamang ng mga Judio ang gagamitin ko. Alam ng lahat ng may malasakit sa relihiyon na kinikilala sa pangkalahatan ang awtoridad ng kautusang ito, bagaman inuunawa ito ng bawat tao ayon sa sariling paghatol. Isinulat ito ng Lumikha ng sanlibutan sa paraang dito nakasalalay ang paniniwala tungkol sa mga bagay. Kaya kung may maghahayag ng katotohanan o may maghahayag ng kasinungalingan, walang pahayag na tatanggapin kung hindi ito maiaayon sa kautusang ito. Dahil ang aking kaalaman ang higit na naaayon sa kautusan, tama lamang ang sinabi kong maraming diyos, at may isang higit sa lahat, hindi kayang unawain, at siyang Diyos ng mga diyos. Na maraming diyos ay itinuturo mismo ng kautusan. Una, sa bahaging ito ng salaysay, ang ahas ay nagsasalita kay Eva, ang unang babae, sinasabi: 'Sa araw na kainin ninyo ang bunga ng punong nagbibigay-kaalaman ng mabuti at masama, magiging gaya kayo ng mga diyos' (Genesis 3:5)—ibig sabihin, gaya ng mga gumawa sa tao. At pagkakain nila, sinabi mismo ng Diyos sa ibang mga diyos: 'Narito, si Adan ay naging gaya ng isa sa atin' (Genesis 3:22). Kaya malinaw na maraming diyos ang nakibahagi sa paggawa ng tao. Noong una ay sinabi rin ng Diyos sa ibang mga diyos, 'Lalangin natin ang tao ayon sa ating larawan at wangis' (Genesis 1:26); at sinabi rin Niya, 'Palayasin natin siya' (Genesis 3:22), at muli, 'Halikayo, bumaba tayo at guluhin natin ang kanilang wika' (Genesis 11:7). Ipinahihiwatig ng lahat ng ito na maraming diyos. Nasusulat din: 'Huwag mong sumpain ang mga diyos ni ang pinuno ng iyong bayan' (Exodo 22:28). At ang pahayag na, 'Ang Diyos lamang ang nanguna sa kanila at walang ibang diyos na kasama Niya' (Deuteronomio 32:12), ay nagpapakitang may ibang mga diyos. Marami pang ibang patotoo sa kautusan, hindi lamang malalabo kundi hayag, na nagtuturong maraming diyos. Isa sa mga ito ang napili sa pamamagitan ng kapalaran upang maging diyos ng mga Judio. Ngunit hindi siya ang tinutukoy ko. Ang tinutukoy ko ay ang Diyos na siya ring Diyos niya, na hindi nakilala maging ng mga Judio. Hindi siya Diyos ng mga Judio kundi Diyos ng mga nakakakilala sa kanya.

Kabanata 40. Sagot ni Pedro.

Nang marinig ito ni Pedro, sumagot siya: Huwag kang matakot, Simon. Narito, hindi namin tinakpan ang aming mga tainga at hindi rin kami tumakas. Sa halip, sasagutin namin sa pamamagitan ng mga salita ng katotohanan ang mga maling sinabi mo. Una, ipinapahayag naming iisa ang Diyos—ang Diyos ng mga Judio. Siya lamang ang Diyos, ang Manlilikha ng langit at lupa, at Siya rin ang Diyos ng lahat ng tinatawag mong mga diyos. Kung maipakikita kong walang nakahihigit sa Kanya at Siya mismo ang higit sa lahat, aaminin mong napakalaki ng iyong kamalian. Sinabi ni Simon: Kahit ayaw ko pang aminin, hindi ba ako pagbibintangan ng mga nakikinig dito na tumatanggi akong kilalanin ang katotohanan kung mapatutunayan mo nga iyon?

Kabanata 41. Ipinagpatuloy ang Sagot.

Sinabi ni Pedro: Kung gayon, makinig ka. Una mong dapat malaman na kahit ipagpalagay nating maraming tinatawag na diyos, gaya ng sinasabi mo, silang lahat ay nasa ilalim ng Diyos ng mga Judio. Walang kapantay ang Diyos na ito at walang maaaring maging higit sa Kanya. Nasusulat na sinabi ng propetang si Moises sa mga Judio: 'Ang Panginoon ninyong Diyos ay Diyos ng mga diyos at Panginoon ng mga panginoon, ang dakilang Diyos' (Deuteronomio 10:17). Kaya kahit maraming tinatawag na mga diyos, ang Diyos ng mga Judio lamang ang tinatawag na Diyos ng mga diyos. Sapagkat hindi lahat ng tinatawag na diyos ay tunay na Diyos. Maging si Moises ay tinawag na diyos para kay Faraon (Exodo 7:1), ngunit malinaw na tao siya. Tinawag ding mga diyos ang mga hukom, bagaman maliwanag na sila'y mga taong namamatay. Tinatawag ding mga diyos ang mga idolo ng mga Hentil, ngunit alam nating hindi sila tunay na mga diyos. Ito'y naging kaparusahan sa masasama: dahil ayaw nilang kilalanin ang tunay na Diyos, hinayaan silang ituring na diyos ang anumang anyo o larawang mapili nila. Dahil tumanggi silang tumanggap ng kaalaman tungkol sa Iisang siyang Diyos ng lahat, gaya ng sinabi ko, pinahintulutan silang magkaroon ng mga diyos na walang anumang magagawa para sa mga sumasamba sa kanila. Sapagkat ano ang maibibigay sa tao ng patay na larawan o maging ng buhay na nilalang, kung ang kapangyarihan sa lahat ng bagay ay nasa Iisa?

Kabanata 42. Mga Anghel na Tagapangalaga.

Kaya ginagamit ang pangalang 'diyos' sa tatlong paraan: una, sa tunay na Diyos; ikalawa, sa lingkod ng tunay na Diyos; at ikatlo, sa isang isinugo na tinatawag sa pangalan ng nagsugo sa kanya upang maipakita nang buo ang awtoridad ng nagsugo. Madalas itong mangyari sa mga anghel. Kapag nagpakita ang isang anghel sa isang matalino at maunawaing tao, itinatanong ng taong iyon ang pangalan ng nagpakita upang malaman niya kapwa ang karangalan ng isinugo at ang awtoridad ng nagsugo. Sapagkat bawat bansa ay may anghel na pinagkatiwalaan ng Diyos na mamahala rito. Kapag nagpapakita ang isa sa mga anghel na ito, maaaring isipin at tawagin siyang diyos ng mga taong pinamamahalaan niya; ngunit kapag tinanong siya, hindi niya inaangkin para sa sarili ang gayong pagkakakilanlan. Hinati ng Kataas-taasang Diyos, na Siya lamang may kapangyarihan sa lahat, ang lahat ng bansa sa lupa sa pitumpu't dalawang bahagi at nagtalaga ng mga anghel bilang mga pinuno sa mga iyon. Ngunit sa pinakadakila sa mga arkanghel ipinagkatiwala ang pamamahala sa mga taong naunang tumanggap sa pagsamba at kaalaman sa Kataas-taasang Diyos. Gaya rin ng sinabi natin, ang mga banal na tao ay nagiging parang mga diyos para sa masasama sapagkat binigyan sila ng kapangyarihan sa buhay at kamatayan ng mga iyon, gaya ng nabanggit natin tungkol kay Moises at sa mga hukom. Kaya nasusulat tungkol sa kanila: 'Huwag mong sumpain ang mga diyos at huwag mong sumpain ang pinuno ng iyong bayan' (Exodo 22:28). Sa ganitong kahulugan tinatawag na mga diyos ang mga pinuno ng iba't ibang bansa. Ngunit si Cristo ang Diyos ng mga pinuno at Hukom ng lahat. Kaya hindi tunay na mga diyos ang mga anghel, mga tao, o anumang nilalang, sapagkat silang lahat ay nasa ilalim ng awtoridad, nilikha, at maaaring magbago: ang mga anghel, sapagkat dati silang wala at pagkatapos ay umiral; ang mga tao, sapagkat namamatay sila; at bawat nilalang, sapagkat maaari itong mawala kung nanaisin ng Lumikha nito na alisin ito. Kaya Siya lamang ang tunay na Diyos—hindi lamang Siya mismo ang nabubuhay; Siya rin ang nagbibigay ng buhay sa iba at maaari Niyang bawiin iyon kung Kanyang naisin.

Kabanata 43. Walang Ibang Diyos Kundi si Jehova.

Kaya ipinahahayag ng Kasulatan sa pangalan ng Diyos ng mga Judio: 'Tingnan ninyo, tingnan ninyo na Ako ang Diyos at wala nang iba maliban sa Akin. Ako ang pumapatay at Ako ang bumubuhay; Ako ang sumusugat at Ako ang nagpapagaling; at walang makapagliligtas mula sa Aking kamay' (Deuteronomio 32:39). Tingnan mo kung paanong sa isang paraang hindi kayang ipahayag ay inuunahan ng Kasulatan ang mga kamaliang lilitaw sa hinaharap—ang mga magsasabing may ibang diyos sa langit o sa lupa bukod sa Diyos ng mga Judio—sa pagsasabing: 'Ang Panginoon ninyong Diyos ay iisang Diyos, sa langit sa itaas at sa lupa sa ibaba; at bukod sa Kanya ay wala nang iba' (Deuteronomio 4:39). Paano mo ngayon nangahas sabihing may ibang Diyos bukod sa Diyos ng mga Judio? At muli, sinasabi ng Kasulatan: 'Narito, sa Panginoon ninyong Diyos ang langit at ang langit ng mga langit, ang lupa, at lahat ng naroon; gayunman, pinili Niya ang inyong mga ninuno upang ibigin sila, at kayo pagkatapos nila' (Deuteronomio 10:14–15). Sa lahat ng panig ay pinatutunayan ng Kasulatan ang hatol na ito: ang Lumikha ng sanlibutan ang tunay at tanging Diyos.

Kabanata 44. Ang Ahas Bilang Pinagmulan ng Paniniwala sa Maraming Diyos.

Kahit may iba pang tinatawag na mga diyos, gaya ng sinabi natin, silang lahat ay nasa ilalim ng kapangyarihan ng Diyos ng mga Judio. Ganito ang sinasabi ng Kasulatan sa mga Judio: 'Ang Panginoon nating Diyos ay Diyos ng mga diyos at Panginoon ng mga panginoon' (Deuteronomio 10:17). Siya lamang din ang iniuutos ng Kasulatan na sambahin: 'Sambahin mo ang Panginoon mong Diyos, at Siya lamang ang paglingkuran mo'; at, 'Dinggin mo, O Israel: ang Panginoon mong Diyos ay iisang Diyos' (Deuteronomio 6:4). Maging ang mga banal na napuspos ng Espiritu ng Diyos at nadiligan ng Kanyang awa ay sumigaw: 'Sino ang gaya Mo sa gitna ng mga diyos, O Panginoon? Sino ang gaya Mo?' At muli: 'Sino ang Diyos maliban sa Panginoon? Sino ang Diyos maliban sa ating Panginoon?' Kaya habang unti-unting umuunlad ang bayan, dahan-dahang itinuro sa kanila ni Moises ang pag-unawa na iisa lamang ang naghaharing Diyos at na sa Iisang Diyos dapat sumampalataya. Sinabi niya: 'Huwag ninyong banggitin ang mga pangalan ng ibang mga diyos.' Walang dudang naalala niya ang kaparusahan sa ahas na unang nagpasok ng pangalan ng 'mga diyos.' Hinatulan ang ahas na kumain ng alabok at itinuring na karapat-dapat sa gayong pagkain dahil siya ang unang nagpasok sa sanlibutan ng pangalan ng maraming diyos. Kung nais mo ring ipakilala ang maraming diyos, mag-ingat kang huwag kang makibahagi sa kaparusahan ng ahas.

Kabanata 45. Walang Maidadahilan para sa Paniniwala sa Maraming Diyos.

Tiyakin mong hindi mo kami mahihikayat na makibahagi sa gayong pagtatangka, ni pahihintulutan naming malinlang mo kami. Sa paghuhukom, hindi magiging dahilan ang pagsasabing ikaw ang nanlinlang sa amin. Hindi rin nakaligtas ang unang babae sa kaparusahan sa pagsasabing nilinlang siya ng ahas; nahatulan siya sa kamatayan dahil naniwala siya sa mali. Kaya nang ituro ni Moises sa bayan ang pananampalataya sa iisang Diyos, sinabi niya: 'Mag-ingat ka upang hindi ka mailigaw palayo sa Panginoon mong Diyos' (Deuteronomio 8:11). Pansinin mong ginamit niya ang parehong salitang ginamit ng unang babae sa pagdadahilang siya'y nalinlang; ngunit wala iyong naitulong sa kanya. Higit pa rito, kahit may bumangong tunay na propeta na gumawa ng mga tanda at kababalaghan ngunit susubukang hikayatin tayong sumamba sa ibang mga diyos bukod sa Diyos ng mga Judio, hindi natin siya dapat paniwalaan. Ganito tayo tinuruan ng banal na kautusan, at sa pamamagitan ng tradisyon ay ipinasa rin nito ang isang mas malalim na tagubilin: 'Kung may lumitaw sa inyo na propeta o nananaginip, at magbigay siya ng tanda o kababalaghan, at mangyari ang tanda o kababalaghang sinabi niya, ngunit sabihin niya sa inyo, "Halikayo at sambahin natin ang ibang mga diyos na hindi ninyo kilala," huwag ninyong pakinggan ang mga salita ng propetang iyon o ng nananaginip na iyon, sapagkat sinusubok kayo upang makita kung iniibig ninyo ang Panginoon ninyong Diyos' (Deuteronomio 13:1–3).

Kabanata 46. Kinilala ni Cristo ang Diyos ng mga Judio.

Kaya ang aming Panginoon din, na gumawa ng mga tanda at kababalaghan, ay nangaral tungkol sa Diyos ng mga Judio; at dahil dito tama kaming maniwala sa Kanyang ipinangaral. Ngunit ikaw, kahit tunay ka pang propeta at makagawa ng mga tanda at kababalaghan gaya ng ipinapangako mo, kung magpapahayag ka ng ibang mga diyos bukod sa tunay na Diyos, malinaw na itinaas ka lamang bilang pagsubok sa bayan ng Diyos; kaya hindi ka maaaring paniwalaan. Siya lamang ang tunay na Diyos—ang Diyos ng mga Judio. Ito ang dahilan kung bakit hindi itinuro ng ating Panginoong Jesu-Cristo sa kanila na hanapin muna kung sino ang Diyos, sapagkat kilala na nila Siya. Sa halip, itinuro Niyang hanapin nila ang Kanyang kaharian at katuwiran (Mateo 6:33), na ang mga eskriba at mga Pariseo, bagaman natanggap nila ang susi ng kaalaman, ay hindi pinapasok ang iba kundi hinaharangan pa sila (Lucas 11:52). Kung hindi nila kilala ang tunay na Diyos, tiyak na hindi sana pinabayaan ng Panginoon ang pinakamahalagang bagay na ito at pagkatapos ay sinaway lamang sila sa maliliit na bagay—tulad ng pagpapalapad ng mga palawit ng kanilang damit, paghahangad ng pinakamataas na upuan sa mga handaan, pananalangin nang nakatayo sa mga lansangan, at iba pang katulad nito. Kung ihahambing sa napakalaking pagkukulang na hindi pagkilala sa Diyos, tunay na maliliit at halos walang kabuluhan ang mga bagay na iyon.

Kabanata 47. Pagtutol ni Simon.

Sumagot si Simon: Sa mismong mga salita ng inyong Guro ko kayo pasisinungalingan, sapagkat maging Siya ay nagpapakilala sa lahat ng tao ng isang Diyos na hindi kilala. Bagaman nakilala ni Adan ang Diyos na lumikha sa kanya at gumawa ng sanlibutan; nakilala rin Siya ni Enoc, sapagkat Siya ang naglipat sa kanya; at nakilala Siya ni Noe, sapagkat Siya ang nag-utos sa kanyang gumawa ng daong. At bagaman nakilala nina Abraham, Isaac, Jacob, Moises, at ng lahat ng tao—maging ng bawat bayan at lahat ng bansa—ang Maylikha ng sanlibutan at kinikilala nilang Siya ay Diyos, gayunman, ang inyong Jesus, na lumitaw matagal na panahon pagkaraan ng mga patriarka, ay nagsabi: ‘Walang nakakakilala sa Anak kundi ang Ama; at walang nakakakilala sa Ama kundi ang Anak at yaong pinili ng Anak na pagpahayagan Niya.’ Kaya maging ang inyong Jesus ay kumikilala na may isa pang Diyos na hindi kayang maunawaan at hindi kilala ng lahat.

Kabanata 48. Sagot ni Pedro.

Sinabi ni Pedro: Hindi mo napapansin na sinasalungat ng sarili mong mga pahayag ang isa’t isa. Sapagkat kung kilala rin ng aming Jesus ang tinatawag mong hindi kilalang Diyos, hindi ikaw lamang ang nakakakilala sa Kanya. At kung kilala Siya ng aming Jesus, tiyak na hindi rin Siya maaaring hindi nakilala ni Moises, na nagpropesiya tungkol sa pagdating ni Jesus. Sapagkat propeta si Moises; at ang nagpropesiya tungkol sa Anak ay walang dudang nakakakilala rin sa Ama. Kung nasa pagpapasya ng Anak kung kanino Niya ihahayag ang Ama, kung gayon ang Anak—na kasama ng Ama mula pa sa pasimula at sa lahat ng salinlahi—ay nagpahayag ng Ama hindi lamang kay Moises kundi pati sa iba pang mga propeta. At kung gayon, malinaw na hindi naging lingid ang Ama sa alinman sa kanila. Ngunit paano maihahayag sa iyo ang Ama gayong hindi ka naniniwala sa Anak, kung walang nakakakilala sa Ama maliban sa taong pinili ng Anak na pagpahayagan Niya? Inihahayag ng Anak ang Ama sa mga nagpaparangal sa Anak gaya ng pagpaparangal nila sa Ama. Juan 5:23

Kabanata 49. Ang Kataas-taasang Liwanag.

Pagkatapos ay sinabi ni Simon: Alalahanin mong sinabi mong may Anak ang Diyos; sa ganitong pahayag ay para mo na ring nilalapastangan Siya. Paano magkakaroon ng anak ang Diyos malibang Siya’y napaiilalim sa mga pagnanasa at kalagayan ng katawan gaya ng mga tao o hayop? Ngunit wala tayong panahon ngayon upang ipakita ko ang sukdulan ng iyong kamangmangan tungkol sa mga bagay na ito, sapagkat nais kong agad magsalita tungkol sa walang-sukat na lawak ng kataas-taasang liwanag. Kaya makinig ka. Sa palagay ko ay may isang kapangyarihang binubuo ng napakalawak at hindi maipahayag na liwanag, na ang kadakilaan ay hindi kayang maunawaan. At ang kapangyarihang ito ay hindi nakikilala maging ng gumawa ng sanlibutan, ni ni Moises na tagapagbigay ng kautusan, ni ng inyong gurong si Jesus.

Kabanata 50. Kapangahasan ni Simon.

Sumagot si Pedro: Hindi ba kabaliwan para sa isang tao na mag-angking may ibang Diyos bukod sa Diyos ng lahat, at sabihing ipinapalagay lamang niyang may isang kapangyarihan, ngunit buong tapang niya itong ipinahahayag sa iba bago pa siya mismo makatiyak sa sinasabi niya? May tao bang napakapangahas na maniniwala sa iyong mga salita gayong nakikita niyang ikaw mismo ay nag-aalinlangan, at aaminin niyang may kapangyarihang hindi kilala ng Diyos na Manlilikha, ni ni Moises, ng mga propeta, ng kautusan, at maging ng aming Gurong si Jesus—isang kapangyarihang napakabuti raw ngunit ayaw magpakilala kaninuman maliban sa iisang tao, at ang taong iyon ay tulad mo! At kung bago nga ang kapangyarihang iyon, bakit hindi ito magbigay sa atin ng bagong pandama bukod sa limang taglay natin, upang sa pandamang iyon na ipagkakaloob nito ay matanggap at maunawaan natin ang mismong bagong kapangyarihang iyon? Kung hindi nito kayang magbigay sa atin ng gayong pandama, paano ito nakapagbigay sa iyo? At kung nagpahayag ito sa iyo, bakit hindi rin sa amin? Kung nauunawaan mo sa sarili mo ang mga bagay na hindi man lamang nakita o naunawaan ng mga propeta, sabihin mo sa amin ngayon kung ano ang iniisip ng bawat isa sa amin. Sapagkat kung nasa iyo ang espiritung nakaaalam ng mga bagay sa itaas ng kalangitan—mga bagay na hindi alam at hindi maunawaan ng lahat—lalo nang mas madali para sa iyo na malaman ang iniisip ng mga taong narito sa lupa. Ngunit kung hindi mo malaman ang iniisip naming nakatayo rito sa harap mo, paano mo masasabi na alam mo ang mga bagay na sinasabi mong walang sinumang nakaaalam?

Kabanata 51. Ang Ikaanim na Pandama.

Ngunit maniwala ka sa akin: hindi mo malalaman kung ano ang liwanag kung hindi ka tumanggap ng paningin at pagkaunawa mula sa liwanag mismo; gayundin sa iba pang mga bagay. Kaya matapos kang tumanggap ng kakayahang umunawa, bumubuo ka ngayon sa iyong guniguni ng isang bagay na mas dakila at mas mataas, na para kang nananaginip, ngunit ang lahat ng palatandaan mo ay hinango mo pa rin sa limang pandamang iyon na ipinagkaloob ng Diyos na hindi mo pinasasalamatan. Kaya tiyakin mo ito: hangga’t wala kang natatagpuang bagong pandama na higit sa limang pandamang tinatamasa nating lahat, hindi mo maaaring igiit na may bagong Diyos. Sumagot si Simon: Yamang ang lahat ng umiiral ay naaayon sa limang pandamang iyon, ang kapangyarihang higit sa lahat ay hindi na makapagdaragdag ng anumang bago. Sinabi ni Pedro: Mali iyan. Mayroon ding ikaanim na pandama—ang paunang pagkaalam. Ang limang pandama ay nakauukol sa pagkilala sa mga bagay, ngunit ang ikaanim ay ukol sa pagkaalam nang maaga; taglay ito ng mga propeta. Kung gayon, paano mo makikilala ang Diyos na sinasabi mong hindi kilala ng lahat, gayong hindi mo nga alam ang maka-propetang pandamang ito na siyang paunang pagkaalam? Nagsimulang magsalita si Simon: Ang kapangyarihang tinutukoy ko—na hindi kayang maunawaan at higit sa lahat, oo, higit pa maging sa Diyos na gumawa ng sanlibutan—ay hindi nakilala ng sinumang anghel, demonyo, Judio, ni ng anumang nilalang na umiiral sa pamamagitan ng Diyos na Manlilikha. Kung gayon, paano ako tuturuan ng kautusan ng Manlilikhang iyon tungkol sa isang bagay na hindi alam ng Manlilikha mismo, gayong hindi rin iyon alam ng kautusan upang maituro nito?

Kabanata 52. Pagpapabulaan sa Pamamagitan ng Kabalintunaan.

Sinabi ni Pedro: Nagtataka ako kung paano ka natuto mula sa kautusan nang higit pa sa kayang malaman o maituro ng kautusan. At paano mo masasabing kumukuha ka sa kautusan ng mga patunay para sa mga bagay na nais mong igiit, gayong sinasabi mong ni ang kautusan ni ang Nagbigay ng kautusan—samakatuwid, ang Manlilikha ng sanlibutan—ay hindi nakaaalam sa mga bagay na sinasabi mo! Isa pa, nagtataka ako kung paanong ikaw na nag-iisang nakaaalam sa mga bagay na ito ay nakatayo lamang dito kasama naming lahat, nakakulong sa hangganan ng maliit na patyo na ito. Nang makita ni Simon na tumatawa si Pedro at ang buong madla, sinabi niya: Tumatawa ka ba, Pedro, habang napakadakila at napakataas ng mga bagay na pinag-uusapan natin? Sumagot si Pedro: Huwag kang magalit, Simon. Wala kaming ginagawa kundi tuparin ang aming pangako. Hindi namin tinatakpan ang aming mga tainga, gaya ng sinabi mo, ni tumakas kami nang marinig naming inilalahad mo ang iyong mga hindi maipahayag na bagay; ni hindi man lamang kami umaalis sa aming kinaroroonan. Sa katunayan, wala ka namang inilalahad na may sapat na pagkakahawig sa katotohanan upang katakutan namin kahit kaunti. Ngunit sa anumang paraan, ipaliwanag mo sa amin ang ibig mong sabihin: paano mo natutuhan mula sa kautusan ang tungkol sa isang Diyos na hindi alam ng kautusan mismo at hindi rin alam ng Nagbigay ng kautusan? Sinabi ni Simon: Kung tapos na kayong tumawa, patutunayan ko ito sa pamamagitan ng malinaw na mga pahayag. Sumagot si Pedro: Sige, titigil ako, upang matutuhan ko sa iyo kung paano mo natutuhan mula sa kautusan ang hindi alam ni ng kautusan ni ng Diyos na nagbigay nito.

Kabanata 53. Kalapastanganan ni Simon.

Sinabi ni Simon: Makinig ka. Maliwanag sa lahat, at tiyak sa paraang hindi na maipaliwanag kung paano, na may isang Diyos na higit na mabuti kaysa sa lahat at mula sa Kanya nagmula ang lahat ng umiiral. Kaya kinakailangang ang lahat ng sumunod sa Kanya ay nasa ilalim Niya, sapagkat Siya ang pinuno at pinakadakila sa lahat. Nang makita ko, ayon sa itinuturo ng kautusan, na sa maraming bagay ay mahina ang Diyos na lumikha sa sanlibutan, samantalang ang kahinaan ay lubos na hindi naaayon sa isang ganap na Diyos, napagtanto kong hindi Siya ganap. Kaya kinakailangan kong magpasiya na may isa pang Diyos na ganap. Sapagkat, gaya ng sinabi ko, ayon sa mismong nasusulat sa kautusan, ang Diyos na ito ay ipinakikitang mahina sa maraming bagay. Una, ang taong Kanyang nilikha ay hindi nanatiling gaya ng Kanyang ninanais. At hindi rin maaaring maging mabuti ang nagbigay sa unang tao ng utos na maaari siyang kumain mula sa lahat ng puno sa paraiso ngunit hindi niya dapat hawakan ang punong nagbibigay ng kaalaman, at na kapag kinain niya iyon ay mamamatay siya. Bakit Niya pagbabawalan ang tao na kumain at malaman kung ano ang mabuti at masama, upang kapag nalaman niya ang mga ito ay maiwasan niya ang masama at piliin ang mabuti? Ngunit hindi Niya ito pinahintulutan. At nang kumain ang tao laban sa utos at malaman kung ano ang mabuti, natutuhan din niyang takpan ang kanyang kahubaran bilang pagpapakita ng paggalang—sapagkat napagtanto niyang hindi nararapat na tumayo siyang hubad sa harap ng kanyang Manlilikha. Gayunman, hinatulan ng Diyos sa kamatayan ang taong natutong magpakita sa Kanya ng paggalang, at isinumpa ang ahas na nagpakita sa tao ng mga bagay na ito. Ngunit kung mapapahamak ang tao sa pamamagitan nito, bakit inilagay pa Niya sa paraiso ang mismong sanhi ng kapahamakan? At kung mabuti ang inilagay Niya roon, hindi gawain ng isang mabuti ang pigilan ang iba sa isang bagay na mabuti.

Kabanata 54. Paano Natutuhan ni Simon mula sa Kautusan ang Hindi Itinuturo ng Kautusan.

Kaya, yamang ayon sa salaysay ng kautusan ay hindi ganap ang gumawa sa tao at sa sanlibutan, ipinauunawa nito sa atin nang walang pag-aalinlangan na may iba pang Diyos na ganap. Sapagkat kinakailangang may isang higit na dakila sa lahat, at dahil sa Kanya nananatili ang bawat nilalang sa wastong kalagayan nito. Kaya, dahil alam kong kailangang may isang higit na mahabagin at higit na makapangyarihan kaysa sa di-ganap na Diyos na nagbigay ng kautusan, naunawaan ko kung ano ang ganap sa pamamagitan ng paghahambing nito sa hindi ganap. Sa ganitong paraan, mula mismo sa Kasulatan ay naunawaan ko ang Diyos na hindi binabanggit doon. Kaya, O Pedro, natutuhan ko mula sa kautusan ang hindi alam ng kautusan. Ngunit kahit walang ibinigay na mga pahiwatig ang kautusan na magpapakitang hindi ganap ang Diyos na gumawa ng sanlibutan, maaari ko pa ring mahinuha mula sa masasamang bagay na ginagawa sa mundong ito at hindi itinutuwid na ang lumikha nito ay alinman sa walang kapangyarihan—kung hindi niya kayang ituwid ang mali—o masama—kung kaya niya ngunit ayaw niyang alisin ang kasamaan. At kung ni hindi niya kaya ni ayaw niya, hindi siya makapangyarihan at hindi rin mabuti. Kaya hindi maiiwasang ipasiya na may isa pang Diyos na higit na dakila at higit na makapangyarihan kaysa sa lahat. Kung may masasabi ka laban dito, sabihin mo.

Kabanata 55. Ibinalik kay Simon ang Kanyang mga Pagtutol.

Sumagot si Pedro: O Simon, ang ganitong mga kahangalan tungkol sa Diyos ay karaniwang iniisip ng mga taong nagbabasa ng kautusan nang walang pagtuturo ng mga guro. Itinuturing nila ang sarili nilang mga guro at inaakalang nauunawaan nila ang kautusan, kahit hindi ito ipinaliwanag sa kanila ng taong natuto mula sa Guro. Gayunman, upang waring sundin din namin ngayon ang aklat ng kautusan ayon sa sarili mong pagkaunawa rito, sinasabi mong ang Manlilikha ng sanlibutan ay parehong walang kapangyarihan at masama. Bakit hindi mo nakikita na ang kapangyarihang sinasabi mong higit sa lahat ay bagsak din sa kaparehong mga paratang? Maaari ring sabihin tungkol dito na wala itong kapangyarihan sapagkat hindi nito itinutuwid ang mga maling ginagawa rito; o kung kaya nito ngunit ayaw nito, masama ito; at kung ni hindi nito kaya ni nais, kapwa ito walang kapangyarihan at hindi ganap. Kaya ang bago mong kapangyarihan ay hindi lamang nasasangkot sa kaparehong paratang kundi sa mas malala pa—sapagkat ipinapalagay ninyong umiiral ito kahit hindi naman. Ang Diyos na lumikha sa sanlibutan ay maliwanag na umiiral dahil sa mismong gawa Niya ng paglikha, gaya ng inaamin mo rin. Ngunit ang kapangyarihang sinasabi mong ikaw lamang ang nakakakilala ay walang anumang palatandaan ng sarili nito na makapagpapatunay man lamang sa atin na ito nga ay umiiral.

Kabanata 56. Walang Diyos na Higit sa Manlilikha.

Anong uri ng asal iyon kung tatalikuran natin ang Diyos na sa Kanyang sanlibutan tayo nabubuhay at mula sa Kanya natin tinatamasa ang lahat ng kailangan sa buhay, upang sumunod sa isang hindi natin kilala—isang nilalang na hindi lamang walang anumang kabutihang ibinibigay sa atin kundi hindi man lamang natin matiyak na umiiral? At sa katotohanan, hindi nga siya umiiral. Kung tatawagin mo siyang liwanag at mas maliwanag pa kaysa sa liwanag na nakikita natin, hiniram mo ang mismong pangalang iyon mula sa Manlilikha ng sanlibutan. Kung sasabihin mong siya ay isang sangkap na higit sa lahat, ang ideyang iyon ay hinango mo rin mula sa Kanya at pinalaki lamang sa pananalita. Kung banggitin mo ang isip, kabutihan, buhay, o anupamang iba, sa Kanya mo rin hiniram ang mga salitang iyon. Kaya kung wala kang anumang tunay na bago tungkol sa kapangyarihang sinasabi mo—hindi lamang sa pagkaunawa kundi maging sa pangalan—paano ka makapagpapakilala ng bagong Diyos gayong wala ka man lamang mahanap na bagong pangalan para sa kanya? Sapagkat hindi lamang ang Manlilikha ng sanlibutan ang tinatawag na Kapangyarihan; gayundin ang mga lingkod ng Kanyang kaluwalhatian at ang buong hukbo sa langit. Hindi ba mas mabuting sumunod tayo sa ating Diyos na Manlilikha bilang Ama na nagtuturo at nagkakaloob sa atin ayon sa Kanyang karunungan? At kung, gaya ng sinasabi mo, may Diyos na higit na mahabagin kaysa sa lahat, tiyak na hindi siya magagalit sa atin; ngunit kung magagalit siya, masama siya. Sapagkat kapag nagagalit at nagpaparusa ang ating Diyos, hindi Siya masama kundi makatarungan, dahil itinutuwid at inaayos Niya ang Kanyang sariling mga anak. Ngunit kung parurusahan tayo ng isang walang anumang kaugnayan sa atin, paano siya matatawag na mabuti? Kung parurusahan niya tayo dahil hindi tayo napadala sa mga hungkag na guniguni upang talikuran ang sarili nating Ama at sumunod sa kanya, paano mo masasabi na napakabuti niya gayong hindi man lamang siya maituturing na makatarungan?

Kabanata 57. Hindi Pagkakatugma ng mga Pahayag ni Simon.

Sinabi ni Simon: Napakalayo na ba ng iyong pagkakamali, Pedro, na hindi mo alam na ang ating mga kaluluwa ay nilikha ng mabuting Diyos na iyon, ang higit na dakila sa lahat, ngunit ibinaba sila bilang mga bihag sa mundong ito? Sumagot si Pedro: Kung gayon, hindi pala siya lingid sa lahat gaya ng sinabi mo kanina. At kung siya ang kapangyarihang higit sa lahat, paano pinahintulutan ng mabuting Diyos na mabihag ang sarili niyang mga kaluluwa? Sinabi ni Simon: Isinugo niya ang Diyos na Manlilikha upang gawin ang sanlibutan; at nang magawa na iyon ng Manlilikha, ipinahayag nitong siya mismo ang Diyos. Sumagot si Pedro: Kung gayon, hindi rin pala siya lingid sa gumawa ng sanlibutan, gaya ng sinabi mo; at hindi rin siya lingid sa mga kaluluwa kung talagang ninakaw sila mula sa kanya. Kanino pa siya maaaring hindi kilala, kung kilala siya ng Manlilikha ng sanlibutan dahil isinugo niya ito, at kilala siya ng lahat ng kaluluwa sapagkat marahas silang inagaw mula sa kanya? Bukod dito, sabihin mo sa amin: hindi ba alam ng nagsugo sa Manlilikha ng sanlibutan na hindi nito tutuparin ang katapatan sa kanya? Kung hindi niya alam, wala siyang paunang kaalaman. Kung alam niya at hinayaan niya, siya mismo ay may pananagutan sa nangyari sapagkat hindi niya ito pinigilan. Ngunit kung hindi niya kayang pigilan, hindi siya makapangyarihan sa lahat. At kung alam niyang mabuti ang gagawin ng Manlilikha at kaya hindi niya ito pinagbawalan, lumilitaw na mas mabuti pa ang Manlilikha na nangahas gumawa ng isang bagay na hindi alam ng nagsugo sa kanya na mabuti.

Kabanata 58. Hindi Makatarungan ang Diyos ni Simon.

Sinabi ni Simon: Tinatanggap niya ang mga lumalapit sa kanya at gumagawa siya ng mabuti sa kanila. Sumagot si Pedro: Walang bago riyan, sapagkat gayon din ang ginagawa ng kinikilala mong Manlilikha ng sanlibutan. Sinabi ni Simon: Ngunit ang mabuting Diyos ay nagbibigay ng kaligtasan basta kilalanin lamang siya, samantalang hinihingi ng Manlilikha ng sanlibutan na tuparin din ang kautusan. Sumagot si Pedro: Kung gayon, inililigtas niya ang mga mangangalunya at mamamatay-tao kung nakikilala nila siya; ngunit ang mabubuti, mapagpigil, at mahabaging tao na hindi nakakikilala sa kanya dahil wala silang kaalaman tungkol sa kanya ay hindi niya inililigtas! Tunay ngang dakila at mabuti ang Diyos na ipinahahayag mo—hindi siya gaanong tagapagligtas ng masasama kundi isang walang awa sa mabubuti. Sinabi ni Simon: Talagang napakahirap para sa tao na makilala siya habang nasa laman, sapagkat mas maitim pa sa lahat ng kadiliman at mas mabigat pa sa lahat ng putik ang katawang bumabalot sa kaluluwa. Sinabi ni Pedro: Napakahirap pala ng hinihingi ng mabuti mong Diyos; ngunit ang tunay na Diyos ay humihingi ng mas madaling bagay. Kaya kung tunay siyang napakabuti, hayaan niya muna tayong manatili sa ating Ama at Manlilikha. Kapag nakahiwalay na tayo sa katawan at nakaalis sa kadilimang tinutukoy mo, mas madali na natin siyang makikilala. Sa gayon ay mas mauunawaan ng kaluluwa na ang Diyos ang Manlilikha nito, mananatili itong kasama Niya, at hindi na magugulo ng sari-saring guniguni. Hindi na rin nito nanaising lumipat sa ibang kapangyarihan na walang ibang nakakakilala kundi si Simon lamang—isang kapangyarihang napakabuti raw ngunit walang sinumang makalalapit dito malibang magkasala muna siya laban sa sarili niyang ama! Hindi ko maunawaan kung paano matatawag na mabuti o makatarungan ang isang kapangyarihang walang makapagpapalugod malibang kumilos siya nang walang paggalang sa gumawa sa kanya.

Kabanata 59. Ang Manlilikha ang Ating Ama.

Sinabi ni Simon: Hindi kawalan ng paggalang ang tumakas patungo sa isang higit na maluwalhati kung mas malaki ang pakinabang at kabutihang makukuha roon. Sumagot si Pedro: Kung, gaya ng sinasabi mo, hindi kawalan ng paggalang ang tumakas patungo sa isang estranghero, lalo namang higit na marangal ang manatili sa sarili nating ama kahit siya ay mahirap. Ngunit kung sa palagay mo ay hindi masama ang iwan ang ating ama at tumakas sa iba dahil mas mabuti raw iyon kaysa sa kanya, at naniniwala kang hindi ito ikagagalit ng ating Manlilikha, lalo nang hindi dapat magalit ang mabuting Diyos dahil, bilang mga estranghero sa kanya, hindi tayo tumakas patungo sa kanya kundi nanatili sa sarili nating Manlilikha. Sa palagay ko pa nga ay lalo niya tayong pupurihin dahil nanatili tayong tapat sa Diyos na lumikha sa atin; iisipin niyang kung tayo sana ang Kanyang mga nilalang, hinding-hindi tayo mahihikayat ng pangako ng iba na talikuran Siya. Sapagkat kung may taong iiwan ang sarili niyang ama dahil nahikayat ng mas mayamang mga pangako at lumipat sa isang estranghero, maaaring iwan din niya ang estrangherong iyon kapag may ibang nangako ng mas higit pa. Lalo itong posible dahil hindi naman talaga siya anak ng estranghero, gayong nagawa niyang iwan maging ang ama niya ayon sa kalikasan. Sinabi ni Simon: Ngunit paano kung ang mga kaluluwa ay mula sa kanya, hindi lamang nila siya kilala, at siya talaga ang kanilang Ama?

Kabanata 60. Ang Manlilikha ang Kataas-taasang Diyos.

Sinabi ni Pedro: Napakahina pala ng ipinakikilala mong Diyos. Sapagkat kung, gaya ng sinasabi mo, siya ang higit na makapangyarihan sa lahat, hindi kapani-paniwalang naagaw ng mas mahina ang pag-aari ng mas malakas. Lucas 11:22 At kung nagawa ng Diyos na Manlilikha na marahas na ibaba ang mga kaluluwa sa mundong ito, paano magiging makatarungan na kapag nahiwalay na sila sa katawan at nakalaya sa gapos ng pagkabihag ay ipatawag sila ng mabuting Diyos upang pagdusahan ang parusa, gayong dahil sa kanyang sariling kapabayaan o kahinaan sila naagaw patungo rito at ibinalot sa katawan na parang nasa kadiliman ng kamangmangan? Para sa akin ay hindi mo nauunawaan kung ano ang isang ama at kung ano ang Diyos. Maaari kong sabihin sa iyo kung saan nagmula ang mga kaluluwa, kailan sila nilikha, at kung paano; ngunit hindi pa ako pinahihintulutang ipahayag ang mga bagay na ito sa iyo habang gayon kalaki ang iyong pagkakamali tungkol sa pagkakilala sa Diyos. Sinabi ni Simon: Darating ang panahong pagsisisihan mong hindi mo naunawaan ang sinabi ko tungkol sa hindi maipahayag na kapangyarihan. Sinabi ni Pedro: Kung gayon, gaya ng paulit-ulit kong sinasabi, bigyan mo kami ng isang bagong pandama—bilang isa kang bagong Diyos, o bilang isang bumaba mula sa kanya—upang sa pamamagitan nito ay makilala namin ang bagong Diyos na sinasabi mo. Sapagkat ang limang pandamang ibinigay sa atin ng Diyos na ating Manlilikha ay nananatiling tapat sa sarili nilang Manlilikha at walang nakikitang ibang Diyos; sapagkat gayon ang itinakda ng kanilang kalikasan.

Kabanata 61. Guniguni.

Sumagot si Simon: Ituon mo ang iyong isip sa mga bagay na sasabihin ko, at hayaang tahakin nito nang payapa ang landas patungo sa mga bagay na ipapakita ko. Makinig ka. Hindi ba kailanman, sa iyong pag-iisip, napaabot mo ang iyong isip sa malalayong lupain o pulo at lubos kang nalubog sa mga iyon, hanggang sa hindi mo na makita ang mga taong nasa harap mo ni malaman kung saan ka nakaupo, dahil sa labis na kasiyahang dulot ng mga bagay na minamasdan mo sa isip? Sinabi ni Pedro: Totoo iyan, Simon; maraming ulit nang nangyari sa akin iyon. Sinabi ni Simon: Kung gayon, sa gayunding paraan, ipaabot mo ngayon ang iyong pandama hanggang sa langit, oo, lampas pa sa langit. Isipin mong kailangang may isang dako sa kabila o labas ng sanlibutan, kung saan walang langit ni lupa at walang anino mula sa mga ito na lumilikha ng kadiliman. At dahil walang mga katawan roon at walang kadilimang dulot ng mga katawan, kinakailangang may napakalawak na liwanag. Isipin mo kung anong uri ng liwanag iyon na kailanman ay hindi sinusundan ng kadiliman. Kung napupuno ng liwanag ng araw na ito ang buong sanlibutan, gaano kalaki kaya ang liwanag na iyon na walang katawan at walang hangganan? Tiyak na napakalaki na kung ihahambing dito, ang liwanag ng araw ay magmumukhang kadiliman sa halip na liwanag.

Kabanata 62. Karanasan ni Pedro sa Guniguni.

Nang magsalita nang gayon si Simon, sumagot si Pedro: Makinig ka ngayon nang may pagtitiis tungkol sa dalawang bagay na ito: ang halimbawa mo tungkol sa pagpapalawak ng pandama, at ang sinasabi mong walang-sukat na liwanag. Alam ko, O Simon, na minsan ay napaabot ko rin sa isip ang aking pandama, gaya ng sinasabi mo, sa mga lupain at pulong malalayong naroroon, at nakita ko ang mga iyon sa aking isip na para bang nakikita ko mismo ng aking mga mata. Noong nasa Capernaum ako at nangingisda, nakaupo ako sa isang bato at hawak ko ang kawit na nakakabit sa pamingwit na ginagamit sa panghuhuli ng isda. Lubhang nalubog ang aking isip kaya hindi ko naramdaman na may isdang kumagat sa kawit, sapagkat sabik na naglalakbay ang aking isip sa minamahal kong Jerusalem, na madalas kong puntahan nang gising upang maghandog at manalangin. Madalas ko ring hangaan ang Cesarea dahil sa mga naririnig ko tungkol dito mula sa iba, at hinahangad kong makita ito. Akala ko ay nakikita ko ito kahit hindi pa ako nakapunta roon, at inilarawan ko sa aking isip ang dapat mayroon ang isang dakilang lungsod—mga tarangkahan, pader, paliguan, lansangan, makikitid na daan, pamilihan, at iba pa—ayon sa nakita ko na sa ibang mga lungsod. Gayon kalaki ang aking kasiyahan sa masinsing pagmamasid na iyon sa isip na, gaya ng sinabi mo, hindi ko na nakita ang taong naroon sa tabi ko ni nalaman kung saan ako nakaupo. Sinabi ni Simon: Ngayon ay tama ang sinasabi mo.

Kabanata 63. Pagkalubog ni Pedro sa Guniguni.

Sinabi ni Pedro: Sa madaling sabi, dahil lubos na abala ang aking isip, hindi ko napansing nakahuli ako ng napakalaking isda na nakakapit sa kawit at hinihila na ang pamingwit mula sa aking kamay. Ang kapatid kong si Andres, na nakaupo sa tabi ko, ay nakitang lubos akong wala sa sarili at halos matumba. Kaya siniko niya ako sa tagiliran na para bang gigisingin ako mula sa pagkakatulog at sinabi: ‘Hindi mo ba nakikita, Pedro, kung gaano kalaking isda ang nahuli mo? Wala ka ba sa sarili kaya nakatulala ka nang ganyan? Sabihin mo sa akin, ano ang nangyayari sa iyo?’ Sandali akong nainis sa kanya dahil inilayo niya ako sa kasiyahan ng mga bagay na minamasdan ko sa isip. Pagkatapos ay sinabi kong wala akong karamdaman; sa halip, sa aking isip ay minamasdan ko ang minamahal na Jerusalem at kasabay nito ang Cesarea. Bagaman naroon ang katawan ko kasama niya, ang isip ko ay lubos na nadala sa mga lugar na iyon. Ngunit siya—hindi ko alam kung saan niya hinugot ang gayong pagkaunawa—ay nagsalita ng isang nakatagong salita ng katotohanan.

Kabanata 64. Pagsaway ni Andres.

‘Tigilan mo iyan,’ sabi niya, ‘O Pedro. Ano ba ang ginagawa mo? Ganyan nagsisimula ang mga taong unti-unting sinasaniban ng demonyo o nagugulo ang pag-iisip. Una, dinadala sila ng mga guniguni sa mga bagay na kaaya-aya at nakalulugod; pagkatapos ay nahuhumaling sila sa walang saysay na pagnanasa sa mga bagay na hindi naman umiiral. Nangyayari ito dahil sa isang karamdaman ng isip: hindi na nila nakikita ang mga bagay na tunay na naroroon, kundi pinagsisikapan nilang makita ang mga bagay na wala. Ganito rin ang nangyayari sa mga dumaranas ng matinding pagkasira ng isip at nag-aakalang maraming anyo ang nakikita nila, sapagkat ang kanilang kaluluwa, na tila napupunit at naaalis sa likas nitong kalagayan dahil sa labis na lamig o init, ay nabibigo sa karaniwang gawain nito. Gayundin, ang mga taong labis na nauuhaw ay maaaring managinip na nakakakita sila ng mga ilog at bukal at na umiinom sila; ngunit nangyayari ito dahil pinahihirapan sila ng pagkatuyo ng katawan na kulang sa tubig. Kaya tiyak na ang ganitong pangyayari ay nagmumula sa isang uri ng karamdaman ng kaluluwa o ng katawan.’

Kabanata 65. Pagkakamali ng Guniguni.

Sa madaling sabi, upang maniwala ka sa sinasabi ko: tungkol sa Jerusalem, na maraming ulit ko nang nakita, ikinuwento ko sa aking kapatid ang mga lugar at pagtitipon ng mga taong inakala kong nakikita ko sa isip. Ngunit tungkol naman sa Cesarea, na hindi ko pa kailanman nakita, iginiit ko pa rin noon na iyon ay gaya ng nabuo ko sa aking isip at guniguni. Nang makarating ako rito at makita kong walang halos anumang katulad ng mga nakita ko sa guniguni, sinisi ko ang aking sarili. Malinaw kong napagtanto na ang mga tarangkahan, pader, at gusaling inilagay ko rito sa isip ay hinango ko lamang sa mga bagay na nakita ko sa ibang lugar—ang pagkakahawig ay kinuha ko mula sa tunay na mga bagay na dati ko nang nakita. Sa katunayan, walang sinuman ang makaguguniguni ng isang bagay na lubos na bago at walang anumang anyong umiral na kailanman. Kahit mag-isip ang isang tao ng mga torong may limang ulo, binubuo lamang niya ang limang ulo mula sa mga torong nakita na niyang may tig-iisang ulo. Kaya ikaw ngayon, kung sa palagay mo ay may tunay kang nakikita sa isip at tumitingin ka sa ibabaw ng kalangitan, walang pag-aalinlangang ang mga larawan mo ay binubuo mula sa mga bagay na nakikita mo habang narito ka sa lupa. Ngunit kung iniisip mong madaling makararating ang iyong isip sa itaas ng kalangitan, na kaya mong maunawaan ang mga bagay na naroroon at makamit ang kaalaman tungkol sa walang-sukat na liwanag, sa palagay ko ay mas madali para sa isang may gayong kakayahan na ipasok ang pandamang nakaaakyat doon sa puso at dibdib ng isa sa aming nakatayo rito at sabihin kung anong mga kaisipan ang kinikimkim niya. Kaya kung masasabi mo ang iniisip ng puso ng sinuman sa amin na hindi pabor sa iyo, marahil ay makapaniwala kami na kaya mong malaman ang mga bagay sa itaas ng kalangitan, bagaman ang mga iyon ay higit na mataas at mahirap unawain.

Kabanata 66. Pag-iral at Pagbuo sa Isip.

Sumagot si Simon: Ikaw na naghabi ng napakaraming walang-saysay na argumento, makinig ka ngayon. Imposibleng may pumasok sa isip ng tao na hindi rin umiiral sa katotohanan at realidad. Sapagkat ang mga bagay na hindi umiiral ay walang anyo; at ang walang anyo ay hindi maaaring lumitaw sa ating pag-iisip. Sinabi ni Pedro: Kung ang lahat ng pumapasok sa isip natin ay tunay na umiiral, tungkol sa lugar ng walang-sukat na lawak na sinasabi mong nasa labas ng sanlibutan, paano kung isipin ng isang tao na iyon ay liwanag at isipin naman ng isa na iyon ay kadiliman? Paano magiging sabay na liwanag at kadiliman ang iisang lugar dahil lamang sa magkaibang iniisip nila tungkol dito? Sinabi ni Simon: Isantabi muna natin ang sinabi ko at sabihin mo sa amin kung ano ang palagay mong nasa ibabaw ng kalangitan.

Kabanata 67. Itinuturo ng Kautusan ang Walang-sukat na Lawak.

Sinabi ni Pedro: Kung naniniwala ka tungkol sa tunay na bukal ng liwanag, maituturo ko sa iyo kung ano at anong uri ang walang-sukat na lawak na iyon. Hindi ako magbibigay ng hungkag na guniguni kundi isang magkakaugnay at kinakailangang paliwanag ng katotohanan. Hindi ako gagamit ng mga mapanlinlang na pangangatwiran kundi ng mga patotoo mula sa kautusan at kalikasan, upang malaman mong ang kautusan mismo ang naglalaman ng dapat nating paniwalaan tungkol sa walang-sukat na lawak. At kung hindi lingid sa kautusan ang turo tungkol dito, tiyak na wala ring ibang bagay na maaaring ganap na lingid dito. Kaya mali ang palagay mong may anumang bagay na hindi nalalaman ng kautusan; lalo nang walang anumang lingid sa Nagbigay ng kautusan. Gayunman, hindi ako makapagsasalita sa iyo tungkol sa walang-sukat at walang hangganang mga bagay hangga’t hindi mo muna tinatanggap ang paliwanag namin tungkol sa mga kalangitang may takdang hangganan, o inilalahad mo ang sarili mong paliwanag tungkol sa mga iyon. Sapagkat kung hindi mo maunawaan ang mga bagay na nakapaloob sa tiyak na mga hangganan, lalo mong hindi malalaman ni matututuhan ang mga bagay na walang hangganan.

Kabanata 68. Ang Nakikitang Langit at ang Di-nakikitang Langit.

Sumagot si Simon: Para sa akin ay mas mabuting maniwala na lamang na umiiral ang Diyos at na ang langit na nakikita natin ang tanging langit sa buong sanlibutan. Sinabi ni Pedro: Hindi gayon. Nararapat nating ipahayag na iisa ang Diyos na tunay na umiiral, ngunit mayroon ding mga kalangitang nilikha Niya, gaya ng sinasabi ng kautusan. May isang mas mataas na langit na kinalalagyan din ng nakikitang kalawakan. Ang mas mataas na langit na iyon ay nananatili magpakailanman at walang katapusan, kasama ng mga naninirahan doon. Ngunit ang nakikitang langit na ito ay mawawasak at lilipas sa katapusan ng sanlibutan, upang pagkatapos ng paghuhukom ay mahayag sa mga banal at karapat-dapat ang langit na higit na matanda at mas mataas. Sumagot si Simon: Maaaring magmukhang totoo ang mga bagay na sinasabi mo sa mga naniniwala rito; ngunit para sa taong humihingi ng katuwiran at patunay, imposibleng maipakita ang mga ito mula sa kautusan, lalo na ang tungkol sa walang-sukat na lawak ng liwanag.

Kabanata 69. Pananampalataya at Katwiran.

Sinabi ni Pedro: Huwag mong isipin na sinasabi naming dapat lamang tanggapin ang mga bagay na ito sa pamamagitan ng pananampalataya. Dapat din itong pagtibayin sa pamamagitan ng katuwiran. Hindi ligtas na ipaubaya ang mga ito sa hubad na pananampalatayang walang katuwiran, sapagkat tiyak na ang katotohanan ay hindi maaaring walang katuwiran. Kaya ang taong tumanggap sa mga bagay na ito na pinagtibay ng katwiran ay hindi madaling mawawalan ng mga ito. Ngunit ang tumatanggap nang walang patunay, dahil lamang sumang-ayon siya sa simpleng pahayag, ay hindi makatitiyak sa katotohanan ng mga iyon at hindi rin niya mapananatiling matatag ang kanyang paniniwala; sapagkat ang madaling maniwala ay madali ring matinag. Ngunit ang taong humingi at nakahanap ng katuwiran para sa mga bagay na pinaniwalaan at tinanggap niya ay para bang nakagapos sa mismong tanikala ng katuwiran, kaya hindi siya madaling maihiwalay sa pinaniniwalaan niya. Kaya habang mas masigasig ang isang tao sa paghiling ng katuwiran, lalo siyang magiging matatag sa pagpapanatili ng kanyang pananampalataya.

Kabanata 70. Pagpapaliban.

Sumagot si Simon: Napakalaki ng ipinapangako mo—na maipakikita mula sa kautusan ang walang-hanggang pag-iral ng liwanag na walang hangganan. Nang sabihin ni Pedro, Ipapakita ko ito anumang oras na nais mo, sumagot si Simon: Gabi na ngayon. Bukas ay haharap akong muli sa iyo at sasalungat sa iyo; at kung mapatutunayan mong nilikha ang sanlibutang ito at walang kamatayan ang mga kaluluwa, tutulungan kita sa iyong pangangaral. Pagkasabi nito, umalis siya. Sumunod sa kanya ang humigit-kumulang ikatlong bahagi ng lahat ng taong dumating kasama niya, na mga isang libong katao. Ngunit ang iba ay lumuhod at nagpatirapa sa harap ni Pedro. Tinawag niya sa kanila ang pangalan ng Diyos, pinalaya ang ilan sa mga demonyo, pinagaling ang ibang maysakit, at pinauwi ang mga tao nang nagagalak, na inuutusang bumalik nang maaga kinabukasan. Nang makaalis na ang mga tao, ipinahanda ni Pedro ang hapag sa lupa, sa bukas na bahagi ng patyo kung saan ginanap ang pagtatalo, at naupo siyang kumain kasama ng labing-isang iyon. Ako naman ay kumain nang nakahilig kasama ng ilan pang nagsisimula nang makinig sa salita ng Diyos at lubhang minamahal.

Kabanata 71. Paghiwalay sa mga Marumi.

Ngunit magiliw akong tiningnan ni Pedro, sapagkat ayaw niyang malungkot ako dahil sa paghihiwalay na iyon, at sinabi niya sa akin: Hindi dahil sa pagmamataas, O Clemente, kaya hindi ako kumakain kasama ng mga hindi pa nalilinis. Natatakot lamang akong baka mapinsala ko ang aking sarili at wala rin akong maitulong sa kanila. Nais kong malaman mo nang tiyak na ang sinumang minsan nang sumamba sa mga diyus-diyosan, yumukod sa mga tinatawag ng mga pagano na mga diyos, o kumain ng mga inihandog sa kanila, ay hindi malaya sa maruming espiritu; sapagkat naging panauhin siya ng mga demonyo at nakibahagi sa demonyong ang larawan ay nabuo niya sa kanyang isip, maging dahil sa takot o sa pag-ibig. 1 Corinto 10:20 Sa gayong paraan ay hindi siya malaya sa maruming espiritu. Kaya kailangan niya ang paglilinis ng bautismo, upang lumabas sa kanya ang maruming espiritung nanahan sa pinakaloob na mga damdamin ng kanyang kaluluwa. At ang mas masama, hindi nito ipinahahalata na nagkukubli ito sa loob, sa takot na mabunyag at mapalayas.

Kabanata 72. Ang Lunas.

Sapagkat gustong manahan ng mga maruming espiritung ito sa katawan ng mga tao upang maisakatuparan nila ang sarili nilang mga pagnanasa sa pamamagitan ng paglilingkod ng mga taong iyon. Inuudyukan nila ang galaw ng kaluluwa tungo sa mga bagay na sila mismo ang naghahangad, at pinipilit ang mga tao na sumunod sa kanilang mga pita hanggang sa maging lubusan silang mga sisidlan ng mga demonyo. Isa sa mga ito si Simon, na dinapuan ng ganitong karamdaman at hindi na magamot ngayon sapagkat ang kanyang kalooban at layunin mismo ang may sakit. Hindi nananahan sa kanya ang demonyo laban sa kanyang kalooban. Kaya kung may magtangkang palayasin ito mula sa kanya, yamang halos hindi na ito maihiwalay sa kanya at, kung baga, naging mismong kaluluwa na niya, waring pinapatay na rin niya si Simon at maaaring magkaroon pa ng sala sa pagpatay. Kaya huwag malungkot ang sinuman sa inyo dahil hiwalay kayong kumakain sa amin. Ang tagal ng paghihiwalay na ito ay nakasalalay sa kagustuhan ng bawat isa. Ang gustong mabautismuhan agad ay sandaling panahon lamang na mahihiwalay; ang gustong magpabautismo sa kalaunan ay mas matagal. Kaya nasa kapangyarihan ng bawat isa kung gaano kaikli o kahaba ang panahon ng kanyang pagsisisi. Nasa inyo, kung kailan ninyo nanaisin, ang paglapit sa aming hapag; hindi ito nakasalalay sa amin, sapagkat hindi kami pinahihintulutang kumain kasama ng sinumang hindi pa nababautismuhan. Sa gayon, kayo ang pumipigil sa amin na kumain kasama ninyo kapag pinatatagal ninyo ang inyong paglilinis at ipinagpapaliban ang bautismo. Pagkasabi nito at matapos magbasbas, kumain siya. Pagkatapos, nang makapagpasalamat na siya sa Diyos, pumasok siya sa bahay at natulog. Gayon din ang ginawa naming lahat, sapagkat gabi na.
