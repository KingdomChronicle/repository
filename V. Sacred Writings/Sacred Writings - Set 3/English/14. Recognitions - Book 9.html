Book 9

Chapter 1. An Explanation.

On the following day, Peter, along with us, hastened early to the place in which the discussion had been held the day before; and when he saw that great crowds had assembled there to hear, and saw the old man with them, he said to him: Old man, it was agreed yesterday that you should confer today with Clement; and that you should either show that nothing takes place apart from genesis, or that Clement should prove that there is no such thing as genesis, but that what we do is in our own power. To this the old man answered: I both remember what was agreed upon, and I keep in memory the words which you spoke after the agreement was made, in which you taught that it is impossible for man to know anything, unless he learn from the true Prophet. Then Peter said: You do not know what I meant; but I shall now explain to you. I spoke of the will and purpose of God, which He had before the world was, and by which purpose He made the world, appointed times, gave the law, promised a world to come to the righteous for the rewarding of their good deeds, and decreed punishments to the unjust according to a judicial sentence. I said that this counsel and this will of God cannot be found out by men, because no man can gather the mind of God from conjectures and opinion, unless a prophet sent by Him declare it. I did not therefore speak of any doctrines or studies, that they cannot be found out or known without a prophet; for I know that both arts and sciences can be known and practised by men, which they have learned, not from the true Prophet, but from human instructors.

Chapter 2. Preliminaries.

Since, therefore, you profess to be conversant with the position of the stars and the courses of the heavenly bodies, and that from these you can convince Clement that all things are subject to Genesis, or that you will learn from him that all things are governed by providence, and that we have something in our own power, it is now time for you two to set about this. To this the old man answered: Now indeed it was not necessary to raise questions of this kind, if it were possible for us to learn from the true Prophet, and to hear in a definite proposition, that anything depends on us and on the freedom of our will; for your yesterday's discourse affected me greatly, in which you disputed concerning the prophetic power. Whence also I assent to and confirm your judgment, that nothing can be known by man with certainty, and without doubt, seeing that he has but a short period of life, and a brief and slender breath, by which he seems to be kept in life. However, since I am understood to have promised to Clement, before I heard anything of the prophetic power, that I should show that all things are subject to Genesis, or that I should learn from him that there is something in ourselves, let him do me this favour, that he first begin, and propound and explain what may be objected: for I, ever since I heard from you a few words concerning the power of prophecy, have, I confess, been confounded, considering the greatness of prescience; nor do I think that anything ought to be received which is collected from conjectures and opinion.

Chapter 3. Beginning of the Discussion.

When the old man had said this, I Clement began to speak as follows: God by His Son created the world as a double house, separated by the interposition of this firmament, which is called heaven; and appointed angelic powers to dwell in the higher, and a multitude of men to be born in this visible world, from among whom He might choose friends for His Son, with whom He might rejoice, and who might be prepared for Him as a beloved bride for a bridegroom. But even till the time of the marriage, which is the manifestation of the world to come, He has appointed a certain power, to choose out and watch over the good ones of those who are born in this world, and to preserve them for His Son, set apart in a certain place of the world, which is without sin; in which there are already some, who are there being prepared, as I said, as a bride adorned for the coming of the bridegroom. For the prince of this world and of the present age is like an adulterer, who corrupts and violates the minds of men, and, seducing them from the love of the true bridegroom, allures them to strange lovers.

Chapter 4. Why the Evil Prince Was Made.

But some one will say, How then was it necessary that that prince should be made, who was to turn away the minds of men from the true prince? Because God, who, as I have said, wished to prepare friends for His Son, did not wish them to be such as by necessity of nature could not be anything else, but such as should desire of their own choice and will to be good; because neither is that praiseworthy which is not desirable, nor is that judged to be good which is not sought for with purpose. For there is no credit in being that from which the necessity of your nature does not admit of your changing. Therefore the providence of God has willed that a multitude of men should be born in this world, that those who should choose a good life might be selected from many. And because He foresaw that the present world could not consist except by variety and inequality, He gave to each mind freedom of motions, according to the diversities of present things, and appointed this prince, through his suggestion of those things which run contrary, that the choice of better things might depend upon the exercise of virtue.

Chapter 5. Necessity of Inequality.

But to make our meaning plainer, we shall explain it by particulars. Was it proper, for example, that all men in this world should be kings, or princes, or lords, or teachers, or lawyers, or geometers, or goldsmiths, or bakers, or smiths, or grammarians, or rich men, or farmers, or perfumers, or fishermen, or poor men? It is certain that all could not be these. Yet all these professions, and many more, the life of men requires, and without these it cannot be passed; therefore inequality is necessary in this world. For there cannot be a king, unless he has subjects over whom he may rule and reign; nor can there be a master, unless he has one over whom he may bear sway; and in like manner of the rest.

Chapter 6. Arrangements of the World for the Exercise of Virtue.

Therefore the Creator, knowing that no one would come to the contest of his own accord, while labour is shunned — that is, to the practice of those professions which we have mentioned, by means of which either the justice or the mercy of every one can be manifested — made for men a body susceptible of hunger, and thirst, and cold, in order that men, being compelled for the sake of supporting their bodies, might come down to all the professions which we have mentioned, by the necessity of livelihood. For we are taught to cultivate every one of these arts, for the sake of food, and drink, and clothing. And in this the purpose of each one's mind is shown, whether he will supply the demands of hunger and cold by means of thefts, and murders, and perjuries, and other crimes of that sort; or whether, keeping justice and mercy and continence, he will fulfil the service of imminent necessity by the practice of a profession and the labour of his hands. For if he supply his bodily wants with justice, and piety, and mercy, he comes forth as a victor in the contest set before him, and is chosen as a friend of the Son of God. But if he serve carnal lusts, by frauds, iniquities, and crimes, he becomes a friend of the prince of this world, and of all demons; by whom he is also taught this, to ascribe to the courses of the stars the errors of his own evil doings, although he chose them of purpose, and willingly. For arts are learned and practised, as we have said, under the compulsion of the desire of food and drink; which desire, when the knowledge of the truth comes to any one, becomes weaker, and frugality takes its place. For what expense have those who use water and bread, and only expect it from God?

Chapter 7. The Old and the New Birth.

There is therefore, as we have said, a certain necessary inequality in the dispensation of the world. Since indeed all men cannot know all things, and accomplish all works, yet all need the use and service of almost all. And on this account it is necessary that one work, and another pay him for his work; that one be servant, and another be master; that one be subject, another be king. But this inequality, which is a necessary provision for the life of men, divine providence has turned into an occasion of justice, mercy, and humanity: that while these things are transacted between man and man, every one may have an opportunity of acting justly with him to whom he has to pay wages for his work; and of acting mercifully to him who, perhaps through sickness or poverty, cannot pay his debt; and of acting humanely towards those who by their creation seem to be subject to him; also of maintaining gentleness towards subjects, and of doing all things according to the law of God. For He has given a law, thereby aiding the minds of men, that they may the more easily perceive how they ought to act with respect to everything, in what way they may escape evil, and in what way tend to future blessings; and how, being regenerate in water, they may by good works extinguish the fire of their old birth. For our first birth descends through the fire of lust, and therefore, by the divine appointment, this second birth is introduced by water, which may extinguish the nature of fire; and that the soul, enlightened by the heavenly Spirit, may cast away the fear of the first birth: provided, however, it so live for the time to come, that it do not at all seek after any of the pleasures of this world, but be, as it were, a pilgrim and a stranger, and a citizen of another city.

Chapter 8. Uses of Evils.

But perhaps you will say, that in those things indeed in which the necessity of nature demands the service of arts and works, any one may have it in his power to maintain justice, and to put what restraint he pleases either upon his desires or his actions; but what shall we say of the sicknesses and infirmities which befall men, and of some being harassed with demons, and fevers, and cold fits, and some being attacked with madness, or losing their reason, and all those things which overwhelm the race of man with innumerable misfortunes? To this we say, that if any one consider the reason of the whole mystery, he will pronounce these things to be more just than those that we have already explained. For God has given a nature to men, by which they may be taught concerning what is good, and to resist evil; that is, they may learn arts, and to resist pleasures, and to set the law of God before them in all things. And for this end He has permitted certain contrary powers to wander up and down in the world, and to strive against us, for the reasons which have been stated before, that by striving with them the palm of victory and the merit of rewards may accrue to the righteous.

Chapter 9. Conceived in Sin.

From this, therefore, it sometimes happens, that if any persons have acted incontinently, and have been willing not so much to resist as to yield, and to give harbour to these demons in themselves, by their noxious breath an intemperate, ill-conditioned, and diseased progeny is begotten. For while lust is wholly gratified, and no care is taken in the copulation, undoubtedly a weak generation is affected with the defects and frailties of those demons by whose instigation these things are done. And therefore parents are responsible for their children's defects of this sort, because they have not observed the law of intercourse. Though there are also more secret causes, by which souls are made subject to these evils, which it is not to our present purpose to state, yet it behooves every one to acknowledge the law of God, that he may learn from it the observance of generation, and avoid causes of impurity, that that which is begotten may be pure. For it is not right, while in the planting of shrubs and the sowing of crops a suitable season is sought for, and the land is cleaned, and all things are suitably prepared, lest haply the seed which is sown be injured and perish, that in the case of man only, who is over all these things, there should be no attention or caution in sowing his seed.

Chapter 10. Tow Smeared with Pitch.

But what, it is said, of the fact that some who in their childhood are free from any bodily defect, yet in process of time fall into those evils, so that some are even violently hurried on to death? Concerning these also the account is at hand, and is almost the same: for those powers which we have said to be contrary to the human race, are in some way invited into the heart of every one by many and diverse lusts, and find a way of entrance; and they have in them such influence and power as can only encourage and incite, but cannot compel or accomplish. If, therefore, any one consents to them, so as to do those things which he wickedly desires, his consent and deed shall find the reward of destruction and the worst kind of death. But if, thinking of the future judgment, he be checked by fear, and reclaim himself, so that he do not accomplish in action what he has conceived in his evil thought, he shall not only escape present destruction, but also future punishments. For every cause of sin seems to be like tow smeared over with pitch, which immediately breaks into flame as soon as it receives the heat of fire; and the kindling of this fire is understood to be the work of demons. If, therefore, any one be found smeared with sins and lusts as with pitch, the fire easily gets the mastery of him. But if the tow be not steeped in the pitch of sin, but in the water of purification and regeneration, the fire of the demons shall not be able to be kindled in it.

Chapter 11. Fear.

But some one will say, And what shall we do now, whom it has already happened to us to be smeared with sins as with pitch? I answer: Nothing; but hasten to be washed, that the fuel of the fire may be cleansed out of you by the invocation of the holy name, and that for the future you may bridle your lusts by fear of the judgment to come, and with all constancy beat back the hostile powers whenever they approach your senses. But you say, If any one fall into love, how shall he be able to contain himself, though he see before his eyes even that river of fire which they call Pyriphlegethon? This is the excuse of those who will not be converted to repentance. But now I would not have you talk of Pyriphlegethon. Place before you human punishments, and see what influence fear has. When any one is brought to punishment for the crime of love, and is bound to the stake to be burned, can he at that time conceive any desire of her whom he loved, or place her image before his eyes? By no means, you will say. You see, then, that present fear cuts off unrighteous desires. But if those who believe in God, and who confess the judgment to come, and the penalty of eternal fire — if they do not refrain from sin, it is certain that they do not believe with full faith: for if faith is certain, fear also becomes certain; but if there be any detect in faith, fear also is weakened, and then the contrary powers find opportunity of entering. And when they have consented to their persuasions, they necessarily become subject also to their power, and by their instigation are driven to the precipices of sin.

Chapter 12. Astrologers.

Therefore the astrologers, being ignorant of such mysteries, think that these things happen by the courses of the heavenly bodies: hence also, in their answers to those who go to them to consult them as to future things, they are deceived in very many instances. Nor is it to be wondered at, for they are not prophets; but, by long practice, the authors of errors find a sort of refuge in those things by which they were deceived, and introduce certain Climacteric Periods, that they may pretend a knowledge of uncertain things. For they represent these Climacterics as times of danger, in which one sometimes is destroyed, sometimes is not destroyed, not knowing that it is not the course of the stars, but the operation of demons, that regulates these things; and those demons, being anxious to confirm the error of astrology, deceive men to sin by mathematical calculations, so that when they suffer the punishment of sin, either by the permission of God or by legal sentence, the astrologer may seem to have spoken truth. And yet they are deceived even in this; for if men be quickly turned to repentance, and remember and fear the future judgment, the punishment of death is remitted to those who are converted to God by the grace of baptism.

Chapter 13. Retribution Here or Hereafter.

But some one will say, Many have committed even murder, and adultery, and other crimes, and have suffered no evil. This indeed rarely happens to men, but to those who know not the counsel of God it frequently seems to happen. But God, who knows all things, knows how and why he who sins does sin, and what cause leads each one to sin. This, however, is in general to be noticed, that if any are evil, not so much in their mind as in their doings, and are not borne to sin under the incitement of purpose, upon them punishment is inflicted more speedily, and more in the present life; for everywhere and always God renders to every one according to his deeds, as He judges to be expedient. But those who practise wickedness of purpose, so that they sometimes even rage against those from whom they have received benefits, and who take no thought for repentance — their punishment He defers to the future. For these men do not, like those of whom we spoke before, deserve to end the punishment of their crimes in the present life; but it is allowed them to occupy the present time as they will, because their correction is not such as to need temporal chastisements, but such as to demand the punishment of eternal fire in hell; and there their souls shall seek repentance, where they shall not be able to find it.

Chapter 14. Knowledge Deadens Lusts.

But if, while in this life, they had placed before their eyes the punishments which they shall then suffer, they would certainly have bridled their lusts, and would in nowise have fallen into sin. For the understanding in the soul has much power for cutting off all its desires, especially when it has acquired the knowledge of heavenly things, by means of which, having received the light of truth, it will turn away from all darkness of evil actions. For as the sun obscures and conceals all the stars by the brightness of his shining, so also the mind, by the light of knowledge, renders all the lusts of the soul ineffective and inactive, sending out upon them the thought of the judgment to come as its rays, so that they can no longer appear in the soul.

Chapter 15. Fear of Men and of God.

But as a proof that the fear of God has much efficacy for the repressing of lusts, take the example of human fear. Who is there among men that does not covet his neighbour's goods? And yet they are restrained, and act honestly, through fear of the punishment which is prescribed by the laws. Through fear, nations are subject to their kings, and armies obey with arms in their hands. Slaves, although they are stronger than their masters, yet through fear submit to their masters' rule. Even wild beasts are tamed by fear; the strongest bulls submit their necks to the yoke, and huge elephants obey their masters, through fear. But why do we use human examples, when even divine are not wanting? Does not the earth itself remain under the fear of precept, which it testifies by its motion and quaking? The sea keeps its prescribed bounds; the angels maintain peace; the stars keep their order, and the rivers their channels: it is certain also that demons are put to flight by fear. And not to lengthen the discourse by too many particulars, see how the fear of God, restraining everything, keeps all things in proper harmony, and in their fixed order. How much more, then, may you be sure that the lusts of demons which arise in your hearts may be extinguished and wholly abolished by the admonition of the fear of God, when even the inciters of lust are themselves put to flight by the influence of fear? You know that these things are so; but if you have anything to answer, proceed.

Chapter 16. Imperfect Conviction.

Then said the old man: My son Clement has wisely framed his argument, so that he has left us nothing to say to these things; but all his discourse which he has delivered on the nature of men has this bearing, that along with the fact that freedom of will is in man, there is also some cause of evil without him, whereby men are indeed incited by various lusts, yet are not compelled to sin; and that for this reason, he said, because fear is much more powerful than they, and it resists and checks the violence of desires, so that, although natural emotions may arise, yet sin may not be committed, those demons being put to flight who incite and inflame these emotions. But these things do not convince me; for I am conscious of certain things from which I know well, that by the arrangement of the heavenly bodies men become murderers or adulterers, and perpetrate other evils; and in like manner honourable and modest women are compelled to act well.

Chapter 17. Astrological Lore.

In short, when Mars, holding the centre in his house, regards Saturn quarterly, with Mercury towards the centre, the full moon coming upon him, in the daily Genesis, he produces murderers, and those who are to fall by the sword, bloody, drunken, lustful, devilish men, inquirers into secrets, malefactors, sacrilegious persons, and such like; especially when there was no one of the good stars looking on. But again Mars himself, having a quarterly position with respect to Venus, in a direction toward the centre, while no good star looks on, produces adulterers and incestuous persons. Venus with the Moon, in the borders and houses of Saturn, if she was with Saturn, and Mars looking on, produces women that are viragos, ready for agriculture, building, and every manly work, to commit adultery with whom they please, and not to be convicted by their husbands, to use no delicacy, no ointments, nor feminine robes and shoes, but to live after the fashion of men. But the unpropitious Venus makes men to be as women, and not to act in any respect as men, if she is with Mars in Aries; on the contrary, she produces women if she is in Capricorn or Aquarius.

Chapter 18. The Reply.

And when the old man had pursued this subject at great length, and had enumerated every kind of mathematical figure, and also the position of the heavenly bodies, wishing thereby to show that fear is not sufficient to restrain lusts, I answered again: Truly, my father, you have argued most learnedly and skilfully; and reason herself invites me to say something in answer to your discourse, since indeed I am acquainted with the science of mathematics, and gladly hold a conference with so learned a man. Listen therefore, while I reply to what you have said that you may learn distinctly that Genesis is not at all from the stars, and that it is possible for those to resist the assault of demons who have recourse to God; and, as I said before, that not only by the fear of God can natural lusts be restrained, but even by the fear of men, as we shall now instruct you.

Chapter 19. Refutation of Astrology.

There are, in every country or kingdom, laws imposed by men, enduring either by writing or simply through custom, which no one easily transgresses. In short, the first Seres, who dwell at the beginning of the world, have a law not to know murder, nor adultery, nor whoredom, and not to commit theft, and not to worship idols; and in all that country, which is very large, there is neither temple, nor image, nor harlot, nor adulteress, nor is any thief brought to trial. But neither is any man ever slain there; and no man's liberty of will is compelled, according to your doctrine, by the fiery star of Mars, to use the sword for the murder of man; nor does Venus in conjunction with Mars compel to adultery, although of course with them Mars occupies the middle circle of heaven every day. But among the Seres the fear of laws is more powerful than the configuration of Genesis .

Chapter 20. Brahmans.

There are likewise among the Bactrians, in the Indian countries, immense multitudes of Brahmans, who also themselves, from the tradition of their ancestors, and peaceful customs and laws, neither commit murder nor adultery, nor worship idols, nor have the practice of eating animal food, are never drunk, never do anything maliciously, but always fear God. And these things indeed they do, though the rest of the Indians commit both murders and adulteries, and worship idols, and are drunken, and practise other wickednesses of this sort. Yea, in the western parts of India itself there is a certain country, where strangers, when they enter it, are taken and slaughtered and eaten; and neither have good stars prevented these men from such wickednesses and from accursed food, nor have malign stars compelled the Brahmans to do any evil. Again, there is a custom among the Persians to marry mothers, and sisters, and daughters. In all that district the Persians contract incestuous marriages.

Chapter 21. Districts of Heaven.

And that those who study mathematics may not have it in their power to use that subterfuge by which they say that there are certain districts of heaven to which it is granted to have some things peculiar to themselves, some of that nation of Persians have gone to foreign countries, who are called Magusæi, of whom there are some to this day in Media, others in Parthia, some also in Egypt, and a considerable number in Galatia and Phrygia, all of whom maintain the form of this incestuous tradition without variation, and hand it down to their posterity to be observed, even although they have changed their district of heaven; nor has Venus with the Moon in the confines and houses of Saturn, with Saturn also and Mars looking on, compelled them to have a Genesis among other men.

Chapter 22. Customs of the Gelones.

Amongst the Geli also there is a custom, that women cultivate the fields, build, and do every manly work; and they are also allowed to have intercourse with whom they please, and are not found fault with by their husbands, or called adulteresses: for they have promiscuous intercourse everywhere, and especially with strangers; they do not use ointments; they do not wear dyed garments, nor shoes. On the other hand, the men of the Gelones are adorned, combed, clothed in soft and various-colored garments, decked with gold, and besmeared with ointments, and that not through lack of manliness, for they are most warlike, and most keen hunters. Yet the whole women of the Gelones had not at their birth the unfavourable Venus in Capricornus or Aquarius; nor had all their men Venus placed with Mars in Aries, by which configuration the Chaldean science asserts that men are born effeminate and dissolute.

Chapter 23. Manners of the Susidæ.

But, further, in Susæ the women use ointments, and indeed of the best sort, being decked with ornaments and precious stones; also they go abroad supported by the aid of their maidservants, with much greater ambition than the men. They do not, however, cultivate modesty, but have intercourse indifferently with whomsoever they please, with slaves and guests, such liberty being allowed them by their husbands; and not only are they not blamed for this, but they also rule over their husbands. And yet the Genesis of all the Susian women has not Venus, with Jupiter and Mars in the middle of the heaven in the houses of Jupiter. In the remoter parts of the East, if a boy be treated unnaturally, when it is discovered, he is killed by his brothers, or his parents, or any of his relations, and is left unburied. And again, among the Gauls, an old law allows boys to be thus treated publicly; and no disgrace is thought to attach to it. And is it possible, that all those who are so basely treated among the Gauls, have had Lucifer with Mercury in the houses of Saturn and the confines of Mars?

Chapter 24. Different Customs of Different Countries.

In the regions of Britain several men have one wife; in Parthia many women have one husband; and each part of the world adheres to its own manners and institutions. None of the Amazons have husbands, but, like animals, they go out from their own territories once a year about the vernal equinox, and live with the men of the neighbouring nation, observing a sort of solemnity the while, and when they have conceived by them they return; and if they bring forth a male child, they cast him away, and rear only females. Now, since the birth of all is at one season, it is absurd to suppose that in the case of males Mars is at the time in equal portions with Saturn, but never in the Genesis of females; and that they have not Mercury placed with Venus in his own houses, so as to produce either painters, or sculptors, or money-changers; or in the houses of Venus, so that perfumers, or singers, or poets might be produced. Among the Saracens, and Upper Libyans, and Moors, and the dwellers about the mouths of the ocean, and also in the remote districts of Germany, and among the Sarmatians and Scythians, and all the nations who dwell in the regions of the Pontic shore, and in the island Chrysea, there is never found a money-changer, nor a sculptor, nor a painter, nor an architect, nor a geometrician, nor a tragedian, nor a poet. Therefore the influence of Mercury and Venus must be wanting among them.

Chapter 25. Not Genesis, But Free-Will.

The Medes alone in all the world, with the greatest care, throw men still breathing to be devoured by dogs; yet they have not Mars with the Moon placed in Cancer all through their daily Genesis . The Indians burn their dead, and the wives of the dead voluntarily offer themselves, and are burned with them. But all the Indian women who are burned alive have not the Sun under the earth in nightly Genesis, with Mars in the regions of Mars. Very many of the Germans end their lives by the halter; but all have not therefore the Moon with Hora begirt by Saturn and Mars. From all this it appears that the fear of the laws bears sway in every country, and the freedom of will which is implanted in man by the Spirit complies with the laws; and Genesis can neither compel the Seres to commit murder, nor the Brahmans to eat flesh, nor the Persians to shun incest, nor the Indians to refrain from burning, nor the Medes from being devoured by dogs, nor the Parthians from having many wives, nor the women of Mesopotamia from preserving their chastity, nor the Greeks from athletic exercises, nor the Gallic boys from being abused; nor can it compel the barbarious nations to be instructed in the studies of the Greeks; but, as we have said, each nation observes its own laws according to free-will, and annuls the decrees of Genesis by the strictness of laws.

Chapter 26. Climates.

But some one skilled in the science of mathematics will say that Genesis is divided into seven parts, which they call climates, and that over each climate one of the seven heavenly bodies bears rule; and that those diverse laws to which we have referred are not given by men, but by those dominant stars according to their will, and that that which pleases the star is observed by men as a law. To this we shall answer, in the first place, that the world is not divided into seven parts; and in the second place, that if it were so, we find many different laws in one part and one country; and therefore there are neither seven laws according to the number of the heavenly bodies, nor twelve according to the number of the signs, nor thirty-six according to that of the divisions of ten degrees; but they are innumerable.

Chapter 27. Doctrine of Climates Untenable.

Moreover, we ought to remember the things which have been mentioned, that in the one country of India there are both persons who feed on human flesh, and persons who abstain even from the flesh of sheep, and birds, and all living creatures; and that the Magusæi marry their mothers and daughters not only in Persia, but that in every nation where they dwell they keep up their incestuous customs. Then, besides, we have mentioned also innumerable nations, which are wholly ignorant of the studies of literature, and also some wise men have changed the laws themselves in several places; and some laws have been voluntarily abandoned, on account of the impossibility of observing them, or on account of their baseness. Assuredly we can easily ascertain how many rulers have changed the laws and customs of nations which they have conquered, and subjected them to their own laws. This is manifestly done by the Romans, who have brought under the Roman law and the civil decrees almost the whole world, and all nations who formerly lived under various laws and customs of their own. It follows, therefore, that the stars of the nations which have been conquered by the Romans have lost their climates and their portions.

Chapter 28. Jewish Customs.

I shall add another thing which may satisfy even the most incredulous. All the Jews who live under the law of Moses circumcise their sons on the eighth day without fail, and shed the blood of the tender infant. But no one of the Gentiles has ever submitted to this on the eighth day; and, on the other hand, no one of the Jews has ever omitted it. How then shall the account of Genesis stand with this, since Jews live in all parts of the world, mixed with Gentiles, and on the eighth day suffer the cutting of a member? And no one of the Gentiles, but only they themselves, as I have said, do this, induced to it not by the compulsion of any star, nor by the perfusion of blood, but by the law of their religion; and in whatever part of the world they are, this sign is familiar to them. But also the fact that one name is among them all, wheresoever they are, does this also come through Genesis? And also that no child born among them is ever exposed, and that on every seventh day they all rest, wherever they may be, and do not go upon a journey, and do not use fire? Exodus 35:3 Why is it, then, that no one of the Jews is compelled by Genesis to go on a journey, or to build, or to sell or buy anything on that day?

Chapter 29. The Gospel More Powerful Than Genesis.

But I shall give a still stronger proof of the matters in hand. For, behold, scarcely seven years have yet passed since the advent of the righteous and true Prophet; and in the course of these, men of all nations coming to Judæa, and moved both by the signs and miracles which they saw, and by the grandeur of His doctrine, received His faith; and then going back to their own countries, they rejected the lawless rites of the Gentiles, and their incestuous marriages. In short, among the Parthians — as Thomas, who is preaching the Gospel among them, has written to us — not many now are addicted to polygamy; nor among the Medes do many throw their dead to dogs; nor are the Persians pleased with intercourse with their mothers, or incestuous marriages with their daughters; nor do the Susian women practise the adulteries that were allowed them; nor has Genesis been able to force those into crimes whom the teaching of religion restrained.

Chapter 30. Genesis Inconsistent with God's Justice.

Behold, from the very matter in which we are now engaged draw an inference, and from the circumstances in which we are now placed deduce a conclusion, how, through a rumour only reaching the ears of men that a Prophet had appeared in Judæa to teach men with signs and miracles to worship one God, all were expecting with prepared and eager minds, even before the coming of my lord Peter, that some one would announce to them what He taught who had appeared. But lest I should seem to carry the enumeration too far, I shall tell you what conclusion ought to be drawn from the whole. Since God is righteous, and since He Himself made the nature of men, how could it be that He should place Genesis in opposition to us, which should compel us to sin, and then that He should punish us when we do sin? Whence it is certain that God punishes no sinner either in the present life or in that to come, except because He knows that he could have conquered, but neglected victory. For even in the present world He takes vengeance upon men, as He did upon those who perished in the deluge, who were all destroyed in one day, yea, in one hour, although it is certain that they were not all born in one hour according to the order of genesis. But it is most absurd to say that it befalls us by nature to suffer evils, if sins had not gone before.

Chapter 31. Value of Knowledge.

And therefore, if we desire salvation, we ought above all to seek after knowledge, being sure that if our mind remain in ignorance, we shall endure not only the evils of genesis, but also whatever other evils from without the demons may please, unless fear of laws and of the judgment to come resist all our desires, and check the violence of sinning. For even human fear does much good, and also much evil, unknown to Genesis, as we have shown above. Therefore our mind is subject to errors in a threefold manner: from those things which come to us through evil custom; or from those lusts which the body naturally stirs up in us; or from those which hostile powers compel us to. But the mind has it in its own nature to oppose and fight against these, when the knowledge of truth shines upon it, by which knowledge is imparted fear of the judgment to come, which is a fit governor of the mind, and which can recall it from the precipices of lusts. That these things, therefore, are in our power, has been sufficiently stated.

Chapter 32. Stubborn Facts.

Now, old man, if you have anything to say in answer to these things, say on. Then said the old man: You have most fully argued, my son; but I, as I said at first, am prevented by my own consciousness from according assent to all this incomparable statement of yours. For I know both my own Genesis and that of my wife, and I know that those things have happened which our Genesis prescribed to each of us; and I cannot now be withdrawn by words from those things which I have ascertained by facts and deeds. In short, since I perceive that you are excellently skilled in this sort of learning, hear the horoscope of my wife, and you shall find the configuration whose issue has occurred. For she had Mars with Venus above the centre, and the Moon setting in the houses of Mars and the confines of Saturn. Now this configuration leads women to be adulteresses, and to love their own slaves, and to end their days in foreign travel and in waters. And this has so come to pass. For she fell in love with her slave, and fearing at once danger and reproach, she fled with him, and going abroad, where she satisfied her love, she perished in the sea.

Chapter 33. An Approaching Recognition.

Then I answered: How know you that she cohabited with her slave abroad, and died in his society? Then the old man said: I know it with perfect certainty; not indeed that she was married to the slave, as indeed I had not even discovered that she loved him. But after she had gone, my brother gave me the whole story, telling me that first she had loved himself; but he, being honourable as a brother, would not pollute his brother's bed with the stain of incest. But she, being both afraid of me, and unable to bear the unhappy reproaches (and yet she should not be blamed for that to which her Genesis compelled her), pretended a dream, and said to me: 'Some one stood by me in a vision, who ordered me to leave the city without delay with my two twins.' When I heard this, being anxious for her safety and that of my sons, I immediately sent away her and the children, retaining with myself one who was younger. For this she said that he had permitted who had given her warning in her sleep.

Chapter 34. The Other Side of the Story.

Then I Clement, understanding that he perchance was my father, was drowned in tears, and my brothers also were ready to rush forward and to disclose the matter; but Peter restrained them, saying: Be quiet, until I give you permission. Therefore Peter, answering, said to the old man: What was the name of your younger son? And he said: Clement. Then Peter: If I shall this day restore to you your most chaste wife and your three sons, will you believe that a modest mind can overcome unreasonable impulses, and that all things that have been spoken by us are true, and that Genesis is nothing? Then said the old man: As it is impossible for you to perform what you have promised, so it is impossible that anything can take place apart from Genesis . Then says Peter: I wish to have all who are here present as witnesses that I shall this day hand over to you your wife, who is living most chastely, with your three sons. And now take a token of these things from this, that I know the whole story much more accurately than you do; and I shall relate the whole occurrences in order, both that you may know them, and that those who are present may learn.

Chapter 35. Revelations.

When he had said this, he turned to the crowds, and thus began: This person whom you see, O men, in this poor garb, is a citizen of the city Rome, descended of the stock of Cæsar himself. His name is Faustinianus. He obtained as his wife a woman of the highest rank, Matthidia by name. By her he had three sons, two of whom were twins; and the one who was the younger, whose name was Clement, is this man! When he said this, he pointed to me with his finger. And his twin sons are these men, Niceta and Aquila, the one of whom was formerly called Faustinus and the other Faustus. But as soon as Peter pronounced our names, all the old man's limbs were weakened, and he fell down in a swoon. But we his sons rushed to him, and embraced and kissed him, fearing that we might not be able to recall his spirit. And while these things were going on, the people were confounded with very wonder.

Chapter 36. New Revelations.

But Peter ordered us to rise from embracing our father, lest we should kill him; and he himself, laying hold of his hand, and lifting him up as from a deep sleep, and gradually reviving him, began to set forth to him the whole transactions as they had really happened: how his brother had fallen in love with Matthidia, and how she, being very modest, had been unwilling to inform her husband of his brother's lawless love, lest she should stir up hostility between the brothers, and bring disgrace upon the family; and how she had wisely pretended a dream, by which she was ordered to depart from the city with her twin sons, leaving the younger one with his father; and how on their voyage they had suffered shipwreck through the violence of a storm; and how, when they were cast upon an island called Antaradus, Matthidia was thrown by a wave upon a rock, but her twin children were seized by pirates and carried to Cæsarea, and there sold to a pious woman, who treated them as sons, and brought them up, and caused them to be educated as gentlemen; and how the pirates had changed their names, and called the one Niceta and the other Aquila; and how afterwards, through common studies and acquaintanceship, they had adhered to Simon; and how they had turned away from him when they saw him to be a magician and a deceiver, and had come to Zacchæus; and how subsequently they had been associated with himself; and how Clement also, setting out from the city for the sake of learning the truth, had, through his acquaintance with Barnabas, come to Cæsarea, and had become known to him, and had adhered to him, and how he had been taught by him the faith of his religion; and also how he had found and recognised his mother begging at Antaradus, and how the whole island rejoiced at his recognition of her; and also concerning her sojourn with her most chaste hostess, and the cure that he had wrought upon her, and concerning the liberality of Clement to those who had been kind to his mother; and how afterwards, when Niceta and Aquila asked who the strange woman was, and had heard the whole story from Clement, they cried out that they were her twin sons Faustinus and Faustus; and how they had unfolded the whole history of what had befallen them; and how afterwards, by the persuasion of Peter himself, they were presented to their mother with caution, lest she should be cut off by the sudden joy.

Chapter 37. Another Recognition.

But while Peter was detailing these things in the hearing of the old man, in a narrative which was most pleasing to the crowd, so that the hearers wept through wonder at the events, and through compassion for sufferings incident to humanity, my mother, hearing (I know not how) of the recognition of my father, rushed into the middle of us in breathless haste, crying out, and saying: Where is my husband, my lord Faustinianus, who has been so long afflicted, wandering from city to city in search of me? While she shouted thus like one demented, and gazed around, the old man, running up, began to embrace and hug her with many tears. And while these things were going on, Peter requested the crowds to disperse, saying that it was unseemly to remain longer; but that opportunity must be afforded them of seeing one another more privately. But tomorrow, said he, if any of you wish it, let them assemble to hear the word.

Chapter 38. Angels Unawares.

When Peter had said this, the crowds dispersed; and when we also were intending to go to our lodging, the master of the house said to us: It is base and wicked that such and so great men should stay in a hostelry, when I have almost my whole house empty, and very many beds spread, and all necessary things provided. But when Peter refused, the wife of the householder prostrated herself before him with her children, and besought him, saying, I entreat you, stay with us. But not even so did Peter consent, until the daughter of those people who asked him, who had been for a long time vexed with an unclean spirit, and bound with chains, who had been shut up in a closet, having had the demon expelled from her, and the door of the closet opened, came with her chains and fell down at Peter's feet, saying: It is right, my lord, that you keep my deliverance-feast here today, and not sadden me or my parents. But when Peter asked what was the meaning of her chains and of her words, her parents, gladdened beyond hope by the recovery of their daughter, were, as it were, thunderstruck with astonishment, and could not speak; but the servants who were in attendance said: This girl has been possessed of a demon from her seventh year, and used to cut, and bite, and even to tear in pieces, all who attempted to approach her, and this she has never ceased to do for twenty years till the present time. Nor could any one cure her, or even approach her, for she rendered many helpless, and even destroyed some; for she was stronger than any man, being doubtless strengthened by the power of the demon. But now, as you see, the demon has fled from your presence, and the doors which were shut with the greatest strength have been opened, and she herself stands before you in her sound mind, asking of you to make the day of her recovery gladsome both to herself and her parents, and to remain with them. When one of the servants had made this statement, and the chains of their own accord were loosened from her hands and feet, Peter, being sure that it was by his means that soundness was restored to the girl, consented to remain with them. And he ordered those also who had remained in the lodging, with his wife, to come over; and every one of us having got a separate bed-chamber, we remained; and having taken food in the usual manner, and given praises to God, we went to sleep in our several apartments.

Aklat 9

Kabanata 1. Isang Paliwanag.

Kinabukasan, maaga kaming nagmadaling kasama ni Pedro patungo sa lugar na pinagdausan ng talakayan noong nakaraang araw. Nang makita niyang napakaraming taong nagtipon upang makinig at naroon din ang matandang lalaki, sinabi niya rito: Matanda, napagkasunduan kahapon na makikipagtalakayan ka ngayon kay Clemente: patutunayan mo na walang nangyayari na hiwalay sa genesis, o patutunayan naman ni Clemente na walang gayong genesis at na nasa sarili nating kapangyarihan ang ating ginagawa. Sumagot ang matanda: Naaalala ko ang napagkasunduan, at naaalala ko rin ang sinabi mo pagkatapos noon, nang ituro mong imposibleng makaalam nang tiyak ang tao maliban kung matuto siya mula sa tunay na Propeta. Sinabi ni Pedro: Hindi mo naunawaan ang ibig kong sabihin, kaya ipaliliwanag ko ngayon. Ang tinutukoy ko ay ang kalooban at layunin ng Diyos bago pa likhain ang sanlibutan—ang layuning ayon dito'y nilikha Niya ang sanlibutan, itinakda ang mga panahon, ibinigay ang kautusan, ipinangako sa mga matuwid ang darating na sanlibutan bilang gantimpala sa kanilang mabubuting gawa, at itinakda ang mga kaparusahan sa di-matuwid ayon sa matuwid na hatol. Sinabi kong hindi matutuklasan ng tao ang ganitong panukala at kalooban ng Diyos sa pamamagitan lamang ng haka at palagay, maliban kung ihayag iyon ng isang propetang isinugo Niya. Hindi ko sinabi na ang lahat ng aral o pag-aaral ay hindi maaaring malaman nang walang propeta; sapagkat alam kong maraming sining at agham ang natututuhan at naisasagawa ng tao mula sa mga gurong tao, hindi mula sa tunay na Propeta.

Kabanata 2. Mga Paunang Usapin.

Yamang ipinahahayag mong bihasa ka sa kalagayan ng mga bituin at sa galaw ng mga katawang makalangit, at mula roon ay kaya mong kumbinsihin si Clemente na ang lahat ay nasasakop ng Genesis, o matutuhan mula sa kanya na ang lahat ay pinamamahalaan ng pagkalinga ng Diyos at na may mga bagay na nasa sarili nating kapangyarihan, panahon na upang simulan ninyong dalawa ang usaping ito. Sumagot ang matanda: Hindi na sana kailangang magbangon ng ganitong mga tanong kung maaari nating matutuhan mula sa tunay na Propeta at marinig sa isang malinaw na pahayag na may anumang nakasalalay sa atin at sa kalayaan ng ating kalooban. Lubha akong naapektuhan ng sinabi mo kahapon tungkol sa kapangyarihang propetiko. Idinagdag niya: Kaya sumasang-ayon ako sa iyong pasya na walang bagay na matitiyak ng tao nang walang pag-aalinlangan, yamang napakaikli lamang ng kanyang buhay at napakahina ng hiningang wari'y nagpapanatili sa kanya. Gayunman, dahil nangako ako kay Clemente bago ko marinig ang tungkol sa kapangyarihang propetiko na patutunayan kong ang lahat ay sakop ng Genesis, o matututuhan ko mula sa kanya na may bagay na nasa ating sariling kapangyarihan, hinihiling kong siya muna ang magsimula at ilahad ang kanyang pagtutol. Sapagkat mula nang marinig ko sa iyo ang ilang salita tungkol sa propesiya ay nalito ako sa pag-iisip sa kadakilaan ng paunang kaalaman; at hindi ko na iniisip na dapat tanggapin ang anumang bunga lamang ng haka at palagay.

Kabanata 3. Simula ng Talakayan.

Pagkasabi nito ng matanda, ako, si Clemente, ay nagsimulang magsalita: Sa pamamagitan ng Kanyang Anak ay nilikha ng Diyos ang sanlibutan na parang isang bahay na may dalawang bahagi, na pinaghihiwalay ng kalawakan na tinatawag na langit. Itinakda Niyang manirahan sa itaas ang mga kapangyarihang angheliko, at sa nakikitang sanlibutang ito ay ipanganak ang maraming tao, upang mula sa kanila ay makapili Siya ng mga kaibigan para sa Kanyang Anak—mga makapagpapagalak sa Kanya at maihahandang tulad ng isang minamahal na nobya para sa isang lalaking ikakasal. Hanggang sa panahon ng kasalan, na siyang pagpapakita ng darating na sanlibutan, nagtakda Siya ng isang kapangyarihan upang piliin at bantayan ang mabubuti sa mga ipinanganak dito, at ingatan sila para sa Kanyang Anak sa isang lugar na hiwalay sa kasalanan. Naroon na ang ilan, inihahandang gaya ng isang nobyang pinalamutian para sa pagdating ng lalaking ikakasal. Sapagkat ang prinsipe ng sanlibutang ito at ng kasalukuyang panahon ay tulad ng isang mangangalunya na dumudungis at lumalapastangan sa isip ng mga tao, inilalayo sila sa pag-ibig sa tunay na lalaking ikakasal at inaakit tungo sa ibang mga mangingibig.

Kabanata 4. Bakit Nilalang ang Masamang Prinsipe.

Ngunit maaaring itanong: Bakit kailangang malikha ang prinsipe na maglalayo sa isip ng mga tao mula sa tunay na Prinsipe? Sapagkat ang Diyos, na nagnanais maghanda ng mga kaibigan para sa Kanyang Anak, ay hindi nagnanais na maging mabuti sila dahil lamang wala silang ibang magagawa ayon sa kanilang kalikasan. Nais Niyang kusang piliin at naisin nilang maging mabuti. Sapagkat walang kapuri-puri sa isang bagay na hindi pinili, at hindi itinuturing na mabuti ang hindi sinadyang hanapin. Walang karangalan sa pagiging isang bagay kung hindi ka naman maaaring maging iba dahil sa pangangailangan ng iyong kalikasan. Kaya niloob ng pagkalinga ng Diyos na maraming tao ang isilang sa sanlibutang ito upang mula sa marami ay mapili yaong kusang pipili ng mabuting buhay. At dahil nakita Niyang hindi maaaring umiral ang kasalukuyang sanlibutan nang walang pagkakaiba at hindi pagkakapantay-pantay, binigyan Niya ang bawat isip ng kalayaan sa pagkilos sa gitna ng sari-saring kalagayan ng buhay, at itinakda ang prinsipe ng sanlibutang ito upang, sa pamamagitan ng mga salungat na mungkahi nito, ang pagpili sa mas mabuting bagay ay mangailangan ng pagsasagawa ng kabutihan.

Kabanata 5. Pangangailangan ng Hindi Pagkakapantay-pantay.

Upang maging mas malinaw ang ibig naming sabihin, ipaliliwanag namin ito sa mga tiyak na halimbawa. Nararapat bang ang lahat ng tao sa sanlibutang ito ay maging mga hari, prinsipe, panginoon, guro, abogado, geometers, panday-ginto, panadero, panday, grammarian, mayaman, magsasaka, tagagawa ng pabango, mangingisda, o dukha? Tiyak na hindi maaaring lahat ay iisa sa mga ito. Gayunman, kailangan ng buhay ng tao ang lahat ng propesyong ito at marami pang iba, at hindi maipagpapatuloy ang buhay kung wala ang mga ito. Kaya kinakailangan ang hindi pagkakapantay-pantay sa sanlibutang ito. Walang hari kung walang mga nasasakupan na kanyang paghaharian; walang amo kung walang pinamumunuan; at gayon din sa iba pang tungkulin.

Kabanata 6. Kaayusan ng Sanlibutan para sa Pagsasanay ng Kabutihan.

Dahil alam ng Manlilikha na walang kusang papasok sa paligsahan kapag iniiwasan ang hirap—alalaong baga, sa pagsasagawa ng mga gawaing at propesyong sa pamamagitan ng mga iyon ay nahahayag ang katarungan o awa ng bawat isa—ginawa Niyang ang katawan ng tao ay makaranas ng gutom, uhaw, at lamig. Dahil dito, napipilitang matuto at gumawa ang mga tao upang tustusan ang kanilang katawan at kabuhayan. Para sa pagkain, inumin, at pananamit kaya natin pinag-aaralan ang iba't ibang sining. Dito nahahayag ang layunin ng isip ng bawat isa: tutugunan ba niya ang gutom at lamig sa pamamagitan ng pagnanakaw, pagpatay, pagsisinungaling sa ilalim ng panunumpa, at iba pang krimen; o, sa pagpapanatili ng katarungan, awa, at pagpipigil sa sarili, tutugunan ba niya ang pangangailangan sa pamamagitan ng marangal na hanapbuhay at paggawa ng kanyang mga kamay? Kung sa katarungan, kabanalan, at awa niya tinutustusan ang mga pangangailangan ng katawan, nagwawagi siya sa paligsahang inilagay sa harap niya at napipiling kaibigan ng Anak ng Diyos. Ngunit kung pinaglilingkuran niya ang pita ng laman sa pamamagitan ng pandaraya, kasamaan, at krimen, nagiging kaibigan siya ng prinsipe ng sanlibutang ito at ng mga demonyo; at tinuturuan siya ng mga iyon na ibintang sa galaw ng mga bituin ang mga kamaliang siya mismo ang kusang pumili. Gaya ng sinabi namin, ang mga sining ay natututuhan at isinasagawa dahil sa pangangailangan sa pagkain at inumin; ngunit kapag dumating sa isang tao ang kaalaman sa katotohanan, humihina ang ganitong pagnanasa at pumapalit ang pagiging payak sa pamumuhay. Sapagkat gaano ba kalaki ang kailangan ng taong nabubuhay sa tubig at tinapay at sa Diyos lamang umaasa?

Kabanata 7. Ang Luma at Bagong Kapanganakan.

Kaya, gaya ng sinabi namin, may kinakailangang hindi pagkakapantay-pantay sa pamamahala ng sanlibutan. Hindi kayang malaman at gawin ng lahat ang lahat ng bagay, gayunman halos kailangan ng bawat isa ang gawain at paglilingkod ng iba. Dahil dito, kinakailangang ang isa ay gumawa at ang isa ay magbayad sa kanyang paggawa; ang isa ay maging lingkod at ang isa ay panginoon; ang isa ay maging nasasakupan at ang isa ay hari. Ngunit ang hindi pagkakapantay-pantay na ito, na kinakailangan para sa buhay ng tao, ay ginawang pagkakataon ng banal na pagkalinga upang maisagawa ang katarungan, awa, at makataong pagtrato. Sa pakikitungo ng tao sa kapwa, nagkakaroon ang bawat isa ng pagkakataong maging makatarungan sa taong dapat niyang bayaran; maawain sa taong dahil sa karamdaman o kahirapan ay hindi makabayad ng utang; at makatao sa mga waring nasa ilalim ng kanyang kapangyarihan. Natututuhan din niyang maging mahinahon sa mga nasasakupan at gawin ang lahat ayon sa kautusan ng Diyos. Ibinigay ng Diyos ang kautusan upang tulungan ang isip ng tao na mas madaling makilala kung paano dapat kumilos sa bawat bagay, paano makaiwas sa kasamaan, at paano tumungo sa darating na mga pagpapala; at kung paano, sa muling pagsilang sa tubig, mapapatay sa pamamagitan ng mabubuting gawa ang apoy ng dating kapanganakan. Sapagkat ang unang kapanganakan ay bumababa sa atin sa pamamagitan ng apoy ng pita; kaya sa banal na kaayusan ay ipinapasok ang ikalawang kapanganakan sa pamamagitan ng tubig upang patayin ang kalikasan ng apoy. Sa gayon, ang kaluluwang nililiwanagan ng makalangit na Espiritu ay makalalaya mula sa takot na kaugnay ng unang kapanganakan, kung mula noon ay mamumuhay itong hindi naghahangad ng mga kalayawan ng sanlibutang ito kundi gaya ng isang manlalakbay, isang dayuhan, at mamamayan ng ibang lungsod.

Kabanata 8. Mga Gamit ng mga Kasamaan.

Ngunit maaaring sabihin mo: Sa mga bagay na hinihingi ng kalikasan ang paggamit ng mga sining at gawain, maaaring piliin ng tao ang katarungan at pigilan ang kanyang pagnanasa at kilos; ngunit ano ang masasabi natin tungkol sa mga karamdaman at kahinaang dumarating sa tao, sa mga pinahihirapan ng mga demonyo, lagnat, panginginig, kabaliwan, pagkawala ng pag-iisip, at sa napakaraming kapahamakang dumadaig sa lahi ng tao? Sinasabi namin na kung uunawain ang kabuuan ng hiwagang ito, makikitang higit pang naaayon sa katarungan ang mga bagay na ito kaysa sa mga nauna naming ipinaliwanag. Binigyan ng Diyos ang tao ng kalikasang may kakayahang matuto tungkol sa mabuti at lumaban sa masama—matuto ng mga sining, supilin ang mga kalayawan, at ilagay ang kautusan ng Diyos sa harap niya sa lahat ng bagay. Para sa layuning ito ay pinahintulutan Niya ang ilang salungat na kapangyarihan na gumala sa sanlibutan at makipaglaban sa atin, upang sa pakikipaglaban sa kanila ay makamit ng mga matuwid ang palma ng tagumpay at ang karapat-dapat na gantimpala.

Kabanata 9. Ipinaglihi sa Kasalanan.

Dahil dito, kung minsan, kapag ang mga tao ay namuhay nang walang pagpipigil at sa halip na lumaban ay kusang sumuko at nagbigay ng puwang sa mga demonyo sa kanilang sarili, ang mapaminsalang impluwensiya ng mga iyon ay nagbubunga ng supling na mahina, magulo ang disposisyon, at madaling magkasakit. Kapag lubusang pinagbibigyan ang pita at walang pag-iingat sa pagsisiping, ang susunod na salinlahi ay maaaring magdala ng mga kahinaan at depektong kaugnay ng masasamang kapangyarihang nag-udyok sa gayong gawain. Kaya may pananagutan ang mga magulang sa ganitong mga kapansanan ng kanilang mga anak kapag hindi nila iginalang ang wastong kaayusan ng pagsisiping. Mayroon ding mas malalalim na sanhi kung bakit napapasailalim ang mga kaluluwa sa ganitong mga kasamaan na hindi natin layuning talakayin ngayon. Gayunman, nararapat kilalanin ng bawat isa ang kautusan ng Diyos upang matutuhan ang wastong kaayusan ng pag-aanak at maiwasan ang mga sanhi ng karumihan, upang maging malinis ang isisilang. Sapagkat kung sa pagtatanim ng mga puno at paghahasik ng binhi ay hinahanap ang tamang panahon, nililinis ang lupa, at maingat na inihahanda ang lahat upang hindi mapinsala at mamatay ang binhi, higit na hindi dapat pabayaan ang pag-iingat sa paghahasik ng binhi ng tao, na higit sa lahat ng mga bagay na ito.

Kabanata 10. Hiblang Pinahiran ng Alkitran.

Ngunit paano naman, sinasabi, ang mga batang walang anumang kapansanan sa katawan ngunit sa paglipas ng panahon ay nahuhulog sa mga kasamaan, at ang ilan ay marahas pang nahahatid sa kamatayan? Halos pareho rin ang paliwanag dito. Ang mga kapangyarihang salungat sa lahi ng tao ay naaanyayahan, wika nga, sa puso ng bawat isa sa pamamagitan ng sari-saring pita at nakakakita ng pasukan. Ang kanilang kapangyarihan ay manghikayat at mag-udyok lamang; hindi sila makapipilit ni ganap na makagagawa para sa tao. Kaya kung pumayag ang isang tao sa kanila at isagawa ang masamang ninanais niya, ang kanyang pagsang-ayon at gawa ay magbubunga ng kapahamakan at pinakamasamang uri ng kamatayan. Ngunit kung, dahil iniisip niya ang darating na paghuhukom, ay pigilin siya ng takot at bawiin ang kanyang sarili upang hindi niya isagawa ang masamang naisip, makaliligtas siya hindi lamang sa kasalukuyang kapahamakan kundi pati sa darating na kaparusahan. Ang bawat sanhi ng kasalanan ay tulad ng hiblang pinahiran ng alkitran na agad nagliliyab kapag tinamaan ng init ng apoy; at ang pagsindi ng apoy na ito ang gawain ng mga demonyo. Kung ang isang tao ay wari'y nababalutan ng alkitran ng kasalanan at pita, madaling mananaig sa kanya ang apoy. Ngunit kung ang hibla ay hindi nakababad sa alkitran ng kasalanan kundi sa tubig ng paglilinis at muling pagsilang, hindi kayang pagliyabin iyon ng apoy ng mga demonyo.

Kabanata 11. Takot.

Ngunit may magsasabi: Ano ang gagawin naming nabalutan na ng mga kasalanan na parang alkitran? Ang sagot ko: Magmadali kayong mahugasan, upang maalis sa inyo ang gatong ng apoy sa pamamagitan ng pagtawag sa banal na Pangalan. Mula noon, supilin ninyo ang inyong mga pita sa pamamagitan ng takot sa darating na paghuhukom at matatag na labanan ang masasamang kapangyarihan tuwing lumalapit sila sa inyong mga pandama. Ngunit sasabihin mo: Kung ang isang tao ay nahulog sa pag-ibig, paano niya mapipigil ang sarili kahit makita pa niya sa harap niya ang ilog ng apoy na tinatawag na Pyriphlegethon? Iyan ang dahilan ng mga ayaw magsisi. Huwag muna nating banggitin ang Pyriphlegethon. Ilagay mo sa harap mo ang mga kaparusahang pantao at tingnan kung gaano kalakas ang takot. Kapag ang isang tao ay dinadala sa parusa dahil sa krimen ng pag-ibig at itinatali sa poste upang sunugin, may puwang pa ba sa sandaling iyon ang pagnanasa sa babaeng minahal niya, o ilalarawan pa ba niya ang mukha nito sa isip? Tiyak na hindi. Nakikita mo, kung gayon, na ang agarang takot ay nakapipigil sa di-matuwid na pagnanasa. Kaya kung ang mga sumasampalataya sa Diyos at nagpapahayag ng darating na paghuhukom at kaparusahan ng walang-hanggang apoy ay hindi umiwas sa kasalanan, malinaw na hindi ganap ang kanilang pananampalataya. Kung tiyak ang pananampalataya, tiyak din ang takot; ngunit kung may kakulangan sa pananampalataya, humihina rin ang takot at nagkakaroon ng pagkakataon ang salungat na mga kapangyarihan na pumasok. Kapag pumayag ang tao sa kanilang panghihikayat, napapasailalim siya sa kanilang impluwensiya at itinutulak sa bangin ng kasalanan.

Kabanata 12. Mga Astrologo.

Dahil hindi alam ng mga astrologo ang ganitong mga hiwaga, iniisip nilang ang mga bagay na ito ay nagaganap dahil sa galaw ng mga katawang makalangit. Kaya sa kanilang mga sagot sa mga taong sumasangguni tungkol sa hinaharap ay madalas silang nagkakamali. Hindi ito kataka-taka, sapagkat hindi sila mga propeta. Sa mahabang pagsasanay, ang mga tagapagtaguyod ng kamalian ay nakahanap ng mga paraan upang pagtakpan ang kanilang pagkakamali at nagpakilala ng tinatawag nilang mga Climacteric Periods, na ipinipilit nilang mga panahon ng panganib kung kailan maaaring mamatay ang isang tao o maaaring hindi. Hindi nila alam na hindi galaw ng mga bituin kundi gawain ng mga demonyo ang nasa likod ng ganitong mga bagay. Ang mga demonyong iyon, na nagnanais patibayin ang kamalian ng astrolohiya, ay nililinlang ang mga tao tungo sa kasalanan sa pamamagitan ng mga kalkulasyong matematikal, upang kapag dumating ang kaparusahan sa kasalanan—sa pagpapahintulot man ng Diyos o sa hatol ng batas—magmukhang nagsabi ng totoo ang astrologo. Gayunman, nagkakamali pa rin sila, sapagkat kung mabilis magsisi ang tao at alalahanin at katakutan ang darating na paghuhukom, maaaring mapawalang-bisa ang hatol ng kamatayan sa mga bumabalik-loob sa Diyos sa pamamagitan ng biyaya ng bautismo.

Kabanata 13. Gantimpala Dito o sa Kabilang-Buhay.

Ngunit may magsasabi: Marami ang pumatay, nangalunya, at gumawa ng iba pang krimen ngunit walang masamang nangyari sa kanila. Bihira itong tunay na mangyari, bagaman sa mga hindi nakaaalam sa panukala ng Diyos ay madalas itong tila gayon. Ang Diyos, na nakaaalam ng lahat ng bagay, ay nakaaalam kung paano at bakit nagkakasala ang bawat isa at kung anong dahilan ang nagtutulak sa kanya. Sa pangkalahatan ay ito ang dapat tandaan: kung ang isang tao ay masama hindi gaanong sa layunin ng isip kundi sa gawa, at hindi sinasadyang yakapin ang kasamaan bilang kanyang pasya, mas mabilis ang kaparusahan sa kanya at madalas sa kasalukuyang buhay. Sapagkat sa lahat ng panahon ay nagbibigay ang Diyos sa bawat isa ayon sa kanyang mga gawa, ayon sa nakikita Niyang makabubuti. Ngunit yaong sadyang nagsasanay sa kasamaan, na nagagalit pa laban sa mga taong gumawa sa kanila ng mabuti at walang pag-iisip tungkol sa pagsisisi—ang kanilang kaparusahan ay ipinagpapaliban Niya sa hinaharap. Hindi tulad ng mga nauna, hindi sapat sa kanila ang pansamantalang kaparusahan sa buhay na ito. Pinahihintulutan silang gugulin ang kasalukuyang panahon ayon sa kanilang nais sapagkat ang kanilang kasamaan ay hindi nangangailangan lamang ng pansamantalang pagtutuwid kundi ng kaparusahan ng walang-hanggang apoy sa impiyerno; at doon hahanapin ng kanilang mga kaluluwa ang pagsisisi kung kailan hindi na nila ito matatagpuan.

Kabanata 14. Pinapahina ng Kaalaman ang mga Pagnanasa.

Kung habang nasa buhay na ito ay inilagay nila sa harap ng kanilang mga mata ang mga kaparusahang daranasin nila roon, tiyak na mapipigil nila ang kanilang mga pita at hindi sila mahuhulog sa kasalanan. Malaki ang kapangyarihan ng pagkaunawa sa kaluluwa upang putulin ang mga pagnanasa nito, lalo na kapag natamo nito ang kaalaman sa makalangit na mga bagay. Sa liwanag ng katotohanan ay tumatalikod ang isip sa lahat ng kadiliman ng masasamang gawa. Kung paanong tinatakpan ng araw sa liwanag nito ang lahat ng bituin, gayundin, sa liwanag ng kaalaman, pinawawalang-lakas ng isip ang mga pita ng kaluluwa. Ipinapadala nito sa kanila na parang mga sinag ang alaala ng darating na paghuhukom, kaya hindi na sila makapagpapakita sa kaluluwa.

Kabanata 15. Takot sa Tao at sa Diyos.

Bilang patunay na napakalakas ng takot sa Diyos sa pagpigil sa pita, tingnan ang halimbawa ng takot sa tao. Sino ang hindi kailanman nagnanais ng pag-aari ng kanyang kapwa? Gayunman, napipigilan ang marami at kumikilos nang matuwid dahil sa takot sa kaparusahang itinakda ng batas. Sa takot ay nagpapasakop ang mga bansa sa kanilang mga hari at sumusunod ang mga hukbo kahit may sandata sa kamay. Ang mga alipin, kahit mas malakas kaysa sa kanilang mga amo, ay sumusunod dahil sa takot. Maging mababangis na hayop ay napaamo ng takot; yumuyuko ang malalakas na toro sa pamatok at sumusunod ang malalaking elepante sa kanilang mga tagapamahala. Ngunit bakit tayo gagamit lamang ng mga halimbawang pantao kung mayroon ding banal? Hindi ba nananatili ang lupa sa ilalim ng kapangyarihan ng utos, na pinatutunayan nito sa pagyanig nito? Pinananatili ng dagat ang itinakdang hangganan, ng mga anghel ang kapayapaan, ng mga bituin ang kanilang kaayusan, at ng mga ilog ang kanilang mga daluyan. Tiyak ding tumatakas ang mga demonyo dahil sa takot. Kaya, upang hindi pahabain ang usapan, tingnan kung paanong ang takot sa Diyos, na pumipigil sa lahat, ay nagpapanatili sa wastong pagkakatugma at kaayusan ng mga bagay. Gaano pa kaya katiyak na ang mga pita na inuudyukan ng mga demonyo sa inyong puso ay maaaring patayin at lubusang alisin sa pamamagitan ng paalala ng takot sa Diyos, yamang pati ang mga mismong nang-uudyok sa pita ay pinatatakas ng takot? Alam mong gayon ang mga bagay na ito; ngunit kung mayroon kang tugon, magsalita ka.

Kabanata 16. Hindi Ganap na Pagkumbinsi.

Pagkatapos ay sinabi ng matanda: Maingat na binuo ng anak kong si Clemente ang kanyang pangangatwiran at halos wala siyang iniwang masasabi laban dito. Ang kabuuan ng kanyang talumpati tungkol sa kalikasan ng tao ay ito: may kalayaan ng kalooban sa tao, ngunit mayroon ding sanhi ng kasamaan sa labas niya na nag-uudyok sa pamamagitan ng iba't ibang pita, bagaman hindi nito mapipilit ang tao na magkasala. Sinabi rin niyang mas makapangyarihan ang takot kaysa sa mga pagnanasang iyon at kaya nitong pigilan ang kanilang karahasan. Kaya kahit bumangon ang likas na damdamin, maaaring hindi maisagawa ang kasalanan, sapagkat napaaalis ang mga demonyong nagpapaliyab sa mga ito. Gayunman, hindi pa rin ako kumbinsido. May mga bagay akong nalalaman mula sa sarili kong karanasan na para sa akin ay nagpapatunay na, ayon sa pagkakaayos ng mga katawang makalangit, nagiging mamamatay-tao o mangangalunya ang mga tao at gumagawa ng iba pang kasamaan; at sa gayunding paraan ay napipilitang kumilos nang mabuti ang mararangal at mahinhin na kababaihan.

Kabanata 17. Kaalaman sa Astrolohiya.

Sa madaling sabi, kapag ang Mars, na nasa gitna sa sarili nitong bahay, ay nasa parisukat na ugnayan kay Saturno, kasama ang Mercury patungo sa gitna at ang kabilugan ng buwan ay sumasakop sa kanya sa araw-araw na Genesis, nagbubunga umano ito ng mga mamamatay-tao at mga taong mamamatay sa tabak—mga madugo, lasing, mapagnasa, mala-demonyo, mahilig sa lihim, gumagawa ng kasamaan at paglapastangan, at mga katulad nito—lalo na kung walang mabuting bituin na nakatingin. Muli, kapag si Mars ay nasa parisukat na ugnayan kay Venus patungo sa gitna at walang mabuting bituin na tumitingin, nagbubunga raw ito ng mga mangangalunya at ng mga nagsasagawa ng incest. Si Venus kasama ang Buwan sa mga hangganan at bahay ni Saturno, kung kasama niya si Saturno at nakatingin si Mars, ay nagbubunga raw ng mga babaeng matapang na parang lalaki, handa sa pagsasaka, pagtatayo, at lahat ng gawaing panlalaki; nakikipagtalik sa sinumang maibigan at hindi nahuhuli ng kanilang asawa; hindi gumagamit ng marangyang bagay, pabango, o pambabaeng damit at sapatos, kundi namumuhay na parang lalaki. Samantala, ang hindi mapalad na Venus ay sinasabing nagpapababa sa mga lalaki na kumilos na parang babae kapag kasama si Mars sa Aries; ngunit sa kabaligtaran ay nagbibigay ng ganitong katangian sa mga babae kapag nasa Capricorn o Aquarius.

Kabanata 18. Ang Tugon.

Nang mahaba nang tinalakay ng matanda ang paksang ito at isa-isang binanggit ang maraming matematikal na hugis at posisyon ng mga katawang makalangit upang ipakitang hindi sapat ang takot upang supilin ang pita, muli akong sumagot: Tunay, ama, naipahayag mo nang may malaking kaalaman at husay ang iyong argumento. Inaanyayahan din ako ng katuwiran na tumugon, sapagkat pamilyar din ako sa kaalamang matematikal at nalulugod akong makipagtalakayan sa isang taong napag-aralan ito. Pakinggan mo, kung gayon, habang sinasagot ko ang sinabi mo, upang malinaw mong malaman na ang Genesis ay hindi nagmumula sa mga bituin; na maaaring labanan ng mga taong lumalapit sa Diyos ang paglusob ng mga demonyo; at, gaya ng sinabi ko noon, na hindi lamang sa takot sa Diyos maaaring supilin ang likas na pita kundi maging sa takot sa tao, gaya ng ipaliliwanag namin ngayon.

Kabanata 19. Pagpapabulaan sa Astrolohiya.

Sa bawat bansa o kaharian ay may mga batas na itinakda ng tao, nakasulat man o pinananatili lamang ng kaugalian, at hindi madaling nilalabag ang mga iyon. Halimbawa, ang mga Seres, na naninirahan sa pinakadulong silangan, ay may batas na huwag pumatay, mangalunya, makiapid, magnakaw, o sumamba sa mga diyus-diyosan. Sa kanilang malawak na lupain ay walang templo, larawan ng diyus-diyosan, patutot, o mangangalunya; wala ring magnanakaw na dinadala sa hukuman, at walang taong pinapatay doon. Ayon sa iyong aral, dapat sana'y pilitin ng maalab na bituing Mars ang kalayaan ng kalooban ng ilan sa kanila upang gumamit ng tabak sa pagpatay, o pilitin ng Venus kasama ng Mars ang ilan sa pangangalunya, yamang araw-araw ding dumaraan sa kanila ang gayong mga posisyon ng langit. Ngunit sa mga Seres, mas makapangyarihan ang takot sa batas kaysa sa sinasabing pagkakaayos ng Genesis.

Kabanata 20. Mga Brahman.

Gayundin sa mga Bactrian, sa mga lupain ng India, ay napakaraming Brahman. Sa tradisyon ng kanilang mga ninuno at sa kanilang mapayapang mga kaugalian at batas, hindi sila pumapatay o nangangalunya, hindi sumasamba sa mga diyus-diyosan, hindi kumakain ng laman ng hayop, hindi nalalasing, at hindi gumagawa ng masama nang sinasadya, kundi laging may takot sa Diyos. Ginagawa nila ang mga ito kahit ang ibang mga Indiano ay pumapatay, nangangalunya, sumasamba sa mga diyus-diyosan, nalalasing, at gumagawa ng iba pang kasamaan. Sa kanlurang bahagi mismo ng India ay may isang lupain kung saan ang mga dayuhan na pumapasok ay dinadakip, pinapatay, at kinakain. Hindi napigil ng mabubuting bituin ang mga taong iyon sa gayong kasamaan at karumal-dumal na pagkain, ni napilit ng masasamang bituin ang mga Brahman na gumawa ng kasamaan. Muli, sa mga Persiano ay kaugalian ang pakikipag-asawa sa mga ina, kapatid na babae, at mga anak na babae; sa buong lupaing iyon ay isinasagawa ang ganitong mga incestuous na pag-aasawa.

Kabanata 21. Mga Sakop ng Kalangitan.

Upang hindi makatakas ang mga nag-aaral ng matematika sa pamamagitan ng pagsasabing may ilang bahagi ng langit na may sariling natatanging impluwensiya, dapat pansinin na may mga Persiano mula sa bansang iyon na lumipat sa ibang mga lupain—tinatawag silang Magusæi. Hanggang ngayon ay mayroon sa Media, Parthia, Ehipto, at marami sa Galatia at Phrygia. Gayunman, saanman sila manirahan, pinananatili nila nang walang pagbabago ang incestuous na tradisyong ito at ipinapasa sa kanilang mga anak, kahit nagbago na ang kanilang bahagi ng langit. Hindi sila pinilit ng Venus kasama ang Buwan sa mga hangganan at bahay ni Saturno, kasama rin si Saturno at tinitingnan ni Mars, na magkaroon ng ibang Genesis sa piling ng ibang mga tao.

Kabanata 22. Mga Kaugalian ng mga Gelones.

Sa mga Geli ay may kaugalian din na ang mga babae ang nagbubungkal ng lupa, nagtatayo, at gumagawa ng lahat ng gawaing itinuturing na panlalaki. Pinahihintulutan din silang makipagtalik sa sinumang maibigan nila at hindi sila sinisisi ng kanilang mga asawa o tinatawag na mangangalunya; malaya ang kanilang pakikipagtalik, lalo na sa mga dayuhan. Hindi sila gumagamit ng pabango, hindi nagsusuot ng makukulay na damit ni sapatos. Sa kabilang dako, ang mga lalaki ng Gelones ay nagpapaganda, nagsusuklay, nagsusuot ng malalambot at sari-saring kulay na kasuutan, nag-aalahas ng ginto, at nagpapahid ng pabango. Hindi ito dahil kulang sila sa pagkalalaki, sapagkat sila'y lubhang mahusay sa digmaan at sa pangangaso. Gayunman, hindi lahat ng babaeng Gelone ay isinilang na may masamang Venus sa Capricorn o Aquarius; ni lahat ng kanilang lalaki ay may Venus kasama si Mars sa Aries, na ayon sa Chaldean na aral ay siyang diumano'y nagbubunga ng mga lalaking malambot at mahalay.

Kabanata 23. Mga Kaugalian ng mga Susidæ.

Bukod dito, sa Susa ay gumagamit ang mga babae ng pinakamahuhusay na pabango at nagpapalamuti ng mga hiyas at mamahaling bato. Lumalabas sila na sinusuportahan ng kanilang mga alilang babae at may higit pang karangyaan kaysa sa mga lalaki. Gayunman, hindi nila pinahahalagahan ang kahinhinan, kundi nakikipagtalik sa sinumang kanilang maibigan, maging alipin o panauhin, at pinahihintulutan sila ng kanilang mga asawa. Hindi lamang sila hindi sinisisi; sila pa ang namumuno sa kanilang mga asawa. Gayunman, hindi lahat ng babaeng Susian ay may Venus, Jupiter, at Mars sa gitna ng langit sa mga bahay ni Jupiter. Sa mas malalayong bahagi ng Silangan, kapag natuklasang ang isang batang lalaki ay ginawan ng di-likas na gawain, pinapatay siya ng kanyang mga kapatid, magulang, o ibang kamag-anak at iniiwang hindi inililibing. Sa kabilang dako, sa mga Gaul ay pinahihintulutan ng isang matandang batas na gawin ito nang hayagan sa mga batang lalaki at walang kahihiyang ikinakabit dito. Posible bang lahat ng gayong batang lalaki sa mga Gaul ay isinilang na may Lucifer kasama si Mercury sa mga bahay ni Saturno at mga hangganan ni Mars?

Kabanata 24. Iba't Ibang Kaugalian ng Iba't Ibang Bansa.

Sa mga lupain ng Britanya, maraming lalaki ang may iisang asawa; sa Parthia naman, maraming babae ang may iisang asawa. Ang bawat bahagi ng sanlibutan ay sumusunod sa sarili nitong mga kaugalian at institusyon. Walang asawa ang mga Amazon; gaya ng mga hayop, minsan sa isang taon, malapit sa vernal equinox, lumalabas sila sa kanilang lupain at nakikisama sa mga lalaki ng karatig na bansa bilang bahagi ng isang uri ng kapistahan. Kapag naglihi na sila, bumabalik sila; at kung lalaki ang isinilang, itinatapon nila, samantalang babae lamang ang kanilang pinalalaki. Dahil halos sa iisang panahon nagaganap ang mga kapanganakan, walang saysay na ipalagay na sa lahat ng lalaking sanggol ay magkapantay si Mars at Saturno ngunit hindi kailanman gayon sa kapanganakan ng mga babae; o na wala silang Mercury kasama si Venus sa sariling mga bahay nito upang magkaroon sana ng mga pintor, iskultor, o nagpapalit ng salapi; o nasa mga bahay ni Venus upang magkaroon ng mga tagagawa ng pabango, mang-aawit, o makata. Sa mga Saracen, mga taga-Upper Libya, mga Moor, mga naninirahan sa bukana ng karagatan, sa malalayong dako ng Germania, sa mga Sarmatian at Scythian, sa lahat ng bansang nasa baybayin ng Pontus, at sa pulo ng Chrysea, hindi natatagpuan ang mga nagpapalit ng salapi, iskultor, pintor, arkitekto, geometers, tragedian, o makata. Kung gayon, ayon sa ganitong pangangatwiran, dapat sabihing wala roon ang impluwensiya nina Mercury at Venus.

Kabanata 25. Hindi Genesis, Kundi Malayang Kalooban.

Sa buong sanlibutan, ang mga Mede lamang ang buong-ingat na nagtatapon ng mga taong humihinga pa upang lamunin ng mga aso; ngunit hindi naman sila araw-araw na may Mars kasama ang Buwan sa Cancer sa kanilang Genesis. Sinusunog ng mga Indiano ang kanilang mga patay, at kusang iniaalay ng mga asawa ng namatay ang sarili upang sunugin kasama nila. Ngunit hindi lahat ng babaeng Indianong sinusunog nang buhay ay may Araw sa ilalim ng lupa sa panggabing Genesis, kasama si Mars sa mga sakop ni Mars. Napakaraming German ang nagwawakas ng buhay sa pamamagitan ng pagbibigti; ngunit hindi dahil dito ay lahat sila may Buwan kasama ang Hora na napalilibutan nina Saturno at Mars. Mula sa lahat ng ito ay lumilitaw na ang takot sa batas ang naghahari sa bawat bansa, at ang kalayaan ng kaloobang inilagay sa tao ng Espiritu ang sumusunod sa mga batas. Hindi kayang pilitin ng Genesis ang mga Seres na pumatay, ang mga Brahman na kumain ng karne, ang mga Persiano na iwasan ang incest, ang mga Indiano na tumigil sa pagsunog, ang mga Mede na huwag hayaang lamunin ng mga aso ang kanilang patay, ang mga Parthian na huwag magkaroon ng maraming asawa, ang mga babae ng Mesopotamia na talikuran ang kanilang kalinisang-puri, ang mga Griyego na iwasan ang mga paligsahang atletiko, o ang mga batang Gaul na iwasang pagmalupitan ayon sa kanilang kaugalian. Ni mapipilit nito ang mga barbarong bansa na mag-aral ng mga pag-aaral ng mga Griyego. Gaya ng sinabi namin, ang bawat bansa ay sumusunod sa sarili nitong mga batas ayon sa malayang kalooban at sa higpit ng mga batas ay pinawawalang-bisa ang mga kautusan ng Genesis.

Kabanata 26. Mga Klima.

Ngunit sasabihin ng isang bihasa sa matematika na ang Genesis ay nahahati sa pitong bahagi na tinatawag nilang mga klima, at sa bawat klima ay naghahari ang isa sa pitong katawang makalangit. Sasabihin niyang ang magkakaibang batas na nabanggit natin ay hindi ibinigay ng tao kundi ng mga naghaharing bituin ayon sa kanilang kalooban, at na ang kinalulugdan ng bituin ang sinusunod ng mga tao bilang batas. Sasagutin natin ito, una, na hindi nahahati ang sanlibutan sa pitong bahagi; at ikalawa, kahit gayon pa, maraming magkakaibang batas sa iisang bahagi at iisang bansa. Kaya walang pitong batas ayon sa bilang ng mga katawang makalangit, ni labindalawa ayon sa bilang ng mga tanda, ni tatlumpu't anim ayon sa mga hati ng tig-sampung digri; sa halip, hindi mabilang ang mga batas.

Kabanata 27. Hindi Mapanghahawakan ang Aral ng mga Klima.

Dapat din nating alalahanin ang mga nabanggit na: sa iisang bansang India ay may mga kumakain ng laman ng tao at mayroon ding umiiwas maging sa karne ng tupa, ibon, at lahat ng may buhay. Ang mga Magusæi ay nakikipag-asawa sa kanilang mga ina at anak na babae hindi lamang sa Persia kundi sa bawat bansang kanilang tinitirhan ay pinananatili nila ang incestuous na kaugalian. Nabanggit din natin ang di-mabilang na mga bansang ganap na walang alam sa mga pag-aaral ng panitikan. Bukod dito, binago ng ilang pantas ang mga batas sa iba't ibang lugar, at ang ilang batas ay kusang iniwan dahil imposible itong sundin o dahil mababa ang uri nito. Madali rin nating mapatutunayan kung ilang mga pinuno ang nagbago ng mga batas at kaugalian ng mga bansang kanilang nasakop at ipinasailalim ang mga iyon sa sarili nilang mga batas. Maliwanag itong ginawa ng mga Romano, na halos buong sanlibutan at ang mga bansang dati'y namumuhay sa sari-saring batas at kaugalian ay dinala sa ilalim ng batas Romano at mga kautusang sibil. Kung gayon, ayon sa aral ng mga klima, nawalan na raw ng kanilang mga klima at bahagi ang mga bituin ng mga bansang nasakop ng Roma.

Kabanata 28. Mga Kaugalian ng mga Judio.

Magdaragdag ako ng isa pang bagay na maaaring makasapat maging sa pinakamahirap maniwala. Lahat ng Judiong namumuhay sa ilalim ng kautusan ni Moises ay tinutuli ang kanilang mga anak na lalaki sa ikawalong araw nang walang pagkukulang, at dumadaloy ang dugo ng sanggol. Ngunit walang Gentil na kusang sumasailalim dito sa ikawalong araw; at wala namang Judio na nagpapabaya rito. Paano, kung gayon, mapananatili ang paliwanag ng Genesis, gayong ang mga Judio ay nakatira sa lahat ng bahagi ng sanlibutan na kasama ng mga Gentil, at sa ikawalong araw ay sumasailalim sa pagputol na ito? Walang Gentil na gumagawa niyon, kundi sila lamang, hindi dahil sa pamimilit ng bituin o sa pag-agos ng dugo kundi dahil sa kautusan ng kanilang relihiyon. Saanman sila naroroon, kilala sa kanila ang tandang ito. Ang pagkakaroon din nila ng iisang pangalang pagkakakilanlan saanman sila naroroon—mula rin ba iyon sa Genesis? Gayundin, walang batang ipinanganak sa kanila ang inilalantad upang mamatay, at sa bawat ikapitong araw ay nagpapahinga silang lahat saanman sila naroroon; hindi naglalakbay at hindi gumagamit ng apoy. Bakit, kung gayon, walang Judiong pinipilit ng Genesis na maglakbay, magtayo, magbenta, o bumili sa araw na iyon?

Kabanata 29. Mas Makapangyarihan ang Ebanghelyo Kaysa Genesis.

Magbibigay pa ako ng higit na malakas na patunay. Tingnan ninyo: halos pitong taon pa lamang ang lumipas mula nang dumating ang matuwid at tunay na Propeta. Sa loob ng panahong iyon, ang mga tao mula sa iba't ibang bansa ay nagtungo sa Judea at, dahil sa mga tanda at kababalaghang nakita nila at sa kadakilaan ng Kanyang aral, tinanggap ang Kanyang pananampalataya. Pagbalik sa kani-kanilang bansa, itinakwil nila ang mga labag-sa-batas na ritwal ng mga Gentil at ang kanilang incestuous na mga pag-aasawa. Sa mga Parthian—gaya ng isinulat sa amin ni Tomas na nangangaral ng Ebanghelyo sa kanila—kaunti na lamang ang nagpapakasawa sa pag-aasawa ng maraming asawa. Sa mga Mede, kaunti na lamang ang nagtatapon ng kanilang patay sa mga aso; hindi na nalulugod ang mga Persiano sa pakikipagtalik sa kanilang mga ina o sa incestuous na pag-aasawa sa kanilang mga anak na babae; hindi na isinasagawa ng mga babaeng Susian ang pangangalunyang pinahintulutan sa kanila. Hindi napilit ng Genesis tungo sa mga krimen ang mga taong pinigil ng aral ng relihiyon.

Kabanata 30. Genesis at ang Katarungan ng Diyos.

Tingnan mo mismo ang usaping ating tinatalakay at mula sa kasalukuyang pangyayari ay gumawa ka ng pasya. Sa pamamagitan lamang ng balitang umabot sa pandinig ng mga tao na may Propeta raw na lumitaw sa Judea, na sa pamamagitan ng mga tanda at kababalaghan ay nagtuturo sa tao na sumamba sa iisang Diyos, naghihintay na ang lahat nang may handa at sabik na isip bago pa dumating ang panginoon kong si Pedro, upang may magpahayag sa kanila ng itinuro ng lumitaw na Propeta. Ngunit upang hindi ko pahabain nang labis ang talaan, sasabihin ko ang konklusyong dapat makuha mula sa kabuuan. Yamang ang Diyos ay matuwid at Siya mismo ang gumawa ng kalikasan ng tao, paano Niya ilalagay laban sa atin ang Genesis na pipilit sa atin na magkasala at pagkatapos ay parurusahan tayo kapag nagkasala tayo? Kaya tiyak na hindi pinarurusahan ng Diyos ang makasalanan, sa buhay mang ito o sa darating, maliban dahil alam Niyang kaya nitong magtagumpay ngunit pinabayaan nito ang tagumpay. Maging sa kasalukuyang sanlibutan ay naghahatid Siya ng kaparusahan, gaya sa mga namatay sa baha: silang lahat ay nalipol sa iisang araw, oo, sa loob ng iisang oras, bagaman malinaw na hindi sila lahat isinilang sa iisang oras ayon sa kaayusan ng genesis. Lubhang walang saysay, kung gayon, na sabihing likas lamang sa atin ang pagdurusa sa masasamang bagay kung walang naunang mga kasalanan.

Kabanata 31. Halaga ng Kaalaman.

Kaya kung nais nating maligtas, higit sa lahat ay dapat nating hanapin ang kaalaman, na nakatitiyak na kung mananatili ang ating isip sa kamangmangan, daranas tayo hindi lamang ng mga kasamaang ibinibintang sa genesis kundi maging ng anumang ibang kasamaan na naisin ng mga demonyong nasa labas natin, maliban kung salungatin ng takot sa batas at sa darating na paghuhukom ang ating mga pagnanasa at pigilin ang puwersa ng pagkakasala. Maging ang takot sa tao ay gumagawa ng maraming mabuti at gayundin ng maraming masama na hindi nalalaman ng Genesis, gaya ng naipakita na natin. Sa tatlong paraan, kung gayon, napapasailalim ang ating isip sa kamalian: sa masasamang kaugaliang dumarating sa atin; sa mga pitang likas na pinupukaw ng katawan; at sa mga bagay na ipinipilit sa atin ng salungat na mga kapangyarihan. Ngunit nasa kalikasan ng isip ang kakayahang tumutol at makipaglaban sa mga ito kapag sumikat dito ang kaalaman sa katotohanan. Sa kaalamang ito ay naibibigay ang takot sa darating na paghuhukom, isang angkop na tagapamahala ng isip na makapagbabalik dito mula sa bangin ng pita. Na ang mga bagay na ito ay nasa ating kapangyarihan ay sapat nang naipahayag.

Kabanata 32. Matitigas na Katotohanan.

Pagkatapos ay sinabi ko: Ngayon, matanda, kung mayroon kang maitutugon sa mga bagay na ito, magsalita ka. Sumagot ang matanda: Lubos mong naipagtanggol ang iyong panig, anak; ngunit gaya ng sinabi ko noong una, pinipigilan ako ng sarili kong kaalaman sa mga nangyari sa akin na sumang-ayon sa napakagandang pahayag mo. Alam ko ang sarili kong Genesis at ang sa aking asawa, at alam kong nangyari sa bawat isa sa amin ang mga bagay na itinakda ng aming Genesis. Hindi ako mailalayo ng mga salita mula sa napatunayan ko sa mga pangyayari at gawa. Dahil nakikita kong napakahusay mong bihasa sa ganitong pag-aaral, pakinggan mo ang horoscope ng aking asawa at makikita mo ang pagkakaayos na ang kinalabasan ay naganap nga. May Mars siya kasama si Venus sa ibabaw ng gitna, at ang Buwan ay lumulubog sa mga bahay ni Mars at sa mga hangganan ni Saturno. Ang ganitong ayos, ayon sa aral, ay nagdadala sa mga babae sa pangangalunya, sa pag-ibig sa sariling alipin, at sa pagtatapos ng kanilang buhay sa banyagang paglalakbay at sa tubig. At gayon nga ang nangyari. Umibig siya sa kanyang alipin at, dahil natatakot sa panganib at kahihiyan, tumakas kasama nito. Sa ibang lupain niya pinagbigyan ang kanyang pag-ibig at namatay siya sa dagat.

Kabanata 33. Papalapit na Pagkakakilanlan.

Sumagot ako: Paano mo nalaman na nakisama siya sa kanyang alipin sa ibang lupain at namatay kasama nito? Sinabi ng matanda: Alam ko iyon nang buong katiyakan; hindi dahil alam kong nagpakasal siya sa alipin—sa katunayan, hindi ko man lamang natuklasan noon na mahal niya ito. Ngunit pagkatapos niyang umalis, sinabi sa akin ng aking kapatid ang buong salaysay. Sinabi niyang una siyang inibig ng aking asawa; ngunit bilang marangal na kapatid ay ayaw niyang dungisan ng incest ang higaan ng kanyang kapatid. Dahil natatakot sa akin ang aking asawa at hindi niya matiis ang kahihiyang ibubunga nito—bagaman hindi raw siya dapat sisihin sa bagay na ipinilit ng kanyang Genesis—nagkunwari siyang nanaginip. Sinabi niya sa akin: ‘May tumayo sa tabi ko sa isang pangitain at inutusan akong agad lisanin ang lungsod kasama ang aking kambal na anak.’ Nang marinig ko iyon, dahil nag-aalala ako sa kaligtasan niya at ng aming mga anak, agad ko silang pinaalis, ngunit pinanatili ko sa akin ang bunso. Sapagkat sinabi niyang iyon ang ipinahintulot ng nagbabala sa kanya sa panaginip.

Kabanata 34. Ang Kabilang Panig ng Salaysay.

Nang maunawaan kong maaaring siya ang aking ama, ako, si Clemente, ay napaiyak nang labis. Handa na ring sumugod ang aking mga kapatid upang ihayag ang katotohanan, ngunit pinigil sila ni Pedro at sinabi: Manahimik muna kayo hanggang bigyan ko kayo ng pahintulot. Pagkatapos ay sinabi ni Pedro sa matanda: Ano ang pangalan ng bunso mong anak? Sumagot siya: Clemente. Sinabi ni Pedro: Kung ngayong araw ay maibabalik ko sa iyo ang iyong napakalinis na asawa at ang tatlo mong anak, maniniwala ka bang kayang daigin ng isang mahinhing isip ang di-makatuwirang mga bugso ng pita, at na totoo ang lahat ng sinabi namin at walang kapangyarihan ang Genesis? Sumagot ang matanda: Kung paanong imposibleng magawa mo ang ipinangako mo, gayon din imposible na may mangyari na hiwalay sa Genesis. Sinabi ni Pedro: Nais kong maging mga saksi ang lahat ng naririto na ngayong araw ay ibibigay ko sa iyo ang iyong asawa, na nabuhay nang buong kalinisang-puri, kasama ang tatlo mong anak. At bilang tanda nito, pakinggan mo: higit kong alam kaysa sa iyo ang buong salaysay. Isasalaysay ko nang sunod-sunod ang lahat ng pangyayari upang malaman mo ang mga iyon at matutuhan din ng lahat ng naririto.

Kabanata 35. Mga Pagbubunyag.

Pagkasabi nito, humarap si Pedro sa karamihan at nagsimula: Ang taong nakikita ninyo rito na nakasuot ng maralitang damit ay mamamayan ng Roma at nagmula sa angkan mismo ni Cesar. Ang pangalan niya ay Faustinianus. Napangasawa niya ang isang babaeng may mataas na katayuan na nagngangalang Matthidia. Nagkaroon sila ng tatlong anak na lalaki, dalawa sa mga iyon ay kambal; at ang bunsong nagngangalang Clemente ay ang lalaking ito. Itinuro niya ako ng daliri. Pagkatapos ay sinabi niya: At ang mga kambal niyang anak ay ang dalawang lalaking ito, sina Niceta at Aquila; ang isa ay dating tinatawag na Faustinus at ang isa naman ay Faustus. Pagkarinig ng matanda sa aming mga pangalan, nanghina ang lahat ng kanyang mga biyas at nawalan siya ng malay. Kami namang kanyang mga anak ay sumugod, niyakap at hinalikan siya, sa takot na baka hindi na siya magising. Samantala, ang mga tao ay nalipos ng matinding pagkamangha.

Kabanata 36. Mga Bagong Pagbubunyag.

Ngunit inutusan kami ni Pedro na tumigil muna sa pagyakap sa aming ama baka lalo namin siyang mapahamak. Hinawakan niya ang kamay nito at ibinangon na parang mula sa mahimbing na tulog, at unti-unting ibinalik ang ulirat. Pagkatapos ay ipinaliwanag niya sa kanya ang buong pangyayari ayon sa tunay na nangyari: kung paanong umibig kay Matthidia ang kapatid ni Faustinianus; kung paanong dahil sa kanyang matinding kahinhinan ay ayaw ni Matthidia na sabihin sa asawa ang labag-sa-batas na pag-ibig ng kapatid nito, upang hindi siya magpasimula ng alitan sa magkapatid at magdala ng kahihiyan sa pamilya; kung paanong matalino niyang nagkunwaring nanaginip na inuutusan siyang lisanin ang lungsod kasama ang kambal at iwan ang bunsong anak sa ama; kung paanong sa kanilang paglalayag ay nawasak ang barko sa lakas ng bagyo; kung paanong itinapon si Matthidia ng alon sa isang bato sa pulong Antaradus, samantalang dinukot naman ng mga pirata ang kanyang kambal at dinala sa Cesarea, kung saan ipinagbili sila sa isang banal na babae na itinuring silang mga anak, pinalaki, at pinag-aral nang marangal; kung paanong binago ng mga pirata ang kanilang mga pangalan at tinawag ang isa na Niceta at ang isa na Aquila; kung paanong kalaunan, dahil sa kanilang magkakatulad na pag-aaral at kakilala, napalapit sila kay Simon, ngunit iniwan siya nang makitang salamangkero at manlilinlang at lumapit kay Zaqueo; at kung paanong sa dakong huli ay nakasama sila ni Pedro. Isinalaysay din niya kung paanong si Clemente, sa paghahanap ng katotohanan, ay umalis sa lungsod, nakilala si Bernabe, nakarating sa Cesarea, naging kilala ni Pedro, sumunod sa kanya, at naturuan sa pananampalataya; kung paanong natagpuan at nakilala niya ang kanyang ina na namamalimos sa Antaradus at nagalak ang buong pulo; kung paanong nanirahan si Matthidia sa isang napakalinis na babaeng nagpatuloy sa kanya at kung paanong pinagaling ni Pedro ang babaeng iyon; kung paanong naging bukas-palad si Clemente sa mga naging mabuti sa kanyang ina; at kung paanong nang tanungin nina Niceta at Aquila kung sino ang di-kilalang babae at marinig ang buong salaysay mula kay Clemente, sumigaw silang sila ang kambal na sina Faustinus at Faustus at isinalaysay ang lahat ng nangyari sa kanila. Pagkatapos, sa maingat na paggabay ni Pedro, ipinakilala silang muli sa kanilang ina upang hindi ito mapinsala ng biglaang labis na galak.

Kabanata 37. Isa Pang Pagkakakilanlan.

Habang isinasalaysay ni Pedro ang mga bagay na ito sa pandinig ng matanda, lubhang nalulugod ang karamihan sa kuwento, anupa't napapaiyak sila sa pagkamangha sa mga pangyayari at sa habag sa mga pagdurusang dumarating sa buhay ng tao. Samantala, hindi ko alam kung paano, ngunit nabalitaan ng aking ina na nakilala na ang aking ama. Humahangos siyang sumugod sa gitna namin, sumisigaw: Nasaan ang aking asawa, ang panginoon kong si Faustinianus, na napakatagal nang nagdurusa at naglalakbay sa iba't ibang lungsod sa paghahanap sa akin? Habang pasigaw niya itong sinasabi na parang wala sa sarili at tumitingin sa paligid, tumakbo sa kanya ang matanda at niyakap siya nang mahigpit na may maraming luha. Habang nangyayari ito, hiniling ni Pedro sa karamihan na umalis na, sapagkat hindi na angkop na manatili pa sila at dapat bigyan ang mag-asawa ng pagkakataong magkita nang mas pribado. Ngunit bukas, sabi niya, kung nais ng sinuman sa inyo, magtipon kayo upang makinig sa salita.

Kabanata 38. Mga Anghel na Hindi Nakilala.

Pagkasabi nito ni Pedro, naghiwa-hiwalay ang karamihan. Nang kami naman ay papunta na sa aming tuluyan, sinabi sa amin ng may-ari ng bahay: Nakakahiya at masama na ang gayong mga dakilang tao ay manatili sa bahay-panuluyan gayong halos walang laman ang buong bahay ko, marami akong handang higaan, at narito ang lahat ng kailangan ninyo. Ngunit tumanggi si Pedro. Nang magkagayo'y lumuhod sa harap niya ang asawa ng may-ari kasama ang kanilang mga anak at nakiusap: Nakikiusap ako sa iyo, manatili kayo sa amin. Gayunman ay hindi pa rin pumayag si Pedro hanggang sa ang anak na babae ng mag-asawa—na matagal nang pinahihirapan ng isang maruming espiritu, nakagapos sa mga tanikala, at nakakulong sa isang silid—ay mapalaya sa demonyo. Nabuksan ang pinto ng silid at lumapit siya na taglay pa ang mga tanikala, nagpatirapa sa paanan ni Pedro, at nagsabi: Nararapat, panginoon ko, na ipagdiwang mo rito ngayon ang kapistahan ng aking paglaya at huwag mo akong palungkutin ni ang aking mga magulang. Nang tanungin ni Pedro kung ano ang kahulugan ng mga tanikala at ng kanyang sinabi, ang mga magulang, na labis ang galak sa di-inaasahang paggaling ng kanilang anak, ay natigilan sa pagkamangha at hindi makapagsalita. Ngunit sinabi ng mga alilang naroroon: Ang dalagang ito ay sinapian ng demonyo mula pa noong ikapitong taon niya. Pinuputol, kinakagat, at nilalapa niya ang sinumang nagtatangkang lumapit, at hindi siya tumigil sa loob ng dalawampung taon. Walang nakapagpagaling o makalapit sa kanya; marami siyang napinsala at ang ilan ay napatay, sapagkat higit siyang malakas kaysa sa sinumang lalaki, walang alinlangang pinalalakas ng demonyo. Ngunit ngayon, gaya ng nakikita ninyo, tumakas ang demonyo sa inyong presensiya; nabuksan ang mga pintuang matibay na nakasara; at nakatayo siya ngayon sa harap ninyo na malinaw ang isip, hinihiling na gawing masaya ang araw ng kanyang paggaling para sa kanya at sa kanyang mga magulang at manatili kayo rito. Pagkasabi nito ng isang alipin, kusang nakalag ang mga tanikala sa mga kamay at paa ng dalaga. Nang matiyak ni Pedro na sa pamamagitan ng kanyang pagdating ay naibalik sa dalaga ang kalusugan, pumayag siyang manatili. Inutusan din niyang papuntahin doon ang mga naiwan sa tuluyan kasama ang kanyang asawa. Bawat isa sa amin ay binigyan ng sariling silid; kumain kami ayon sa aming nakagawian, nagbigay ng papuri sa Diyos, at pagkatapos ay natulog sa kani-kaniyang silid.
