Chapter I. Mattidia is Baptized in the Sea

Much earlier than usual Peter awoke, and came to us, and awaking us, said: Let Faustinus and Faustinianus, along with Clement and the household, accompany me, that we may go to some sheltered spot by the sea, and there be able to baptize her without attracting observation. Accordingly, when we had come to the sea-shore, he baptized her between some rocks, which supplied a place at once free from wind and dust. But we brothers, along with our brother and some others, retired because of the women and bathed, and coming again to the women, we took them along with us, and thus we went to a secret place and prayed. Then Peter, on account of the multitude, sent the women on before, ordering them to go to their lodging by another way, and he permitted us alone of the men to accompany our mother and the rest of the women. We went then to our lodging, and while waiting for Peter's arrival, we conversed with each other. Peter came several hours after, and breaking the bread for the Eucharist, and putting salt upon it, he gave it first to our mother, and, after her, to us her sons. And thus we took food along with her and blessed God.

Chapter II. The Reason of Peter's Lateness

Then, at length, Peter seeing that the multitude had entered, sat down, and bidding us sit down beside him, he related first of all why he had sent us on before him after the baptism, and why he himself had been late in returning. He said that the following was the reason: At the time that you came up, he says, an old man, a workman, entered along with you, concealing himself out of curiosity. He had watched us before, as he himself afterwards confessed, in order to see what we were doing when we entered into the sheltered place, and then he came out secretly and followed us. And coming up to me at a convenient place, and addressing me, he said, 'For a long time I have been following you and wishing to talk with you, but I was afraid that you might be angry with me, as if I were instigated by curiosity; but now I shall tell you, if you please, what I think is the truth.' And I replied, 'Tell us what you think is good, and we shall approve your conduct, even should what you say not be really good, since with a good purpose you have been anxious to state what you deem to be good.'

Chapter III. The Old Man Does Not Believe in God or Providence

The old man began to speak as follows: 'When I saw you after you had bathed in the sea retire into the secret place, I went up and secretly watched what might be your object in entering into a secret place, and when I saw you pray, I retired; but taking pity on you, I waited that I might speak with you when you came out, and prevail on you not to be led astray. For there is neither God nor providence; but all things are subject to Genesis. Of this I am fully assured in consequence of what I have myself endured, having for a long time made a careful study of the science. Do not therefore be deceived, my child. For whether you pray or not, you must endure what is assigned to you by Genesis. For if prayers could have done anything or any good, I myself should now be in better circumstances. And now, unless my needy garments mislead you, you will not refuse to believe what I say. I was once in affluent circumstances; I sacrificed much to the gods, I gave liberally to the needy; and yet, though I prayed and acted piously, I was not able to escape my destiny.' And I said: 'What are the calamities you have endured?' And he answered: 'I need not tell you now; perhaps at the end you shall learn who I am, and who are my parents, and into what straitened circumstances I have fallen. But at present I wish you to become fully assured that everything is subject to Genesis.'

Chapter IV. Peter's Arguments Against Genesis

And I said: 'If all things are subject to Genesis, and you are fully convinced that this is the case, your thoughts and advice are contrary to your own opinion. For if it is impossible even to think in opposition to Genesis, why do you toil in vain, advising me to do what cannot be done? Yea, moreover, even if Genesis subsists, do not make haste to prevail on me not to worship Him who is also Lord of the stars, by whose wish that a thing should not take place, that thing becomes an impossibility. For always that which is subject must obey that which rules. As far, however, as the worship of the common gods is concerned, that is superfluous, if Genesis has sway. For neither does anything happen contrary to what seems good to fate, nor are they themselves able to do anything, since they are subject to their own universal Genesis. If Genesis exists, there is this objection to it, that that which is not first has the rule; or, in other words, the uncreated cannot be subject, for the uncreated, as being uncreated, has nothing that is older than itself.'

Chapter V. Practical Refutation of Genesis

While we were thus talking, a great multitude gathered round us. And then I looked to the multitude, and said: 'I and my tribe have had handed down to us from our ancestors the worship of God, and we have a commandment to give no heed to Genesis, I mean to the science of astrology; and therefore I gave no attention to it. For this reason I have no skill in astrology, but I shall state that in which I have skill. Since I am unable to refute Genesis by an appeal to the science which relates to Genesis, I wish to prove in another way that the affairs of this world are managed by a providence, and that each one will receive reward or punishment according to his actions. Whether he shall do so now or hereafter, is a matter of no consequence to me; all I affirm is, that each one without doubt will reap the fruit of his deeds. The proof that there is no Genesis is this. If any one of you present has been deprived of eyes, or has his hand maimed, or his foot lame, or some other part of the body wrong, and if it is utterly incurable, and entirely beyond the range of the medical profession,—a case, indeed, which not even the astrologers profess to cure, for no such cure has taken place within the lapse of a vast period,—yet I praying to God will cure it, although it could never have been set right by Genesis. Since this is so, do not they sin who blaspheme the God that fashioned all things?' And the old man answered: 'Is it then blasphemy to say that all things are subject to Genesis?' And I replied: 'Most certainly it is. For if all the sins of men, and all their acts of impiety and licentiousness, owe their origin to the stars, and if the stars have been appointed by God to do this work, so as to be the efficient causes of all evils, then the sins of all are traced up to Him who placed Genesis in the stars.'

Chapter VI. The Old Man Opposes His Personal Experience to the Argument of Peter

And the old man answered: 'You have spoken truly, and yet, notwithstanding all your incomparable demonstration, I am prevented from yielding assent by my own personal knowledge. For I was an astrologer, and dwelt first at Rome; and then forming a friendship with one who was of the family of Cæsar, I ascertained accurately the genesis of himself and his wife. And tracing their history, I find all the deeds actually accomplished in exact accordance with their genesis, and therefore I cannot yield to your argument. For the arrangement of her genesis was that which makes women commit adultery, fall in love with their own slaves, and perish abroad in the water. And this actually took place; for she fell in love with her own slave, and not being able to bear the reproach, she fled with him, hurried to a foreign land, shared his bed, and perished in the sea.'

Chapter VII. The Old Man Tells His Story

And I answered: 'How then do you know that she who fled and took up her residence in a foreign land married the slave, and marrying him died?' And the old man said: 'I am quite sure that this is true, not indeed that she married him, for I did not know even that she fell in love with him; but after her departure, a brother of her husband's told me the whole story of her passion, and how he acted as an honourable man, and did not, as being his brother, wish to pollute his couch, and how she the wretched woman (for she is not blameable, inasmuch as she was compelled to do and suffer all this in consequence of Genesis) longed for him, and yet stood in awe of him and his reproaches, and how she devised a dream, whether true or false I cannot tell; for he stated that she said, Some one in a vision stood by me, and ordered me to leave the city of the Romans immediately with my children. But her husband being anxious that she should be saved with his sons, sent them immediately to Athens for their education, accompanied by their mother and slaves, while he kept the third and youngest son with himself, for he who gave the warning in the dream permitted this son to remain with his father. And when a long time had elapsed, during which he received no letters from her, he himself sent frequently to Athens, and at length took me, as the truest of all his friends, and went in search of her. And much did I exert myself along with him in the course of our travels with all eagerness; for I remembered that, in the old times of his prosperity, he had given me a share of all he had and loved me above all his friends. At length we set sail from Rome itself, and so we arrived in these parts of Syria, and we landed at Seleucia, and not many days after we had landed he died of a broken heart. But I came here, and have procured my livelihood from that day till this by the work of my hands.'

Chapter VIII. The Old Man Gives Information in Regard to Faustus the Father of Clement

When the old man had thus spoken, I knew from what he said that the old man who he stated had died, was no other than your father. I did not wish, however, to communicate your circumstances to him until I should confer with you. But I ascertained where his lodging was, and I pointed out mine to him; and to make sure that my conjecture was right, I put this one question to him: 'What was the name of the old man?' And he said, 'Faustus.' 'And what were the names of his twin sons?' And he answered, 'Faustinus and Faustinianus.' 'What was the name of the third son?' He said, 'Clement.' 'What was their mother's name?' He said, 'Mattidia.' Accordingly, from compassion, I shed tears along with him, and, dismissing the multitudes, I came to you, in order that I might take counsel with you after we had partaken of food together. But I did not wish to disclose the matter to you before we had partaken of food, lest perchance you should be overcome by sorrow, and continue sad on the day of baptism, when even angels rejoice. At these statements of Peter we all fell a weeping along with our mother. But he beholding us in tears, said: Now let each one of you, through fear of God, bear bravely what has been said: for certainly it was not today that your father died, but long ago, as you conjecturing said.

Chapter IX. Faustus Himself Appears

When Peter said this, our mother could no longer endure it, but cried out, Alas! My husband! loving us, you died by your own decision, while we are still alive, see the light, and have just partaken of food. This one scream had not yet ceased, when, lo! The old man came in, and at the same time wishing to inquire into the cause of the cry, he looked on the woman and said, What does this mean? Whom do I see? And going up to her, and looking at her, and being looked at more carefully, he embraced her. But they were like to die through the sudden joy, and wishing to speak to each other, they could not get the power in consequence of their unsatisfied joy, for they were seized with speechlessness. But not long after, our mother said to him: I now have you, Faustus, in every way the dearest being to me. How then are you alive, when we heard a short time ago that you were dead? But these are our sons, Faustinus, Faustinianus, and Clement. And when she said this, we all three fell on him, and kissed him, and in rather an indistinct way we recalled his form to our memory.

Chapter X. Faustus Explains His Narrative to Peter

Peter seeing this, said: Are you Faustus, the husband of this woman, and the father of her children? And he said: I am. And Peter said: How, then, did you relate to me your own history as if it were another's; telling me of your toils, and sorrow, and burial? And our father answered: Being of the family of Cæsar, and not wishing to be discovered, I devised the narrative in another's name, in order that it might not be perceived who I was. For I knew that, if I were recognised, the governors in the place would learn this, and recall me to gratify Cæsar, and would bestow upon me that former prosperity to which I had formerly bidden adieu with all the resolution I could summon. For I could not give myself up to a luxurious life when I had pronounced the strongest condemnation on myself, because I believed that I had been the cause of death to those who were loved by me.

Chapter XI. Discussion on Genesis

And Peter said: You did this according to your resolution. But in regard to Genesis, were you merely playing a part when you affirmed it, or were you in earnest in asserting that it existed? Our father said: I will not speak falsely to you. I was in earnest when I maintained that Genesis existed. For I am not uninitiated in the science; on the contrary, I associated with one who is the best of the astrologers, an Egyptian of the name of Annubion, who became my friend in the commencement of my travels, and disclosed to me the death of my wife and children. And Peter said: Are you not now convinced by facts, that the doctrine of Genesis has no firm foundation? And my father answered: I must lay before you all the ideas that occur to my mind, that listening to them I may understand your refutation of them. I know, indeed, that astrologers both make many mistakes, and frequently speak the truth. I suspect, therefore, that they speak the truth so far as they are accurately acquainted with the science, and that their mistakes are the result of ignorance; so that I conjecture that the science has a firm foundation, but that the astrologers themselves speak what is false solely on account of ignorance, because they cannot know all things with absolute accuracy. And Peter answered: Consider whether their speaking of the truth is not accidental, and whether they do not make their declarations without knowing the matters accurately. For it must by all means happen that, when many prophecies are uttered, some of them should come true. And the old man said: How, then, is it possible to be fully convinced of this, whether the science of Genesis has a sure foundation or not?

Chapter XII. Clement Undertakes the Discussion

When both were silent, I said: Since I know accurately the science, but our lord and our father are not in this condition, I should like if Annubion himself were here, to have a discussion with him in the presence of my father. For thus would the matter be able to become public, when one practically acquainted with the subject has held the discussion with one equally informed. And our father answered: Where, then, is it possible to fall in with Annubion? And Peter said: In Antioch, for I learn that Simon Magus is there, whose inseparable companion Annubion is. When, then, we go there, if we come upon them, the discussion can take place. And so, when we had discussed many subjects, and rejoiced at the recognition and given thanks to God, evening came down upon us, and we turned to sleep.


Kabanata I. Binautismuhan si Mattidia sa Dagat

Mas maaga kaysa karaniwan nagising si Pedro at lumapit sa amin, at pagkagising sa amin ay sinabi: Sumama sa akin sina Faustinus at Faustinianus, kasama si Clemente at ang sambahayan, upang pumunta tayo sa isang ligtas at tagong lugar sa tabi ng dagat at doon ay mabautismuhan natin siya nang hindi nakaaakit ng pansin. Kaya nang makarating kami sa dalampasigan, binautismuhan niya siya sa pagitan ng ilang bato na naglaan ng lugar na ligtas kapwa sa hangin at alikabok. Ngunit kaming magkakapatid, kasama ang aming kapatid at ilan pang iba, ay lumayo dahil sa mga babae at naligo. Pagbalik namin sa mga babae, isinama namin sila at nagtungo kami sa isang lihim na lugar at nanalangin. Pagkatapos, dahil sa dami ng tao, pinauna ni Pedro ang mga babae at inutusan silang bumalik sa kanilang matutuluyan sa ibang daan; at sa mga lalaki ay kami lamang ang pinahintulutan niyang samahan ang aming ina at ang iba pang mga babae. Pumunta kami sa aming matutuluyan at, habang hinihintay ang pagdating ni Pedro, nag-usap-usap kami. Makalipas ang ilang oras ay dumating si Pedro at, nang hatiin ang tinapay para sa Eukaristiya at lagyan iyon ng asin, ibinigay muna niya iyon sa aming ina at pagkatapos ay sa aming mga anak niya. Sa gayon ay kumain kami kasama niya at pinagpala ang Diyos.

Kabanata II. Ang Dahilan ng Pagkahuli ni Pedro

Sa wakas, nang makita ni Pedro na nakapasok na ang karamihan, umupo siya at pinaupo kami sa tabi niya. Una sa lahat ay isinalaysay niya kung bakit niya kami pinauna pagkatapos ng bautismo at kung bakit siya mismo ay nahuling bumalik. Sinabi niyang ito ang dahilan: Noong umaahon kayo, sabi niya, may isang matandang lalaki, isang manggagawa, na pumasok kasabay ninyo at nagkubli dahil sa pag-uusisa. Matagal na pala niya tayong minamasdan, gaya ng inamin niya kalaunan, upang makita kung ano ang ginagawa natin kapag pumapasok sa tagong lugar; pagkatapos ay palihim siyang lumabas at sumunod sa atin. Nang maabutan niya ako sa isang angkop na lugar, kinausap niya ako at sinabi, ‘Matagal na kitang sinusundan at nais kitang makausap, ngunit natatakot akong baka magalit ka sa akin na para bang udyok lamang ako ng pag-uusisa; ngunit ngayon, kung mamarapatin mo, sasabihin ko sa iyo ang inaakala kong katotohanan.’ At sumagot ako, ‘Sabihin mo sa amin ang inaakala mong mabuti, at sasang-ayunan namin ang iyong ginawa kahit ang sasabihin mo ay hindi pala tunay na mabuti, sapagkat sa mabuting layunin ay sabik mong ipahayag ang inaakala mong mabuti.’

Kabanata III. Hindi Naniniwala ang Matandang Lalaki sa Diyos o sa Providensiya

Nagsimulang magsalita ang matandang lalaki nang ganito: ‘Nang makita ko kayong pagkatapos maligo sa dagat ay pumasok sa lihim na lugar, lumapit ako at palihim na minatyagan kung ano ang layunin ninyo sa pagpasok sa isang tagong lugar; at nang makita kong nananalangin kayo, umalis ako. Ngunit dahil naawa ako sa inyo, naghintay ako upang makausap kayo sa paglabas ninyo at hikayatin kayong huwag maligaw. Sapagkat walang Diyos at walang providensiya; lahat ng bagay ay saklaw ng Genesis. Lubos akong nakatitiyak dito dahil sa mga dinanas ko mismo, yamang matagal kong masusing pinag-aralan ang agham na ito. Kaya huwag kang padaya, anak ko. Manalangin ka man o hindi, kailangan mong tiisin ang itinakda sa iyo ng Genesis. Sapagkat kung may anumang magagawa o anumang mabuting maidudulot ang mga panalangin, mas mabuti sana ang kalagayan ko ngayon. At ngayon, malibang malinlang ka ng aking maralitang kasuotan, hindi mo tatanggihang paniwalaan ang sinasabi ko. Dati akong nasa masaganang kalagayan; marami akong inihahandog sa mga diyos, bukas-palad akong nagbibigay sa mga nangangailangan; gayunman, bagaman nanalangin ako at namuhay nang maka-Diyos, hindi ko natakasan ang aking kapalaran.’ At sinabi ko, ‘Ano ang mga kapahamakang dinanas mo?’ Sumagot siya, ‘Hindi ko kailangang sabihin sa iyo ngayon; marahil sa wakas ay malalaman mo kung sino ako, kung sino ang aking mga magulang, at sa anong hirap ako bumagsak. Ngunit sa ngayon ay nais kong lubos kang makatiyak na ang lahat ay saklaw ng Genesis.’

Kabanata IV. Mga Pangangatwiran ni Pedro Laban sa Genesis

At sinabi ko: ‘Kung ang lahat ay saklaw ng Genesis at lubos kang kumbinsidong gayon nga, salungat sa sarili mong opinyon ang iyong mga iniisip at payo. Sapagkat kung imposibleng mag-isip man lamang laban sa Genesis, bakit ka nagpapakahirap nang walang saysay sa pagpapayo sa akin na gawin ang hindi maaaring gawin? Higit pa rito, kahit pa umiiral ang Genesis, huwag kang magmadaling hikayatin akong huwag sambahin Siya na Panginoon din ng mga bituin, na kapag ninais Niyang huwag mangyari ang isang bagay ay nagiging imposible itong mangyari. Sapagkat ang napasasakop ay laging kailangang sumunod sa naghahari. Tungkol naman sa pagsamba sa mga karaniwang diyos, iyon ay walang kabuluhan kung naghahari ang Genesis. Sapagkat walang nangyayari laban sa itinuturing na mabuti ng kapalaran, at sila mismo ay walang magagawa yamang saklaw din sila ng kanilang pangkalahatang Genesis. Kung umiiral ang Genesis, ito ang pagtutol laban dito: ang hindi unang umiiral ang siyang naghahari; o, sa ibang pananalita, ang hindi nilikha ay hindi maaaring mapasailalim, sapagkat ang hindi nilikha, yamang hindi nilikha, ay walang anumang mas matanda kaysa sa sarili nito.’

Kabanata V. Praktikal na Pagpapabulaan sa Genesis

Habang nag-uusap kami nang ganito, isang malaking karamihan ang nagtipon sa paligid namin. Pagkatapos ay tumingin ako sa karamihan at sinabi: ‘Ako at ang aking angkan ay tumanggap mula sa aming mga ninuno ng pagsamba sa Diyos, at mayroon kaming utos na huwag pansinin ang Genesis—ang ibig kong sabihin ay ang agham ng astrolohiya—kaya hindi ko ito pinag-aralan. Dahil dito ay wala akong kasanayan sa astrolohiya, ngunit sasabihin ko ang bagay na may kasanayan ako. Yamang hindi ko kayang pabulaanan ang Genesis sa pamamagitan ng agham na nauukol sa Genesis, nais kong patunayan sa ibang paraan na ang mga gawain ng sanlibutang ito ay pinamamahalaan ng isang providensiya at na ang bawat isa ay tatanggap ng gantimpala o kaparusahan ayon sa kanyang mga gawa. Kung tatanggapin niya iyon ngayon o sa hinaharap ay walang malaking kabuluhan sa akin; ang pinaninindigan ko lamang ay tiyak na aanihin ng bawat isa ang bunga ng kanyang mga gawa. Ito ang patunay na walang Genesis. Kung may sinuman sa inyong naririto na nawalan ng mga mata, o napinsala ang kamay, o pilay ang paa, o may ibang bahagi ng katawan na mali, at iyon ay lubos na hindi magamot at ganap na lampas sa kakayahan ng medisina—isang kalagayan na maging ang mga astrologo ay hindi nag-aangking kayang pagalingin, sapagkat walang ganitong paggaling na nangyari sa napakahabang panahon—gayunman, sa pamamagitan ng aking pananalangin sa Diyos ay pagagalingin ko iyon, bagaman hindi iyon kailanman maitutuwid ng Genesis. Kung gayon nga ito, hindi ba nagkakasala ang mga namumusong sa Diyos na humubog sa lahat ng bagay?’ Sumagot ang matandang lalaki: ‘Pamumusong ba, kung gayon, ang sabihin na ang lahat ng bagay ay saklaw ng Genesis?’ Sumagot ako: ‘Tiyak na gayon. Sapagkat kung ang lahat ng kasalanan ng mga tao at lahat ng kanilang gawa ng kawalang-kabanalan at kahalayan ay nagmumula sa mga bituin, at kung itinalaga ng Diyos ang mga bituin upang gawin ang gawaing ito bilang mabisang sanhi ng lahat ng kasamaan, kung gayon ang mga kasalanan ng lahat ay mauuwi sa Kanya na naglagay ng Genesis sa mga bituin.’

Kabanata VI. Ipinantapat ng Matandang Lalaki ang Sarili Niyang Karanasan sa Pangangatwiran ni Pedro

At sumagot ang matandang lalaki: ‘Tama ang sinabi mo, ngunit sa kabila ng walang-kapantay mong pagpapakita, nahahadlangan akong sumang-ayon dahil sa sarili kong kaalaman mula sa karanasan. Sapagkat ako'y dating astrologo at nanirahan muna sa Roma; pagkatapos, nang maging kaibigan ko ang isang lalaking kabilang sa pamilya ni Cesar, tumpak kong nalaman ang genesis niya at ng kanyang asawa. At nang siyasatin ko ang kanilang kasaysayan, natagpuan kong ang lahat ng gawaing aktuwal na nangyari ay eksaktong naaayon sa kanilang genesis, kaya hindi ako makasunod sa iyong pangangatwiran. Sapagkat ang kaayusan ng kanyang genesis ay yaong nagpapagawa sa mga babae ng pangangalunya, nagpapaibig sa kanila sa sarili nilang mga alipin, at nagpapahamak sa kanila sa tubig sa ibang lupain. At aktuwal ngang nangyari ito; sapagkat umibig siya sa sarili niyang alipin at, dahil hindi niya matiis ang kahihiyan, tumakas siyang kasama nito, nagmadali sa isang banyagang lupain, nakihiga sa kanya, at napahamak sa dagat.’

Kabanata VII. Isinalaysay ng Matandang Lalaki ang Kanyang Kuwento

At sumagot ako: ‘Paano mo, kung gayon, nalaman na ang babaeng tumakas at nanirahan sa banyagang lupain ay nagpakasal sa alipin at, matapos pakasalan siya, ay namatay?’ Sinabi ng matandang lalaki: ‘Lubos akong nakatitiyak na totoo ito—hindi nga na pinakasalan niya ito, sapagkat hindi ko man lamang nalaman na umibig siya rito; ngunit pagkatapos ng kanyang pag-alis, sinabi sa akin ng isang kapatid ng kanyang asawa ang buong kuwento ng kanyang pagnanasa, at kung paanong kumilos siyang isang marangal na lalaki at, dahil kapatid siya ng kanyang asawa, ay ayaw niyang dungisan ang higaan nito; at kung paanong ang kaawa-awang babae—sapagkat hindi siya masisisi, yamang napilitan siyang gawin at danasin ang lahat ng ito dahil sa Genesis—ay nanabik sa kanya ngunit natakot din sa kanya at sa kanyang mga pagsaway; at kung paanong gumawa siya ng isang panaginip, kung totoo man o huwad ay hindi ko masabi, sapagkat sinabi niyang sinabi ng babae, May isang tumayo sa tabi ko sa isang pangitain at inutusan akong agad lisanin ang lungsod ng mga Romano kasama ang aking mga anak. Ngunit ang kanyang asawa, dahil sabik na mailigtas siya kasama ng kanyang mga anak, ay agad silang ipinadala sa Atenas para sa kanilang pag-aaral, kasama ang kanilang ina at mga alipin, samantalang pinanatili niya sa sarili ang ikatlo at bunsong anak, sapagkat pinahintulutan ng nagbigay ng babala sa panaginip na manatili ang anak na ito sa kanyang ama. At nang lumipas ang mahabang panahon na wala siyang natatanggap na mga liham mula sa kanya, paulit-ulit siyang nagpadala sa Atenas at sa wakas ay isinama ako, bilang pinakatapat sa lahat ng kanyang mga kaibigan, at umalis upang hanapin siya. Lubha akong nagsikap kasama niya sa aming mga paglalakbay nang buong sigasig; sapagkat naalala ko na noong mga panahon ng kanyang kasaganaan ay binigyan niya ako ng bahagi sa lahat ng mayroon siya at minahal ako nang higit sa lahat ng kanyang mga kaibigan. Sa wakas ay naglayag kami mula mismo sa Roma at nakarating sa mga bahaging ito ng Siria, at dumaong kami sa Seleucia; at hindi maraming araw matapos kaming dumaong ay namatay siya dahil sa wasak na puso. Ngunit ako'y pumunta rito at mula sa araw na iyon hanggang ngayon ay kinikita ko ang aking ikabubuhay sa pamamagitan ng paggawa ng aking mga kamay.’

Kabanata VIII. Nagbigay ang Matandang Lalaki ng Impormasyon Tungkol kay Faustus na Ama ni Clemente

Nang makapagsalita nang ganito ang matandang lalaki, nalaman ko mula sa kanyang sinabi na ang matandang lalaking sinabi niyang namatay ay walang iba kundi ang inyong ama. Gayunman, ayaw kong ipaalam sa kanya ang inyong kalagayan hanggang makapagsangguni muna ako sa inyo. Ngunit nalaman ko kung saan ang kanyang matutuluyan at itinuro ko rin sa kanya ang sa akin; at upang matiyak na tama ang aking hinala, isang tanong lamang ang ibinigay ko sa kanya: ‘Ano ang pangalan ng matandang lalaki?’ At sinabi niya, ‘Faustus.’ ‘At ano ang mga pangalan ng kanyang kambal na anak?’ Sumagot siya, ‘Faustinus at Faustinianus.’ ‘Ano ang pangalan ng ikatlong anak?’ Sinabi niya, ‘Clemente.’ ‘Ano ang pangalan ng kanilang ina?’ Sinabi niya, ‘Mattidia.’ Kaya dahil sa habag ay umiyak akong kasama niya at, matapos pauwiin ang karamihan, pumunta ako sa inyo upang makapagsangguni sa inyo pagkatapos nating kumain nang magkakasama. Ngunit ayaw kong ibunyag sa inyo ang bagay bago tayo kumain, baka madaig kayo ng lungkot at manatiling malungkot sa araw ng bautismo, na maging ang mga anghel ay nagagalak. Sa mga sinabi ni Pedro ay napaiyak kaming lahat kasama ng aming ina. Ngunit nang makita niya kaming umiiyak, sinabi niya: Ngayon ay tiisin ng bawat isa sa inyo nang buong tapang ang sinabi dahil sa takot sa Diyos; sapagkat tiyak na hindi ngayong araw namatay ang inyong ama kundi matagal na, gaya ng sinabi ninyo sa inyong hinala.

Kabanata IX. Lumitaw Mismo si Faustus

Nang sabihin ito ni Pedro, hindi na nakatiis ang aming ina kundi sumigaw, Ay! Asawa ko! Sa pagmamahal mo sa amin ay namatay ka dahil sa sarili mong pasiya, samantalang buhay pa kami, nakikita ang liwanag, at katatapos lamang kumain. Hindi pa natatapos ang sigaw na ito nang, narito! Pumasok ang matandang lalaki at, kasabay ng pagnanais na malaman ang sanhi ng sigaw, tumingin siya sa babae at sinabi, Ano ang ibig sabihin nito? Sino ang nakikita ko? Lumapit siya sa kanya at, habang tinitingnan siyang mabuti at siya rin ay masusing tinitingnan, niyakap niya siya. Ngunit muntik silang mamatay dahil sa biglaang kagalakan, at bagaman nais nilang magsalita sa isa't isa, hindi nila magawang magsalita dahil sa di-masukat na kagalakan; nawalan sila ng tinig. Ngunit hindi nagtagal ay sinabi ng aming ina sa kanya: Ngayon ay nasa akin ka na, Faustus, ikaw na sa lahat ng paraan ang pinakamamahal ko. Paano ka buhay gayong kani-kanina lamang ay narinig naming patay ka na? Ngunit narito ang ating mga anak, sina Faustinus, Faustinianus, at Clemente. Pagkasabi niya nito, kaming tatlo ay yumakap sa kanya at humalik, at sa malabong paraan ay naalala namin ang kanyang anyo.

Kabanata X. Ipinaliwanag ni Faustus kay Pedro ang Kanyang Salaysay

Nang makita ito ni Pedro, sinabi niya: Ikaw ba si Faustus, asawa ng babaeng ito at ama ng kanyang mga anak? At sinabi niya: Ako nga. Sinabi ni Pedro: Paano, kung gayon, mo isinalaysay sa akin ang sarili mong kasaysayan na para bang sa ibang tao iyon, at sinabi sa akin ang iyong mga pagsisikap, dalamhati, at paglilibing? Sumagot ang aming ama: Dahil kabilang ako sa pamilya ni Cesar at ayaw kong makilala, gumawa ako ng salaysay sa pangalan ng iba upang hindi malaman kung sino ako. Sapagkat alam kong kung makikilala ako, malalaman ito ng mga gobernador sa lugar, ipatatawag nila akong muli upang pagbigyan si Cesar, at ibabalik sa akin ang dating kasaganaan na tinalikuran ko noon sa buong tibay ng pasiyang kaya kong tipunin. Sapagkat hindi ko maibibigay ang aking sarili sa marangyang buhay matapos kong bigkasin ang pinakamabigat na hatol laban sa sarili ko, dahil naniwala akong ako ang naging sanhi ng pagkamatay ng mga taong minahal ko.

Kabanata XI. Talakayan Tungkol sa Genesis

At sinabi ni Pedro: Ginawa mo iyon ayon sa iyong pasiya. Ngunit tungkol sa Genesis, gumaganap ka lamang ba ng papel nang pinagtibay mo iyon, o seryoso ka talagang iginiit na umiiral iyon? Sinabi ng aming ama: Hindi ako magsisinungaling sa iyo. Seryoso ako nang panindigan kong umiiral ang Genesis. Sapagkat hindi ako baguhan sa agham na iyon; sa kabaligtaran, nakasama ko ang isa sa pinakamahusay na mga astrologo, isang Ehipsiyong nagngangalang Annubion, na naging kaibigan ko sa simula ng aking mga paglalakbay at nagsabi sa akin tungkol sa kamatayan ng aking asawa at mga anak. At sinabi ni Pedro: Hindi ka pa ba nakukumbinsi ngayon ng mga pangyayari na walang matatag na saligan ang aral ng Genesis? Sumagot ang aking ama: Kailangan kong ilahad sa iyo ang lahat ng kaisipang pumapasok sa aking isip upang, sa pakikinig sa mga iyon, maunawaan ko ang iyong pagpapabulaan. Alam kong maraming pagkakamali ang ginagawa ng mga astrologo at madalas din silang nagsasabi ng totoo. Kaya pinaghihinalaan kong nagsasabi sila ng totoo hanggang sa antas na tumpak nilang nauunawaan ang agham, at ang kanilang mga pagkakamali ay bunga ng kamangmangan; kaya inaakala kong may matatag na saligan ang agham ngunit nagsasabi ng mali ang mismong mga astrologo dahil lamang sa kamangmangan, sapagkat hindi nila malalaman ang lahat nang lubos na tumpak. Sumagot si Pedro: Isaalang-alang mo kung hindi kaya nagkataon lamang ang pagsasabi nila ng totoo at kung hindi nila binibigkas ang kanilang mga pahayag nang hindi tumpak na nalalaman ang mga bagay. Sapagkat kailangang mangyari na kapag maraming propesiya ang binigkas, ang ilan sa mga iyon ay matutupad. At sinabi ng matandang lalaki: Paano nga magiging posible na lubos na makatiyak dito, kung may tiyak na saligan ang agham ng Genesis o wala?

Kabanata XII. Si Clemente ang Humawak sa Talakayan

Nang kapwa sila manahimik, sinabi ko: Yamang tumpak kong nalalaman ang agham na ito, ngunit ang aming panginoon at ang aming ama ay hindi nasa gayong kalagayan, nais ko sanang naririto mismo si Annubion upang makipagtalakayan ako sa kanya sa harap ng aking ama. Sapagkat sa gayon ay maihahayag sa lahat ang usapin kapag ang isang tunay na may praktikal na kaalaman sa paksa ay nakipagtalakayan sa isang kapantay na may sapat ding kaalaman. Sumagot ang aming ama: Saan, kung gayon, posibleng matagpuan si Annubion? At sinabi ni Pedro: Sa Antioquia, sapagkat nalaman kong naroon si Simon Magus, na laging kasama ni Annubion. Kaya kapag pumunta tayo roon, kung matagpuan natin sila, maaaring mangyari ang talakayan. At matapos naming talakayin ang maraming paksa, magalak sa aming muling pagkakakilanlan, at magpasalamat sa Diyos, sumapit ang gabi at humiga kami upang matulog.
